## What the course is, in three sentences

It is a members-only, fourteen-lesson Christian premarital course for engaged or seriously courting couples. Each lesson is a short recording plus a private preparation and a couple's conversation. It is not counselling, and it does not store the couple's answers.

## Questions for the reviewer: Lesson 7 (Sex)

1. Does the lesson treat 1 Corinthians 7:3 to 5 fairly, including the reciprocal instructions, the meaning placed on "give", and the phrase "by agreement"? Does it avoid claiming more from those words than the passage supports, and avoid any reading that could be used to pressure a partner into sex?
2. Is the consent wording clear enough: that either person can say no, that agreement is freely given, and that no one owes sex because of an engagement?
3. Is the private-listening arrangement safe, and is it right that "If it helps, tell someone" is omitted?
4. Is the sexual-health and past-history wording appropriate, without being graphic, and does it avoid shaming?
5. Would anything here be harmful to someone who has experienced sexual abuse, coercion or sexual dysfunction? What would you change?
6. Does anything appear medically or legally questionable, or unsuitable across different countries? Please flag anything that should be checked by a healthcare or legal professional rather than attempting to verify matters outside your qualifications.
7. Is it unmistakably clear that the exercise uses words only and does not invite sexual activity or "compatibility testing" during the engagement?

## Questions for the reviewer: Lesson 12 (Past Relationships)

1. Does the lesson separate choices a person made from harm done to them clearly and kindly? Could a survivor read any sentence as saying they must confess or disclose what was done to them?
2. Is "material information" defined well enough that it cannot be used by a controlling partner to demand every detail, and yet does not let anyone conceal something that seriously affects the marriage?
3. Is the alternative route (private preparation and a confidential plan with a support person, when a joint conversation is unsafe) adequate?
4. Are the rule for the person receiving a disclosure ("what you need to decide", not "detail to picture it") and the closing question ("Do I need more time, information or support?") sound?
5. Is the treatment of forgiveness and grace (Colossians 3:9 to 10, and the brief mention of 2 Corinthians 5:17) theologically fair, without suggesting that grace removes consequences or that the past defines a person?
6. Is anything here likely to cause harm, or to be misread, by someone with a painful history?

## What a helpful reply looks like

Please answer each numbered question concisely but specifically. Where you identify a concern, quote the sentence and suggest replacement wording where possible. Then list: (a) any sentence you would change, with your suggested wording; (b) anything missing; (c) whether you would be comfortable with the lesson going live as it stands, after your changes.
