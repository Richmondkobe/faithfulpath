# Course Design: Talk Before You Marry

**Faithful Path Community. Revised after third-party review.**
Based on the book *Talk Before You Marry* (Practical Toolkit and all six chapters), using the Faithful Path Lesson Template, Version 1.0.

**Status: APPROVED COURSE STRUCTURE, Version 1.3.** Original-language and historical notes remain provisional and must be verified before transcript drafting or recording. Approved for drafting Start Here, lesson transcripts, worksheets and lesson pages. Decisions taken after review are in Section 7.

**Main revision in Draft 2:** the course is reframed around biblical preparation for covenant marriage. Safety guidance is kept complete in Start Here, is a short link on every other lesson, and is stronger only in the lessons where the subject genuinely needs it.

---

## 1. What this course is

**The primary message.** God calls couples to enter marriage in truth, love, wisdom, faithfulness and mutual honour. These conversations help them bring their real expectations into the light before making a covenant. Safety protects that journey; it does not define every page.

A guided, pastoral companion for engaged Christian couples that takes the book's **fourteen-week route** and turns it into fourteen lessons.

The book's own purpose, in one line: take two people who can talk easily about most things and give them the ability to talk honestly about the things that are hard. The goal is not a couple who agrees on everything, but a couple who can tell each other the truth and still be safe.

**Who it is for**
- Engaged Christian couples (the book's main audience).
- Couples in a serious relationship deciding whether to marry.
- Married couples who want to revisit these conversations.
- Pastors and counsellors using the nine rooms as a session framework (a separate facilitator guide first; see Section 7).

**What it is not**
- Not a test, a score or a certificate of readiness. The book says so plainly: "There is no score to pass."
- Not a replacement for personal pastoral care, premarital counselling or qualified help when these are needed.
- Not safe for every couple. If there is coercion, intimidation, violence or fear of retaliation, the joint exercises stop (see Section 5).

**Voice.** Pastoral, unhurried, honest. A pastor sitting with a couple, not a clinic. The book's own tone ("Go slowly. Answer honestly, especially when nobody would know if you did not.") is the model.

**Sequence rule (from the book).** The order is deliberate and each lesson depends on the one before. Lessons 1 to 5 build the capacity; Lessons 6 to 14 spend it. Learners should not skip ahead to the rooms because they "look like the practical part".

**Two rooms couples will want to skip.** Room 4 (Children) if they have already agreed not to have children, and Room 8 (What You're Marrying Into) if neither has an ex or a child from before. The book says do not skip either. The course should say so in those lessons, in the author's words.

**Pace.** One lesson a week is the minimum route, not a deadline. Repeat a week when a conversation needs more time. Do not wait for the assigned week to address an urgent concern. Time guidance from the book: Private Inventory about 140 minutes alone plus 20 together; Chapter 4 practice about 65 minutes; Chapter 5 practice about 75 minutes; each room discussion 45 to 60 minutes.

### The teaching pattern (author's direction, refined after review)

This is a pastoral, Scripture-centred course, not a psychology course. Psychology serves only as occasional practical insight. Every recording and transcript follows the same five movements, in the spirit of the author's Instagram teachings:

1. **Read the passage.** Hear the anchor Scripture in its immediate context.
2. **Explain the passage.** Who is speaking, what is happening, and what the passage is teaching.
3. **Draw out the biblical truth.** State the main principle for Christian marriage.
4. **Make it real.** Use a story or scene from the book (for example, the couple deciding where they will live, or the phone call at 11 p.m.).
5. **Practise it today.** Apply the truth and lead straight into that week's one practice.

**Original-language guidance.** Word study is welcome as part of the author's teaching style, but it is optional in each lesson, not required. Use one Greek or Hebrew word only when it genuinely clarifies something an ordinary English reader may miss. Explain it in one or two plain sentences, without asking the learner to remember the original word. Never treat a word's origin or parts as its meaning in a particular passage.

**Historical context.** Keep it short and safe: who is speaking to whom, and what is happening. Do not build a lesson on reconstructed details of a city, household or meeting that the text does not give.

**Accuracy.** Every original-language and historical claim below is provisional and must be checked against a lexicon and a reliable commentary before transcript drafting or recording. The safety guidance stays short and in its place (Section 5) and never takes over the teaching.

## 4. Lesson designs

Scripture marked **(book)** comes from the book's own "Scripture for Reflection" or in-chapter references. Scripture marked **(proposed)** is a suggestion by the drafter, not from the book, and needs the author's choice and an ESV check.

### Lesson 1. Name What You're Afraid Of
- **Idea:** Stop treating avoidance as harmony. Silence feels like peace, but unspoken concerns do not disappear.
- **Scripture:** Ephesians 4:25 (book range, 4:25 to 32): speaking truth to one another.
- **Original-language note (optional):** Greek *pseudos* (falsehood) in Ephesians 4:25. Context: Paul addresses believers about their new life together.
- **Objectives:**
  - See why silence can feel like peace and what it quietly costs.
  - Recognise the six patterns: avoidance, assumption, people-pleasing, defensiveness, conflict avoidance, unspoken expectations.
  - Notice what you each learned about conflict before you met.
  - Understand that a healthy engagement is not one with no disagreement.
- **Take one step:** Each of you, alone, names three subjects you have been avoiding. Then together you choose one manageable subject to practise on. (Book: The Three Subjects.)
- **Tell someone:** Included. Suggest a trusted pastor or mentor couple, never required.
- **Go deeper:** Worksheet: The Three Subjects. Chapter 1.
- **Let it settle:** (1) What am I calling peace that may actually be silence? (2) Lord, what am I afraid will happen if I speak?
- **Notes:** The book adds "a note if your list is blank". Keep it.

### Lesson 2. Build a Relationship That Can Hold Disagreement
- **Idea:** Make honesty survivable before you demand it. Covenant, not contract.
- **Scripture:** 1 Corinthians 13:4 to 7 (book).
- **Original-language note (optional, one only):** either patience and kindness from 1 Corinthians 13:4, or covenant from Malachi 2:14 as a supporting passage. Not both in one recording. Do not present "long-tempered" as the secret meaning of *makrothymeō*; patience in context matters more than the word's parts.
- **Objectives:**
  - Understand why honesty needs safety first.
  - Agree the rules for how you speak, handle tension and repair afterwards.
  - Choose the moment for a hard conversation.
  - Learn what real repair sounds like.
- **Take one step:** Agree your Rules of Engagement: which rules matter most, a pause signal and how you come back. (Book: the eleven rules in three groups; Exercise: Your Rules of Engagement.)
- **Tell someone:** Included, gently.
- **Go deeper:** Worksheet: Rules and Repair Plan. Chapter 2.
- **Let it settle:** (1) Which rule will be hardest for me to keep? (2) Is there something I need to repair today?
- **Notes:** Book includes "In six weeks" review. Build a reminder into the course. The rule "We will not use Scripture as a weapon" should appear prominently.

### Lesson 3. Tell Yourself the Truth First
- **Idea:** You cannot say what you have not admitted. Find your unspoken rules; sort preference, conviction and deal-breaker.
- **Scripture:** Psalm 139:23 to 24 (proposed).
- **Original-language note (optional):** Hebrew *ḥāqar* (search) and *yādaʿ* (know) in Psalm 139:23 to 24. Context: the psalmist praying before God.
- **Objectives:**
  - Find the expectations you never knew were rules.
  - Sort what you want into preference, conviction or deal-breaker.
  - Learn to say what you actually want in everyday terms.
  - Know that this work is done alone, in private.
- **Take one step:** Begin the Private Inventory alone, starting with the three questions (about 30 minutes). Finish the rest over several sittings. (Book: The Private Inventory, about 140 minutes alone.)
- **Tell someone:** **Omitted.** The book says your partner will not see these answers. The lesson should not encourage disclosure.
- **Go deeper:** Worksheet: Private Inventory Sheet. Chapter 3.
- **Let it settle:** (1) What have I already decided I will simply put up with? (2) Lord, where have I not been honest with myself?
- **Notes:** **Privacy-critical lesson.** Fuller warning here. Nothing written in this lesson is stored on the site. Include the book's reminder: keep notes somewhere private and secure, and do not keep sensitive material where someone could find or use it to harm you.
- **Biblical centre:** honesty before God and before each other; knowing your own mind before you speak.

### Lesson 4. Learn to Say the Hard Thing
- **Idea:** The skill of raising it. The first sentence, what actually happened, saying it clearly without hinting, what to raise and what to let go. Lesson 4 prepares the speaker; the conversation itself follows Lesson 5.
- **Scripture:** Ephesians 4:29 to 32 (book range).
- **Original-language note (optional):** Greek *sapros* (corrupt speech) set against *oikodomē* (building up) in Ephesians 4:29.
- **Objectives:**
  - Begin with a first sentence that has somewhere to go.
  - Name what happened, how it affected you and what you are asking for now.
  - Say it clearly without hinting, testing or wearing your partner down.
  - Discern what to raise, what can genuinely be released and what needs support beyond the conversation.
- **Take one step:** Prepare three sentences alone for the subject you chose in Lesson 1, and choose a time for the conversation after both of you have completed Lesson 5. Do not begin the conversation yet. Lesson 4 prepares the speaker. (Book: The First Real Conversation, Parts One and the planning steps.)
- **Tell someone:** Included, optional.
- **Go deeper:** Worksheet: Conversation Record. Chapter 4.
- **Let it settle:** (1) Is there something I have been hinting at instead of saying? (2) Lord, help me say it without wounding.
- **Notes:** Include the book's "If it went badly" guidance.

### Lesson 5. Learn to Hear the Hard Thing
- **Idea:** The harder skill. Receive without defending; listening is not waiting; find the information.
- **Scripture:** Primary: James 1:19 to 20 (book). Supporting passage in the teaching: Proverbs 12:1 (book).
- **Teaching note (no word study):** teach the deliberate contrast in James 1:19: eager to hear, restrained in speaking and restrained in anger. Proverbs 12:1 supports receiving correction, with no separate Hebrew study.
- **Objectives:**
  - See what defensiveness teaches a partner over time.
  - Listen to understand, not to reply.
  - Use the repeat-back rule.
  - Find the useful information inside a hard sentence.
- **Take one step:** Hold the first real conversation prepared in Lesson 4: the person raising it reads their three sentences, and the person receiving it asks a question, repeats back and checks before responding, then agree one concrete response. Review the conversation, not the subject. When you are both ready, swap roles for a second conversation. (Book: Chapter 4 exercise, Parts Two to Four, and Chapter 5 exercise, about 75 minutes.)
- **Tell someone:** Included, optional.
- **Go deeper:** Worksheet: Conversation Record. Chapter 5.
- **Let it settle:** (1) What correction do I resist? (2) Lord, make me quick to listen and slow to anger.

### Lesson 6. Room 1: Money
- **Idea:** Money conversations become conversations about security, shame, fairness and trust.
- **Scripture:** Proverbs 27:23 to 24 (primary): know the condition of your flocks and give attention to your herds. Luke 14:28 is a supporting passage in the teaching only, with its contextual qualification (it concerns the cost of discipleship, not household budgeting).
- **Biblical centre:** honesty, stewardship and faithful planning. Money reveals trust, shame, generosity and responsibility, not only figures and rules.
- **Teaching note (no word study):** Proverbs 27:23 to 24 is about knowing the real state of what you steward. Luke 14:28 is used only briefly, and the transcript says plainly that the passage concerns the cost of discipleship, not financial planning.
- **Objectives:**
  - Notice how money can touch trust, stewardship, generosity and shame.
  - Share your real financial picture honestly, including anything that materially affects your shared life.
  - Agree one financial rule and name one matter still to check.
  - Ask whether any important financial information still needs to be disclosed.
- **Take one step:** Each of you completes the Money Snapshot separately. Exchange the relevant information, then agree **one financial rule** and **one matter that still needs checking**. (Book: four parts, the facts, the meaning, the rules, the hard one.)
- **Go deeper:** Worksheets: Money Snapshot and First Household Budget. Chapter 6, Room 1.
- **Let it settle:** (1) What does money make me feel that I have not told anyone? (2) Lord, teach us to be honest with what we have and owe.
- **Notes:** Fuller warning about **financial control**: if money is being used to control, threaten or trap one partner, that is a safety matter, not a budgeting one. The book asks couples to cover sensitive identifiers when reviewing documents. Say so.

### Lesson 7. Room 2: Sex
- **Idea:** Marital intimacy is shaped by covenant love, mutual care, honesty, patience and honour, never entitlement or pressure. Couples arrive with different histories: some reserved, some experienced, some carrying experiences they did not choose.
- **Scripture:** 1 Corinthians 7:3 to 5 (book). The transcript must explain its mutual and reciprocal nature, as the book does.
- **Biblical centre:** mutual honour, love and freely given intimacy within marriage.
- **Teaching note (no word study):** teach the reciprocal structure of 1 Corinthians 7:3 to 5, where Paul addresses both spouses in parallel. Emphasise mutual care and mutual responsibility, and make clear the passage does not justify coercion. Do not build a study on *opheilē* ("what is owed"), which could sound like one spouse has an enforceable claim.
- **Objectives:**
  - Name what you were each taught about sex and marriage.
  - Say what you believe now, with honesty and without pressure.
  - Name your fears, and agree what needs agreeing, in patience and honour.
  - Ask whether there is anything important your partner needs to know before you marry.
- **Take one step:** Prepare privately, then one 45 to 60 minute conversation. (Five parts.)
- **Tell someone:** **Omitted.**
- **Go deeper:** Worksheet: Conversation Record. Chapter 6, Room 2.
- **Let it settle:** (1) What do I most need my partner to understand about me here? (2) Lord, keep us from shame and from pressure.
- **Notes:** **Care-critical lesson.** Tone stays modest and pastoral, with the positive biblical centre first. Normal audio and transcript, with a simple private-listening note (not "transcript-first"). Provide the book's medical and support notes as written. Fuller warning on **consent and past experiences**. Nothing here should be presented as the answer to fear or coercion.

### Lesson 8. Room 3: Family and Boundaries
- **Idea:** You can love your parents, mean well in every direction, and still create serious problems if you never clarify where the family you came from ends and the one you are forming begins.
- **Scripture:** Genesis 2:24 (book). Reviewer: the right anchor.
- **Biblical centre:** leaving, holding fast and forming a new household.
- **Original-language note (optional):** Hebrew *ʿāzab* (leave) and *dābaq* (hold fast) in Genesis 2:24. Context: do not claim that all households in ancient Israel followed one identical arrangement.
- **Objectives:**
  - Understand how your responsibilities to your family change as you form a new household.
  - Agree what information is shared with families.
  - Decide how you will handle family conflict.
  - Agree one boundary, who will communicate it, and when you will review it.
- **Take one step:** Prepare privately, then agree **one boundary that will protect the household you are forming**, who will communicate it, and when you will review it. (Book: four parts.)
- **Go deeper:** Worksheet: Conversation Record. Chapter 6, Room 3.
- **Let it settle:** (1) Where do I still put my family of origin first? (2) Lord, help us honour our families and build our own household.

### Lesson 9. Room 4: Children
- **Idea:** Two people can say "we both want children" and be holding an enormous disagreement without knowing it.
- **Scripture:** James 4:13 to 15 (reviewer-recommended: holding family plans with humility). Psalm 127:3 is an alternative only if the lesson makes clear it does not imply every faithful marriage will have children.
- **Biblical centre:** receiving parenthood with humility, because human plans are not guarantees.
- **Original-language note:** none. The *atmis* note was removed after review.
- **Objectives:**
  - Describe the picture of family life each of you holds, including timing, parenting and discipline.
  - Begin talking about work, childcare and how you were each raised.
  - Face infertility, loss and plans that do not go as expected.
  - Be honest if you genuinely differ on whether to become parents.
- **Take one step:** Prepare privately, then one conversation. (Five parts.)
- **Go deeper:** Worksheet: Conversation Record. Chapter 6, Room 4.
- **Let it settle:** (1) What am I assuming my partner wants here? (2) Lord, give us honesty about what may not go to plan.
- **Notes:** Include the book's reminder not to skip this room even if you have agreed not to have children.

### Lesson 10. Room 5: Faith and Church
- **Idea:** Two people can agree on the word "Christian" without ever defining what it means in the daily running of a marriage.
- **Scripture:** Colossians 3:16 to 17 (reviewer-recommended: the word of Christ dwelling richly, and ordinary life done in Christ's name). Joshua 24:15 was dropped because it can be used as a declaration imposed by one person over the household.
- **Biblical centre:** an ordinary home in which Christ's word shapes worship, decisions and character. The book's principle must stay prominent: "Do not marry someone's spiritual potential. Marry the person in front of you."
- **Original-language note:** none, following review.
- **Objectives:**
  - Describe your own faith honestly, including what it looks like on an ordinary Tuesday.
  - Plan a practical week of worship, church and prayer.
  - Identify the important conversations still needed about authority, decisions and raising children in the faith.
  - Discern your relationship from who you both are now, rather than who you hope the other will become.
- **Take one step:** Prepare privately, then one conversation. (Five parts.)
- **Go deeper:** Worksheet: Conversation Record. Chapter 6, Room 5.
- **Let it settle:** (1) What do I actually need faith to look like in our home? (2) Lord, keep us from using Your name to silence one another.
- **Notes:** The book's closing prayer is well suited to this lesson.

### Lesson 11. Room 6: Roles
- **Idea:** Two people can agree on "biblical roles" while holding two completely different pictures of what marriage will look like.
- **Scripture:** Ephesians 5:21 to 33 (book).
- **Biblical centre:** sacrificial love, mutual honour and responsibility.
- **Teaching note (no word study):** teach the whole of Ephesians 5:21 to 33: verse 21 introduces the section; the passage addresses conduct within a Christian household; husbands are called to Christlike, self-giving love; the couple must explain what their convictions mean in ordinary life; Scripture must not be used to silence either person. *Hypotassō* may be mentioned but must not carry the conclusion. Any note on first-century household codes needs careful sourcing and must not become the authority over the text. The book does not declare complementarian or egalitarian marriage the winner.
- **Objectives:**
  - Put theological words such as headship, submission and partnership into ordinary language.
  - Compare what each person imagines married life will look like.
  - Discuss how tasks and major decisions will be handled.
  - Consider whether both people can speak, disagree and be heard.
- **Take one step:** Each of you writes a description of an **ordinary Tuesday five years into your marriage**, then you compare and talk. (Book: five parts, including test cases. The ordinary Tuesday exercise must appear explicitly in the lesson, not only inside the chapter.)
- **Go deeper:** Worksheet: Household Responsibilities Sheet (plus Conversation Record). Chapter 6, Room 6.
- **Let it settle:** (1) What am I assuming my partner will simply do? (2) Lord, teach us mutual care that protects each person's dignity and voice.
- **Notes:** The course must keep the book's refusal to declare either complementarianism or egalitarianism the automatic winner ("Complementarian marriages and egalitarian marriages can both be healthy"). Its purpose is to help a couple see what their words mean in practice. The book's Scripture prompt stresses dignity, consent and each person's ability to speak. Keep that framing. Fuller warning on **authority**: where "roles" language is used to silence, control or override one partner, the couple should not continue the joint exercise.

### Lesson 12. Room 7: Past Relationships
- **Idea:** The past becomes present tense quickly: comparison, and other ways it slips in. Disclose what is relevant, not everything.
- **Scripture:** Colossians 3:9 to 10 (reviewer-recommended): put away falsehood and live truthfully as people being renewed in Christ. The transcript may still mention 2 Corinthians 5:17 when discussing grace and identity, and must state plainly that becoming new in Christ does not erase consequences or remove the need for honest disclosure.
- **Biblical centre:** grace does not remove the responsibility to tell consequential truth.
- **Original-language note (optional):** the clothing picture of putting off and putting on in Colossians 3:9 to 10.
- **Objectives:**
  - Decide what is relevant to share, and what is not.
  - See what your history taught you and how the past shows up in the present.
  - Disclose relevant truth with grace and wisdom.
  - Ask the closing question together.
- **Take one step:** Prepare privately, then one conversation. (Five parts. Book: relevance first, separately.)
- **Tell someone:** **Omitted.**
- **Go deeper:** Worksheet: Conversation Record. Chapter 6, Room 7.
- **Let it settle:** (1) What relevant truth do I need to speak with grace and wisdom? (2) How can God's grace help me be honest without living in shame? (The earlier wording, "What am I carrying that I have not yet let God touch?", was changed because it can imply unresolved pain means the learner has not allowed God to work.)
- **Notes:** Fuller warning on **past experiences**. The book does not require graphic accounts of past experience. Say so. Nothing written in this lesson is stored on the site.

### Lesson 13. Room 8: What You're Marrying Into
- **Idea:** You are not marrying into a blank slate. A child, an ex, a dependent parent, a business, an obligation already exists.
- **Scripture:** Galatians 6:2 to 5 (reviewer-recommended: bearing one another's burdens while each person also carries personal responsibility).
- **Biblical centre:** the couple supports one another, but marriage does not make one person's prior obligations invisible or automatically transfer all responsibility to the other.
- **Teaching note (no word study):** Paul calls believers to carry one another's burdens, while also asking each person to examine and carry their own responsibilities. These are not competing commands: Christian love gives help without removing personal responsibility. Do not draw a rigid distinction between the two Greek nouns for burden and load.
- **Objectives:**
  - Take inventory of what already exists: a child, an ex, a dependent relative, a debt, a business, a promise.
  - Say what has not yet been said.
  - Plan for co-parenting and money where they apply.
  - Ask the hard question together.
- **Take one step:** Prepare privately, then one conversation. (Five parts.)
- **Go deeper:** Worksheet: Existing Obligations Sheet. Chapter 6, Room 8.
- **Let it settle:** (1) What already has a claim on our marriage that we have not named? (2) Lord, give us wisdom for what we are carrying in.
- **Notes:** Include the book's reminder not to skip this room.

### Lesson 14. Room 9: Conflict Itself
- **Idea:** The other eight rooms were subjects. This one is how you fight. Become observers of your own marriage. The goal is a couple who can say: "Even when we disagreed, I knew I was safe with you."
- **Scripture:** Ephesians 4:26 to 27 (book range). Anger is not automatically sin, but it quickly becomes an occasion for sinful words, actions and settled resentment.
- **Original-language note:** none. The optional *topos* note was removed after review; the ESV English "opportunity" is kept.
- **Objectives:**
  - See your conflict as a pattern: pursue and withdraw, escalate, or avoid.
  - Write your Conflict Covenant.
  - Use the optional Readiness Review to consider what your experience has shown. It is not a score.
  - Know what to do when you find a real disagreement.
- **Take one step:** Prepare separately, then meet to build your **Conflict Covenant**. This is the only required practice. (Book: Exercise, parts one and two.)
- **Go deeper:** Worksheets: Readiness Review (kept here, **not** a second required practice, and never a score) and Rules and Repair Plan. Chapter 6, Room 9. Also the Weekly Check-In.
- **Let it settle:** (1) Do I feel safe to speak in this relationship? (2) Lord, keep us in truth and in love.
- **Biblical centre:** truth, self-control, repentance, forgiveness and timely repair.
- **Notes:** Fuller warning on **conflict and the Readiness Review**. The book's caution must appear here: abuse is not a shared communication loop, and the language of "our cycle" must never assign equal blame for abusive conduct.
- **Final-lesson completion message (draft, revised):** "You have come to the end of this route, but the conversation does not end here. Finishing does not declare that you are ready or not ready to marry. It means you have begun bringing your relationship into the light and learning to speak the truth in love. Keep your weekly check-in, return to these rooms when life changes, and continue seeking God's wisdom together. May the God of peace lead you in truth, love and faithfulness."

## 5. Safety design (three levels)

The book is clear that joint exercises can increase risk in an abusive relationship. Safety guidance protects the journey; it does not define every page. It works at three levels.

**Level 1. Start Here: the complete guidance.** Learners acknowledge they have read it before beginning. It covers:
- The difference between ordinary disagreement and abuse.
- When not to attempt joint exercises.
- Confidential and specialist support.
- Emergency guidance, and safer-device guidance if a phone or browser may be monitored.
- Appropriate kinds of professional help: a pastor for spiritual care; a qualified counsellor for ordinary conflict; a healthcare professional for physical or sexual health; a legal adviser for legal questions; specialist support for trauma, addiction or abuse.

**Level 2. A short standard block in "Need more support?" on every lesson:**

> If this conversation feels too heavy to carry alone, you may speak with a trusted pastor, mentor or qualified professional. If you are afraid of your partner's response or do not feel free to speak, pause the joint exercise and visit Safety and Support.

**Level 3. Fuller topic-specific warnings, only where the subject genuinely needs it:**

| Lesson | Warning focus |
|---|---|
| 3 Private Inventory | Privacy of the private work; nothing stored; keep notes safe |
| 6 Money | Financial control |
| 7 Sex | Consent and past experiences |
| 11 Roles | Authority used to silence or control |
| 12 Past Relationships | Past experiences; no graphic accounts required |
| 14 Conflict / Readiness Review | Abuse is not a shared communication loop |

All other lessons carry only the standard block.

**Location-aware help.** One Safety and Support page, aware of location, holds regional services. Telephone numbers and service names stay out of permanent lesson transcripts because they change.

**Do not require the private inventory** or graphic accounts of past experience.

## 7. Decisions taken after review

| # | Question | Decision |
