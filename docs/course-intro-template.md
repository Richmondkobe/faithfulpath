# Faithful Path Course Introduction Template ("Start Here")

**Standard pattern for the first page of every Faithful Path course.**
**Faithful Path Course Introduction Template, Version 1.0.** Approved after third-party review. Companion to the Faithful Path Lesson Template, Version 1.0.

**Scope:** This template is for the unnumbered Start Here page that opens a Faithful Path course. It is not a lesson and does not use the standard lesson structure. It may summarise essential safety guidance, but the complete location-aware guidance belongs on the course's Safety and Support page. Retreat sessions, check-ins, full safety pages and course-completion pages use their own templates.

**Short courses:** brevity is permitted. Sections should not be padded to look complete.

---

## 1. Purpose and voice

A course introduction should feel like a pastor welcoming someone at the door, explaining what lies ahead and what to expect, before asking anything of them.

**Voice rules** (the same as the Lesson Template)
- Pastoral, warm, unhurried. Plain words. Short sentences.
- Invite, never command.
- Nothing is scored, graded or diagnostic.
- Scripture is the anchor, not decoration.
- Avoid clinical vocabulary. Prefer: burden, season, step, rest, practice, care, walk with someone.
- Rest is allowed. Stopping for today is a good outcome.

**What this page must do**
1. Say plainly what the course is for, in biblical terms.
2. Say who it is for and what it is not.
3. Show how the route works, without pressure.
4. Explain privacy honestly.
5. Give the essential safety guidance needed before beginning, and link to the complete Safety and Support page, where the course topic needs it.
6. Let the learner begin when ready, or stop.

## 3. Section specifications

### 1. Course title, promise and Scripture
- Course title.
- One-sentence promise in plain words: what the course will help the learner understand, practise or begin, not a guaranteed outcome. This protects courses dealing with marriage, grief, worry or spiritual exhaustion.
- One anchor Scripture, with a one-line note on how it shapes the course.
- Quote only the permitted amount under the chosen translation's licence. Where necessary, use the reference with a faithful paraphrase and encourage learners to read the full passage in a Bible they trust.

### 2. Why this course exists
- Three to five short paragraphs in the author's own voice.
- The biblical purpose first. The problem it answers second.
- No statistics used to frighten.

### 3. Who it is for, and what it is not
- **Who it is for:** a short list.
- **What it is not:** state the limits honestly, in pastoral words. Never claim the course certifies readiness, cures, or replaces personal pastoral care or qualified help.

### 4. How the route works
- Number of lessons and the recommended pace.
- Whether lessons are open or released over time.
- Time guidance where useful.
- A permission line: repeat a week when needed, go slower, take breaks.

### 5. How to use it
- Practical guidance for using the course. Include only what applies.
- **Formats.** Say whether learners can watch, listen or read, and whether transcripts, captions or downloadable materials are available. First-time learners need to know how the course works before entering Lesson 1.
- **Couples and pairs.** When two people take a course together, each person should use their own account unless the course explicitly says otherwise. Private preparation belongs to the person completing it. Joint conversation does not create a right to see private notes. This is a standard rule for every relationship course.
- Other practical guidance: private work done alone, distance participation, what to keep private.

### 6. Privacy
The page must answer four questions in concrete language, not a general paragraph:
1. What account and progress information is stored?
2. Are reflections or written answers stored?
3. Can a partner, pastor or course leader see anything?
4. What remains only in a downloaded worksheet, notebook or the learner's device?

A simple pattern:
- **The site stores:** your account and lesson-completion progress.
- **The site does not store:** answers to reflection questions.
- **Your worksheets:** remain on your device or in your notebook unless you choose to share them.

Do not call writing "private" unless the page explains who can technically access it and how it is stored. The exact wording must match the platform. Never promise more secrecy than the system provides.

### 7. Safety and support
- Give the essential guidance needed before beginning.
- Explain the difference between ordinary difficulty and circumstances in which the learner should pause.
- Link clearly to the complete, location-aware Safety and Support page.
- Keep telephone numbers and service names on that separate page so they can be maintained.
- Where safety is especially relevant, require the simple acknowledgement in Section 9.
- Keep Start Here pastoral and readable. It is not a policy page.

### 8. Your guide (optional)
- Two or three sentences about the teacher and why they can be trusted with this subject.

### 9. Ready to begin?
Flow, in this exact order:

1. Prompt: **"Ready to begin?"**
2. Where required: **"I have read the safety guidance above and know where to find further help."** This is an acknowledgement, not a test and not a declaration that the learner is safe. It unlocks the actions below rather than looking like a third unrelated button.
3. After acknowledgement, show:
   - **"Begin Lesson 1: [Title]"** (primary)
   - **"Stop here for today"** (secondary)
   - **"Visit Safety and Support"** (a text link, not a third button; relationship courses, or others where the topic warrants it, and shown only where needed)

Notes:
- On Start Here, "Begin Lesson 1" is primary because the learner is entering the course. "Stop here for today" remains clearly visible and carries no language of failure. This is deliberate and is not an inconsistency with the Lesson Template, where "Stop here for today" is primary.
- Some learners will reach the safety guidance and realise that beginning joint exercises is not appropriate. "Visit Safety and Support" is their route.

### 10. Footer links and citation notice
- Course support links (the same ones the lessons carry).
- Citation notice: ESV or the translation used; book copyright line where the course draws on a book.

## 5. Adapting to a new course

Change freely: content, Scripture, wording, pace, safety depth.
Do not change: section order, the "nothing scored" rule, the honest privacy statement, the "Stop here for today" option beside "Begin".

**Topic notes**
- **Worry, burnout, grief:** short help note in place of full safety guidance.
- **Marriage and relationships:** essential safety summary with a link to the full page, the acknowledgement, the "Visit Safety and Support" action, and clear guidance for two people using separate accounts.
- **Leadership and ministry:** short help note; add guidance on confidentiality where others' stories are discussed.

---

## 6. Review checklist (for the third-party reviewer)

1. Does the page feel like a pastor welcoming someone, not a policy document?
2. Is the biblical purpose stated before the problem?
3. Are the limits honest, without sounding clinical?
4. Does the privacy section answer the four questions, in words that match what the platform really does?
5. Is the safety summary enough to begin safely, without turning the page into a policy document?
6. Is it easy to begin, and equally easy to stop for today?
7. What would you change, add, or remove?
