# My Next Faithful Step

Twenty lessons ago this course made a promise about what it would not do: it would
not choose a partner for you, predict the future, or produce a score telling you
whether your relationship passes. It has kept that promise, and this page keeps it
too.

What it asks for is one step. Not the whole plan, not the final answer — the next
thing, chosen freely, that you can actually do.

> If you are not currently dating, this page is not asking you to choose a direction
> about a relationship you do not have. Your step is different: look back at what
> Lesson 3 showed you about your own requirements and what Lesson 20 showed you about
> the life you are building now, and choose one thing from there.

## First

Is there a safety concern — fear, coercion, control, violence, stalking, or pressure
from family or church that you cannot refuse?

If so, specialist safety support becomes the priority before every other option. Use
a safer device if monitoring may be present. Do not record your choice or planned
action on this page; contact an appropriate specialist service and let them help
determine the safest timing. **Finding Help Where You Live →**

## Before you choose

Three things worth holding as you decide.

**A step is not a verdict.** Choosing to slow down is not a decision to end. Choosing
to continue is not a promise that you will marry. You are naming what comes next, not
signing for the rest of it.

**You are allowed to change it.** If new evidence, clearer understanding or changed
circumstances alter what is wise, return and choose again. That is not failure.
Repeatedly changing direction without anything becoming clearer, however, may itself
be something to take to counsel.

**Pressure does not decide your timetable.** Family expectations, church enthusiasm, a
partner's urgency or an age you once had in mind cannot supply your consent. Real
medical, legal or practical constraints may deserve honest attention, but they should
be considered openly rather than used to force an answer.

## Choose one

**Continue with wisdom.**
You have looked at what is difficult, named it, and believe it can be carried or
changed. Lesson 17 is where the shape of that lives: one or two things to work on,
what change would look like, help matched to the problem, and a review point suited
to what you are actually watching. Tell the other person what you have decided rather
than keeping it as a private assessment.

**Have a necessary conversation.**
There is something specific that has not been said — a cost you have never named, a
boundary you have never stated, an assumption one of you has been carrying. Choose
the time and the place so that it has room. If the conversation would not be safe,
this is not your step; the last option on this list is.

**Seek counsel.**
You need a view from outside before you can weigh this. Lesson 14 sets the standard:
chosen for wisdom, competence and freedom from the outcome rather than for
convenience or for the answer you expect. Decide what specific question you are
asking and what is yours to share.

**Slow down and observe.**
Commitment is running ahead of knowledge, or a decision is approaching faster than
your clarity about it. Lesson 18 gives this a shape — a stated reason, a proposed
form, something specific that may become clearer and a defined review point. You do
not need permission to decline the next level of commitment. If the relationship is
to continue during the slowdown, however, both of you must freely agree on what that
continuation means; the other person may choose not to wait.

**End the relationship.**
You have seen it, and you are not waiting for more. Lesson 19 is about doing that
clearly and kindly, and its first question is the safety question — answer that
before you plan anything else. You do not need certainty, a villain, or their
agreement. A freely given no is sufficient.

**Seek specialist safety support.**
This replaces the others rather than sitting beside them. Do not wait until you have
finished the course, and do not announce a decision to someone whose reaction you
fear.

**Rest before deciding further.**
Some people arrive here not needing a decision but needing to stop. If you have been
examining this relationship for weeks and every question now produces the same tired
answer, that is a reason to put it down for a while. Rest is a legitimate step — with
one caution: it is a rest from deciding, not a rest from acting on something you
already know is unsafe.

If another person is waiting for an answer or believes the relationship is
progressing, communicate that you are not ready to move forward and, where possible,
give an honest horizon for revisiting the question. Rest is not ghosting, indefinite
suspension or a request that someone wait without choosing. They remain free to
decide that they cannot continue in uncertainty.

## Then write two lines

One sentence naming the step. One sentence naming the first appropriate action and,
where useful, when you intend to take it. If safety is involved, do not write it
here; timing belongs in the plan made with a specialist.

That is all. A step named but not begun has a way of becoming a step you meant to
take.

## One more thing

This course has asked a great deal of you. It has asked you to look at your own
motives, your family, your money, your history, your fear. It has asked you to
observe someone you care about with an honesty that is not comfortable. And it has
asked you, repeatedly, to accept that you might have to act on what you saw.

Whatever you have chosen, hold on to the thing that made the choosing possible:
**before you say yes to anyone, you have to be free to say no.** That freedom is not a
threat to a good relationship. It is what makes a yes worth having — because a yes
that could not have been a no is not a gift, and it never was.

Take the step at the pace that truth, freedom and safety require.
