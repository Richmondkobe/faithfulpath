# Patterns, Not Isolated Impressions

Module 2 asked you to observe. Module 3 asked you to participate — to hold lines,
say things aloud, discuss family expectations, talk about money, and share or
receive information about the past.

That is a different kind of knowledge. Watching someone shows you what they do.
Participating shows you what happens when your needs, limits, history or
expectations become part of the relationship — especially when they differ from
what the other person wants.

This page is for reading what that produced. Nothing is scored, and nothing here
decides anything.

> If you are not currently dating, do not complete this page about an imagined
> person. Read it as preparation, and look back instead at what Lesson 8 showed
> you about your own limits and what Lesson 12's four questions showed you about
> your own past. Your route pauses here until dating becomes relevant.

## Five questions

**What pattern is becoming visible?**
Look across the five lessons rather than within one. A boundary pushed in Lesson
8, a family expectation used as leverage in Lesson 10, money treated as shared
before anything was shared in Lesson 11 — those may be three incidents, or one
pattern appearing in three places. Ask what has happened more than once, or across
more than one area of life.

And note separately anything serious enough that repetition is not required.
Severity and pattern both count. Neither replaces the other.

**What remains unknown?**
Some lessons will have produced blanks rather than answers. Which conversations
have not happened? Which questions have you not asked, and why? Distinguish among
*we have not reached the appropriate stage*, *this belongs to their legitimate
privacy*, *asking would not currently be safe*, and *I avoided asking because I did
not want the answer*. These require different responses.

**Is there something I have been explaining away?**
Lesson 4 gave you this discipline and Lesson 6 gave it a harder edge. Read back
what you wrote there. Explanations that looked reasonable one at a time can look
different stacked together.

**Is there something healthy I have failed to recognise?**
This runs the other way and matters too. Recognising healthy conduct keeps careful
discernment from becoming habitual suspicion. It does not, however, cancel
violence, coercion, stalking, serious deception or another safety concern. Green
flags are not credits that offset harm.

**What needs clarification?**
Identify what may require a safe conversation, more time and natural observation,
appropriate mutual verification, or outside counsel. Verification must be lawful,
relevant and proportionate; it does not mean accessing private accounts,
contacting former partners or relatives behind someone's back, or obtaining
confidential records. Do not manufacture a test, and do not initiate a
conversation if you fear the person's response.

## One thing this module can tell you that Module 2 could not

Module 2 asked whether you could see them clearly. Module 3 asked something else:
**what happened when you took up space?**

You set limits. You said no to something. You raised an expectation of your own.
You disclosed, or were disclosed to. Look at what came back — not at whether you
got your way, because a healthy person may disagree with you, decline a request,
or conclude that two legitimate boundaries do not fit together. Look at whether
you were free to speak at all.

If saying no brought pressure, punishment or spiritual reframing, that is not a
difference of preference. If you found yourself not saying things in order to keep
the peace, notice that too, and notice how long it has been happening.

## Before Module 4

Module 4 is about discernment: testing a claim that God has spoken, deciding whose
counsel deserves weight, asking whether a shared life is actually possible, and
facing the hardest case in the course — two good people who should not marry each
other.

It assumes you have gathered something to discern with. If this page has shown you
how little is actually in hand, that is useful rather than a failure. Where no
safety or serious character concern exists, ordinary time in ordinary settings may
let more emerge. Some unknowns need a direct conversation or independent
confirmation instead. Time should not be used to postpone action on something
already serious.

And if something in this module has left you unable to answer the five questions
honestly — because answering them would require a conversation you are afraid to
have — that fear is the finding, and it belongs somewhere other than this page.
