# Welcome

Before we begin, I want to tell you plainly what this course is and what it is
not.

You are probably here because there is a decision somewhere in front of you.
Perhaps you are wondering whether you are ready to date at all. Perhaps you are
in a relationship and something has been bothering you that you cannot yet name.
Perhaps other people have opinions about your relationship and you are no longer
sure which of the voices in your head is yours. Perhaps engagement is being
discussed and you feel a pressure to be certain that you are not certain about.

**This course will help you see your relationship more clearly. It will not
choose a partner for you, predict the future or produce a score telling you
whether your relationship passes.**

What it will do is give you twenty lessons and twenty tools, each one designed to
turn a vague impression into something you can actually look at. What have you
observed, as opposed to hoped? Which pressures are shaping your judgement? What
has been explained away? What has not yet been asked? By the end, you should be
better able to describe your situation using observed evidence rather than
relying only on a vague impression. What you feel matters, particularly where you
feel afraid, pressured or unable to speak freely. The tools will help you examine
those feelings alongside patterns of behaviour, unanswered questions, Scripture
and wise counsel.

I write as a pastor. I have spent more than twenty years sitting with people
before, during and after their relationships, in several countries. Where I say
"in my experience," that is exactly what it is. Where Scripture speaks directly,
I quote it. Where I am applying a passage beyond its original setting, I say so,
because you should always be able to tell the difference between what God has
said and what I think follows from it.

Two things this course is not. It is not a premarital course — if you become
engaged, *Talk Before You Marry* is what comes next, and the final module will
tell you how to recognise that threshold. And it is not a substitute for help. It
will say that many times, and it means it. Where fear, coercion or danger is
present, the ordinary work of dating well is not what you need first, and the
course will tell you where to go instead.

Take it slowly. There is no prize for finishing quickly, and several of these
lessons are heavier than they look.
