## Audio script and transcript

*(About three minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Let me show you how this course works. It is simple.

Most teaching lessons have the same three steps.

First, listen. Each teaching lesson begins with a short audio. If you prefer to read, you can open the transcript instead.

Second, think. After the teaching, there is one main question. You can think about it privately or write your answer. Writing is always optional.

Third, take one step. Each lesson offers one small, practical step. It is not a long list of homework—just one thing you can actually do.

That is all you need to do.

If you want to go deeper, many lessons also have an optional workbook. The workbook divides bigger questions into short screens. You can use it now, later, or not at all. It is there to help you, not to test you.

Here are a few important things to know.

Nothing in this course is scored. No lesson will tell you whether your relationship passes or fails. When a page offers choices, it is showing you possible paths. It is not choosing for you.

“Not yet” is always an acceptable answer. Sometimes the honest answer is that you do not know enough yet. Recognising that is real progress.

Your answers belong to you. The course will not share them with another person, and nobody is entitled to demand that you show them. Some ordinary answers may be saved to your account so you can return later. Safety-related answers and conversation guides are never saved.

However, someone who can access your phone, computer, or account may be able to see your saved answers. If that could put you at risk, do not write them here. Use a safer device if you can, or think about the questions without writing.

Use this course with wise people. The lessons will often encourage you to speak with someone trustworthy who is not controlled or influenced by the person you are dating. The course works best alongside that kind of support, not instead of it.

Please go slowly. One lesson in an evening may be a good pace, but you are free to go more slowly. Some lessons may feel heavy. If a lesson leaves you distressed or overwhelmed, stop and return later. You can also seek qualified support instead of trying to finish alone.

On the next page, you will choose where to begin.

## A few things to know

* **Nothing is scored.** No lesson will tell you whether your relationship passes or fails.
* **“Not yet” is always an acceptable answer.**
* **Writing is optional.** You can simply think about the questions.
* **The workbooks are optional.** Use them now, later, or not at all.
* **Your answers belong to you.** The course does not share them with another person. Some ordinary answers may be saved so you can return later. Safety-related answers and conversation guides are never saved.
* **Use this course with wise people,** not instead of them. Lesson 14 will help you choose people who are wise and independent.
* **Go slowly.** One lesson in an evening may be a good pace. You can stop and return at any time.

> **If someone may be able to see your phone, computer, or account,** do not write anything that could put you at risk. Use a safer device if you can, or think about the questions without writing. **Find help where you live →**

## Using the workbooks

* **Complete your own answers first.** Some workbooks include an optional conversation guide for two people. Answer privately before you talk together.
* **You never have to show anyone your private answers.** You choose what to say aloud.
* **Conversation guides are never saved.** They are there only to help you talk.
* **Only use a conversation guide if you can both speak freely and safely.** Never share answers with someone who may use them to pressure, punish, control, or monitor you.
