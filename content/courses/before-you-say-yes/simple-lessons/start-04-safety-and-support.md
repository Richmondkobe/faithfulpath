## Audio script and transcript

*(About three minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed "Read the transcript" section.)*

Before you begin the lessons, I need to say something important about safety.

Most of this course is about the ordinary work of dating well: knowing yourself, choosing wisely, working on real difficulties, and deciding with clear eyes. The ordinary exercises assume that both people can speak freely and safely.

But that is not true in every relationship.

The ordinary advice in this course is not what you need first if you are afraid of the person you are dating; if you have been threatened, hit, sexually pressured, followed, tracked, isolated from people who care about you, or controlled through money; if you have to watch every word to avoid their reaction; or if family, church, or community pressure is taking away your freedom to choose a relationship or marriage.

Here is why. Working on the relationship, slowing down, having an honest conversation, or going to couples counselling all assume that you are safe. Where fear, abuse, or control is present, those steps may increase risk.

Please know where to find specialist help. The Find Help Where You Live page lists services by country. It is linked from every page of this course. You never have to finish a lesson or acknowledge anything before reaching it.

If you think someone may be watching your phone, computer, or account, use a safer device if you can — one that person cannot access. Remember that some ordinary answers in this course may save to your account. If someone might see them, do not write anything that could put you at risk. A specialist service can help you think through digital safety for your situation.

If you are in immediate danger, stop now. If it is safe to do so, contact emergency services where you are, or go to the safest place available to you.

This course is not a replacement for specialist help. But a route to help is available from every page.

When you are ready, press “I understand — continue.”

## When the ordinary advice is not for you

The ordinary lessons in this course are **not what you need first** if:

- you are afraid of the person you are dating
- you have been threatened, hit, sexually pressured, followed, tracked, isolated from people who care about you, or controlled through money
- you have to manage your words or behaviour to avoid their reaction
- your family, church, or community is pressuring you into a relationship or marriage

Working on the relationship, slowing down, honest conversations, and couples counselling all assume safety. Where fear, abuse, or control is present, these approaches may increase risk.
