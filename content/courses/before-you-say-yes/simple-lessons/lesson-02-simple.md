## One truth to remember

Using the name “Christian” is not the same as following Christ in the same direction.

## Key Scripture

"Do not be unequally yoked with unbelievers." — 2 Corinthians 6:14 (ESV)

Paul was warning believers against partnerships that pull them away from loyalty to Christ. Although this passage is not only about marriage, its principle matters deeply in marriage because marriage joins two lives closely together. Paul says more directly in 1 Corinthians 7:39 that a believer may marry “only in the Lord.”

## Audio script and transcript

*(About seven minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed "Read the full transcript" section.)*

Welcome to Lesson 2.

You have probably heard the phrase “equally yoked.” Many Christians use it as a simple test: Is this person a Christian? If yes, go ahead. If no, stop.

That test is a good start. But it is not enough. Let me show you why.

First, what is a yoke? A yoke is a wooden bar that joins two animals together, so they can pull a plough or a cart. If one animal is much stronger, or walks faster, or pulls in a different direction, the work becomes hard. Both animals get hurt.

So the picture is not two people standing in the same church. The picture is two people pulling together, in the same direction, for many years.

Here is why this matters. The word "Christian" can mean many different things. In some countries, it is written on an identity card. For some people, it means the family they were born into. For others, it only means "I believe God exists."

But the Bible says that even demons believe God exists. Believing that God is real is not the same as trusting and following Christ.

Now, you cannot see into someone's heart. Only God can do that. It is not your job to decide whether someone is saved. But Jesus said you will recognise people by their fruit. And fruit is something you can see over time.

So instead of asking only whether someone uses the name “Christian,” let us ask five simple questions.

The first question is: Is their faith their own? Some people follow Jesus personally. Others simply follow their family’s religion. Ask them gently how they came to faith. Then listen. Can they speak honestly about their own trust in Christ, or only about what their family and church do?

The second question is: What does their faith produce? Look at how they treat people. Are they kind to people who cannot help them? Do they keep their word? Can they apologise and take responsibility when they are wrong? Going to church is good, but character provides visible evidence of what is shaping a person’s life.

The third question is: Do you share the same foundation? Some beliefs are central to the Christian faith: who Jesus is, his death and resurrection, salvation through him, and the authority of Scripture. If you disagree about these, you may mean different things when you use the word “Christian.” Other differences, such as worship style, may be easier to live with. Do not guess what someone believes from the church they attend. Ask them.

The fourth question is: Do they belong to a church where people know them? Not merely attend, but belong. If nobody else knows their spiritual life, you may be the only person observing it. That can make it difficult to see clearly.

The fifth question is: Where are they going? A yoke is about moving together. Two sincere Christians can want very different lives. One may want to remain in their hometown. The other may feel called to serve overseas. Ask where they believe God is leading them.

Let me say what this lesson does not mean.

It does not mean you need a perfect Christian. Nobody is perfect. A newer believer who is hungry to grow may be a better partner than someone who has been in church all their life but stopped growing years ago.

It also does not mean that sharing the same faith is enough to marry. Two sincere Christians can still be wrong for each other. We will look at that later, in Lesson 15.

If you are already married to someone who does not share your faith, this lesson is not telling you to leave. First Corinthians 7:12–16 addresses believers who are already in such marriages and does not command automatic separation. Please speak with a trustworthy pastor about how to live faithfully where you are.

One more warning: some people date someone who is not a believer, hoping that person will change. Hope is not a plan. If someone comes to faith while pursuing you, give that faith time to grow roots of its own.

Finally, remember: a yoke has two sides. Every question I have asked about them, you should ask about yourself too.

Pause for a moment.

If you are dating someone, ask yourself: How much do I actually know about their faith? And how much have I just assumed? If you are not dating, ask: Is my own faith personal, growing, and known by others?

*[Pause for five seconds.]*

Do not be ashamed if your honest answer is “not much yet.” That is useful to know.

Here is your one step for today. Choose one question to ask naturally, without turning the conversation into an interview. For example: “How did you come to know Christ?” or “What has God been teaching you lately?” If you are not dating, write your own answer to one of those questions.

Let's pray.

Lord Jesus, thank you that you know every heart. Help me see clearly what is real and not just what I hope is true. Make my own faith honest, humble, and growing. Guide me toward a partner who walks with you, and help me to be someone who walks with you too. In your name I pray, amen.

*This workbook will not tell you whether someone is saved. It helps you notice what you have actually seen, and what you have only assumed.*

### Screen 1 — My own faith

Answer each with **Yes / Partly / Not yet**, and add one short example.

1. My faith in Christ is personal, not just my family's or my culture's.
2. My faith shows in how I treat people, even when I am tired or hurt.
3. I know which beliefs are most important to me, and which matter less.
4. I belong to a church where people know me and can encourage or correct me.
5. I can describe where I believe God is leading my life.

*“Partly” or “Not yet” does not disqualify you. It simply identifies an area where you may need to grow. The yoke has two sides.*

### Screen 2 — One area to work on

Which part of my spiritual life most needs attention now? What small step could I take?

> **Not dating anyone right now?** Stop here and go to Screen 10. You can come back to Screens 3–9 later.

### How to answer Screens 3–8

For each sentence about the other person, choose one:

- **Seen** — I have seen this, and can say when.
- **Not seen yet** — I haven't seen it either way.
- **Concern** — I have seen the opposite.
- **Not talked about** — this hasn't come up.

Add a short example where you can. “Seen—when their business failed, they continued seeking God and Christian community” is more useful than writing only “Seen.”

### Screen 3 — Is their faith their own?

1. In their own words, how did they come to faith?
2. Their faith shapes their choices even when family or church are not watching.
3. Their faith was there before this relationship, and does not depend on it.

### Screen 4 — What does their faith produce?

1. Which fruit of the Spirit have I actually seen in them? (Love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control — Galatians 5:22–23.)
2. I have seen them admit a wrong, own it, and change.
3. They are kind and respectful to people who cannot help them.

### Screen 5 — Do we believe the same important things?

1. We agree on the centre of the Christian faith: one God—Father, Son and Holy Spirit; Jesus Christ, fully God and fully human; his death and resurrection; salvation through Christ; and the authority of Scripture.
2. What differences do I know about?
3. Which of these would become daily decisions in a marriage — children, baptism, giving, church?
4. Have I actually asked, or have I guessed from their church?

### Screen 6 — Do they belong to a church?

1. They belong to a church where people know them and can encourage or correct them.
2. If they have no church right now, they value Christian community and are moving towards it.
3. We could belong to the same church.

### Screen 7 — Where are they going?

1. In their words, where do they want their life to go?
2. That is a direction I could walk in beside them.
3. Their faith is growing, and they can accept correction.

### Screen 8 — Spiritual freedom

*This screen is different. A “Concern” here may indicate spiritual pressure or control, not merely a difference between you. Speak with someone trustworthy before moving forward.*

1. I can ask spiritual questions without being shamed or frightened.
2. They do not use the Bible, prophecy, or spiritual authority to win arguments.
3. They can admit they may have misunderstood God.
4. I feel free to ask advice from people outside the relationship.
5. They have never pushed me to move faster by saying God confirmed it.

### Screen 9 — What your answers may show

This is not a result. It is a way to read your own answers.

**Look first at any “Concern.”** Is it an ordinary area where the person is still growing, or does it reveal a direction that does not fit the relationship you are considering? Base your decision on who the person is now, not only on who you hope they may become. Treat a concern on Screen 8 seriously and seek wise counsel.

**Then look at "Not seen yet" and "Not talked about."** Sort each one:

- **About the centre of the faith** (Jesus, salvation, the Bible) → Don't move towards being exclusive or engaged until this is clear. If you find you don't share the same foundation, don't treat it as a small thing to fix later.
- **About church, baptism, giving, or children** → Talk about it openly. Ask for advice if you need it.
- **About character and growth** → Slow down. Give it time to show.

*Many unknowns do not necessarily mean the relationship is bad. They mean it is too early to reach a confident conclusion.*

### Screen 10 — Choose one question

Choose **one** to ask in normal conversation over the coming weeks. If you are not dating, write your own answer.

- ☐ How did you come to know Christ?
- ☐ Can you remember a time your faith affected a hard decision?
- ☐ What do you believe that other Christians disagree with?
- ☐ Who in your church would know if you were struggling?
- ☐ If you could give your life to one thing, what would it be?
- ☐ What has God been teaching you lately?

*How they answer can tell you something. But don't judge by nervousness alone. Notice whether they answer honestly, ask for time, or explain why it's hard to answer.*

### Screen 11 — Mistakes to avoid

- **Dating someone to convert them.** Hope is not a plan. (1 Corinthians 7:12–16 is about people already married when they came to faith.)
- **Assuming that the most spiritual-sounding person is the safest.** Confident prayers and Bible knowledge may show that someone knows Christian language. They do not, by themselves, prove mature character.
- **Rushing new faith.** If they came to faith while pursuing you, give it time to grow on its own.
- **Forgetting the yoke has two sides.** Every question applies to you too.

### Screen 12 — Private journal

1. How much of what I believed about their faith did I actually see, and how much did I assume?
2. What evidence would they see in my life that my faith is personal, growing, and known by others? Where might they honestly say "Not seen yet"?

*Pray for them by name — for their faith, not only for the relationship. If you are not dating, pray that your own faith grows strong.*

> **If you are dating someone who is not a Christian,** you are not being condemned. But please be honest — with yourself, with them, and with God — about where this can lead. Talk with a pastor or a mature Christian friend soon.
>
> **If you are already married to someone who does not share your faith,** this lesson is not telling you to leave. Please seek trustworthy pastoral support.
