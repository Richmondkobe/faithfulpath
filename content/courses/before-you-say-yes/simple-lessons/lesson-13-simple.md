How can I test a claim that God has spoken about my relationship — whether it is someone else's claim or my own?

## One truth to remember

No one else's claim that God has spoken can replace your own free and prayerful choice.

## Key Scripture

"Do not despise prophecies, but test everything; hold fast what is good." — 1 Thessalonians 5:20–21 (ESV)

Paul wrote this to a church that valued prophecy. He did not tell them to stop listening for God. He told them to test what they believed they had heard. Testing is not unbelief. It is what the Bible tells us to do.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About nine minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed "Read the transcript" section.)*

Welcome to Lesson 13.

"God told me you're going to be my wife."

Some of you have said words like these. Some of you have heard them. Maybe from someone you were dating. Maybe from a pastor, a visiting prophet, or a mother who had been praying. And almost every Christian who has dated has asked the quieter question inside: "Is this God? How would I know?"

Christians believe different things about how God speaks today. Some believe God often guides through impressions, dreams, and prophetic words. Others are much more cautious. This lesson does not try to settle that debate. It offers tests from the Bible that Christians from many traditions can use.

Let's start with what is clear. God has already told us a lot about choosing a spouse. Marry someone who follows Christ. Look for truth, holiness, and good character. Do not use spiritual words to cover up lies, pressure, or danger. Be wise, be patient, seek counsel, and test everything.

Notice something else. The Bible does not teach that God has hidden one special person, and your job is to find them. Paul says a Christian widow is free to marry "whom she wishes, only in the Lord." Within faithfulness to Christ, you have real freedom to choose. Choosing a spouse is not a treasure hunt where one wrong step means you missed "the one."

Now, there is nothing wrong with believing God has led you towards someone. Many good marriages began that way. The problem comes when "God told me" is said out loud to the other person.

Here is why. First, the other person cannot check your private experience. Second, it puts heavy pressure on them. Once God's name is used, saying no can feel like saying no to God. Third, strong feelings can feel like God's voice. Loneliness, fear, and desire can all feel like conviction from the inside. And fourth, sincere people can be sincerely wrong.

So please hear this clearly. You do not need a competing word from God in order to say no. Your own free and prayerful choice matters. No one else's experience can make that choice for you.

So how do you test a claim? The workbook has eight tests. Let me make this simple and share five of them now.

First, does it go against the Bible? God does not contradict his word. A "word" telling you to marry someone who does not follow Christ, or someone unsafe, is not from him, however strong it felt.

Second, does it ask you to ignore what you have seen? If the character evidence is poor, or you have seen the red flags from Lesson 6, a claim that asks you to ignore them is asking you to trust a feeling over the facts.

Third, does it rush you? God's guidance can wait for time, observation, and counsel. Pressure to decide quickly is not proof that God is in a hurry.

Fourth, does it cut you off from wise advice? Some spiritual experiences are private, and that is fine. But a claim that must be hidden from safe, wise people who could help you think it through is a warning sign.

Fifth, is the person open to being wrong? This may be the most important test. A humble person can say, "This is what I believe God has shown me. I may be mistaken. And you are free to decide." A person who cannot say that has confused their own opinion with God's authority.

Let me also mention three things people often rely on too much.

"I have peace about it." Peace is worth noticing. But attraction can feel like peace about an unwise choice, and fear can feel like anxiety about a right one. Peace is not enough on its own.

"God opened a door." Circumstances matter. But an open door is not an order to walk through it.

And signs, sometimes called "fleeces." "If they text me before nine o'clock, it's a sign." That is not guidance. It is chance dressed up as revelation.

Let me say what this lesson does not mean.

It does not mean God does not guide people. He promises wisdom to anyone who asks. It does not mean every leader is wrong. A wise pastor's counsel can deserve real weight. But a pastor who tells you whom to marry has gone beyond counsel. And if a leader uses their position, private information, or fear of spiritual consequences to push you into a relationship, that is a serious safeguarding concern.

And if you are in a relationship that is harming you, you do not need a special word from God to leave. God has already spoken: "The prudent sees danger and hides himself."

What if you are the one who feels God is leading you towards someone? Hold it with open hands. In most cases, do not announce it. Instead, say something simple and honest, like, "I'd like to get to know you better." If it is from God, testing will not harm it. If it is not, testing is a mercy.

Pause for a moment.

If God's answer about this relationship were no, would you be willing to accept it?

*[Pause for five seconds.]*

Be honest with yourself. Your answer may show you something important.

Here is your one step for today. If someone has told you, "God told me," write down what they actually want you to do, in plain words, without the spiritual language. Then share it with one wise person outside the relationship. If you feel God is leading you, write the simple, honest sentence you could say instead.

Let's pray.

Lord, I do not want to be deceived, and I do not want to deceive myself. Where your name has been used to move me, help me see what is really being asked. Where I have used your name to get what I wanted, forgive me. Teach me to want your will more than I want this to be your will. And give me the wisdom you promised — not a sign or a feeling, but clear wisdom to see what is in front of me and decide honestly. In Jesus' name, amen.

That is enough for today. You can go on to the next lesson, open the workbook, or stop here and come back later.

## Need support?

When a spiritual claim is used to override your judgement, silence your questions, rush your decision, or make you afraid to say no, the warning sign from Lesson 6 is present — whether the person is a partner, a parent, or a leader. Take the spiritual words away, look at what is actually being asked, and talk to someone outside the situation.

**If a church leader is the one applying pressure,** the person who would normally help you is the person involved. Get help from outside that leader's influence — for example, an independent safeguarding professional, a truly independent safeguarding office, a specialist service, or a qualified counsellor not connected to the church. Someone more senior in the same church structure is not automatically independent.

If a leader has used private pastoral information, threatened you with being excluded or losing a ministry role, or discouraged you from talking to anyone outside their authority, treat it as a safeguarding matter, not a disagreement. If threats, stalking, or physical danger are present, the safety guidance from Lesson 6 applies. **Find help where you live →**

## Part 2 — Eight warning tests

*For each, choose **Yes** (this concern applies), **No**, or **Not sure**.*

### Screen 2 — Tests 1–4

1. The claim goes against the Bible.
2. The claim asks me to ignore what I have seen about character or safety.
3. The claim pressures me to act before there is time to test it.
4. The claim needs to be hidden from wise, safe people.

### Screen 3 — Tests 5–8

1. The claim tries to make my decision for me.
2. The claim has not been weighed by independent Christians who know the relationship and are free to disagree.
3. The person uses fear, guilt, urgency, or promised spiritual reward to push me.
4. The person cannot admit they might be wrong.

> No private claim binds your conscience. A "Yes" is an extra warning. Several are a reason to talk to someone outside the relationship. **And no number of "No" answers proves that God spoke.** These tests can show reasons not to trust a claim. They cannot prove that a private word came from God.

## Part 3 — My own sense of leading

### Screen 4 — If I feel God is leading me

1. What I sense, in my own words:
2. Could an ordinary desire or fear be feeling spiritual? *(Think of Lesson 1: loneliness, pressure, desire, age, comparison, wanting to be rescued.)*
3. If I run the eight tests on my own claim, do any come back "Yes"?
4. If the other person says no, can I accept it without saying they have rejected God?
5. What simple, honest sentence could I say instead of announcing it?

### Screen 5 — Things I may rely on too much

1. Am I relying mainly on a feeling of peace? What might be causing that peace — or that lack of peace?
2. Am I treating an open door as a command?
3. Have I set up a sign or a "fleece"?

## Part 4 — Deciding on solid ground

### Screen 6 — Set the claim aside

*Now answer without using the spiritual claim at all.*

1. **Lesson 2:** Do we share the same foundation of faith?
2. **Lesson 3:** Are my must-haves still intact?
3. **Lessons 5 and 7:** What character and green flags have I actually seen?
4. **Lesson 6:** Are there any red flags or safety concerns?
5. **Counsel:** What have wise, independent people said so far? *(You will look at this more in Lesson 14.)*

*A private claim can never make up for missing character, deep incompatibility, lack of free consent, or safety concerns. A decision to go forward should rest on things both people can see and talk about: shared faith, character, compatibility, wise counsel, freedom, and safety. And anyone may say no without having to prove their reasons.*

### Screen 7 — Spiritual pressure (part 1)

1. A spiritual claim is being used to override my judgement, silence my questions, rush me, or make me afraid to say no.
2. I have been told that saying no will bring God's punishment or make me miss God's will.
3. Someone says my consent does not matter because God has already decided.

## Part 6 — Next step and journal

### Screen 9 — If I used "God told me" to pressure someone

*Only if this applies to you.*

If contact is still appropriate and welcome, correct it once, plainly:

> "I should not have put it that way. My sense of guidance does not decide for you, and you are free to say no."

Do not use this to restart the relationship or ask for another answer. If they have ended contact, asked for distance, or blocked you, respect that. Show the change through your behaviour, not another message.

### Screen 10 — Private journal

1. Am I truly willing for God's answer to be no? If not, what would it cost me — and is that cost the real reason I am hoping for a yes?
2. Could I keep following God without this relationship? Answer slowly. The question is not whether it would hurt.

*"If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach" (James 1:5). Ask for the wisdom God has promised. You may or may not receive a strong impression. You do not need one to obey what the Bible has already made clear. God may have been speaking all along, in the ordinary ways — Scripture, wisdom, counsel, time, and character — while you were waiting for something extraordinary.*
