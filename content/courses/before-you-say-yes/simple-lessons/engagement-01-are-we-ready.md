## One truth to remember

A good engagement is not a leap. It is a step where you can both see the ground beneath you.

## Key Scripture

“By wisdom a house is built, and by understanding it is established.” — Proverbs 24:3 (ESV)

You met this verse in Lesson 15. It fits here too. Engagement is when two people begin making formal plans for a shared life. Wisdom and understanding should guide that decision from the beginning.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About seven minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed "Read the full transcript" section.)*

This course has been about dating. It has deliberately stopped before engagement. But some of you are in a relationship that has been considered carefully, and you are now facing a different question. Not “Should we continue?” but “Are we ready to move towards marriage?”

This page will help you see whether you have reached that point.

First, what is engagement? Engagement is a shared, public intention to marry. It is serious, but it is not yet marriage. Marriage begins with vows.

That matters in two ways.

Engagement is not a light thing. You are telling your families, your church, and each other that you intend to marry. Ending an engagement can be painful and costly. So engagement should not be used to discover whether you want marriage with this person. Before engagement, both of you should already be able to say that freely. Engagement is a season to prepare.

But engagement can still be paused or ended. New information can still come to light. Pausing or ending an engagement is far better than entering a marriage simply because invitations have been printed or plans have been announced.

Before engagement, some things are not just helpful. They are essential. Let me make this simple. Here are five conditions. If any one is missing, engagement is not the next step, whatever else is true.

First, you both freely want to marry. Not one of you wanting it and the other going along. A reluctant yes does not become free just because it is finally spoken.

Second, neither of you is being pressured or frightened. If saying “not yet” would bring threats, punishment, spiritual condemnation, or family anger, you are not free to decide. Pressure does not have to be physical. It can be emotional, spiritual, financial, or from family.

Third, no deep difference is being ignored. If Lesson 15 showed you something “Incompatible,” engagement will not fix it. It will only make the commitment more public and harder to leave.

Fourth, no serious concern that could affect trust, safety, or the decision to marry is still unresolved. If you are in a slowdown, complete the agreed review first. If a serious problem was only recently admitted, allow enough time to see responsibility and lasting change rather than relying on promises.

Fifth, you both understand what engagement is: a season to prepare for marriage, not an experiment to see if you like the idea.

And please remember: if there is fear, abuse, or control, this is not a difficulty to work through inside the relationship. Begin with individual specialist support.

If those five conditions are in place, there are some signs that you may be near the threshold. You have faced the important questions honestly. You have seen each other in ordinary life, including when you are tired, disappointed, or disagreeing. What you each say about yourselves matches what the other has seen. Wise people know about your relationship and are free to speak honestly about it. You have talked about the shape of married life — where you may live, work, family, money, and faith — not just the wedding day. And either of you can raise a hard question without being punished.

These are not a scorecard. No number of them makes the decision for you.

Now, watch for the pressures that push couples into engagement too early.

Family may be eager for grandchildren. Church may love a wedding. You may be afraid of time running out. Physical desire may be strong. The relationship may have lasted so long that everyone assumes you will marry. Or one of you may be ready while the other is not.

Each of these pressures can be real. But none is a reason to get engaged. A long relationship is not owed an engagement. And if you are the one who is ready, say so honestly and then let the other person answer freely. Do not turn your readiness into their obligation.

Let me say what this page does not mean.

It does not mean engagement should be delayed forever. It does not mean you need perfect certainty. It means the decision should be made freely, by both of you, with your eyes open.

So how do you approach it well? Talk about marriage before any formal proposal. A proposal can be a surprise in its timing, but the question itself should not be. Ask wise people separately, “Is there anything you have seen that we should look at before we do this?” Work through the questions on the next page before the ring, not after. Then decide together and say it plainly.

When you do that, the proposal becomes a celebration of a decision already made together, not pressure to make the decision in the moment.

## Five essential conditions

*These are not boxes to tick. If any one is missing, engagement is not the next step, whatever else is true.*

1. **Both of you freely want to marry.** A reluctant yes is not made free just because it is finally spoken.
2. **Neither of you is being pressured or frightened** — emotionally, spiritually, financially, or by family.
3. **No deep difference is being ignored.** Engagement does not fix something “Incompatible.” It makes the commitment more public and harder to leave.
4. **No serious concern affecting trust, safety, or the decision to marry is still unresolved.** Complete any agreed slowdown and review first.
5. **You both understand what engagement is** — a season to prepare for marriage, not an experiment.

> **If there is fear, abuse, or control,** this is not something to work through inside the relationship. Begin with individual specialist support. **Find help where you live →**

## Signs you may be near the threshold

*These are not a scorecard. No number of them makes the decision.*

- You have faced the important questions honestly.
- You know each other in ordinary life — tired, disappointed, disagreeing, with family, and with money.
- What each of you says about yourself matches what the other has seen.
- Your relationship is known, and wise people are free to speak honestly about it.
- You have talked about married life, not just the wedding.
- Either of you can raise a hard question, ask for time, or say “I’m not sure” without being punished.

## Pressures that push couples too early

- **Family** eager for marriage or grandchildren.
- **Church** excitement about a wedding.
- **Age and time** — fear of running out of time.
- **Physical desire** — real and good, but not proof that this relationship is ready. Engagement is not sexual permission.
- **Momentum** — “We’ve been together so long.” A long relationship is not owed an engagement.
- **One person pushing** — readiness can be unequal. Do not turn your readiness into the other person’s obligation.

## How to approach the decision

1. **Talk about marriage before any proposal.** The timing can be a surprise. The question should not be.
2. **Ask wise people separately:** “Is there anything you have seen that we should look at before we do this?”
3. **Work through the questions on the next page** — before the ring, not after.
4. **Decide together, and say it plainly.** Then the proposal can celebrate a decision already made freely by both of you.

## Need support?

If you cannot say “not yet” without fear of what would happen, you are not currently free to make this decision, and the joint questions on the next page are not the right tool. Begin with a specialist service for individual guidance — a domestic-abuse service, or a forced-marriage or family-pressure service if the pressure comes from family or community. Do not raise your hesitation at home first, and do not arrange mediation.

If a church leader is pushing the decision, seek advice from outside that leader's influence. Someone more senior in the same structure is not automatically independent. **Find help where you live →**

## If you are not near the threshold

If you realise that one of the five conditions is missing, a serious concern is still unresolved, one of you is unsure, or the push is coming from outside, that is not failure. It is exactly the kind of seeing this course has asked of you from the beginning. Return to the lesson that fits. Explain honestly what remains unresolved and, where appropriate, when you hope to revisit it. The other person is free to decide that they cannot keep waiting. Do not move towards engagement until you can both do so freely.
