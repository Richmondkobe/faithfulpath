# Lesson 18 — When It Is Wise to Slow Down

## Today's question

If my honest answer is "not yet," how do I say it wisely, without drifting or keeping someone waiting forever?

## One truth to remember

A wise "not yet" is just as deliberate as a yes or a no.

## Key Scripture

"Desire without knowledge is not good, and whoever makes haste with his feet misses his way." — Proverbs 19:2 (ESV)

This proverb is about life in general, not dating. But the principle fits well. Wanting something is not the same as knowing it. And speed is not a virtue when the road ahead is unclear.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About eight minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed "Read the transcript" section.)*

Welcome to Lesson 18.

In the last lesson, we looked at the first honest answer to "Should we continue?" — yes. This lesson is about the second honest answer: not yet.

"Not yet" is often misused. Some people use it to avoid deciding. They do not commit, and they do not end the relationship. They just wait and hope the decision will make itself. Other people use "not yet" to put the other person under watch, as if they must earn the right to continue. Neither of these is what this lesson means.

A wise "not yet" is just as deliberate as a yes or a no. It says, "There is something I do not know yet, and I need to know it before I can say more. So I am going to find out, rather than guessing or waiting for the question to go away."

So what does slowing down mean? It means not taking the next step for a while. The next step might be becoming exclusive, meeting each other's families as future in-laws, talking about engagement, or making plans together. You pause that step for a set period and a clear reason, so that something specific can become clearer. It does not always mean seeing each other less.

Let me make this simple. A wise slowdown has three things.

First, it has a reason. "I just need more time" is not a reason. "I need to see whether the pattern with money changes now that we have both talked about it" is a reason.

Second, it has a shape. You both know what is paused and what is not. Are you still exclusive? Still seeing each other's families? Still talking about the future? Couples may answer these differently. But they need to be answered. Otherwise one person thinks you are still moving forward, and the other thinks you have stopped.

Third, it has an end. Not necessarily a date when everything is solved, but a date when you will look honestly at what you have learned and decide again. Without an end, "not yet" slowly becomes "never." And the person waiting is being kept, rather than being treated honestly.

When is "not yet" the right answer? Perhaps a problem has just been named, and there has not been time to see change. Perhaps the relationship has moved very fast, and you know each other's hopes far better than each other's habits. Perhaps grief, illness, or a job crisis is distorting everything. Perhaps wise people have raised a concern, and you are not sure they are wrong. Perhaps one of you is ready for engagement and the other is not. Or perhaps you honestly do not know what you feel yet.

But be honest about that last one. A person cannot wait forever for you to discover whether you want them.

When is "not yet" the wrong answer?

When you already know. Some people reach this lesson having already recognised, in Lesson 16, that the relationship cannot work. Slowing down will not fix a deep difference in direction. It will only make the ending later and more painful.

When it is a way of keeping someone. Some people slow things down because they are not sure they want the other person, but they are sure they do not want to lose them. That is not fair. If you are unsure, say so honestly, and accept that they are free to decide what to do with that.

When you keep pausing and starting again. If the same question keeps coming back unanswered, more time by itself is unlikely to answer it. You may need wise advice or an honest decision.

And most importantly, when the relationship is unsafe. Do not slow down an unsafe relationship. Slowing down assumes both people can speak and act freely. Where there is fear, control, threats, or violence, there is no safe way to wait and see.

So how do you slow down well? Say it, and say why. A pause decided privately is really a withdrawal, and the other person will feel rejected without knowing why. If it does not feel safe to say it directly, do not arrange a private confrontation. Get support first.

Be clear about the shape. You do not need permission to decline the next step. That is your choice. But if the relationship continues during the pause, you both need to agree what continuing will look like.

Name what you are looking for, and make it something you can actually see. You cannot see inside someone's heart.

Keep living ordinary life. Spend time with each other's friends, run errands, and notice how you both act when tired. Do not spend the whole time in intense talks about the relationship. And do not set traps, check their phone, or read their messages to speed things up.

Respect their freedom. They may agree to slow down. Or they may say they cannot wait, or do not want to. That is a legitimate answer, even if it hurts. A person asked to slow down is not obliged to stay.

And do not get engaged during this time. Engagement is a public promise. It is not a way to settle a private uncertainty.

A word about the review. The workbook helps you choose a suitable review date. Ninety days may be a useful checkpoint for some concerns, but it is not a rule. A simple question may need only a few weeks. A long-standing pattern may need much longer and may require professional advice. Either of you may reconsider before the date arrives.

Let me say what this lesson does not mean, because this is very important.

It does not mean you owe the relationship a waiting period. Continuing requires two free yeses. Walking away requires only one person's no. Nobody has to persuade the other to stay.

Pause for a moment.

If you are thinking about slowing down, what is the one specific thing you need to see more clearly?

*[Pause for five seconds.]*

If you cannot name it, that may tell you something too.

Here is your one step for today. Write one sentence that states your reason for slowing down. Make it specific. Not "I need more time," but something like, "I need to see whether our pattern with money changes."

Let's pray.

Lord, thank you that you are never in a hurry and never late. Give me patience where I need to wait, and honesty where I need to speak. Keep me from using "not yet" to avoid a decision, and keep me from holding anyone in uncertainty. Help me say yes or no when the time comes, and mean it. In Jesus' name, amen.

That is enough for today. You can go on to the next lesson, open the workbook, or stop here and come back later.

## Need support?

Patience and working on problems are available where there is goodwill and freedom. Neither is ever the price you must pay before you are allowed to leave. You do not owe this relationship a waiting period, and no review date obliges you to stay until it arrives.

If what you are facing is fear rather than uncertainty, slowing down is the wrong tool, and it can be dangerous. It keeps you in place while you watch someone you are afraid of. Begin with a specialist abuse service for individual guidance, and do not announce a pause to someone whose reaction you fear. **Find help where you live →**

## Part 1 — At the start

### Screen 1 — The reason

1. What is the specific concern I need to see more clearly?
2. Which kind is it? (A newly named problem · a pace that has run ahead · outside pressure · a concern from wise people · one of us more ready than the other · I am unsure of my feelings)
3. What evidence do I already have — not just fear or suspicion?
4. What information is missing?
5. Can time clarify this, or do I actually need to decide now?

### Screen 2 — An honest check

*Read these honestly. Nothing here is saved.*

Am I slowing down for any of these reasons?

- To avoid a decision I have already made.
- To put the other person under watch or to punish them.
- To keep the other person while I decide.

*If any of these is true, slowing down is not the right step. Lesson 16 or Lesson 19 may help.*

### Screen 3 — The shape (conversation guide, part 1)

> **Only if you can both speak freely and safely.** Nothing on this screen is saved.

Talk through and agree together, in words:

1. What continues during this time, and what pauses?
2. Are we still exclusive?
3. How often will we talk and see each other?
4. What will we tell family, friends, or church?
5. What privacy will each of us keep?

### Screen 4 — The shape (conversation guide, part 2)

> Nothing on this screen is saved.

1. What will neither of us do before the review — such as getting engaged, moving in together, combining money, or telling families we are serious?
2. What happens if one of us no longer wants to continue?
3. Have we agreed this together, clearly and in words?

*If you cannot agree on a shape, the honest outcome may be an ending rather than an unclear pause.*

### Screen 5 — What we are looking for

1. I will know the concern is getting better if I see:
2. I will know it is not getting better if I see:
3. I will know I was asking the wrong question if:
4. Is what I am looking for something I can actually see — or am I expecting them to show me what is in their heart?
5. Can this become clear naturally, without provoking, watching, or invading their privacy?

### Screen 6 — The time period

1. The review date:
2. Why does this length of time fit the concern?
3. If the concern involves addiction, serious money problems, a long-standing harmful pattern, or the practical effects of a health condition: what qualified help has the person freely chosen to seek? What can a professional reasonably say about realistic expectations — without deciding our future for us or sharing private medical information?

*Ninety days is a practical checkpoint, not a rule. It may be too long for a simple question, or too short for a long-standing pattern.*

### Screen 7 — Support

1. Who knows we are doing this?
2. What may they know, and what stays private?
3. What help, if any, will either of us seek during this time?
4. Do I still have at least one wise person outside the relationship?

### Screen 8 — Our freedom

*Read these. There is nothing to fill in.*

- The other person may decide not to wait. That is a legitimate answer.
- Either of us may reconsider before the review date if new information, a deep difference, or a safety concern appears. The date is a reminder to look honestly — not a promise to stay until it arrives.

## Part 2 — At the review

*Complete these on your own before discussing anything together.*

### Screen 9 — What I saw

1. Looking back at Screen 5: what did I see that suggests the concern is getting better? What suggests it is not?
2. Did we spend this time in ordinary life, or mainly in talks about the relationship?
3. What happened when we disagreed?
4. What happened when one of us was tired, ill, disappointed, or under pressure?
5. Did anything new come up that I did not expect?

### Screen 10 — The quality of change (part 1)

1. Did their words and actions match more and more?
2. Did the change depend on reminders, being watched, or fear of losing the relationship?
3. Did it continue after the conversation about it had passed?
4. Was responsibility accepted without blaming, hiding, or resentment?

### Screen 11 — The quality of change (part 2)

1. Were setbacks admitted, or hidden?
2. Has the change shown up in more than one situation?
3. Did either of us feel constantly examined, rather than known?

### Screen 12 — What I now know

1. What do I know now that I did not know at the start?
2. Which of Lesson 17's four kinds of difficulty has this turned out to be?
3. If it is a personal pattern: whose is it, and has that person taken up the help it needs?
4. If it is outside pressure: has enough become clear, even if the pressure has not ended?
5. Did slowing down bring clarity — or only a break from deciding?

### Screen 13 — The whole relationship (part 1)

*Mark each **Yes / Not sure / No**. Nothing on this screen is saved.*

1. Our shared faith foundation is still intact.
2. No safety concern has come up.
3. The main direction of our lives still fits together.
4. Attraction and freely chosen desire are present — in both of us.

### Screen 14 — The whole relationship (part 2)

1. I can say no to this person without being punished for it.
2. I have stayed connected to independent, wise people.
3. There has been enjoyment and friendship during this time, not only watching and questioning.
4. I have taken the original concern back to wise, independent people. What do they think has changed, stayed the same, or become clearer?

### Screen 15 — Comparing together (optional conversation guide)

> **Only if you can both speak freely and safely.** Nothing on this screen is saved. Neither person has to show the other their private answers.

Talk through:

1. What did each of us notice during this time?
2. Has the concern become clearer? In what way?
3. What does each of us want now?

*Remember: continuing needs two free yeses. Walking away needs only one person's no. Neither of you has to persuade the other to stay.*

### Screen 16 — Where this leaves me

1. Has this same question come back unanswered from an earlier pause? What did each earlier pause make clear?
2. Is the question now smaller than it was — or exactly the same size?
3. In my own words: what was the concern, what do I now know, what does the whole relationship look like, and what has wise counsel added?

**Choose one:**

- ☐ **Continue**, using the approach from Lesson 17.
- ☐ **Slow down again, with a narrower question.** The new question is \_\_\_\_\_\_ and the new review date is \_\_\_\_\_\_. *If this is not the first pause, write what each earlier pause made clear, and talk to a wise adviser before agreeing to another.*
- ☐ **Walk away.** *(Lesson 19)*
- ☐ **Seek suitable professional advice before deciding.**
- ☐ **I am not ready to choose yet.**

> **No combination of answers makes this decision.** Continuing needs two free yeses. Walking away needs only one person's no. A slowdown that ends in a clear, kind no has done exactly what it was for.

### Screen 17 — Private journal

1. If I continue, will it be because the concern has been resolved — or because the review date arrived and I do not want another hard conversation?
2. If I end it, what am I responding to — the original concern, what the review showed, ongoing uncertainty, or my own free decision that I no longer want to continue? *None of these requires me to prove the other person wrong.*

*Whatever you decide, thank God for the honesty of these weeks. Ask for courage to act on it — to go forward without pretending the concern never existed, or to end things without pretending the relationship had no good in it. "Let your yes be yes and your no be no" (James 5:12). That is what slowing down is for: to help both of you say one or the other, and mean it.*
