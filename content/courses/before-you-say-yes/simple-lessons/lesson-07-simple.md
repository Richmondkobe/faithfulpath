## One truth to remember

Real goodness is often quiet. It shows in ordinary moments, again and again.

## Key Scripture

“That your love may abound more and more, with knowledge and all discernment, so that you may approve what is excellent.” — Philippians 1:9–10 (ESV)

Paul was praying for a whole church, not for a dating couple. But notice what he assumes. Love and wisdom are meant to grow together. Together, they help us recognise what is truly good.

## Ten green flags

1. They respect your "no."
2. You can disagree honestly, and respect remains.
3. They take responsibility and try to put things right.
4. Their words and actions match more and more over time.
5. They respect your friends, family, and your own identity.
6. They can handle disappointment without punishing or blaming.
7. They tell you important truths, even when it is inconvenient.
8. They can learn and change their mind.
9. They are glad when you grow, even in areas that have nothing to do with them.
10. You both give and receive. It is not always one person who adapts, waits, or apologises.

Welcome to Lesson 7.

The last lesson was about danger. This lesson is about goodness.

Healthy character can be easy to overlook because it is often quiet: a promise kept without fuss, the truth told when it costs something, a “no” accepted without sulking, or a long friendship treated with care. These moments may not feel dramatic, but they tell you something important.

Let me begin with what is not enough on its own.

Politeness is good, but it only shows that someone knows how to behave. Going to church is good, but it cannot prove what is in a person's heart. Romantic gestures can be sincere and beautiful. But a gift tells you less than the pattern around it. Spiritual language may reflect real faith, but speaking well is not the same as being changed. And being popular is not the same as having integrity.

None of these things is bad. Each one is good information. But none of them, by itself, shows you a person's character.

Constant agreement is not always a sign of compatibility. Early in a relationship, someone may be nervous, eager to please, or still discovering what they think. But a healthy relationship should gradually make room for both people to express real opinions and disagree respectfully. If you never hear the other person’s honest view, you may not yet know them well.

So what should you look for? The lesson page lists ten green flags. Each one describes something you can observe between the two of you. Let us focus on five of them now.

First, they respect your no. You say no to something, change a plan, or set a limit, and they accept it. They may ask a question or talk about it again later, respectfully. But they do not wear you down, punish you, or make their love depend on you agreeing. This matters because, in marriage, you will need to say no to each other many times.

Second, you can disagree honestly. You disagree about something important, and respect remains. Feelings may be hurt for a while. That is normal. But if someone needs time, they say so, and then they come back to talk. They do not use silence as a punishment. The Bible calls this "speaking the truth in love." Both parts matter: the truth, and the love.

Third, they take responsibility and try to put things right. A healthy apology names what they did. It does not blame you for raising it, and it is followed by real effort to change. Proverbs 28:13 joins confession with turning away from sin. Saying sorry matters, but changed behaviour is also part of repair.

Fourth, their words and actions match more and more. They say they value honesty, and they are honest when it costs them. They say they will call, and they call. This green flag makes all the others believable.

Fifth, they are glad for you to have your own life. They do not need you to give up your friends, family, church, or interests. They are happy when good things happen to you, even things that have nothing to do with them. They do not need you to be smaller so they can feel bigger.

Let me say what this lesson does not mean.

It does not mean you are looking for someone perfect. Nobody shows all ten green flags all the time. The most important green flag is not a perfect record. It is a growing ability to notice harm, listen, put things right, and change.

But this is about ordinary failures. Violence, sexual pressure, stalking, threats, and control are not chances to watch someone apologise. Safety comes first.

Now, at some point, a relationship that is going well reaches a question. Should we become exclusive?

Being exclusive means both of you freely agree to stop pursuing anyone else for a season, so you can get to know each other more seriously. It is not engagement. It does not mean you belong to each other. It is not permission to speed up physically or emotionally. And either of you can still slow down or end the relationship.

In some cultures, dating is exclusive from the beginning. If that is your situation, these questions still apply. You simply ask them earlier.

Here is one thing that causes a lot of pain. Two people say they are "exclusive," but they mean different things. So say it in plain words. What does it mean for each of you?

A word about online and long-distance relationships: long conversations and video calls can create deep feelings quickly. They may show you something real, but they cannot replace meeting safely in person, seeing ordinary life, and independently confirming who someone is. Never send money, gifts, banking information, or cryptocurrency to someone you have only known online. Do not travel into a situation where you would depend entirely on them for money, transport, accommodation, or a way home. Do not discuss engagement until identity and important circumstances have been confirmed independently.

Pause for a moment.

Think about someone you are dating, or someone you know well. Which green flag have you actually seen, with your own eyes, recently?

*[Pause for five seconds.]*

If you cannot think of one yet, that is not a verdict on them. It simply means you need more time.

Here is your one step for today. Write down one green flag you have actually seen recently. Include the date and what happened. If you are not dating, choose one green flag that you want to show more consistently with your friends or family.

Let's pray.

Father, thank you for the quiet goodness you place in people. Help me to notice it, and not to be distracted by what only looks impressive. Let my love grow with wisdom, and my wisdom grow with love. And make me the kind of person who shows these green flags too. In Jesus' name, amen.

That is enough for today. You can go on to the next lesson, open the workbook, or stop here and come back later.

Becoming exclusive can make stepping back feel harder, but it never takes away your freedom to slow down or end the relationship. If anything from Lesson 6 is still unresolved, do not move forward on hope. Do not use a conversation about exclusivity to test a safety concern. Get individual guidance first.

## Part 1 — What I have seen

*For each, choose* ***Seen / Not seen yet / Mixed / Concern****, and write what happened and when. "Seems kind" is a feeling. "Apologised on the 14th for snapping, named what he did, didn't blame me, and has been different since" is evidence.*

### Screen 1 — Green flags (1–5)

1. They respect my no.
2. We can disagree honestly, and respect remains.
3. When they got something wrong, they listened, took responsibility, tried to put it right, and changed over time.
4. Their words and actions match more and more.
5. They respect my friends, family, and my own identity.

### Screen 2 — Green flags (6–10)

1. They can handle disappointment without punishing or blaming.
2. They tell me important truths, even when inconvenient.
3. They can learn and change their mind.
4. They are glad when I grow, even in areas that have nothing to do with them.
5. We both give and receive — it is not always one person who adapts or apologises.

## Part 2 — What I offer

### Screen 3 — My green flags (part 1)

1. Can I accept their no without pressure or punishment?
2. Can I disagree without withdrawing love?
3. When I cause hurt, can I listen, apologise, and put it right?
4. Do my words and actions match?

### Screen 4 — My green flags (part 2)

1. Do I encourage their healthy friendships and growth?
2. Can they be fully themselves around me?
3. Am I telling them the truth they need to make a free decision?

## Part 3 — Looking back

### Screen 5 — Checking earlier lessons

1. **Lesson 2:** Do we share the same foundation of faith? Is anything important still unknown?
2. **Lesson 3:** Has anything in my must-have boxes been bent?
3. **Lesson 5:** Which places of character have I seen? Which are still empty, and why?
4. **Lesson 6:** Is there any safety concern, controlling pattern, or question that needs specialist advice?
5. Where possible, have we each met the other's friends or family? What did my people actually say?

## Part 4 — Conversation guide

> Only have this conversation if you can both speak freely and safely. If you are afraid to disagree, say “not yet,” or end the conversation, do not use these questions to test the relationship. Get individual guidance first.

*Have the conversation in person if possible, without rushing, and with both of you free to say, “I am not sure.” These screens are prompts only. Do not enter shared answers into the course. Afterwards, you may privately record what became clearer to you.*

### Screen 6 — What "exclusive" means

1. What does "exclusive" mean to each of us, in plain words?
2. What changes about dating apps, private messages, and talking with other possible partners?
3. Will we tell people about the relationship? If not, why not?
4. What are we moving towards? What might show us that this relationship is not moving towards marriage?
5. What will we do if one of us later wants to end being exclusive?

### Screen 7 — Pace and boundaries

1. What pace feels wise for the next few months? Does either of us feel rushed?
2. What would we want to see, and how much time would we want, before talking about engagement?
3. What physical boundaries are we each committed to? *(Lesson 9 goes deeper.)*
4. What access to each other's phones and messages do we expect, and what privacy will each of us keep?
5. What other boundaries matter to us — time, money, emotions?

### Screen 8 — Other people and hard moments

1. Who will know we are exclusive, and when? Is there anyone either of us wants to keep this from? Why?
2. Whose advice will each of us seek, and are we both free to seek it?
3. When we disagree, how does each of us tend to react, and what do we need from the other?
4. If one of us has a concern, how do we want it raised?
5. Is there anything about my current commitments, family responsibilities, financial obligations, health needs, convictions, or future plans that you need to know before deciding? *(Share what is relevant to a free decision without demanding private records or a complete personal history.)*

*Being exclusive does not give either person the right to every private detail, passwords, medical records, or a full history of past hurts. Important truth and healthy privacy can go together. Lesson 12 covers what needs to be known, and when.*

## Part 5 — My decision

### Screen 9 — What became clearer?

*Complete this privately after the conversation. Do not copy the other person’s private disclosures into your account.*

1. What did I understand more clearly?
2. Did I feel free to speak honestly, disagree, and ask for time?
3. Is there one important matter that still needs time, advice, or another conversation?

### Screen 10 — Checking myself (part 1)

Answer **Yes / Partly / No**.

1. I have known this person long enough to see beyond first impressions.
2. I have seen green flags with evidence, not just felt good about them.
3. I have not found a safety concern, and I feel free to seek outside advice if one comes up.
4. My must-haves are still intact.

### Screen 11 — Checking myself (part 2)

Answer **Yes / Partly / No**.

1. We mean the same thing by "exclusive," and we have said so.
2. I can say no in this relationship, and I am still myself.
3. Where possible, people who love me have met them, and I have listened to what they said.
4. I am offering the green flags I am looking for.

### Screen 12 — My decision

Choose one:

- **Yes** — Based on what I know now, I freely choose to be exclusive, and I will keep learning.
- **Not yet** — I am not ready to choose this freely. What I need next: more time, an important conversation, outside advice, or clarity about a concern.
- **No** — I am not choosing to be exclusive. If it is safe, I will say so honestly and respectfully. If I fear their reaction, I will get individual safety guidance before telling them or meeting in person.

*If you chose Yes, write today's date. You will come back to this in Lesson 18.*

*"Not yet" is not automatically a verdict on the person. But do not let "not yet" become endless waiting when a serious concern is already clear.*

### Screen 13 — Private journal

1. Have I been treating politeness, church attendance, romantic gestures, spiritual words, popularity, or constant agreement as proof of character? Which ones?
2. Setting those aside, what real evidence of character have I actually seen?

*Pray Philippians 1:9–10 for yourself and for this person — that what is truly good in each of you would be seen clearly, and that love and wisdom would grow together.*
