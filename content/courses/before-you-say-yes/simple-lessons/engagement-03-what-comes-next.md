## One truth to remember

The freedom to say no is what makes a yes mean anything at all.

## Key Scripture

“Ponder the path of your feet; then all your ways will be sure.” — Proverbs 4:26 (ESV)

You do not need to know your whole future today. You do need to look honestly at where you are standing and choose your next step with wisdom.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About six minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed "Read the full transcript" section.)*

You have reached the end of this course.

What happens now depends on what the questions showed you. Let me make this simple. There are four honest places you might be standing.

First, you may not be ready yet. One of you said “not yet,” or a question opened something that is still unresolved. That is not a failed course. It is the course working. The purpose of these questions is to discover important things before a public promise, not after one.

Return to the lesson that fits. Explain honestly what remains unresolved and, where appropriate, when you hope to look at it again. Do not ask the other person to wait indefinitely. They are free to decide that they cannot keep waiting. And remember: a review date is not a promise that the answer will be yes.

Second, you may have decided not to continue. Lesson 19 will help you end the relationship clearly and kindly. Answer its safety question before you plan or announce anything. A freely given no is enough. You do not need certainty, agreement, a villain, or a case that would satisfy someone else.

Third, you may be ready to move towards engagement, or you may already have become engaged. If so, the work is not over. It changes. Engagement is a season of preparation. The conversations that belong to it go deeper than anything in this dating course: money, family, faith, sex, conflict, children, and the shape of your marriage. Begin them before wedding plans gather speed, because plans are much easier to make than to undo.

Please find good premarital preparation. Choose a trained person or programme that will ask thoughtful, difficult questions rather than simply helping you plan a ceremony. Ask what will be covered, how disagreements will be handled, and whether serious concerns will be named clearly. If there is ever fear, pressure, or abuse, seek individual specialist help, not joint counselling.

Hold on to this: engagement does not take away your freedom to respond to new information. An engagement can be paused. It can be ended. Either is far better than entering a marriage simply because invitations have been printed, deposits have been paid, or plans have been announced.

Fourth, you may not be dating at all. That was a perfectly good way to take this course. What you did was not practice for later. It was the real work. You have looked honestly at your motives, what you are looking for, your patterns, and your limits. Keep what you wrote. It may be more honest now than it would be later, in the middle of strong feelings.

Now, let me say what this course does not give you. It gives you no score, no verdict on your relationship, and no certificate saying you are ready to marry.

What it has given you is language for things that may once have been only a feeling. It has given you tools to separate what you have seen from what you have hoped. And it has given you some important differences that are hard to forget: attraction and wisdom, charm and character, a difficulty and a danger, a good person and a right relationship, one event and a pattern, privacy and secrecy.

Support does not end here. Find Help Where You Live is always available, whether or not you are working through lessons and whether or not you return to this course.

If something came up during these lessons that is bigger than a course — past harm, a fear that will not lift, or a pattern you cannot break alone — please take it to someone qualified. That is not a sign that you did this badly. It is a wise response to having seen something important.

This course began by promising that it would not choose a partner for you, predict your future, or tell you whether your relationship passes. It has kept that promise. I hope you can now see why.

The freedom to say no is not a threat to a good relationship. It is what makes a yes mean anything at all. A yes that could never have been a no is not freely given. Whatever you have decided, and whatever comes next, carry that with you.

Let us pray.

Lord, thank you for every step of this journey. Thank you for truth, even when it was hard to hear, and for grace, even when I needed it most. Go with me into whatever comes next. If I marry, help me build a home founded on you. If I do not, help me live a full and faithful life with you. And in every relationship, help me love with honesty, freedom, and care. In Jesus' name, amen.

Go well.

## Where are you standing?

### If you are not ready yet

One of you said “not yet,” or something is still unresolved. **That is not a failed course. It is the course working.**

Go back to the lesson that fits:

- **Direction or faith** → Lessons 2, 15, and 16
- **Moving too fast** → Lesson 18
- **A difficulty you can both name and safely work on** → Lesson 17
- **Fear, pressure, or abuse** → individual specialist support. **Find help where you live →**

Explain honestly what remains unresolved and, where appropriate, when you hope to look at it again. The other person is free to decide that they cannot keep waiting. *A review date is not a promise that the answer will be yes.*

### If you decide not to continue

**Lesson 19** will help you end it clearly and kindly. **Answer its safety question first**, before you plan or announce anything. If you are afraid of how the person will react, plan the ending with a specialist service.

*A freely given no is enough. You do not need certainty, agreement, a villain, or a case that would satisfy someone else.*

### If you are moving towards engagement or are engaged

The work is not over. It changes.

- **Begin the deeper conversations early** — money, family, faith, sex, conflict, children, and the shape of your marriage — before wedding plans gather speed.
- **Find good premarital preparation.** Choose a trained person or programme that will ask thoughtful, difficult questions rather than simply helping you plan a ceremony. Ask what will be covered, how disagreements will be handled, and whether serious concerns will be named clearly.
- **If there is fear, pressure, or abuse,** seek individual specialist help, not joint counselling.

> **Engagement does not take away your freedom to respond to new information.** An engagement can be paused, and it can be ended. Either is far better than a marriage entered because invitations have been printed or plans announced. If something troubles you after the yes, Lesson 16 is still here, and so is the support page.

*When it is available, **Talk Before You Marry** is the natural next course for thorough Christian premarital preparation.*

### If you are not dating

That was a perfectly good way to take this course. **What you did was not practice. It was the real work.**

Keep what you wrote. Lessons 1, 3, and 7 all produce material you may want when there is someone to think about — and it may be more honest now than it would be later.

## What this course does not give you

**No score. No verdict on your relationship. No certificate saying you are ready to marry.** If the course records that you finished, that means only that you completed the material. It does not approve or certify your relationship.

## What it has given you

Language for things that may once have been only a feeling. Tools to separate what you have seen from what you have hoped. And some important differences that are hard to forget:

- attraction and wisdom
- charm and character
- a difficulty and a danger
- a good person and a right relationship
- one event and a pattern
- privacy and secrecy

## A last word

> **The freedom to say no is not a threat to a good relationship. It is what makes a yes mean anything at all.**

Whatever you have decided, and whatever comes next, carry that with you.
