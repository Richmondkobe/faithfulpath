## Audio script and transcript

*(About six minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

You have reached the fourth check-in in this course.

In Module 4, you learned how to examine a claim that God has spoken. You thought about whose advice to trust. You asked whether your two lives could fit together. And you faced one of the hardest questions: Can two good Christians still be wrong for each other?

This check-in asks one simple question: What is your next wise step?

This page will not give you a score or make the decision for you. No ordinary choice means that your relationship has passed or failed.

Before anything else, one question comes first: Is there a safety concern?

Are you afraid of the person? Is there pressure, control, violence, stalking, or spiritual or family pressure that you do not feel free to refuse?

If so, stop here. Safety comes before the rest of this course. The lessons that follow assume a relationship in which you are free to speak, disagree, seek advice, and leave. Please go to Find Help Where You Live and seek individual specialist guidance.

If there is no safety concern, look at what you actually know.

Remember the four places information can come from: what the person told you, what you hoped or assumed, what other people told you, and what you have observed yourself.

None of these should be accepted without thought. Something you observed may still need context. Something another person said may be accurate, biased, or incomplete. Weigh the quality of the evidence, not simply the amount.

Now ask three questions.

What is clear? What could you describe to a wise person using specific examples rather than only a hope, fear, or general feeling?

What is still unclear—and why? Perhaps the relationship has not reached that stage. Perhaps the matter is private. Perhaps you have avoided asking because you fear the answer. Or perhaps asking does not feel safe.

What have wise people added? Have independent people identified the same specific concern or encouragement? What did they actually observe, and what did they hear only from you?

Pause for a moment.

What is one thing that is clearer now than it was before this module?

*[Pause for five seconds.]*

Next, you will choose your next step.

There are five ordinary choices and one separate safety route. The ordinary choices are not grades, and one is not automatically better than another. Choose the step that fits what you know now—not the outcome you hope will eventually happen.

Let me say two important things before Module 5.

First, waiting is still a decision. Continuing to learn about the relationship can be wise. But waiting becomes avoidance when you are using it to postpone a conclusion you have already reached.

Here is a simple question: Can you name what you are still waiting to learn, how it may reasonably become clear, and when you will review it?

Second, you do not owe the relationship an experiment. Module 5 will offer ways to continue, slow down, and work on a specific problem. Those options are available, but they are not requirements. You do not have to try one more thing before you are allowed to say no.

In Module 5, the course moves from what you see to what you will do. You will look at when it is wise to continue, when to slow down, when to walk away, and how to face the fear that no one else will come.

Let us pray.

Lord, thank you for everything you have shown me so far. Help me be honest about what I know and what I do not know. Keep me from rushing, and keep me from hiding behind endless waiting. Give me wisdom to choose my next step and courage to take it. Whatever I decide, help me walk in truth, faithfulness, and peace. In Jesus’ name, amen.

### ☐ Address a specific concern

I can name the concern clearly.

If it begins as a feeling, I will describe what I feel, what seems connected to it, and what remains uncertain. I will not present a feeling as proof.

If we can both speak freely and safely, I will choose a suitable time and place to talk. If I become afraid of the person’s reaction, I will stop and seek individual guidance instead.

If the concern involves health, law, money, addiction, or safeguarding, I will seek suitable professional advice rather than expecting one conversation to solve it.
