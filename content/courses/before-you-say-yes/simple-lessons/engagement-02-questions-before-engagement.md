# Engagement 2 — Questions to Answer Before Engagement

## Today's question

Are we both freely and honestly ready to declare that we intend to marry?

## One truth to remember

Engagement needs two free and informed yeses. One no ends the question. One "not yet" postpones it.

## Key Scripture

"Let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger." — James 1:19 (ESV)

James was teaching Christians how to receive truth with humility. The principle matters here too. Honest answers need to be heard without pressure, argument, or anger.

## The rule for these questions

> **Engagement requires two free and informed yeses.**
> **One "no" ends the proposal. One "not yet" postpones it.**
> **Neither answer may be argued into a yes.**
>
> A yes can also change before engagement. And becoming engaged does not take away either person's freedom to raise concerns, pause, or end the engagement later.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About five minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed "Read the full transcript" section.)*

Welcome to the second engagement page.

The questions on this page are threshold questions. They do not ask whether you are ready to be married. They ask whether you are ready to declare that you intend to marry. Before engagement, you should know whether you broadly agree on the big areas of married life: faith, money, family, children, and the shape of your marriage. You should also know whether any important fact would change your willingness to go forward. The detailed planning comes later, during the engagement season.

These questions do not give a score. They do not approve or reject your relationship. They simply help you both decide with your eyes open.

Let me make the rule for these questions simple. There are three parts.

First, engagement needs two free and informed yeses.

Second, one no ends the proposal. One "not yet" postpones it.

Third, neither answer may be argued into a yes. A "not yet" that is argued into a yes was never really a yes.

So how should you answer these questions? Answer them separately, in writing, before you talk about them together. Do not skip a question just because it feels awkward. "Not sure" is an honest answer, not a failure.

You may take several days. There are many questions, and they deserve time.

Your answers are private. Neither of you has to show the other what you wrote. When you talk together, you each decide what to say out loud. Screens that ask about fear, pressure, or freedom are not saved.

Now, please listen carefully to this safety note. Do not do these questions together if you are afraid of the other person. Do not do them together if saying "not yet" would bring threats, punishment, spiritual condemnation, or anger from family. If any question shows you fear, pressure, being watched, or that you cannot safely say "not yet," stop. Do not compare answers. Do not raise it with the other person. Seek independent specialist help first.

One more thing about honesty. Some questions ask whether there is important information the other person needs to know. That means something that could reasonably affect their willingness to marry — for example, a previous marriage, children, serious debt, a legal or immigration matter, an active addiction, or a health condition that would significantly shape married life. It does not mean anyone is entitled to every private detail, confidential records, or the full story of past trauma. Privacy is healthy. Hiding an important fact is not.

When you have both finished, the page will guide you through comparing your answers. Start with the questions where your answers differ. But also look at answers that match but are vague. Agreement is not just giving the same answer. It is understanding what each person means by it.

If either of you answers no, receive that answer as final. It does not need an explanation you find good enough.

If either of you answers "not yet," name what is still unresolved, and go back to the lesson that fits. Do not set an engagement date.

And if you both answer yes, freely, with the hard questions faced and no serious concern left open, you may be near the threshold of engagement. This page does not certify your relationship. It helps you approach that step with your eyes open.

Let us pray.

Lord, give us honesty as we answer these questions. Help us tell the truth, even when it is uncomfortable. Help us listen to each other without pressure. If our answer is yes, let it be free and full. If it is "not yet" or no, give us courage to say it and grace to receive it. In Jesus' name, amen.

## How to answer

- Answer **separately, in writing,** before you talk together.
- For judgement questions, answer **Yes / Not sure / No**, with a short explanation.
- For information questions, answer in one or two sentences.
- **"Not sure" is an honest answer,** not a failure.
- If a question cannot be answered safely, or asks for information that is not yours to share, note that and seek individual guidance.
- You may take several days.

## Part 1 — Free choice

### Screen 1 — Do I freely want this?

*Nothing on this screen is saved.*

1. Do I want to marry this person? Not "could I" or "should I" — do I?
2. Would I still want to if nobody knew we were together and nobody was waiting for an answer?
3. If I said "not yet," what would happen?
4. If they said "not yet," what would I do?
5. Has either of us ever said yes to something in this relationship because saying no felt impossible?

> If the answer to question 5 is yes, do not move towards engagement until you have looked at what happened, on your own and with the right support. **A relationship is not ready for a bigger promise while free consent in the present relationship is uncertain.** If fear or pressure was involved, use the safety route, not this joint exercise.

## Part 2 — What we have faced

### Screen 2 — Open questions

1. Which lessons and workbooks have we used, and what did they show us?
2. Is any concern still open?
3. Is anything still under review that has not been settled?
4. Is there anything I have decided to live with, but have not told them?
5. Am I hoping engagement will fix something?

## Part 3 — What we know

### Screen 3 — What I have seen

1. What have I seen in the ordinary situations we have shared — disappointment, disagreement, plans falling through, responsibility, or pressure?
2. Did what I saw match who they say they are?
3. In what important situations have I not yet seen them? Does that matter before engagement?
4. What have they seen of me in the same situations?
5. Which of their habits do I already know I will find hard — and have I told them?

### Screen 4 — Important information

*Important information means something that could reasonably affect the other person's willingness to marry — such as a previous marriage, children or dependants, serious debt, a legal or immigration matter, an active addiction, a health condition that would significantly shape married life, or an existing commitment. It does not mean every private detail, confidential records, another person's secrets, or the full story of past trauma.*

1. Is there any important fact about my past, my responsibilities, or my present situation that could reasonably affect their decision?
2. Have I shared it, at a suitable level of detail?
3. Am I keeping healthy privacy — or hiding something because I fear their answer?
4. Am I being asked to share anything that is not mine to share?

## Part 4 — Faith and direction

### Screen 5 — Our spiritual foundation

*Nothing on this screen is saved because one question concerns spiritual pressure.*

1. Do we both personally trust and follow Christ — not just rely on family, church attendance, or a label?
2. Does the faith each of us talks about match the life the other has seen?
3. Can we pray, read the Bible, and disagree about spiritual things without performing or controlling?
4. Is there a big difference in belief, church commitment, or spiritual direction that we are putting off?
5. Has either of us used God, the Bible, prophecy, or a pastor's authority to pressure the other?

### Screen 6 — Our direction

1. Have we talked about what married life would actually look like — where we would live, work, family shape, whether we want children, and the place of God?
2. Do our two pictures broadly match, or is one of us assuming the other will change their mind?
3. Is there a difference in calling, location, children, or the shape of marriage that we have agreed to "sort out later"?
4. Do we agree on what engagement is, and roughly how long we expect it to last?

## Part 5 — Counsel

### Screen 7 — Wise voices (part 1)

1. Which wise, independent people know each of us and our relationship well enough to speak into it?
2. Have we each asked them, separately, whether there is anything we should look at before engagement?
3. What specific concern, if any, came back — and what did we do with it?
4. Have we dismissed a real, specific concern just because it was inconvenient?

### Screen 8 — Wise voices (part 2)

1. Have we separated genuine concern from prejudice, possessiveness, personal preference, or fear of losing influence?
2. Can our advisers respect a decision different from the one they would prefer?
3. Is there anyone we have avoided asking?

## Part 6 — Pressure

### Screen 9 — Where pressure comes from

*Nothing on this screen is saved.*

1. Who, apart from the two of us, wants this engagement — and how much does that affect my answer?
2. Which pressure on me is strongest right now: age or time, fear no one else will come, physical desire, how long we have been together, the other person's readiness, family, or church?
3. If I set these pressures aside, what reasons for engagement remain?

### Screen 10 — Deadlines and plans

1. Can I handle the disappointment that a "not yet" might cause?
2. Have we tied engagement to an artificial deadline?
3. Have any deposits, announcements, or family plans been made that now make us feel unable to reconsider?

## Part 7 — The relationship itself

### Screen 11 — Freedom in the relationship

*Nothing on this screen is saved.*

1. Can I raise a hard question, disagree, or say "not yet" without revenge, long silences, fear, contempt, or spiritual pressure?
2. Can this person feel disappointed without making me responsible for fixing it?
3. Do I like them — not only love them — and enjoy their company in ordinary life?
4. Can I keep my conscience, my voice, my friendships, my responsibilities, and healthy independence in this relationship?

### Screen 12 — Who I am becoming

*Nothing on this screen is saved.*

1. Am I becoming more truthful, responsible, loving, and spiritually mature — or more secretive, fearful, and dependent?
2. Have I given up an important conviction or friendship just to keep this relationship?
3. When I imagine ordinary married life, not just the wedding, what do I feel — and what evidence seems connected to that feeling?
4. If I feel hesitant: is it the normal nervousness of a serious commitment, or is it linked to something specific I have seen again and again?

## Part 8 — Ready for engagement itself

### Screen 13 — Preparing well (part 1)

1. Have we already talked about marriage clearly enough that a proposal will not require an unprepared answer?
2. Are we both willing to do thorough premarital preparation?
3. Are we willing to wait before setting a wedding date until the major premarital conversations have begun?

### Screen 14 — Preparing well (part 2)

1. If engagement reveals a serious concern, are we both willing to pause — rather than letting deposits, announcements, or embarrassment decide for us?
2. Do we understand that **engagement gives neither person sexual rights nor more control over the other**?
3. Have we agreed that either of us is still free to raise concerns after engagement?

## Part 9 — My answer

### Screen 15 — My decision

*Nothing on this screen is saved or shown back to you.*

**Choose one:**

- ☐ **Yes** — I freely want to move towards engagement.
- ☐ **Not yet** — I need more information, more time, or something resolved first.
- ☐ **No** — I do not intend to marry this person.

Then:

1. What has shaped my answer?
2. What pressure makes it hard to say?
3. Am I hoping their answer will rescue me from owning mine?
4. **If "not yet":** what exactly is missing, and when would be a suitable time to look again? *(A review date is not a promise that the answer will be yes.)*
5. **If "no":** am I ready to say so clearly, rather than asking them to wait indefinitely? Who will I talk to about my answer before we compare?

> **If either answer is "no," do not become engaged. If either answer is "not yet," do not become engaged yet.**

## Part 10 — Comparing your answers

### Screen 16 — Conversation guide for two

> **Only if you can both speak freely and safely.** Nothing on this screen is saved. Neither of you has to show the other your written answers. You each choose what to say aloud.

1. **Start where your answers differ.** But also look at answers that match but are vague or based on assumptions. *Agreement is not giving the same answer. It is understanding what each person means by it.*
2. **Where the difference is about direction or faith,** go back to Lessons 2, 15, and 16. **Where it is about safety or freedom,** go to Lesson 6. **Where it is about pace or readiness,** go to Lesson 18.
3. **If either of you said "no,"** receive it as final. It does not need an explanation you find good enough, and it is not an invitation to argue.
4. **If either of you said "not yet,"** name what is still unresolved, and go back to the lesson that fits. Do not set an engagement date. *A "not yet" that is argued into a yes was never a yes.*
5. **If you both freely said yes** — with the hard questions faced and no serious concern left open — you may be near the threshold of engagement. These questions do not certify your relationship. They help you approach that step with your eyes open.

## Need support?

If these questions have shown you that you cannot safely say "not yet," that is the most important thing this page has told you. It is no longer a question about engagement. Begin with a specialist service for individual guidance — a domestic-abuse service, or a forced-marriage or family-pressure service if the pressure comes from family or community. Do not compare answers, do not raise the concern with anyone whose reaction you fear, and do not agree to mediation as the first response to pressure or abuse. **Find help where you live →**
