# Lesson 17 — When It Is Wise to Continue

## Today's question

## One truth to remember

A wise yes is a decision, not a drift.

## Key Scripture

"With all humility and gentleness, with patience, bearing with one another in love." — Ephesians 4:2 (ESV)

Paul is describing life in the church, not dating. But humility, gentleness, and patience matter wherever imperfect people share life. "Bearing with" means making room for what is imperfect in someone. It does not mean carrying another adult's responsibilities, hiding harm, or accepting behaviour that destroys safety or dignity.

## Four kinds of difficulty

| Kind | What it looks like | What usually helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Skill**                                   | You both want the same thing, but do not know how to get there.                                                  | Learning, practice, sometimes a counsellor.                                        |
| **Outside pressure**                        | Illness, grief, work, money, immigration, or family stress is weighing on you.                                   | Name the pressure before judging the relationship.                                 |
| **Personal pattern**                        | One person's long-standing habit — unmanaged anger, withdrawal, fear, dishonesty, money problems, or substance use — is showing up again. | Individual work, often with professional help. It is not the partner's job to fix. |
| **Different directions**                    | You want different lives or marriages.                                                                           | Effort alone cannot make opposite futures fit. Return to Lessons 15 and 16.         |

*These kinds can overlap and change over time. And naming the kind never excuses harm. If there is fear, control, violence, or loss of freedom, go to Lesson 6.*

## Audio script and transcript

*(About eight minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed "Read the full transcript" section.)*

Welcome to Lesson 17.

Most of this course has been about seeing clearly. Module 5 is about what to do once you have seen.

There are three honest answers to the question, "Should we continue?" Yes. Not yet. And no. Each one can be done badly. This lesson is about yes. Not a yes with your eyes closed, but a yes with your eyes open.

Some of you recognised your relationship in Lessons 15 and 16, and Lesson 19 is for you. But many of you thought, "That's not us. But we do have problems." And you wondered whether your problems were the kind that should end a relationship.

Many difficulties between two people of good character are ordinary. But some reveal a deeper incompatibility, a personal pattern that needs help, or a safety concern. This lesson helps you tell the difference.

Here is the first important point. Continuing should be a decision, not a drift.

Many relationships are never actually decided. They just keep going. Ending would take a hard conversation. Getting married would take a conversation nobody is ready for. So months pass. The relationship becomes a habit, then an assumption, then a wedding. Nobody ever really said yes. They just never said no.

A wise yes is different. It says, "I have looked at what is difficult. I have named it. I believe it can be carried or changed. And I am choosing to keep going." A decision like that can be reviewed later. A drift cannot.

Not every problem is the same kind of problem. The lesson page shows four kinds of difficulty, and it helps to know which one you are facing. A difficulty caused by outside pressure, like grief or money stress, needs a different response from a long-standing personal habit, like anger or withdrawal. And when two people want completely different lives, trying harder does not usually change that.

So how can you tell if a difficulty is workable? Let me make this simple. A workable difficulty usually has three things.

First, you can both name it specifically. "We fight a lot" is too vague. "When one of us is hurt, we both go quiet, and the silence lasts for days" is specific. You cannot work on a fog. But being specific does not mean pretending every problem is equally both people's fault. If one person keeps lying, say so honestly.

Second, you are both working on it. This is the part most often missing. One person sees the problem, raises it, and prays about it. The other person agrees it is a problem, and then nothing changes. A relationship where one person does all the work is not being repaired. It is being managed. And the person managing it will eventually run out of strength.

Third, there is evidence of change. Not a promise. Evidence. Promises are easy in a crisis. Look for something that actually happened differently. The silence lasted one day instead of four. The apology came without being demanded. Small, repeated changes are better evidence than one big gesture.

If a problem was only discovered recently, it is too soon to expect lasting change. If you both freely choose to continue, agree on a response and a review date. But do not move towards engagement during that time just because of good intentions.

Also be careful. If someone behaves differently for three weeks only after you say, "I will leave if this continues," that is not enough to show lasting change. Short-term improvement after a crisis may be a good start. Real change continues when there is no crisis.

And even if a difficulty is workable, that alone is not a reason to continue. You must still look at the whole relationship: shared faith, character, compatibility, attraction, freedom, wise advice, and safety.

If you do choose to continue, do it well. Tell each other what you have decided and what you are working on. Choose one or two things, not everything. Get the right help for the problem: a pastor for spiritual support, a counsellor for relationship patterns, or a specialist for addiction, debt, or legal matters. And remember: you are not the other person's treatment plan.

Keep your wise friends close. People who decide to continue may be tempted to avoid friends who raised concerns. Stay connected instead. If you are working on a particular difficulty, set a review date that suits the problem. This helps you see whether anything is really changing.

Let me say what this lesson does not mean.

Do not continue just because ending feels like giving up. Before marriage, stopping is not giving up.

Do not continue just because you have already invested so much time, money, or emotion. The question is not what it has cost. The question is what it is.

Do not continue just because of a promise, or because a problem is only admitted when you threaten to leave.

Do not continue because you fear no one else will come. Lesson 20 is for that fear. It is a real fear, but it is not a reason.

And most importantly, if what you are facing is harm — fear, control, threats, violence, or constant contempt — this lesson does not apply. Do not call it "working on things." That language can keep people in danger.

Finally, a word about grace. A relationship is not a project to be engineered. The reason to continue, when continuing is right, is not that every difficulty has been fixed. It is that you have seen each other clearly — faults, habits, and all — and you still choose each other. And you are both willing to keep growing.

Pause for a moment.

Have you actually chosen this relationship — or have you simply never ended it?

*[Pause for five seconds.]*

Your honest answer matters.

Here is your one step for today. Write one sentence that names the main difficulty in your relationship specifically, without labelling the person. For example: "When one of us is hurt, we both go quiet for days."

Let's pray.

Lord, thank you for the gift of patience. Help me bear with ordinary weakness in love, without carrying what is not mine to carry. Give me humility to see my own part in what is hard, and courage to name what is not mine. If we continue, help us do it honestly and together. And keep me from drifting where you are calling me to decide. In Jesus' name, amen.

That is enough for today. You can go on to the next lesson, open the workbook, or stop here and come back later.

## Need support?

Effort is available to you. It is never owed. No one has to try something first to earn the right to say no, and nothing in this course creates an obligation to keep going.

If what you are facing is harm rather than an ordinary difficulty — fear, control, threats, violence, or constant contempt — this lesson is the wrong tool, and "working on it" language will make it harder to see clearly. Begin with a specialist abuse service for individual guidance, not a joint conversation or couples counselling.

If the difficulty is a personal pattern and it is yours, individual help is the right step, and it is a good thing to seek. If it is theirs, you may support them, but you cannot do the work for them. **Find help where you live →**

## Part 1 — What I am continuing despite

### Screen 1 — What happened

*Describe what was done, not a label for the person.*

1. What happened, in one or two sentences?
2. When did it last happen?
3. What did each of us do? *(Be accurate. Do not divide blame equally just to be fair.)*
4. What effect did it have on each of us?
5. How serious was it, and how often does it happen?

### Screen 2 — Checking seriousness

1. Does it involve lying, or anyone losing their freedom?
2. If I raised it safely, what happened? If I did not raise it, was fear of their reaction part of the reason?
3. Does the other person see it as a difficulty, in their own words?

> **Is anyone afraid?** If yes, stop here. This workbook is not the right tool. Go to Lesson 6. **Find help where you live →** *(Nothing is recorded about this question.)*

## Part 2 — What kind of difficulty is it?

### Screen 3 — Naming the kind

*Tick all that apply. More than one is normal.*

1. **Skill** — Do we share the goal, but lack the method?
2. **Outside pressure** — Is something outside the relationship driving this?
3. **Personal pattern** — Whose pattern is it, how does it affect the other person, and what individual or specialist help does it need?
4. **Different directions** — Do we want different lives or marriages?

> If you ticked "Different directions," Lessons 15 and 16 are a better place for it than this workbook.

## Part 3 — Is it being worked on together?

### Screen 4 — Shared effort

1. Did we agree on specific responsibilities? What were they?
2. Has each of us followed through?
3. Does one of us need repeated reminders?
4. Is the burden reasonably shared — not necessarily equal, but fair, given whose behaviour is involved?
5. Can either of us admit when the approach is not working?

*Acting on a request that was agreed is real effort. It does not always need to be spontaneous.*

## Part 4 — What has actually changed?

### Screen 5 — Signs of change (part 1)

1. What has actually happened differently?
2. Has the improvement continued outside a crisis — or only after I threatened to leave?
3. Has it reduced the effect on the other person?
4. Is the change visible without one person watching all the time?

### Screen 6 — Signs of change (part 2)

1. When there were setbacks, were they admitted and put right?
2. Is the pace of change reasonable for how serious the problem is?
3. Is the relationship more honest, respectful, and free than before?
4. What has not changed at all?

*One change is an encouraging start, not enough evidence. If the problem was only recently discovered, write that there has not yet been time for change to show. The purpose is clarity — not constant watching or a secret test.*

## Part 5 — The whole relationship

*A workable difficulty is not, by itself, a reason to continue. Mark each* ***Yes / Not sure / No****.*

### Screen 7 — The whole picture (part 1)

1. Our shared faith foundation is still intact.
2. No serious safety concern has been found.
3. The main direction of our lives still fits together.
4. Attraction and freely chosen desire are still present.

### Screen 8 — The whole picture (part 2)

1. I can say no to this person without being punished for it.
2. I am still connected to independent, wise people.
3. Neither of us is treating engagement as proof that the problem is solved.
4. The relationship still has enjoyment and friendship, not only hard work.

> **Every "No" or "Not sure" deserves attention, but some concerns are more serious than others.** A concern about safety or freedom comes first. No count decides the outcome. And remember: good things do not cancel out harm. **Green flags are not points that make up for violence, pressure, stalking, serious lies, or control.**

## Part 6 — Where this leaves me

### Screen 9 — My next step

1. What I am continuing despite, if I am continuing:
2. What kind of difficulty it is, and whose work it needs:
3. What has actually changed, and what has not:
4. What Screens 7–8 raised, and what wise counsel has added:

**Choose one:**

- ☐ **Continue, and work on it.**
- ☐ **Continue without increasing commitment,** with a review date and no move towards engagement until things are clearer. *(Lesson 18)*
- ☐ **Slow the relationship down.** *(Lesson 18)*
- ☐ **Reconsider the relationship.** *(Lesson 16, then Lesson 19)*
- ☐ **Seek suitable professional advice before deciding.**
- ☐ **I am not ready to choose yet.**

> **No combination of answers makes this decision.** One unaddressed personal pattern or difference in direction may matter more than several good signs. Continuing today does not oblige you to continue at the review. Either person may reconsider sooner if new information, an incompatibility, or a safety concern appears.

## Part 7 — If we continue

### Screen 10 — Conversation guide for two

> **Only if you can both speak freely and safely.** Nothing on this screen is saved.

Talk through:

1. What are the one or two things we are working on, and what is each of us responsible for?
2. What support does each of us need, and from whom? Which trusted adviser or professional, if any, will help — and what is it appropriate to share with them?
3. What would show real progress? What would show that we need to reconsider?
4. When will we review this — at a time that suits the problem?
5. What commitments will we **not** make before that review?

### Screen 11 — Private journal

1. Have I actually chosen this relationship, or have I only never ended it? If I am choosing it, what am I choosing it despite?
2. If I stopped raising the difficulty, would it still be worked on?

*Continuing with wisdom is not the same as continuing with certainty — and you will not have certainty. What you can have is honesty: with the other person, with wise counsel, and with yourself. Ask God for humility to see your own part in what is difficult, courage to name what is not yours, and patience to bear with ordinary weakness without carrying what is not yours to carry.*
