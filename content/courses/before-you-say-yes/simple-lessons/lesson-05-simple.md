## One truth to remember

Charm can make a strong first impression, but character is seen in small things over time.

## Key Scripture

“One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much, and one who is dishonest in a very little is also dishonest in much.” — Luke 16:10 (ESV)

Jesus was teaching about faithfulness and stewardship, not about choosing a spouse. But the principle helps us here. Character often shows in small moments, because people are less careful to impress when they think a moment does not matter.

Welcome to Lesson 5.

Most of us have met someone with unusual charm. When they walk into a room, people notice. They pray beautifully. Maybe they lead worship, preach, or run the youth group. When they show interest in you, you feel chosen.

Sometimes people like this are also honest, kind, faithful, and safe. Sometimes they are not. The problem is that, early on, real character and a polished image can look very similar.

So let us make this simple. What is charm? Charm is the ability to attract attention and make people feel comfortable or impressed. It may include confidence, humour, good speaking, natural leadership, or fluent spiritual language.

Charm is not bad. Many godly people have it, and it can serve the church well. But charm is not proof of good character. It is not proof of bad character either. It simply cannot tell you enough.

Character is different. Character is what remains when a person is no longer trying to make a good impression. Is this person honest when honesty costs them something? Are they faithful when nobody is watching? Can they hear that they are wrong?

One kind act may be sincere, but it cannot reveal a whole character. Patterns become clearer over time.

Here is why this matters. Charm does not reveal enough about character, and it can sometimes distract people from what is missing. Someone who is skilled at being liked may receive trust before their character has been tested. This can happen in churches, where gifted people are quickly given visible roles and spiritual language can be mistaken for spiritual maturity.

The Bible gives us an example. King David's son Absalom stood at the city gate. He greeted people warmly. He listened to their complaints. He made them feel seen. The Bible says he "stole the hearts" of the people. But his warmth had a purpose. He was building loyalty so that he could take his father's throne.

That does not mean every charming person is like Absalom. It means that making people feel special may come from genuine care, a desire for influence, or a mixture of motives. Time and behaviour help reveal the difference.

So where can you see character? Here are five places where it often shows.

First, notice how they treat people they do not need to impress: waiters, cleaners, drivers, junior colleagues, and children. Someone who is warm towards you but regularly disrespectful towards others is showing an important difference in their behaviour. Be fair, though. Shyness, tiredness, or a direct cultural style is not the same as contempt.

Second, how they handle disappointment. Plans fall through. You say a reasonable no. Some people are gracious. Some sulk. Some punish. How they treat you when you are the cause of their disappointment is one of the most important things you will learn.

Third, how they respond to correction. Can they listen without attacking back? Can they come back later and admit what was true? The Bible says we should be "quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger." A person who cannot be corrected finds it very hard to grow.

Fourth, notice honesty in small matters: casual lies, cutting corners when nobody is watching, and small promises kept or broken. Do not build a verdict from one ordinary mistake. Look for patterns, responsibility, and change. This is where Jesus’ words about “a very little” are helpful.

Fifth, how they speak about other people. Do they often mock, criticise, or share other people's private business? The way someone talks about others when they are not there may show how they will one day talk about you.

Let me add one fairness note. Some people go quiet or need time to think when they are upset. That can come from shyness, anxiety, or past hurt. Needing time is not the same as punishing someone with silence. The question is whether they come back and talk.

Let me say what this lesson does not mean.

It does not mean you should test people or set traps. You are watching ordinary life, not creating situations. It also does not mean you are looking for a perfect person. Everyone fails sometimes. So watch what happens after someone fails. Do they notice? Do they take responsibility? Do they try to put things right? Do they change over time?

But remember this: some actions are serious even the first time. Violence, sexual pressure, threats, and stalking are not made safe by a good apology.

And one more thing: if you have been hurt by a charming person, confidence may now make you cautious. That response is understandable. But confidence alone does not prove that someone is unsafe, just as charm alone does not prove that someone is safe. Charm is neither a red flag nor a green flag by itself.

Pause for a moment.

Think of someone you admire or are drawn to. What have you actually seen them do in a small, ordinary moment?

*[Pause for five seconds.]*

If you cannot think of much yet, that simply means you need more time and ordinary experience before reaching a conclusion.

Here is your one step for today. Choose one of the five places we talked about. Write down one thing you have actually seen, and when you saw it. If you are not dating, ask what another person would see in you in that same place.

Let's pray.

Lord, you see what I cannot see. Help me not to be impressed by what is only on the surface. Give me patience to see real character, and honesty to see my own. Make me faithful in small things, even when no one is watching. In Jesus' name, amen.

## Part 1 — Nine places character shows

*For each one, write what you have seen and when. If you have not seen anything yet, write "not seen yet." If something concerned you, note how serious it was, whether it has happened before, and how they responded if it was safely talked about.*

> Do not bring something up with the person just to complete this workbook. If talking about it could lead to threats, punishment, being watched, or danger, leave that part blank and get individual support first.

### Screen 1 — Family, and people who cannot help them

1. **Family:** What have I seen about their honesty, responsibility, and boundaries with family?
2. If they are distant from their family, is there a reasonable explanation, such as safety or past harm? *(Where there has been abuse, do not expect them to reconnect.)*
3. Am I judging them for not having the family I wish they had?
4. **People who cannot help them:** How do they treat waiters, cleaners, drivers, junior colleagues, or children? Any concern?

### Screen 2 — Former partners, and authority

1. **Former partners:** Do they speak with appropriate honesty and respect for privacy? Are there unresolved commitments, ongoing conflict, or repeated comparisons with you?
2. Where a past relationship involved ordinary conflict, can they reflect on their own choices? *(Memory can vary, especially after trauma. If they describe abuse, do not expect them to accept blame for being harmed.)*
3. **Authority:** How do they respond to fair limits from bosses, pastors, or leaders?
4. Can they disagree honestly without lying, revenge, or contempt? Do they describe every leader as unreasonable?

*Leaving a job or church does not prove rebellion, and staying does not prove faithfulness. Ask what happened.*

### Screen 3 — Money, disappointment, and correction

1. **Money:** What have I seen? Are they careful with their own money but careless with mine? *(Lesson 11 covers this more.)*
2. **Disappointment:** How do they react when plans fall through — especially when I am the cause?
3. **Correction:** Can they listen without attacking back, and later admit what was true?

*Some people go quiet or need time. That is not the same as punishment. The question is whether they come back and talk.*

### Screen 4 — Small honesty, and how they speak of others

1. **Small honesty:** Casual lies, cut corners, small promises kept or broken, and borrowed things returned—what have I seen? Have I noticed a pattern, or only one ordinary mistake?
2. **How they speak about others:** Do they often mock, criticise, or share other people's private business?

## Part 2 — Key moments

### Screen 5 — When I said "no"

1. A time I said no, changed a plan, or set a boundary:
2. How did they respond straight away?
3. Did they respect my answer without pressure, repeated persuading, threats, or going cold to punish me?
4. If they needed time to process, did they say so and come back without making me fix their feelings?
5. Did I feel free to keep my decision?

### Screen 6 — When they got something wrong

1. What happened, and how serious was it?
2. Did they recognise it without being forced?
3. Did they take responsibility for their part?
4. Did they try to put it right, and did their behaviour change later?
5. Did I feel free to bring it up?

> **Remember:** violence, sexual pressure, real threats, stalking, and serious controlling behaviour are not made safe by a good apology.

## Part 3 — The bigger picture

### Screen 7 — Public and private

Answer **Seen / Not seen yet / Mixed / Concern**.

1. The good things others see in public are not contradicted by cruelty, dishonesty, or contempt in private.
2. They are just as kind and patient when nobody will hear about it.
3. They keep the standards they expect of others.
4. They keep private promises as carefully as public ones.
5. They can celebrate other people and respond appropriately when attention moves to someone else.

### Screen 8 — People who have known them longer

1. Who have I naturally met, with the person's knowledge, who has known them for a long time?
2. What have I seen directly about those relationships?
3. If I have not met their friends, family, or church, is there a reasonable explanation?

> Do not ask others for private reports about the person behind their back.

### Screen 9 — Reading the whole picture

1. Which places are still empty? Is that because ordinary life has not shown me yet, or because I have been kept away?
2. Are my concerns one-off events or patterns? How serious are they?
3. Does what I have seen match what they say about themselves?

### Screen 10 — Private journal

1. Do I tend to be too impressed by confidence and gifting — or too suspicious of it? What has that cost me before?
2. Is there anything in this workbook I am reluctant to write down honestly? Why?

*Pray for this person honestly — not first for the relationship, but for them, that they would be faithful in small things. Then pray the same for yourself. Character can be built. It is built the same way it is seen: in small things, over time, with repair after failure.*
