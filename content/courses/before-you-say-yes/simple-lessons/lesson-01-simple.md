## Audio script and transcript

*(About seven minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Welcome to Lesson 1.

Let’s begin with a simple question. Why do you want a relationship?

Maybe you want love. Maybe you want a close companion, a husband or wife, or children. Maybe you want someone with whom to build a life. These are good desires. God made people for relationship. Wanting a relationship does not make you weak. It does not make you less spiritual.

But there is a second question. It is more important. Why do you want a relationship right now?

Here is why this matters. The reason you want a relationship can change what you are willing to ignore.

Let me give you some examples.

Imagine that you feel very lonely. Then a new person starts calling every day. That attention feels wonderful. It may even feel like love. But the relief of not being alone can make the relationship look healthier than it really is.

Or imagine that your family keeps asking, “When are you getting married?” After a while, almost any relationship can seem better than hearing that question again. But if you say yes mainly to stop the questions, you are not choosing a partner freely. You are trying to end a conversation.

Sometimes the pressure is age. You may think, “Time is running out. This could be my last chance.” Fear like that can make a wrong relationship look acceptable.

Physical attraction is powerful too. Attraction is good. God made it. But attraction cannot tell you if someone is honest, kind, responsible, or safe.

Some people compare themselves with others. A friend gets engaged. A cousin has a baby. You start to feel behind. But there is no timetable that you have failed.

And some people hope a relationship will fix their life. They think, “When the right person comes, everything will finally start.” But a partner cannot build your life for you. Sadly, a person who wants control may take advantage of someone who is desperate to be rescued.

Let’s make this simple. Loneliness, family pressure, age, attraction, comparison, and the hope of being rescued are all real. Feeling them does not mean you should not date. The problem starts when one of them begins making your decisions for you.

So what does being ready look like?

It does not mean you never feel lonely. It does not mean your life is perfect. Nobody’s life is perfect.

Being ready means this: you are not asking another person to be your whole source of peace, worth, or purpose. Your deepest identity and security come from God.

A ready person can want a relationship and still say no to the wrong one. They can enjoy attention without mistaking it for good character. They can listen to their family without letting their family choose for them.

Now, some Christians have been told, “Just be content in Christ, and stop wanting a relationship.” Let’s look at that carefully.

The Bible does teach contentment. The apostle Paul said he had learned to be content in every situation. Contentment means being at peace with God while you still wait. It does not mean you stop wanting things. Paul was content in prison. But he still preferred freedom.

So you can want marriage very much and still trust God. And when you trust God, you do not have to grab the first person who comes along. You are free to say no. And a person who is free to say no can date wisely.

That is why this course starts here. Everything else in this course depends on this one thing. You need to know you will be okay even if a relationship does not work out.

If you are already in a relationship, this lesson is still for you. Ask yourself honestly: “What influenced me to say yes to this person? Would I still choose this relationship without that pressure?”

Pause for a moment.

Which pressure affects you most right now? Loneliness? Family or church pressure? Age? Physical attraction? Comparing yourself with others? Or the hope that someone will fix your life?

*[Pause for five seconds.]*

Don’t judge yourself for your answer. Being honest is where wisdom begins.

Here is your one step for today. Write down the strongest reason you want a relationship. Then ask yourself: “Could this reason make me ignore something important?”

Let’s pray.

Father, thank you that I do not have to hide my desire for love. Help me understand my own heart. Show me where fear, pressure, or loneliness may be shaping my choices. Give me courage to wait and wisdom to see clearly. Help me to say yes or no without being controlled by fear. Lead me in your way. In Jesus’ name, amen.

That is enough for today. You can go on to the next lesson, open the workbook, or stop here and come back later.

# Go deeper — Lesson 1 workbook (optional)

*Only shown when the learner taps “Open the workbook.” Each screen below is one short page. Nothing is scored or added up. Your answers save to your account, but the course does not share them with anyone. Someone who can access your account, device, or browser may still be able to see what you write.*

**How to answer:** For each sentence, choose how true it is for you right now.

1 = Not true · 2 = A little true · 3 = Partly true · 4 = Mostly true · 5 = Very true

### Screen 1 — Loneliness

1. I often feel very lonely.
2. When someone gives me attention, I get attached quickly.
3. I have stayed, or thought about staying, in a bad relationship because being alone felt worse.
4. I have friends and habits that help me when I feel lonely.

### Screen 2 — Family and church pressure

1. People close to me keep asking when I will get married.
2. I feel like the odd one out at church or in my family because I am single.
3. I have started or stayed in a relationship partly to please other people.
4. I can hear other people’s opinions without letting them decide for me.

### Screen 3 — Physical attraction

1. Sexual desire is a big part of why I want a relationship.
2. I find it hard to imagine a relationship without a strong physical spark at the start.
3. In the past, attraction made me ignore things I should have noticed.
4. I have healthy ways of handling sexual desire that don’t depend on rushing into a relationship.

### Screen 4 — Age

1. I feel that time is running out for me.
2. I worry that being careful could mean missing my last chance.
3. I have lowered my standards, or thought about it, because of my age.
4. I can think about my age honestly without it making me hurry.

### Screen 5 — Comparing myself with others

1. Seeing engagements, weddings, or babies online affects my mood.
2. I feel behind my friends or brothers and sisters.
3. I have wanted a relationship mainly because of what other people seem to have.
4. I can be happy for others without measuring my life against theirs.

### Screen 6 — Hoping someone will fix my life

1. I hope a relationship will make me feel settled or secure.
2. I sometimes feel my life won’t really begin until I am married.
3. I have been drawn to someone mainly because they could fix my life, or I could fix theirs.
4. I am building my own life, whether or not a relationship comes.

### Screen 7 — What your answers may show

This is not a result. It is a way to read your own answers.

* **If sentences 1 and 2 are high, but 3 is low:** the pressure is there, but it does not appear to have been making your decisions. Notice that and protect it.
* **If sentence 3 is high:** this pressure has already affected a decision. Write it down. Next time you feel strongly about someone, ask, “Is this pressure talking?”
* **If sentence 4 is low:** you may have few ways to cope with this pressure except finding a partner. That is something you can start working on now.

### Screen 8 — Strengths you already have

Tick any that are true.

* ☐ My relationship with God stays steady whether I am dating or not.
* ☐ I have friends and a church family.
* ☐ Someone in my life would tell me the truth about a relationship, and I would listen.
* ☐ I could say no to the wrong person, even if I liked them.
* ☐ I can receive correction without immediately feeling attacked.
* ☐ I am taking care of my own life—my work or study, money, health, and past.

*These strengths don’t cancel the pressures. They show what is already helping you. Anything you could not tick shows an area to grow.*

### Screen 9 — Write it down

1. Which pressure is strongest for you right now? Did that surprise you?
2. Think of a past relationship or strong attraction. Which pressure was most active? What did it make you—or someone you know—ignore?

*You may pray Psalm 139:23: “Search me, O God, and know my heart!”*

### Screen 10 — One area to grow

Choose one only. Write your next small step.

* ☐ A steadier daily walk with God
* ☐ Closer friendships or a real place in a church
* ☐ A clearer sense of my purpose or work
* ☐ More stable money or practical life
* ☐ Healing from a past relationship, loss, or hurt
* ☐ Learning to be alone without it feeling like a crisis
* ☐ Other: __________

### Screen 11 — Private journal

1. Finish honestly: “If I never married, the hardest thing to accept would be __________.”
2. Finish this too: “If I never married, my life would still be worth living because __________.”

If the second sentence is hard to finish, that is not failure. It is important, and it is worth talking about with someone you trust.
