## One truth to remember

God has not promised you a spouse, but he has promised never to leave you.

## Key Scripture

"How long, O LORD? Will you forget me forever?" — Psalm 13:1 (ESV)

David says to God what many polite Christians would not dare to say. The psalm does not tell us his circumstances changed before the end. What changes is his hold on what he knows about God. Honest prayer is not a lack of faith. It is in the Bible because God can bear it.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About eleven minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed "Read the transcript" section.)*

Welcome to Lesson 20.

Many lessons in this course have pointed here. Lesson 17 said, "Do not continue because you are afraid there will be no one else." Lesson 19 said it again. And Lesson 16 sent people who were told no to this lesson.

I have kept saying that this fear is not a reason. Now let's talk about what it actually is.

First, let me take it seriously, because it is often not taken seriously. Single Christians are told to wait, to trust, and to be content in Christ. And then they are quietly judged when the waiting turns into fear.

But the fear of never finding anyone is one of the most ordinary human fears. It is not a spiritual failure. It is what it feels like to want something good, to have no control over whether it comes, and to watch time pass.

So let me say it plainly. You may be afraid you will never find anyone. That fear is real. It is common. And God is not disappointed in you for having it. What matters is not whether you feel it, but what you let it do.

Here is why this matters. Fear is a poor adviser. Sometimes it rushes you. Sometimes it freezes you. Either way, it can make avoiding loss more important than seeing clearly.

Let me make this simple. Here are five things this fear often does.

First, it lowers the bar. A warning sign that would have ended a relationship two years ago now seems like something you can live with. The list you made in Lesson 3 slowly gets edited, one must-have at a time, until it describes whoever is in front of you.

Second, it makes staying sound spiritual. "God has been so good to bring me this person," said about a relationship that is honestly not good. Being grateful to have someone becomes an argument that you must keep them.

Third, it rushes you. If you believe this is your last chance, you will grab it faster than you should. The "not yet" from Lesson 18 feels like a risk you cannot afford. But the real risk is marrying in a hurry.

Fourth, it makes you the one who always gives way. If you believe this is your last chance, you will give up your convictions, your friendships, and parts of yourself to keep it. And you will call that love.

Fifth, it makes it hard to hear a no. If someone ended things with you, fear can turn grief into a campaign. "They'll come round. God will change their heart. If I just explain it properly."

You do not have to make the fear disappear before you can decide well. But you do need to recognise it, separate it from the evidence, and refuse to let it make the final decision.

"Never finding anyone" is often several fears wearing one coat. For some, it is loneliness. For some, it is feeling unwanted. For some, it is grieving the life they hoped for, perhaps marriage or children. For some, it is age and time. For some, it is what family or church will think. And for many, underneath it all, is a fear about God. "If God is good, and I have asked for this, and it has not come — what does that mean?"

So let's look at what the Bible actually says.

Here is something many Christian singles are not told, or are told so gently that they miss it. God has not promised you a spouse. There is no verse that promises it.

"Delight yourself in the LORD, and he will give you the desires of your heart" is from a psalm about God's faithfulness to the righteous. It is not a guarantee of marriage, and it has broken the hearts of people who took it as one. "I know the plans I have for you" was spoken to a whole nation in exile, about their return home. God keeps his promises. So it matters what he actually promised.

What the Bible says about singleness may surprise you. Paul, who was unmarried, said that an unmarried person is free to give undivided attention to the Lord. He was not criticising marriage. He defended marriage in the same chapter. But he refused to treat singleness as a lesser life or a waiting room. And Jesus himself was never married. So being married cannot be the measure of a complete or faithful life.

That does not mean you have to want singleness. You are allowed to want marriage. You are allowed to grieve if it does not come. But you do not have to believe that God's goodness to you depends on whether it comes. He is not withholding a promise. He never made that promise.

What God has promised is this: he will never leave you or forsake you (Hebrews 13:5). Nothing can separate you from his love in Christ (Romans 8:38–39). And Christ is with his people always (Matthew 28:20). Those promises do not make an unmet longing painless. But they mean an unmet longing is not proof that God has stopped loving you.

Let me be honest about time, too. I will not tell you that there is someone for everyone. Some people who want to marry do not. You may marry, and you may not. You cannot know that today. If you do not marry, your life will still be a life, and it can be a good one. And if having children is part of your hope, age and health can matter. Talk with a doctor about your own situation, rather than relying on general assumptions or fear.

But please hear this. The possibility that you may not marry is never a reason to enter a marriage the evidence does not support. "Some marriage is better than none" is fear's maths, and it is wrong. A frightened yes to an unsuitable person does not solve the fear. It just gives the fear a house to live in.

If you were the one told no, the fear may now be telling you two things: that you will never find this again, and that if you had done something differently, you would still have it. Both may feel true. Neither is a reason to act. Do not pursue them or pressure them. Do not stay in contact hoping to change their mind. And do not tell yourself that God will change their heart. That can become a way of avoiding grief. One person's no is not a verdict on your worth, and it cannot predict your future.

Let me say what this lesson does not mean.

It does not mean your desire for marriage is wrong. It does not mean you should stop hoping. And it does not mean you have to face this fear alone.

Say it out loud to someone you trust. You may discover that others understand this fear too. Bring it to God without editing it. And start building the life the fear says is impossible: friendships, a community where you are truly known, work that matters, and a place where you belong and can welcome others. That is not a consolation prize while you wait. It is your life.

Pause for a moment.

If you believed that being single would be painful but survivable, and that it would not reduce your worth, what would you decide about your current situation?

*[Pause for five seconds.]*

Your answer may show you what the fear has been hiding.

Here is your one step for today. Say this sentence to one safe, trusted person: "I am afraid I will never find anyone, and I think it may be affecting my decisions." Choose someone who will listen, not someone who will quickly promise it will all work out.

Let's pray.

Lord, I am afraid there will be no one, and I have been letting that fear choose for me. Take the panic out of my hands. Help me desire a faithful life more than I fear a different future. Help me tell the truth about what I can see, even when it costs me something I have been holding tightly. I do not know what is coming. You have not promised me marriage. But you have promised never to leave me. Help me trust and live in that promise today. In Jesus' name, amen.

That is the end of the teaching in this course. You can go on to your final step, open the workbook, or stop here and come back later.

## Need support?

Sometimes this fear is not really about dating. It may be part of a wider anxiety, an old wound, or a way of thinking about yourself that has been there for years.

If it is constant, if it is affecting your sleep, your work, or your other relationships, or if it makes you feel worthless without a partner, please talk to a qualified counsellor. That is not a sign of weakness. It is a wise response to a burden that has become too heavy to carry alone.

> **If the fear has become hopelessness** — if you are thinking about harming yourself or not wanting to live — seek crisis support now. If you believe you may act on those thoughts, contact emergency or urgent mental-health services immediately. If it is safe and possible, tell someone you trust, and do not stay alone. ### Screen 2 — In my current or most recent relationship

## Part 4 — Deciding without the fear

### Screen 7 — Advising someone I love

*Imagine you are advising someone you love, who shares your beliefs and situation, but who is not controlled by the fear that this is their last chance.*

1. If I believed being single would be painful but survivable — and would not reduce my worth — what would the evidence lead me to do?
2. Which response is wisest now: continue, slow down, or end? What evidence supports it?
3. What part of me resists that answer, and why?
4. Which lesson should I go back to — 17, 18, or 19?
5. Who will help me act on what I have decided?

## Part 5 — What I believe

*Mark each: **I believe this** · **I understand it, but struggle to feel it** · **I am not yet convinced**.*

### Screen 8 — What I believe (part 1)

1. God has not promised me a spouse, and his goodness to me does not depend on one.
2. A single life can be a whole life before God, not a waiting room.
3. I am allowed to want marriage, and to grieve if it does not come.

### Screen 9 — What I believe (part 2)

1. An unsafe or deeply unsuitable marriage is not a safer choice just because the alternative may be singleness.
2. One person's no is not the world's verdict on me.
3. My worth is not decided by whether I am chosen.

*Take anything you struggle with to someone who will not simply reassure you, but will help you think it through.*

## Part 6 — The life I am building now

### Screen 10 — Building my life

1. Which friendships am I investing in?
2. Where am I truly known, not just present?
3. What work or service matters to me?
4. How am I creating a place where I belong and can welcome others?
5. Is there an expectation I need to grieve? With whom could I share that?

*Grief may return at certain ages, weddings, or anniversaries. That does not mean you have failed to grieve well.*

### Screen 11 — Private journal

1. When did this fear begin, or become strong? Is there a particular age, event, or comparison that feeds it?
2. What would I start building now if I stopped treating my life as a waiting room?

*Pray Psalm 13:1 without editing it: "How long, O LORD?" Then keep reading to the end of the psalm. It moves from anguish towards remembered trust. Ask God not for false certainty, but for grace to live faithfully while the future is unknown — and for the fear to be his to carry, yours to live beside, and no longer the one making your decisions.*
