## One truth to remember

Attraction may open the door, but wisdom and time help you decide whether to walk through it.

## Key Scripture

“Man looks on the outward appearance, but the LORD looks on the heart.” — 1 Samuel 16:7 (ESV)

Samuel was looking for Israel’s next king, not a spouse. He saw someone whose outward appearance seemed impressive and assumed the choice was obvious. God corrected him: what people notice first is not always what matters most. We cannot see another person’s heart as God does, but we can observe what their character produces over time.

Welcome to Lesson 4.

“There was just something about him.” “I knew the moment I saw her.” Many people who have fallen in love have said something like this.

Christians often make one of two mistakes with attraction. Some treat it as everything. If the feeling is strong, it must be love. And if it is love, it must be right. Others treat attraction as something to be suspicious of, as if the most spiritual choice is someone you feel nothing for.

This lesson says no to both.

Attraction is a gift from God. The Song of Songs celebrates romantic and physical delight. Wanting someone is not shameful. But attraction has a limit. It can invite you to get to know someone. It cannot tell you whether to build a life with them.

Here is why. Attraction often happens quickly. You do not choose to find someone's smile appealing. But character takes time to see. Attraction can tell you that you enjoy being with someone. It cannot tell you if they are honest, kind, faithful, or safe. It cannot tell you how they handle money, disappointment, or anger.

The Bible says, "Charm is deceitful, and beauty is vain." That does not mean beauty is bad. It means beauty cannot promise what it seems to promise.

There is a bigger problem. Strong attraction can change how you see things. When you really like someone, you may start explaining things for them.

For example, a hurtful comment becomes “just their sense of humour.” Being late repeatedly becomes “they are so busy.” A flash of anger becomes “they are passionate.” If someone you were not attracted to behaved in the same way, you might notice it more quickly.

This happens to intelligent, sensible people. It does not mean you are foolish. It means attraction is doing what attraction does.

Here is something else to notice. A very strong feeling can seem like a sign that “this person is different.” But strong feelings are not proof of good character. Sometimes the feeling is strong because two people genuinely connect. Sometimes it is strong because you have been lonely for a long time. Sometimes the other person is skilled at making someone feel special very quickly. And sometimes the pull becomes stronger because their attention comes and goes—warmth, then coldness, then warmth again. That changing attention can feel more powerful than steady kindness. We will return to this in Lesson 6.

So how do you move from attraction to wisdom? Let me make this simple. Here are five things you can do.

First, give it time. Attraction is quick. Character is slow.

Second, let ordinary life show you who they are. Everyone is at their best on a date. You want to see them when they are tired, when plans go wrong, with people they do not need to impress, and when you say a reasonable no. But do not set up tests or try to provoke them. Simply spend time together in normal life.

Third, separate what you feel from what you know. The “feel” list may fill quickly. The “know” list takes time and needs evidence.

Fourth, notice what you are explaining away. Not every explanation is an excuse. But ask three questions. How serious was it? Has it happened before? And what did they do when it was talked about?

Fifth, listen to wise people who are not caught up in your feelings. Friends, family members, mentors, or church leaders may notice something you have missed. They are not always right, and prejudice or a desire for control can affect their opinions. But when trustworthy people independently raise the same evidence-based concern, do not dismiss it too quickly.

Let me say what this lesson does not mean.

It does not mean attraction is unimportant. Do not promise marriage while simply assuming that attraction will appear later. Sometimes attraction grows as trust grows, and sometimes it does not. You do not need to pretend that feelings are present when they are not.

And if you feel little attraction to someone you admire, do not force it, and do not feel guilty. Give it honest time. If it does not grow, being truthful is not failing anyone.

Pause for a moment.

Think of someone you are drawn to, now or in the past. If the physical attraction were removed, what would be left?

Give me patience to wait, eyes to see clearly, and wisdom to know the difference between a feeling and the truth. In Jesus' name, amen.

*Use this when you are attracted to someone. If you are not drawn to anyone right now, do Screen 1 about your past patterns, and keep the rest for later.*

### Screen 1 — What draws me

1. What first drew me to this person? Be honest, even if it feels embarrassing.
2. Which kind of attraction is strongest — physical, emotional, intellectual, or spiritual? Which is weakest?
3. Is this person like people I have been drawn to before? How?
4. Does anything about them feel familiar from my family or my past? Does it feel comforting because it is healthy, or just familiar because I have lived it before?

### Screen 3 — What I have explained away

*Use a new screen for each thing that happened.*

> Do not bring this up with the person just to complete this screen. If talking about it could lead to threats, punishment, being watched, or danger, leave question 4 blank and get individual support first.

1. What happened?
2. What explanation did I give for it?
3. How serious was it? Has it happened before?
4. What did they do when it was talked about — or has it not been talked about?
5. What would I tell a friend who described this in their relationship?

### Screen 4 — The pace

Answer **Yes / Partly / No**.

1. I am not making promises bigger than what I actually know about this person.
2. Strong feelings have not pushed me to decide quickly.
3. I can slow things down without fearing they will punish me, go cold, or lose interest.
4. I have kept up my other friendships since this began.

### Screen 5 — What I have seen

Answer **Yes / Partly / No**.

1. I have seen them in more than one kind of setting.
2. I have seen how they handle inconvenience or disappointment.
3. I have seen how they treat people they do not need to impress.
4. I have seen how they respond when I say a reasonable "no."
5. Their attention has been steady, not coming and going.

### Screen 6 — What your "No" answers may mean

*This is not a result. It is a way to read your own answers.*

- **A “No” because you have not known them long** means you need more time before a bigger commitment.
- **Attention that comes and goes** is worth looking at as a pattern. Is it just a busy life? Or is it warmth, then coldness, that leaves you anxious and easier to control?
- **A “No” involving pressure, being cut off from friends, punishment, or disrespect for your pace** needs attention now. Speak with someone you trust. If you fear the person’s reaction, get individual support before confronting them. **Find help where you live →**

### Screen 7 — What others see

1. What have the people who know me actually seen this person do?
2. Have trustworthy people independently raised a similar concern?
3. What evidence supports their concern, and what evidence goes against it?
4. Could prejudice, control, or their own interests be affecting their opinion?
5. Am I brushing off the concern because I do not like what it would mean?

### Screen 8 — If I feel little attraction

*Only if this applies to you.*

1. What do I admire about this person?
2. Have I spent time with them in ordinary, relaxed settings?
3. Do I feel guilty for not being attracted? Where is that guilt coming from?
4. Is there any honest sign that attraction is growing?

*Use this if the relationship is moving faster than what you know about the person.*

2. One boundary I will bring back:
3. One friendship or normal activity I will reconnect with:
4. One person I will ask for honest advice:
5. The date I will look at this again:

### Screen 10 — What I have not seen yet

Look back at Screen 5. What is one thing normal life has not yet shown you?

Do not create a test or set up a disappointment. Just notice what is missing — time with their friends, everyday responsibilities, or plans that are not carefully arranged dates. Write it down, and come back to it when ordinary life shows you more.

### Screen 11 — Private journal

1. If the physical attraction were removed, what would be left?
2. What concern about this person am I tempted to play down because I am attracted to them? What would I tell a friend in the same situation?

*If this lesson made you uncomfortable, that feeling is worth looking at — but it is not a verdict. It could be wisdom, worry, or an old hurt. Do not ignore it and do not blindly obey it. Pray about it, compare it with what you have actually seen, and talk it over with someone wise.*
