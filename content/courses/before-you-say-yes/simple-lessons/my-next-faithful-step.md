## Audio script and transcript

*(About six minutes. Read slowly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed "Read the transcript" section.)*

You have reached the end of the teaching in this course. Well done.

When this course began, it made a promise about what it would not do. It would not choose a partner for you. It would not predict your future. And it would not give you a score telling you whether your relationship passes or fails. This page keeps that promise too.

What it asks for is one step. Not the whole plan. Not the final answer. Just the next thing, chosen freely, that you can actually do.

Before anything else, one question comes first. Is there a safety concern? Are you afraid of the person? Is there control, violence, stalking, or pressure from family or church that you do not feel free to refuse?

If so, specialist safety support comes before every other choice. Please do not write your plans on this page. Contact a specialist service and let them help you decide what to do and when. If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services or go somewhere safer now. Otherwise, go to Find Help Where You Live.

If there is no safety concern, here are three things to remember as you choose.

First, a step is not a verdict. Choosing to slow down is not a decision to end. Choosing to continue is not a promise to marry. You are naming what comes next, not committing to the rest of your life.

Second, you are allowed to change your step. If new evidence or clearer understanding changes what is wise, come back and choose again. That is not failure. But if you keep changing direction and nothing becomes clearer, take that to a wise person.

Third, pressure does not set your timetable. Family expectations, church excitement, a partner's urgency, or an age you once had in mind cannot give your consent for you. Real medical, legal, or practical limits may need honest attention. But they should be looked at openly, not used to force an answer.

On this page, you will see six ordinary choices: continue with wisdom, have a necessary conversation, seek wise counsel, slow down, end the relationship, or rest before deciding further. There is also a separate safety route.

A word about rest. Some people reach this page not needing a decision, but needing to stop. If you have been thinking about this relationship for weeks, and every question now brings the same tired answer, it is fine to put it down for a while. But rest is a break from deciding. It is not a break from acting on something you already know is unsafe. And if someone is waiting for your answer, tell them honestly that you are not ready yet. They are free to decide that they cannot keep waiting.

Once you have chosen, write two short lines. One sentence naming your step, and one sentence naming the first thing you will do. A step that is named but never begun has a way of remaining a wish.

Now, one more thing.

This course has asked a lot of you. It has asked you to look honestly at your motives, your family, your money, your past, and your fears. It has asked you to look at someone you care about with an honesty that is not always comfortable. And it has asked you, again and again, to be willing to act on what you saw.

Whatever you have chosen, hold on to the truth that made choosing possible. Before you say yes to anyone, you must be free to say no.

That freedom is not a threat to a good relationship. It is what makes a yes worth having. A yes that could never have been a no is not freely given.

Take your next step at the pace that truth, freedom, and safety require.

If your relationship is moving towards engagement, the optional Engagement section is next. If it is not, you may finish here and return whenever you need to review what you have learned.

Let us pray.

Lord, thank you for walking with me through every lesson. Thank you for showing me things I needed to see, even the hard ones. Give me courage to take my next step, whatever it is. Keep me free, keep me honest, and keep me safe. Whether I marry or not, help me live a whole and faithful life with you. And when the time comes to say yes or no, help me say it with love and mean it. In Jesus' name, amen.

## Choose one ordinary step

*You may change your choice at any time. None of these is a better result than the others.*

### ☐ Continue with wisdom

I have named what is difficult and believe it can be carried or changed. **Lesson 17** will help: one or two things to work on, what change would look like, the right help, and a review point. If we can both speak freely and safely, I will tell the other person what I have decided rather than keeping it as a private judgement.

### ☐ Have a necessary conversation

Something specific has not been said — a cost, a boundary, or an assumption. I will choose a time and place that give it room. *If this conversation would not be safe, this is not my step. The safety route is.*

### ☐ Seek wise counsel

I need a view from outside before I can weigh this. **Lesson 14** will help me choose someone wise, independent, and suitably informed — and decide what specific question to ask.

### ☐ Slow down and observe

Commitment is running ahead of what I know. **Lesson 18** will help me give the slowdown a reason, a shape, and a review point. *The other person may choose not to wait.*

### ☐ End the relationship

I have seen clearly, and I am not waiting for more. **Lesson 19** will help me end it clearly and kindly. *Its first question is the safety question. I will answer that before planning anything else.* A freely given no is enough.

### ☐ Rest before deciding further

I need to stop for a while. *This is a rest from deciding, not from acting on something I know is unsafe.* If someone is waiting for my answer, I will tell them honestly that I am not ready and when I hope to revisit it. They are free to decide that they cannot keep waiting.
