# What Does the Evidence Require?

Four lessons: testing a claim that God has spoken, deciding whose counsel deserves
weight, asking whether two lives can be built into one, and facing the hardest case
— two good people who may not be right for each other.

This page asks what all of that now requires of you. It produces no recommendation
and no score. Nothing you write here decides anything, and no combination of
answers means your relationship passes or fails.

> If you are not currently dating, read this page rather than completing it. What
> is worth carrying forward from this module is Lesson 13's tests, which you may
> need before anyone applies spiritual pressure to you, and Lesson 14's map, which
> is worth building before you need it.

## First, the only question that changes the order

Is there a safety concern — fear, coercion, control, violence, stalking, spiritual
or family pressure that you cannot refuse?

If so, that comes before everything else on this page, and before any further
lesson. Not because the rest does not matter, but because the rest assumes a
relationship you are free to leave and free to speak inside.

## What the evidence actually consists of

Before deciding what it requires, be honest about what you have. Four kinds, as the
Module 2 pause set out: what they said, what you hoped or assumed, what others
reported, and what you observed. Weigh sources by their quality, not by counting
them. Pattern and severity both matter, and neither replaces the other.

Then three questions:

- **What is now clear?** Not what you suspect. What you could describe to a wise
  person in specific terms, with something behind it.
- **What is still genuinely unclear?** And is it unclear because you have not
  reached that stage, because it is their legitimate privacy, because asking would
  not be safe, or because you have avoided it?
- **What has counsel added?** Lesson 14 asked you to weigh rather than count. Did
  independent voices converge on anything specific — a concern or an
  encouragement?

## Where that leaves you

Choose the one that fits now. You may change it, and none is a better result than
the others. Several may look plausible; pick the one that describes your *next* step
rather than your eventual hope.

**Continue learning about the relationship.**
You have not identified a specific action beyond continuing ordinary discernment.
There is genuinely more to learn, and ordinary life has not yet provided it. Name
what remains unknown and how you would reasonably expect to learn it, so that
"continue" does not become indefinite waiting.

**Address a specific concern.**
You can name the concern with some specificity. Feelings such as fear, unease or
persistent reluctance may be important information, but the conversation should not
depend on presenting a feeling as proof. Describe what you feel, what appears
connected to it and what remains uncertain.

Where both of you can speak freely and the issue is appropriate for direct
discussion, arrange a conversation at a time and place that gives it room. If the
concern involves health, law, finance, addiction, safeguarding or another area
beyond your competence, seek appropriate advice rather than expecting one
relationship conversation to resolve it.

**Seek wise independent counsel.**
Identify the safest and most relevant independent person or professional to
approach, chosen for wisdom, competence and freedom from the relationship's outcome
rather than convenience. Decide what specific question you need to ask and what
information is yours to share.

**Slow the relationship down.**
Commitment is running ahead of knowledge, or a decision is approaching faster than
your clarity about it. Lesson 18 gives this a structure — a defined period,
something specific tried, a review point — so that slowing down means something
rather than drifting.

**Move towards ending it.**
Either because Lesson 15 found something in the third group, or because Lesson 16
described what you have been unable to say. Lesson 19 is about doing that well. If
you fear their reaction, begin with the safety guidance there rather than with a
conversation.

**Prioritise safety and specialist support.**
This replaces the others rather than sitting beside them. Do not wait to finish the
course.

## Two things worth saying before Module 5

**A decision to wait is a decision.** Choosing to continue learning is legitimate,
and it becomes something else when it is used to postpone a conclusion you have
already reached. The test is whether you could say what you are still waiting to
see.

**You do not owe this relationship an experiment.** Module 5 will offer ways to
continue, to slow down, and to repair. Those are available to you. None of them is
a requirement before you may decide, and no one is obliged to try something first
in order to earn the right to say no.

## Before Module 5

Module 5 is where the course stops asking what you see and starts asking what you
will do: continue with wisdom, slow down, walk away, or face the fear that has been
sitting underneath the whole decision — that there may be no one else.

Bring what you have written. Lessons 17 and 18 both use it, and Lesson 18's review
will send you back to the tools from Lessons 1, 7 and 8 to see what has changed.
