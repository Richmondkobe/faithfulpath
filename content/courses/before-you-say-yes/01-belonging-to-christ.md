# If You Are Not Sure You Belong to Christ

This course assumes that you are a Christian — someone who has trusted Christ
and wants to date, and one day marry, in a way that honours him.

But courses do not check credentials at the door, and some people will be here
because they are dating a Christian, or were raised in church, or are simply
curious. Others have been Christians for years and are, if they are honest, not
sure what that has ever meant.

If that is you, I want to say something before we go further, because the rest of
the course will not make full sense without it.

Christianity is not a set of relationship rules. It is not a moral standard for
dating, or a way of ensuring a good marriage. It is the news that God, who made
you, has come to you in Jesus Christ — that Jesus lived the life you have not
lived, died the death your sin deserved, and rose from the dead so that everyone
who trusts him is forgiven, made new, and brought into God's family forever. That
is the foundation. Everything this course says about wisdom, boundaries,
character and discernment stands on it, and nothing it says can replace it.

Belonging to Christ is not the same as attending church, being raised by
Christian parents, holding Christian values, or having a Christian partner. It is
a matter of trust: turning from the life you have been running on your own terms,
and resting your whole weight on Jesus — his death for your sins and his life for
your righteousness. The Bible calls this repentance and faith, and it says that
everyone who comes to Christ this way is received; nobody is received because
they performed repentance perfectly (John 6:37; Romans 10:9–13).

If you are not sure whether you belong to Christ, do not pretend certainty merely
to move past this page. You may continue, but understand that this course's
vision of dating grows out of following Jesus, not merely adopting Christian
relationship rules.

Begin with one of the Gospels — Mark gives a concise account, while John gives
sustained attention to who Jesus is. Speak with a trustworthy pastor or mature
Christian and ask your questions plainly. And pray, in your own words, something
like this: *God, I do not know if I belong to you. I want to. Show me who Jesus
is, and help me trust him.* The prayer itself is not a formula that saves you;
Christ does. Use it as an honest beginning, then keep looking to Jesus in
Scripture, and speak with someone who can help you understand repentance and
faith.
