# Questions Before Engagement

These are threshold questions. They ask whether you are ready to **declare an
intention** to marry, not whether you are ready to marry. Before engagement you should
know whether you broadly agree on the direction of the major areas of married life —
faith, money, family, children, the shape of the marriage — and whether any material
fact would change your willingness to proceed. The detailed work of expectations and
practical decisions belongs to the premarital season that follows.

**These questions do not score, and they do not certify a relationship.** They exist
so that the decision to become engaged is made with both people's eyes open.

> ### The rule this exercise runs on
> **Engagement requires two free and informed yeses.**
> **One *no* ends the proposal. One *not yet* postpones it.**
> **Neither answer may be negotiated into a yes.**
>
> A yes may also be changed before engagement. Becoming engaged does not remove
> either person's freedom to raise concerns, pause or end the engagement later.

## How to answer

Complete the questions **separately, in writing, without discussing your answers
first.** Where a question asks for a judgement, answer *Yes / Not sure / No* and add a
brief explanation — *not sure* is an honest answer, not a failure. Where a question
asks for information, answer in one or two sentences. Do not skip a question because
it feels awkward. If a question cannot be answered safely, or touches information that
is not yours to disclose, note that and seek individual guidance.

> **Safety.** Do not complete these questions jointly if you are afraid of the other
> person, if saying *not yet* would bring threats, punishment, spiritual condemnation
> or family retaliation, or if this document could be used against you. If any answer
> reveals fear, coercion, surveillance or an inability to say *not yet*, **stop the
> exercise.** Do not compare answers or raise them with the other person. Do not
> record anything here that could place you at risk. Seek independent specialist help.
> **Finding Help Where You Live →**

> **Privacy.** Your answers are yours. Neither person is required to show the other
> their written answers, and neither may share the other's without permission. You
> each decide what to say aloud when you compare. Anything recorded as shared is
> entered only after both of you have agreed the wording. If someone may monitor your
> account, device or browser, use a safer device or keep your answers off this page
> entirely.

### 1. Free intention

- Do I want to marry this person? Not *could I*, not *should I* — do I?
- Would I still want to if nobody else knew we were together and nobody was waiting
  for an answer?
- If I said *not yet*, what would happen?
- If they said *not yet*, what would I do?
- Has either of us ever said yes to something in this relationship because no felt
  impossible?

> If the last answer is yes, do not proceed to engagement until you have examined what
> happened independently and with appropriate support. **A relationship is not ready
> for a greater promise while consent within the present relationship is uncertain.**
> If fear or coercion was involved, use the safety route rather than raising it
> through this joint exercise.

### 2. What we have faced

- Which of the tools from Lessons 15 to 19 have we used, and what did each show?
- Is there a concern from any of them that is still open?
- Is anything currently under review that has not yet been closed?
- Is there anything I have decided to live with that I have not told them I am living
  with?
- Is there anything I am hoping engagement will fix?

### 3. What we know

- What have I observed across the varied, ordinary circumstances we have actually
  shared — especially disappointment, disagreement, disrupted plans, responsibility or
  pressure?
- Did what I observed match who they say they are?
- In what important circumstances have I not yet observed them, and does that missing
  knowledge matter before engagement?
- What have they observed of me in the same conditions?
- Which of their patterns do I already know I will find hard, and have I said so?

**On material information.** This means something a reasonable person might consider
important when freely deciding whether to move towards marriage — a previous marriage,
children or dependants, substantial debt or financial obligations, criminal or
immigration matters affecting the shared future, an active addiction, or an existing
commitment that would significantly shape married life. It does **not** mean either
person is entitled to every private detail, to another person's confidential
information, or to a complete account of past trauma. Privacy is legitimate; secrecy
about a material fact is not.

- Is there any material fact about my history, responsibilities or present
  circumstances that could reasonably affect their decision?
- Have I disclosed it, at an appropriate level of detail?
- Am I preserving legitimate privacy, or concealing something because I fear their
  answer?
- Is any information I am being asked to disclose not mine to share?

### 4. Spiritual foundation

- Do we both personally confess and follow Christ, rather than relying on family,
  church attendance or a label?
- Does the faith each of us professes broadly agree with the life the other has
  observed?
- Can we pray, read Scripture and disagree about spiritual matters without performance
  or control?
- Is there a substantial difference in doctrine, church commitment or spiritual
  direction that we are postponing until later?
- Has either of us used God, Scripture, prophecy or pastoral authority to pressure the
  other?

### 5. Direction

- Have we talked about what our married life would actually look like — where, with
  what work, what shape of family, whether we want children, what place for God?
- Do our two pictures broadly match, or has one of us been assuming the other will
  come round?
- Is there a directional difference — calling, location, children, the shape of
  marriage — that we have agreed to sort out later?
- Do we agree on what engagement is, and roughly how long we expect it to be?

### 6. Counsel

- Which wise, independent people know each of us and the relationship well enough to
  offer relevant observations?
- Have we each asked them directly and independently whether there is anything we
  should look at before engagement?
- What concrete concern, if any, came back — and what did we do with it?
- Have we dismissed a credible, concrete concern merely because it was inconvenient?
- Have we distinguished genuine concern from prejudice, possessiveness, personal
  preference or resistance to losing influence?
- Are our advisers able to respect a decision different from the one they prefer?
- Is there anyone we have avoided asking?

### 7. Pressure

- Who, apart from the two of us, wants this engagement — and how much does that matter
  to my answer?
- Which pressure on me is currently strongest: age or time, fear that no one else will
  come, sexual boundaries, the length of the relationship, the other person's
  readiness, family, church?
- As far as I can separate my own desire from these pressures, what reasons for
  engagement remain?
- Am I able to tolerate the disappointment that a *not yet* might cause?
- Have we attached engagement to an artificial deadline?
- Have any deposits, announcements or family plans been made that now make us feel
  unable to reconsider?

### 8. The relationship itself

- Can I raise a hard question, disagree or say *not yet* without retaliation,
  prolonged withdrawal, intimidation, contempt or the misuse of spiritual authority?
- Can this person feel disappointed without making me responsible for removing their
  disappointment?
- Do I like them — not only love them — and enjoy their company in ordinary life?
- Can I retain my conscience, voice, friendships, responsibilities and appropriate
  independence in this relationship?
- Am I becoming more truthful, responsible, loving and spiritually mature — or more
  secretive, fearful and dependent?
- Have I abandoned an important conviction or relationship merely to preserve this
  one?
- When I imagine the ordinary marriage rather than the wedding, what do I feel — and
  what experiences, expectations or evidence appear to be producing that response?
- If I feel reluctance: is it a passing anxiety about a serious commitment, or is it
  connected to something specific I have repeatedly observed?

### 9. Readiness for the engagement itself

- Have we already discussed marriage clearly enough that a proposal, or its equivalent
  in our culture, will not require an unprepared answer?
- Are we both willing to complete thorough premarital preparation?
- Are we willing to delay setting a wedding date until the major premarital
  conversations have begun?
- If engagement reveals a serious concern, are we both willing to pause rather than
  letting deposits, announcements or embarrassment decide for us?
- Do we understand that **engagement permits neither sexual entitlement nor greater
  control over the other person**?
- Have we agreed that either person remains free to raise concerns after engagement?

### 10. The decision

Choose one.

- **Yes** — I freely want to move towards engagement.
- **Not yet** — I need further information, time or resolution.
- **No** — I do not intend to marry this person.

Then:

- What has informed my answer?
- What pressure makes it difficult to say?
- Am I hoping the other person's answer will rescue me from owning mine?
- If *not yet*: what specifically is missing, what would allow an honest review, and
  when would an appropriate review point be? A review point is not a promise that the
  eventual answer will be yes.
- If *no*: am I prepared to say so clearly rather than requesting an indefinite wait?
- Who I will talk to about my answer before we compare:

> **If either answer is *no*, do not become engaged. If either answer is *not yet*, do
> not become engaged yet.**

## Comparing your answers

Begin with the questions where your answers differ — but do not ignore matching
answers that are vague, unsupported or based on assumption. **Agreement is not giving
the same answer; it is understanding what each person means by it.**

Where the differences are about direction or spiritual foundation, Lessons 2, 15 and
16 are where they belong. Where they are about safety or freedom, Lesson 6. Where they
are about pace or readiness, Lesson 18.

**If either person answers *no*, receive that answer as final.** It does not require an
explanation you consider sufficient, and it is not an invitation to make a case.

**If either answers *not yet*, name what remains unresolved and return to the relevant
lesson without setting an engagement date.** A *not yet* that is argued into a yes was
never a yes.

**If both answer *yes* freely** — with the threshold questions faced, no serious concern
left open, and a mutual intention towards marriage — you may be near the threshold of
engagement. This exercise does not certify the relationship. It helps you approach
that threshold with your eyes open.

## Need support?

If completing these questions has shown you that you cannot say *not yet* safely, that
is the most important thing this page has told you, and it is not a question about
engagement any more. Begin with a specialist service for individual guidance — a
domestic-abuse service, or one specialising in forced marriage and family coercion
where the pressure comes from family or community. Do not compare answers, do not
raise the concern directly with any person whose reaction you fear, and do not agree
to mediation as the first response to coercion or abuse.
