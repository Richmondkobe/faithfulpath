## Go now

Some situations should not wait for a routine appointment or a review date.

**If you are in immediate danger,** contact the emergency service where you are
if it is safe to do so, or move to the safest available place. Do not delay
urgent protection in order to finish a worksheet, collect belongings, obtain
pastoral permission or make a perfect plan.

**If you are thinking about harming yourself, or not wanting to live,** seek
crisis support now. If it is safe and possible, tell someone trustworthy and do
not remain alone. If you believe you may act on those thoughts, contact emergency
or urgent mental-health services immediately.

**If you have been strangled,** seek urgent medical attention even if you have no
visible injuries and currently feel well. Strangulation can cause serious
internal injury and delayed complications.

**If you have recently been sexually assaulted or injured,** seek medical and
specialist support as soon as you can — even if you do not know whether you want
to report it. A specialist service can explain the medical, forensic and
reporting options where you live. In many places, receiving care does not require
an immediate decision about reporting, but confidentiality, safeguarding and
reporting requirements vary by country, service, age and circumstances. Where
possible, ask what may have to be reported before disclosing identifying details.

**If you are afraid of the person you are dating,** or have been threatened, hit,
sexually pressured, followed, tracked, isolated or financially controlled,
contact a specialist abuse service before acting on anything in Lesson 19. Do not
use ordinary joint counselling or family mediation. Do not wait for it to get
worse.

**If you are being forced or pressured into a marriage,** or fear punishment,
removal or travel abroad for refusing one, contact a forced-marriage or
family-coercion service before saying anything at home. Do not travel abroad at a
family's request while this is unresolved. Specialist forced-marriage services
generally operate in office hours; outside those hours, contact a 24-hour
domestic abuse service, or the police or emergency services where there is
immediate risk.

**If the other person threatens to harm themselves if you leave,** take it
seriously without making yourself responsible for preventing it. Contact the
appropriate crisis or emergency service. Where it is safe, you may also tell a
responsible person close to them. Do not promise secrecy, do not become their
sole crisis contact, and do not stay in or resume the relationship in exchange
for their safety.

**If you are under eighteen,** contact a child-protection or youth service and
tell a safe adult who is not involved in the pressure or harm. If a parent or
guardian is the source of the danger, seek an independent safeguarding
professional.

## Seek specialist advice promptly

Some patterns are not ordinary relationship difficulty, even when nobody has been
hit. Contact a specialist abuse service — not ordinary couples counselling — if
someone is deliberately isolating you from friends, family or church; monitoring
you or controlling whom you see; or making you manage your words and behaviour to
prevent intimidation, punishment or retaliation. If you are unsure whether it is
abuse, the specialist service can help you work that out. You do not need to
arrive with a diagnosis.

## Which help for which problem

One principle governs this whole page: **seek spiritual help for spiritual
discernment, clinical help for clinical needs, legal help for legal questions,
and specialist safety help for coercion or abuse.** These can work alongside each
other. They should not impersonate one another. One person may hold more than one
qualification — some pastors are also licensed therapists — so what matters is
the capacity in which they are helping you and whether they stay within its
limits.

**A pastor or mature Christian mentor** — for biblical and spiritual guidance,
testing a spiritual claim (Lesson 13), the counsel circle of Lesson 14, prayer
and accountability. Not the sole specialist for abuse, addiction, legal questions
or mental-health needs beyond their training. Pastoral counselling and licensed
therapy are not automatically the same service; ask what training, supervision,
safeguarding policy and confidentiality arrangements apply.

**A suitably qualified counsellor or therapist** — for recurring emotional and
relational patterns (Lessons 3, 12 and 17), for the fear of Lesson 20 when it is
constant or tied to a sense of worthlessness, for grief after an ending, for the
effects of a harmful relationship once you are out of it, and for couples work
where both people can speak freely and individual screening has found no fear,
coercive control or abuse. If either person is afraid, get specialist individual
advice before any joint session. "Christian" in the title is not a qualification
on its own: look for recognised training, registration or licensing where
applicable, supervision, an ethical code and a complaints process — and ask how
they integrate faith rather than assuming the word guarantees clinical competence
or sound theology.

**An addiction or recovery professional** — for substance use, gambling or a
compulsive behavioural pattern, yours or theirs. A dating relationship cannot
treat these, and a promise to stop is not treatment.

**An independent, appropriately regulated financial professional or reputable
non-profit debt service** — for substantial debt, insolvency, joint financial
commitments or decisions beyond your competence (Lesson 11). A "financial
adviser" may primarily sell investment products; for debt, a non-profit service
is often the better first call, and many countries have free ones. Where money is
being withheld or used to control you, start with a specialist abuse service
instead.

**A legal adviser** — for property, jointly committed money, immigration, custody
or a child, a broken engagement with financial consequences, or harassment and
stalking. Take legal advice from a legal adviser, not from a pastor or friend,
however wise. Rights and reporting options differ by country. Legal advice should
never delay movement to safety in an emergency.

**A specialist abuse service** — for fear, coercion, control, violence, stalking,
sexual pressure or financial exploitation, at any stage of a relationship or
after it. They help you plan, and they know where to send you for everything
else. They are also the right first call for digital safety if you suspect
monitoring.

**A forced-marriage or family-coercion service** — for a marriage being arranged
against your will, or a family or community enforcing a relationship you have not
chosen.

**A doctor or qualified medical professional** — for fertility questions, sexual
and reproductive health, pregnancy, injuries, medication, health conditions, or
physical symptoms linked to stress and low mood. Each person is responsible for
their own medical care; a partner is not entitled to private records or
unrestricted access to appointments.

## Consider it

None of these is a verdict. Each is a reason to bring someone qualified in.

- A pattern is entrenched, has appeared in previous relationships, or has not
  responded to what you have tried.
- The same difficulty keeps returning through repeated pauses and resumptions.
- The difficulty involves addiction, a health condition, serious debt or a legal
  matter.
- You are the only one working on the problem, and you are running out.
- The fear of being alone is constant, affects your sleep, work or other
  relationships, or makes you feel worthless without a partner.
- Grief after an ending is becoming disabling, or comes with persistent
  hopelessness. There is no timetable for grief; the question is not whether you
  are over it but whether you have enough support and can keep functioning
  safely.
- You cannot tell whether what you are experiencing is ordinary difficulty or
  something more, and reading about it has not helped.

## What good help looks like

- It asks questions before giving answers, and does not coerce, shame or
  spiritually manipulate you towards staying, leaving, marrying or reconciling.
  It may make clear recommendations, especially about safety, but it explains the
  basis for them.
- It respects your freedom to decide, including to decide differently from the
  adviser.
- It knows its limits and refers you on.
- It never asks you to sit in a joint session with someone you are afraid of, and
  never treats abuse as a communication problem.
- It explains confidentiality before significant disclosure — how records are
  kept, and when information may have to be shared. These limits vary by
  profession and country. Do not assume an informal conversation with a pastor
  carries the same rules as licensed therapy; ask who may be told, and when.
- It does not use Scripture, prophecy or spiritual authority to override your
  judgement or your safety.

If an adviser claims private certainty about whom God requires you to marry,
treats disagreement as rebellion, or insists on joint sessions after you have
said you are afraid, you are free to stop and find someone else. That is not
disloyalty. It is the same discernment this course asks of you throughout.

**Questions to ask anyone you are considering:** What qualification or training
do you hold? Are you registered, licensed or professionally accountable, and to
whom? What experience do you have with this concern? What are the limits of
confidentiality? Do you keep notes, and who can access them? What happens if you
believe someone is in immediate danger? How do you handle faith within your work?
What is your policy where abuse or coercive control may be present? Where will
you refer me if this is outside your competence?

## A last word

Seeking help is not a failure of faith or a failure of the relationship. It is
what wise people do with a burden too heavy to carry alone. "Where there is no
guidance, a people falls, but in an abundance of counselors there is safety"
(Proverbs 11:14). The proverb concerns the governance of a nation rather than
dating, but the principle holds. You were never meant to do this alone.
