# Am I Ready to Date?

You have done three pieces of work: examined why you want a relationship, looked
at what "equally yoked" actually asks of you, and written down who you are and
what you require.

This page is not a test, and it will not tell you whether you are allowed to
date. It is a place to stop and read your own answers before going further.

## Look back before you look forward

Take a few minutes with what you wrote. You are not re-doing the tools — only
noticing.

- **From Lesson 1:** which motive was loudest? Has it already influenced a
  decision you made?
- **From Lesson 2:** what did Part A show you about your own faith — what it
  produces, where you belong, where you are going?
- **From Lesson 3:** which box was hardest to fill honestly? Was anything in your
  first two boxes really a preference you are afraid to hold loosely?

Then answer one question in your own words: **what has become clearer over these
three lessons that was not clear before?**

## Where does that leave you?

Choose the one that fits. You can change it later, and none of them is a better
result than the others.

**I am ready to begin dating thoughtfully.**
It means you believe you now have enough clarity about your motives, your faith
and what you require to begin dating thoughtfully. That clarity will continue to
develop as you gain experience and seek counsel. Continue to Module 2, which is
about choosing wisely.

**I need to strengthen some areas first.**
Something in Lesson 1's next faithful step or Lesson 3's Part E is unfinished,
and you would rather do that work before adding another person to it. This is not
a delay or a failure. Name the one area and the one next step, and set a date to
look at this page again. Module 2 will still be here.

**I have noticed something I need counsel about.**
Something surfaced that deserves more than private reflection — a recurring
pattern, a concern from Lesson 2's spiritual freedom and pressure section, a past
experience that still needs care, or a line in your own list that you have
already bent. For ordinary discernment, take it to a wise, independent person. If
it involves fear, coercion, abuse, stalking, sexual pressure or surveillance,
begin with **Finding Help Where You Live** and seek appropriate individual or
specialist support. Lesson 14 will help you build a circle of counsel, but you do
not have to wait until then.

**I am already dating, and I am continuing to Module 2.**
The first three lessons remain useful when you are already dating — more useful
in some ways, because you can consider them in relation to an actual relationship
rather than an imagined one. Keep what you wrote. Lesson 7 and Lesson 15 will
both ask for it.

## Before you go on

Nothing here produces a score, and no combination of answers means your
relationship passes or fails. "Not yet" remains a legitimate conclusion at every
point in this course.

One thing worth saying plainly before Module 2 begins. The next module asks you
to observe another person — their character, their patterns, the things that do
not match what they say. That work is ordinary and healthy in an ordinary
relationship.

If you are afraid of the person you are dating, or if observing them honestly
feels dangerous rather than simply uncomfortable, that is not a sign that you
need to try harder at Module 2. Go first to **Finding Help Where You Live**. If it
is safe and useful to continue reading, Lesson 6 will help you distinguish
ordinary difficulties from warning patterns.
