# What Comes Next?

You have reached the end of the course. What happens now depends on what the
questions showed you, and there are four honest places to be standing.

## If you are not ready

One of you answered *not yet*, or a question opened something that is still open. That
is not a failed course. It is the course working — the whole point of threshold
questions is that they can be answered before a public promise rather than after one.

Go back to the lesson that fits what surfaced. Something unresolved about direction or
spiritual foundation belongs with Lessons 2, 15 and 16. A pace that has outrun
knowledge belongs with Lesson 18. A difficulty that both of you can name, and are free
and safe to work on together, belongs with Lesson 17. Fear, coercion or abuse belongs
with individual specialist support instead.

Then give the other person an honest horizon rather than an indefinite wait, and
remember what the last page said: a review point is not a promise that the eventual
answer will be yes.

## If you decide not to continue

You may have arrived here and found the answer is no.

Lesson 19 is where the practical help is — how to say it clearly and kindly where that
is safe, and, if you are afraid of how it will be received, how to plan the ending
with an individual specialist service. Answer the safety question there before you
plan or announce anything.

A freely given no is sufficient. You do not need certainty, agreement, a villain, or a
case that would satisfy someone else.

## If you become engaged

The work is not over; it changes.

Engagement is a season of preparation, and the conversations that belong to it are
more detailed than anything this course has asked: money, family, faith, sex,
conflict, children, the shape of the marriage itself. Begin them before wedding plans
gather momentum, because plans are much easier to make than to unmake.

**Seek competent premarital preparation.** Choose someone who will ask difficult
questions rather than merely prepare a ceremony — ask what training they have, what
they will cover, and whether they are willing to tell an engaged couple something they
do not want to hear. Where fear, coercion or abuse is present, seek individual
specialist help rather than joint counselling.

And hold on to this: **engagement does not remove your freedom to respond to new
information.** Premarital preparation may bring previously unexamined matters into view
— that is one reason engagement remains a season of preparation rather than a
guarantee. An engagement can be paused, and it can be ended, and either is far better
than a marriage entered because the invitations were printed. If something troubles
you after the yes, Lesson 16 is still there, and so is the support page.

## If you are not dating

You may have come through this course with no particular person in view. That was a
legitimate way to take it, and what you did is not preparation for a real version
later — it is the work itself. You have examined your motives, requirements, patterns
and limits. You have clearer language for what to look for and what deserves attention
rather than explanation.

Keep what you wrote. Lessons 1, 3 and 7 all produce material you will want when there
is someone to think about, and it will be more honest now than it would be then.

## What this course does not give you

No score. No verdict on your relationship, and no certificate claiming that you are
ready to marry. If the course records completion, that means only that you completed
the material; it does not approve or certify your relationship.

What it has given you is language for things that were previously just a feeling,
tools for distinguishing what you have observed from what you have hoped, and a set of
distinctions that are hard to unlearn: attraction and discernment, charisma and
character, a difficulty and a danger, a good person and a right relationship, pattern
and severity, privacy and secrecy.

## Support does not end here

**Finding Help Where You Live** stays available to you whether or not you are working
through lessons, and whether or not you ever return to this course. You do not need to
be mid-lesson to use it, and you never needed to finish anything to reach it.

If something surfaced during these twenty lessons that is bigger than a course — past
harm, a fear that will not lift, a pattern you cannot interrupt alone — that is worth
taking to someone qualified. It is not a sign that you did this badly. It is the most
sensible possible response to having looked honestly at something difficult.

## A last word

This course began by saying it would not choose a partner for you, predict your
future, or tell you whether your relationship passes. It has kept that promise, and I
hope by now you can see why it was worth keeping.

The freedom to say no is not a threat to a good relationship. It is what makes a yes
mean anything at all — because a yes that could not have been a no is not a gift.
Whatever you have decided, and whatever comes next, carry that with you.

Go well.
