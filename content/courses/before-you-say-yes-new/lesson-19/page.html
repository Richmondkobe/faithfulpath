Transcript and Scripture references

1 · How do I end a relationship well, and before God?

Welcome back. This is Lesson 19, in Part Five: Continue, Slow Down or Walk Away.

We have walked two of the three paths: continuing with wisdom, and slowing down well. This lesson is about the third: walking away. I will picture it as the walker on the path, still carrying the lamp, and not walking alone, because the Lord is near.

And we carry one question with us: how do I end a relationship well, and before God?

2 · Before anything else

Before anything else, one question. Are you afraid of how this person will react if you end the relationship?

If the answer is yes, or if you are not sure, the ordinary advice in this lesson is not for you, and following it could put you at risk. Please contact a specialist service before you make plans or tell the person. If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services or go somewhere safer first. The section on ending safely, later in this lesson, and the help page beneath it, are for you.

If you are not afraid, we begin with Scripture.

3 · So far as it depends on you

Our passage comes from Paul's letter to the Romans:

"Repay no one evil for evil, but give thought to do what is honorable in the sight of all. If possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all."

4 · If possible, so far as it depends on you

Notice the two small phrases at the start of the second verse: "if possible" and "so far as it depends on you". Paul does not pretend that peace is always possible, or that it is ever entirely in one person's hands. He asks you to do your part: not to repay wrong with wrong, to act honourably where others can see, and to seek peace as far as you are able.

That matters for this lesson. Living peaceably with all does not mean that every relationship must continue. Sometimes an honest, kind ending is the peaceable way. Staying in something that should end is not peace, only postponement.

5 · Love that is genuine

Paul wrote to the churches in Rome, and this chapter begins his description of the life of people who have received God's mercy: love that is genuine, a hatred of evil, a firm hold on what is good, and a refusal to repay wrong with wrong. He was not writing about dating. But he was writing about how believers treat people when relationships are hard, and that is exactly where an ending sits.

Later we will come to a second passage, from a psalm of David: "The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit." It was not written about the end of a dating relationship either. But its promise belongs to all God's hurting people.

6 · Two very different endings

Seeing is not the same as acting. Many people know, and stay, for months or years, because ending a relationship is one of the hardest ordinary things a person does, and Christians are rarely taught how to end something well.

This lesson covers two very different situations, and they must be kept apart. The first is the ordinary ending, between two people of goodwill. What matters most there is honesty and kindness. The second is an ending where the other person's reaction may itself be the danger. What matters most there is safety, and the rules are different.

7 · Point 1: Knowing it is time

Our first point is knowing it is time. You do not need certainty. You do not need a villain. You do not need the other person's agreement, your family's permission, or a word from God that overrides your judgement. Discernment is allowed to conclude no, and that is what it was for.

The seriousness of your reflection should match the seriousness and length of the relationship. Someone three dates in does not need a worksheet to decide that this is not for them. You do not owe every dating relationship a long programme of repair. And you never owe an unsafe relationship more time.

8 · When it is time

The incompatibility is directional. You want materially different lives, and time and effort have not changed that. The evidence did not come. A difficulty was named, a fair period was allowed, and the change did not appear, or appeared only under threat. Help was refused. The other person admits a serious problem whenever a breakup is threatened, and keeps declining the help it requires. Acknowledgement that exists to prevent an ending is not responsibility.

The whole relationship no longer holds: something that cannot be worked around, like the shared foundation, the freedom to say no, or freely chosen desire on both sides. And there is harm. If fear, coercion, control, intimidation, violence, sexual pressure or financial exploitation is present, you do not need to have tried everything. You need to be safe.

9 · What ending is not

And be truthful about what ending is not. It is not a threat to produce change. A breakup announced in order to be talked out of is a manipulation, and it teaches the other person that your no does not mean no. It is not a test of whether they will fight for you: if you want to know whether they care, ask. And it is not a request for reassurance.

If you are using an ending as any of these, stop and say what you actually want. But you remain free to end a dating relationship without proving that your reasons are perfect. If what you want is for it to be over, that is reason enough.

10 · Point 2: Ending well: clarity

Our second point is ending well, and it begins with clarity, because ambiguity is a slow cruelty.

A person who is being ended with deserves to know that this is what is happening. Not "I need some space". Not "let's take a break and see". If you have decided, say that you have decided, in words that cannot be heard as a question: "I have decided to end our relationship. I am not asking you to change my mind." It will feel hard as you say it. It is far kinder than the alternative: a person spending weeks trying to reopen a door you had already closed, without being told it was closed.

11 · Directly, where it is safe

Do it directly, where it is safe to. Where there is no safety concern, and the relationship has been serious, a face-to-face conversation is usually the most respectful way. Choose a neutral place, with enough privacy for dignity but with other people nearby, a clear exit and your own transport. Not in the middle of a family occasion, at church, or in front of friends.

And do not leave them to work it out from your silence. The slow fade is not a gentler ending; it is an ending the other person is made to perform on your behalf. Where a relationship has been brief, or largely online, a phone call or a clear written message is not a failure of courage. Match the medium to the relationship, not to your discomfort.

12 · A reason, not a case

Say why, briefly and without a verdict. In an established relationship, it is respectful to give a brief and truthful reason. A reason is not a case. "We want different lives, and I have come to believe that will not change." One or two sentences that are true. Then stop.

Resist two temptations. The first is to soften the reason until it is no longer true, like "it's not you, I just need to focus on myself". It will not survive their first question. The second is to make the reason so complete that it becomes an indictment. If the relationship is ending because of their conduct, you may say so, plainly, once. You do not need to make them agree.

13 · No negotiation, and no borrowed authority

Do not negotiate, and do not offer a smaller relationship. They may propose changes, promises, counselling or a different pace. If you had wanted those, Lessons 17 and 18 were the place for them. And do not promise immediate friendship as a consolation. Friendship may be possible later, but it should not be used to soften the decision.

And do not use God to avoid owning your decision. You may have prayed, sought counsel and reached the decision before God; that is how it should be. But "I don't have peace about it" or "God is leading me elsewhere" can be a way of avoiding a reason you could give, and attributing the decision to God leaves the other person unable to respond. Say I have decided, and take responsibility for the judgement you have made.

14 · Privately first, then step back

Prepare privately; do not announce publicly. It is reasonable to tell one or two trustworthy people beforehand, for prayer, support or safety. But the other person should not hear it through gossip, or from someone else, before you have told them. In a shared church or friendship group, agree if you can on a brief public account you can both give. But neither person may forbid the other from speaking truthfully and confidentially to a pastor, a counsellor or a trusted supporter.

Then step back. A period of no contact is not cruelty; it is how both people learn to be single again. Name a length, and keep to it. You can ask the other person to respect it, but it does not need their agreement. Where you share responsibilities, at work, in church or in practical matters, it may need adjusting to brief, practical contact only. Return what needs returning. Their grief is real, and it is not evidence that you were wrong. Offer kindness in the ending, and then allow their friends, family or pastor to support them.

15 · Point 3: If they will not accept it

Our third point is what to do if they will not accept it. They keep calling. They turn up. They write long messages explaining why you are mistaken, or enlist family or a pastor. You have said no. You do not owe further explanation, or a hearing for the case they wish to make. Repeat the decision once, in the same words, and then stop responding. Every reply, however firm, is a sign that the conversation is still open. But if there are threats, stalking or intimidation, you do not need to repeat your decision at all. Safety comes first: do not reply, keep a record, and follow the guidance on ending safely, with a specialist service.

If they threaten to harm themselves, take the threat seriously, without making yourself responsible for preventing it. Contact an emergency or crisis service, or alert someone equipped to help: a family member of theirs, a pastor, a close friend. Do not resume the relationship, go to them alone, or bargain with your decision to keep them safe. The right response is to hand the risk to people who can carry it.

And watch for escalation. There is a difference between a grieving person who cannot yet let go and a person who will not let you go. Contact that continues after you have stopped replying, that reaches your workplace or family, that includes threats, or turns up where you are, is no longer grief. It is the situation the next part of this lesson is about.

16 · Ending safely (1)

Now, ending safely. This is for anyone who is afraid of what will happen when they end the relationship. If you are not sure whether that describes you, listen anyway. Fear is sometimes recognised only when it is named.

Abuse services in many countries report that the time around the ending of a relationship is when the risk of harm from a controlling or violent partner is highest. No lesson can give you a safety plan fitted to your circumstances, but a specialist service can, so find one. If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services or go somewhere safer first; planning must never delay protection.

If you are afraid, do not arrange a private conversation to end it. The instinct to do it properly, face to face, is a good instinct in an ordinary ending and a dangerous one here. A phone call or a message is not cowardice. Do not give notice; do not say "we need to talk this weekend", because a controlling person uses the notice period. Tell at least one trustworthy person or advocate what you are doing and when, identify the safest place you can go, and let a specialist service help you plan the order of events.

17 · Ending safely (2)

If you suspect that your phone, computer or accounts are being monitored, do not suddenly delete apps, disable access or change settings on that device. Sudden changes may alert the person, and may destroy evidence you will later want. Use a safer device, one they have never accessed, to contact a specialist service, and let them help you decide what to change, and when.

Keep a record. If there have been threats, unwanted contact or incidents, write them down with dates, and do not keep the only copy where the other person can reach it.

If your family or community is the pressure, including a marriage being arranged against your will, do not use ordinary family mediation or joint pastoral counselling. Both assume everyone is free to speak, and you are not. Contact a service that specialises in forced marriage or family pressure first. And if you live together, share finances, or there is a child or a pregnancy, take proper legal advice, but safety first, then the paperwork.

18 · Point 4: After

Our fourth point is what comes after. Whatever kind of ending it was, there is grief on the other side of it, and it is allowed.

If you were the one who ended it, you may be surprised by how much it hurts, and you may take the hurt as a sign that you were wrong. It is not. Grief is the cost of having cared, not evidence that you should have stayed. Let it run. Do not fill the space immediately with someone else.

If you were the one who was told no, receive it as final. Do not stay in contact for the hidden purpose of changing their mind, and do not turn it into a spiritual contest. Grieve it as the real loss it is. And if the loss awakens a fear that no one else will come, Lesson 20 was written for you.

19 · Learning, and the Lord who is near

After an ordinary ending, take the ending back to the people who walked with you through the relationship. Not to be told you were right, but to learn. What did you see late that you could have seen early? Which of your own patterns were in this? The next relationship will begin with whatever you did not learn from this one.

After an abusive relationship, that is not the question. Abuse is the responsibility of the person who chose it, never of the person who was harmed, and recovery is not an examination of your part in it. It means safety, support and healing, often with specialist help. Be gentle with yourself about how long it took. People stay for reasons that make sense from the inside: hope, love, fear, faith, children, money, shame. Leaving is usually a process rather than an event. You are out. That is what matters.

Ending a relationship, even one that had to end, is a loss, and you do not have to pretend otherwise before God. "The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit." You have done a hard thing. Let him be near.

20 · Bringing it together

Let us bring it together. First, the safety question: if you are afraid of how they will react, contact a specialist before anything else. You do not need certainty or a villain to end a relationship; discernment may conclude no. End it well: clearly, directly where it is safe, briefly and truthfully, without negotiation, and owning your decision. If they will not accept it, repeat it once and stop. And afterwards, grieve, learn, and let the Lord be near. So far as it depends on you, that is how to live peaceably, even in an ending.

21 · One step now

Here is one step for today. Write one sentence you would want to be true of how you end a relationship. For example: "I want them to know clearly that it is over, and to know that I was kind."

If it is safe, write it privately, or simply think it through before God. And if you are afraid, your next step is not a conversation. It is contacting a specialist service.

It is private and optional, and nothing you type is saved. If this worksheet could be discovered by the other person, do not complete it; use a safer device, or speak to a specialist service instead.

22 · Prayer

Let us pray.

Lord, I have been carrying this for a long time, and I am tired. Give me courage to be honest and show me the safest way to take the next step. Keep me from taking back a wise decision simply because it is hard. I trust the other person to you, without making myself responsible for their choices. And when it is done and the room is quiet, be near. You have promised to be near the brokenhearted, and I will need you. In Jesus' name, amen.
