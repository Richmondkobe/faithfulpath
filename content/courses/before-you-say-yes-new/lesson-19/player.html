Lesson 19 · Book chapter 19 · Part 5

When to Walk Away

How do I end a relationship well, and before God?

Before anything else: are you afraid of how this person will react if you end the relationship?

If yes, or not sure: the ordinary advice in this lesson is not for you. Contact a specialist service before you make plans or tell the person. In immediate danger: emergency services, or somewhere safer first.

Not afraid? We begin with Scripture.

Romans 12 · Paul’s letter to the churches in Rome

“Repay no one evil for evil, but give thought to do what is honorable in the sight of all. If possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all.”Romans 12:17–18 (ESV)

Understand the original meaning

“If possible”“So far as it depends on you”

Peace is not always possible, and never entirely in one person’s hands. Do your part.

Living peaceably with all does not mean every relationship must continue. An honest, kind ending can be the peaceable way.

Explore the background

The life of people who have received God’s mercy

genuine lovehatred of evilholding to goodno repaying wrong

Not written about dating, but about how believers treat people when relationships are hard.

“The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit.”Psalm 34:18 (ESV)

Understand the teaching

Seeing is not the same as acting.

An ordinary ending: honesty and kindness.

An ending where you are afraid: safety. The rules are different.

Point 1 · Knowing it is time

You do not need…

certaintya villaintheir agreementpermissiona word overriding your judgement

Discernment is allowed to conclude no.

Match the reflection to the relationship. You never owe an unsafe relationship more time.

When it is time

  1. •The incompatibility is directionalMaterially different lives, and time has not changed that.
  2. •The evidence did not comeOr it appeared only under threat.
  3. •Help was refusedAcknowledgement that exists to prevent an ending is not responsibility.
  4. •The whole relationship no longer holdsThe shared foundation, the freedom to say no, freely chosen desire.

There is harm? You do not need to have tried everything. You need to be safe.

What an ending is not

Not a threat to produce change. Not a test of whether they will fight for you. Not a request for reassurance.

If what you want is for it to be over, that is reason enough.

Point 2 · Ending well

Clarity, because ambiguity is a slow cruelty.

Not “I need some space”. Not “let’s take a break and see”.

“I have decided to end our relationship. I am not asking you to change my mind.”

Directly, where it is safe to

A neutral place: other people nearby, a clear exit, your own transport. Not at church, a family occasion, or in front of friends.

The slow fade is an ending the other person is made to perform on your behalf.

Match the medium to the relationship, not to your discomfort.

Say why, briefly and without a verdict.

“We want different lives, and I have come to believe that will not change.”

One or two true sentences. Then stop. A reason is not a case.

Do not soften it until it is untrue; do not make it an indictment.

No negotiation, and no borrowed authority

  1. •Do not negotiatePromises, counselling or a different pace belonged to Lessons 17 and 18. No immediate friendship as a consolation.
  2. •Do not use God to avoid owning your decisionPrayer and counsel are right; “I don’t have peace” leaves them unable to respond. Say “I have decided”.

Prepare privately; do not announce publicly.

They should not hear it from someone else. No one may forbid the other from speaking confidentially to a pastor, counsellor or supporter.

Then step back: a named period of no contact. It does not need their agreement; shared responsibilities may need brief, practical contact only.

Offer kindness in the ending; let their friends, family or pastor support them.

Point 3 · If they will not accept it

Repeat it once, in the same words. Then stop responding.

Threats, stalking or intimidation? You do not need to repeat it at all. Safety comes first: do not reply, keep a record, and go to ending safely, with a specialist.

A threat of self-harm: take it seriously, and hand it to people who can carry it. Do not go back, go alone, or bargain your decision.

Ending safely: for anyone afraid of what will happen when they end it

The time around a separation can be when the risk is highest. A specialist service can help you plan: find one.

Do not arrange a private conversation to end it. A call or a message is not cowardice. Do not give notice.

Tell a trustworthy person or advocate; know the safest place you can go; let a specialist plan the order of events with you.

Ending safely (continued)

  1. •If your devices may be watchedDo not suddenly delete apps or change settings there. Use a safer device to contact a specialist.
  2. •Keep a recordThreats and incidents, with dates, where they cannot reach it.
  3. •If family or community is the pressureNo family mediation or joint counselling: a specialist service first.

Living together, finances, a child or a pregnancy? Safety first, then the paperwork.

Point 4 · After: grief, healing and hope

There is grief on the other side, and it is allowed.

Grief is the cost of having cared, not evidence that you should have stayed.

Told no? Receive it as final, and grieve it as a real loss. Afraid no one else will come? Lesson 20 was written for you.

After an ordinary ending: learn, with the people who walked with you.

After an abusive relationship: the harm was not your fault. Recovery means safety, support and healing. Leaving is usually a process, not an event.

“The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit.”Psalm 34:18 (ESV)

Bringing it together

  1. Safety first: if you are afraid, contact a specialist before anything else
  2. No certainty or villain needed: discernment may conclude no
  3. End well: clearly, directly where safe, briefly and truthfully, without negotiation, owning it
  4. If they will not accept it, repeat once and stop (with threats, do not reply); then grieve, learn, and let the Lord be near

One step now

Write one sentence you would want to be true of how you end a relationship.

“I want them to know clearly that it is over, and to know that I was kind.”

If you are afraid: your next step is not a conversation. It is contacting a specialist service.

Prayer

Lord, I have been carrying this for a long time, and I am tired. Give me courage to be honest and show me the safest way to take the next step. Keep me from taking back a wise decision simply because it is hard.

I trust the other person to you, without making myself responsible for their choices. And when it is done and the room is quiet, be near. You have promised to be near the brokenhearted, and I will need you. In Jesus’ name, amen.
