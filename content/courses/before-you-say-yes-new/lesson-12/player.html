Lesson 12 · Book chapter 12 · Part 3

Their Past Matters — But Not the Way You Think

How should I weigh someone’s past, and my own?

Everyone you might marry has a past. You will need to know some of it. You are not entitled to all of it.

Not destiny. Not irrelevant. Neither is true, and neither is Christian.

Wisdom considers both the history and what has followed.

1 Timothy 1:12–16 · Paul looks back on his own life

“…though formerly I was a blasphemer, persecutor, and insolent opponent. But I received mercy… and the grace of our Lord overflowed for me with the faith and love that are in Christ Jesus.”1 Timothy 1:13–14 (ESV)

“…Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the foremost. But I received mercy for this reason, that in me, as the foremost, Jesus Christ might display his perfect patience…”1 Timothy 1:15–16 (ESV)

Understand the original meaning

ἠλεήθην

ēleēthēn ✱ · “I received mercy”, from ἐλεέω (eleeō), to show mercy

Mercy was given, not earned.

“Of whom I am the foremost”: Paul neither hid his past nor was ruled by it.

Explore the background

Saul “was ravaging the church” (Acts 8:3). Then the risen Jesus met him.

In Jerusalem, the disciples were afraid of him at first (Acts 9:26). Caution did not vanish overnight.

“…he judged me faithful, appointing me to his service.”1 Timothy 1:12 (ESV)

The reach of grace, not a rule that every repentant person suits every relationship.

Point 1 · What the past cannot tell you

Whether a person is forgiven: that is between them and God.

“…as far as the east is from the west, so far does he remove our transgressions from us.”Psalm 103:12 (ESV)

“If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come.”2 Corinthians 5:17 (ESV)

God’s forgiveness is not ours to grant or withhold. We can still weigh present conduct, trust and safety.

Forgiveness does not erase consequences, restore trust automatically, or create a right to marriage.

Declining a relationship is not the same as denying forgiveness.

Not a lesser Christian, a damaged gift, or a second-class spouse.

And not, on its own, whether they would be a good husband or wife.

Point 2 · What the past can tell you

Four questions

Truth
Truth

Shared honestly, at the right time, and consistent over months?

Responsibility
Responsibility

Where they caused harm, can they own it, without blaming others?

Response
Response

What concrete steps have followed? Done, not just described.

Present effect
Present effect

What obligations, risks or patterns are still active?

An honest account of a difficult history is worth more than a clean one.

A person who was harmed should never be pressed into false equal blame.

Some particular pasts

1Previous relationships

What ended, why, and what did they learn?

A repeated pattern raises a question. It does not supply the answer.

2Divorce

Legally final? Spoken of with discretion, or contempt?

Do they own their part, where there was one?

Never require a survivor to invent equal blame.

Christians differ

Christians differ on when remarriage after divorce is permitted. This course does not settle it for you.

Know your church’s teaching, and theirs. Take the question to your pastor early.

Before engagement: children and custody · support · property · legal matters · contact with a former spouse · safety

1Children

A child is a person, not baggage.

Their wellbeing before your convenience.

Dating a parent does not make you a parent.

2Addiction

Recovery is a process, not a flawless record.

The concern: active addiction with deception or refusal of help.

A partner cannot be the treatment plan.

Sexual historynot owed the details; current commitments and health information that affects the other person need honesty
Criminal historysome conduct affects safety; violence or sexual offending: specialist safeguarding advice
Faith historywhat do they believe now, and does their life increasingly align with it?

Demanding a list of someone’s sexual history is a warning sign about you.

Dating discernment cannot replace a professional risk assessment.

Trauma is not a past to evaluate

What was done to a person is not something they did. It is a wound.

Survivors decide when, how much, and whether to tell.

“There are things in my past I’m not ready to talk about” is a complete and honest answer for that stage.

When someone tells you

The response that helps is simple, and it is not a set of questions.

“Thank you for trusting me. I am sorry this happened. It was not your fault. You do not have to give me details. What would help you feel respected and safe now?”

Not “why” questions. Not “are you healed?” Not on that day.

If you are the survivor: the timing is yours, and the detail is yours.

Point 3 · What needs to be known, and when

What a reasonable person would need, to make an informed decision

Early datingnothing detailed
Before exclusivitya current marriage · children · another relationship · safety risks · active addiction · relevant violence
Before engagementprevious marriages · custody and support · debt or legal matters · relevant criminal history · health information that affects the other person

Never owed: the details of trauma · a sexual history · every past relationship · another person’s confidence

Disclosure is mutual: the same standard, the same timetable.

ReceivingThanks first · never ammunition · time to think · free to decide · confidential, unless someone is in danger
GivingAt the right stage, plainly · what happened, what you did, where you are now · a safe setting

If they cannot receive it, that is painful, and it is also information.

Point 4 · Neither record decides it

Neither record decides it.

A difficult past does not stop someone becoming a faithful spouse. “I received mercy.”

A clean record does not guarantee character: untested is not proven.

Look for present truth, responsibility, wisdom and sustained conduct.

A word about safety

A history of violence towards a partner, a child, or anyone vulnerable is a present safety matter.

“That was the old me” needs long, external, specific evidence, and even then you are not obliged to continue.

If a child or vulnerable adult may be in danger: do not investigate or confront alone. Seek safeguarding advice. Do not promise secrecy.

Where you are right now

Not datingask the four questions of yourself first
Unsettleddo not decide in the moment; declining is not condemning
Not yet toldtell them, carefully, with support

Harm done to you: you deserve care and support. Harm you caused: take responsibility; seek qualified help. Where anyone may be at risk: safeguarding first.

Not a lesson’s work. A season’s.

Bringing it together

  1. “I received mercy”: the past is neither destiny nor irrelevant (1 Timothy 1:13)
  2. Four questions: truth, responsibility, response, present effect
  3. Know what is needed, when; never demand what is not yours to know
  4. Neither record decides it. Declining is not condemning.

One step now

Write one thing about your own past that grace has changed.

“I used to hide my mistakes; now I can name them.” · “I no longer believe my past makes me second-class.”

If it is safe, write it privately, or simply think it through before God.

The full Past Relationship Reflection is below this lesson (optional). It is not a file on anyone: never write the details of anyone’s trauma, or your own. If someone else may access your device or papers, simply think through the questions instead.

Prayer

Father, thank you that you remove our sins as far as the east is from the west. Help me to see my own past as forgiven.

Help me to treat other people’s pasts with grace and honesty. Give me wisdom to see what still matters today, without holding against anyone what you have forgiven.

And where harm has been done to me or to someone I love, bring healing and safe help. In Jesus’ name, amen.

May you see clearly both what happened and what has followed, without ignoring what is still in the room.
