Lesson 17 · Book chapter 17 · Part 5

When to Continue With Wisdom

How do I know this difficulty can be worked through?

“That is not us, but we do have problems.”

Many difficulties are ordinary friction, not reasons to end. Others reveal incompatibility, a personal pattern, or a safety concern.

Facing harm, not a difficulty? This lesson does not apply: Lesson 6 comes first.

Ephesians 4 · written from prison

“I therefore, a prisoner for the Lord, urge you to walk in a manner worthy of the calling to which you have been called, with all humility and gentleness, with patience, bearing with one another in love, eager to maintain the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.”Ephesians 4:1–3 (ESV)

Understand the original meaning

ἀνέχομαι

anechomai ✱ · “bear with”: to bear with or tolerate patiently

“…bearing with one another and, if one has a complaint against another, forgiving each other…”Colossians 3:13 (ESV)

Patience with ordinary weakness. Not the carrying of harm.

Explore the background

“Put on then, as God’s chosen ones, holy and beloved, compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience… And above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony.”Colossians 3:12, 14 (ESV)

Christian patience within the church: not a command to remain in every dating relationship.

Point 1 · Continuing is a decision, not a drift

Continuing is a decision, not a drift.

A habit, then an assumption, then a wedding. Nobody said yes; they just never said no.

A wise yes is a deliberate one: named, believed workable, and chosen.

A deliberate yes can be reviewed. A drift cannot.

Cannot say what you are continuing despite, or towards? Go back to Lessons 15 and 16.

A yes with nothing named is not a decision. It is a postponement.

Point 2 · Four kinds of difficulty

Not every problem is the same problem.

  1. •1 A skill difficultyThe same goal, no method yet. Generally responds to effort.
  2. •2 External strainHealth, grief, work, immigration, family or money. Name it before treating it as a verdict.
  1. •3 A personal patternAnger, withdrawal, fear, dishonesty, money, substances: individual work, often professional help.
  2. •4 A directional incompatibilityMaterially different lives or marriages: trying harder does not change what a person wants.

Skill: repair · Strain: patience and practical help · Pattern: individual work · Direction: reconsider (Lesson 16)

The kinds overlap. Sort it now, work on it, and sort it again at the review.

Point 3 · What makes a difficulty workable

“We fight a lot” is a fog. “When one of us is hurt, we both withdraw, and the silence lasts for days” can be worked on.

“They are a terrible person” is a verdict. Naming it honestly may mean naming one person’s conduct.

Mutually addressed: the feature most often missing.

One person carrying all the effort is not repair. It is being managed, and the manager will run out.

Mutual does not mean equal blame: both act on their own part.

Evidence of change: not a promise, but something that happened differently.

The silence lasted a day instead of four. The apology came without being extracted.

Newly named? Agree a response and allow time, but do not move towards engagement on good intentions alone.

Movement is usually uneven.

After “I will leave if this continues”: perhaps a beginning. Look for change that continues after the crisis.

Safer, more honestRecognised earlierQuicker recoveryLasting outside a crisisLess burdenHelp sought

Point 4 · Continuing well

Continuing well:

  1. •Say it out loud, togetherNot a secret performance review or a disguised ultimatum.
  2. •One or two prioritiesNot everything: start with what is urgent, or unlocks wider change.
  3. •What change would look likeObservable and realistic: “We will talk within a day rather than a week.”

A measure that ignores trauma, illness or a different pace becomes a stick rather than a guide.

  1. •Appropriate help, matched to the problemThe dating partner must never become the other person’s treatment programme.
  2. •Keep your counsel informedBut never ask friends to monitor, test or report on them.
  3. •A review point suited to the problemTo prevent indefinite waiting, not to create an artificial deadline (Lesson 18).

Engagement is not proof that a problem has been solved.

When continuing is the wrong decision

  1. •Not because ending feels like giving upBefore the vows, stopping is not giving up.
  2. •Not because you have invested so muchWeigh it honestly; it does not obligate you to continue.
  3. •Not on the strength of a promiseA promise may begin change; it cannot substitute for it.

When continuing is the wrong decision (continued)

  1. •Not where a problem is admitted only when a breakup is threatened…and help is repeatedly refused. That is not taking responsibility.
  2. •Not from fear there will be no one elseLesson 20 is for that fear. It is real, and it is not a reason.

Fear, coercion, control, intimidation or violence? Harm is not a shared repair project. Go back to Lesson 6.

A relationship is not a problem to be solved.

You have seen each other clearly, and you still choose each other.

Forbearance: patience with ordinary weakness, while speaking truth in love. Never carrying another adult’s responsibilities, concealing harm, or accepting what destroys safety or dignity.

Bringing it together

  1. Continuing is a decision, not a drift
  2. Know the kind: skill, strain, pattern or direction
  3. Workable: named, mutual, with evidence of change
  4. Continue well: out loud, together, with help, counsel and a review, and never to rename harm

One step now

Name one difficulty, and which of the four kinds it is.

“When we disagree, we both go quiet for days. I think that is a skill difficulty.”

If it is safe, write it privately, or simply think it through before God.

The full Continue, Repair or Reconsider Worksheet is below this lesson (optional): for ordinary difficulties where both can speak freely, not for fear or harm. Nothing is saved. If someone else may access your device or papers, simply think through the questions instead.

Prayer

Lord, thank you for the gift of patience. Help me bear with ordinary weakness in love, without carrying what is not mine to carry. Give me humility to see my own part in what is hard, and courage to name what is not mine.

If we continue, help us do it honestly and together. And keep me from drifting where you are calling me to decide. In Jesus’ name, amen.
