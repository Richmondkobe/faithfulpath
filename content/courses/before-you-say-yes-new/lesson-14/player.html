Who Should Have a Voice in Your Relationship?

Whose counsel should I seek, and how do I weigh it?

Tell no oneThe largest decision of your life, made inside the relationship alone.
Tell everyoneEvery opinion gathered, none weighed.

Keep the decision where it belongs: with you.

Counsel is good; control is not. Guidance is on the page beneath this lesson.

1 Kings 12 · Rehoboam at Shechem
“Your father made our yoke heavy.”1 Kings 12:4 (ESV)

“If you will be a servant to this people today and serve them, and speak good words to them when you answer them, then they will be your servants forever.”1 Kings 12:7 (ESV)

“But he abandoned the counsel that the old men gave him and took counsel with the young men who had grown up with him and stood before him.”1 Kings 12:8 (ESV)

“My little finger is thicker than my father’s thighs.” 1 Kings 12:10
“My father disciplined you with whips, but I will discipline you with scorpions.” 1 Kings 12:11 (ESV)

Understand the original meaning

“Without counsel plans fail, but with many advisers they succeed.”Proverbs 15:22 (ESV)

סוֹד

sôḏ ✱ · “counsel”: confidential conversation, a close circle, counsel shared together

“We used to take sweet counsel together…” Psalm 55:14 Remembered after a betrayal: closeness alone is not wisdom.

The aim of counsel is a wiser decision, not a consensus.

Explore the background

The older men had served Solomon and advised kindness. The younger men had grown up with Rehoboam and urged harshness. He asked the older men first, then followed the younger men.

God was at work to fulfil his word (1 Kings 12:15), and the choice was still Rehoboam’s own.

The lesson we draw: ask the people who will agree with you, and call it counsel.

Point 1 · Why you need other voices

“The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, but a wise man listens to advice.”Proverbs 12:15 (ESV)

Your judgement, however good, is partial.

Counsel is not only for warning. Encouragement is information too.

Point 2 · Whose voice, and what each one sees

  1. •Parents and familyHistory and patterns. A blessing freely given is a gift; no one can supply or remove an adult’s consent.
  2. •Pastors and church leadersBiblical, pastoral perspective; they refer beyond their competence. Not authority to choose your spouse (Hebrews 13:17).
  1. •FriendsWhat has changed in you. “Faithful are the wounds of a friend…” (Proverbs 27:6)
  2. •Mentors and mature couplesHonesty, respect, repair and humility, not longevity alone.
  3. •ProfessionalsWithin their competence. Specialist services wherever safety is in question.
  1. •Their peopleNot counsellors to you, but witnesses. No investigations, no secret references.
  2. •God, through prayer and his wordNot one adviser among many: sought before anyone else. Yet our understanding remains fallible.
Counsel caninform, warn, encourage, and show you what you cannot see
Counsel cannotdecide for you, consent for you, or guarantee an outcome

It cannot see what only you see. Nor every private harm: tell them the truth.

If you are the only one who sees the good, would it still look like a reason if you described it plainly?

Not a crowd. A few people who are:

WiseHonestIndependentKnowingDiscreetSafe

“A person who has never disagreed with you is not a counsellor. They are an audience.”

A small, varied circle: your history, your life now, mature Christian judgement, and a professional where needed. Three or four is a guideline, not a rule.

Not only people who will approve, and not only people who will not.

Ask early, before you have decided. Ask specific questions.

  1. •“What specific change have you noticed in me?”
  2. •“Are you concerned about safety, compatibility or preference?”
  3. •“What would change your view?”
  4. •“Are you advising me, or asking me to obey you?”

Do not curate the story. No secret tests, no forced interviews, no joint meeting where safety is in question.

Respect private information. But confidentiality must never stop you seeking help about abuse, threats or serious danger. Share only what is necessary, with a safe person or specialist service.

Point 3 · Weighing what you hear

  1. •Observation over impressionStill, ask what produced an impression of fear.
  2. •Is it independent?Shared bias, or talked first? One voice, not three.
  3. •Do independent voices converge?Take it very seriously. And examine the lone voice.
  4. •Safety, or preference?Never dismiss a credible safety concern by outvoting it.

“…you have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has.”Job 42:7 (ESV)

Sincerity is not accuracy. Weigh counsel. Do not simply obey it.

Point 4 · When counsel becomes control

A voice is not a vote, and it is not a veto.

Parents who demand to approve
Leaders who instruct, not advise
Friends who make it conditionalOr a legitimate boundary? A cost to count, not a command.

“For each will have to bear his own load.”Galatians 6:5 (ESV) We bear one another’s burdens, but we cannot hand over our own responsibility.

Gathering counsel so someone else can be blamed is not humility. It is abdication.

Too many voicesShrink the circle.Never counsel: public posts, screenshots, prayer-group gossip, an AI as your only counsellor.
Too few voicesAsk why.Discouraged by the person you are dating? That is information. Isolated? Start with one person, this week.

A word about safety

  1. •Discouraged from counsel?Preference, privacy, or a pattern of isolation?
  2. •Forbidden or punished?Potential coercive control: seek help for yourself.
  3. •A leader pressing the decision?Advice outside that leader’s influence (Lesson 13).
  4. •Violence, stalking or danger?Lesson 6’s safety guidance applies.
  5. •Possible forced marriage?No family mediation; Lesson 10’s guidance applies.

If you are afraid to tell anyone, that fear is the most important thing to tell someone.

Told no one?Tell one person, starting with the safest.
Told everyone?Choose three or four; let the rest simply be glad for you.
Counsel converging?On a concern, take it seriously. On encouragement, receive it.

The Counsel Map below this lesson can help you see why.

Bringing it together

  1. You need other voices: no one sees their own relationship whole
  2. A small circle: wise, honest, independent enough, knowing, discreet, safe
  3. Weigh: observation over impression, independent voices, safety above preference
  4. A voice is not a vote or a veto: counsel informs your decision; it does not make it

One step now

Name one mature person you have not yet asked.

“The couple who lead our home group have known me for years; I will ask them what they have noticed.”

If it is safe, write the name or initials privately, or simply hold the name before God.

The full Counsel Map is below this lesson (optional). Use initials, and record nothing told to you in confidence. If someone else may access your device or papers, simply think through the questions instead.

Prayer

Give the people around me wisdom to speak well, and give me courage to decide faithfully. In Jesus’ name, amen.
