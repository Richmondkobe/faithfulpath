When to Slow Down

When is “not yet” the right answer?

“Not yet”: the least discussed answer, and the most misused.

Not a way to avoid deciding. Not a punishment or a probation.

A wise “not yet” is as deliberate as a yes or a no.

Fear, coercion or control? Do not slow down: Lesson 6 comes first.

Proverbs 19 · a single proverb

“Desire without knowledge is not good, and whoever makes haste with his feet misses his way.”Proverbs 19:2 (ESV)

Understand the original meaning

חָטָא

ḥāṭāʾ ✱ · to miss (a mark), to sin. Proverbs 19:2 uses a form of this verb: חוֹטֵא (ḥōṭēʾ), “misses”.

“…every one could sling a stone at a hair and not miss.”Judges 20:16 (ESV)

Wanting to arrive is not enough. Moving without knowledge can lead us away from the right path.

Explore the background

No dating timetable: a principle that transfers.

“For everything there is a season, and a time for every matter under heaven.”Ecclesiastes 3:1 (ESV)

“…that you not stir up or awaken love until it pleases.”Song of Songs 2:7 (ESV)

A refrain in a love poem: not a rule, and not a timetable for dating.

Point 1 · What slowing down is

Reducing the pace at which commitment deepens

For a defined period and a stated reason, so that something specific can be seen.

Not seeing each other less, but not yet taking the next step: exclusivity, families as in-laws, engagement, merged finances or plans.

A decision, not a drift: a reason, a shape and an end.

  1. •A reason“I need to see whether the pattern with money changes”, not “I just need more time”.
  2. •A shapeBoth people know what is paused and what is not.
  3. •An endA point to look again and decide. Without it, “not yet” quietly becomes “never”.

Point 2 · When “not yet” is the right answer

When “not yet” is right

  1. •The evidence is missingA newly named difficulty has not had time to show change. Do not deepen commitment on intentions.
  2. •The pace has outrun the knowledgeYou know each other’s hopes better than each other’s habits. Slowing down is catching up.
  3. •External strain is distorting the pictureGrief, illness, a job crisis, a visa: ask whether you have gained clarity, not only whether it has ended.

When “not yet” is right (continued)

  1. •Counsel is concernedSeveral trusted people raise the same concrete concern, and you are not sure they are wrong.
  2. •Readiness is unequalSlowing down protects both: one is not pressured, the other is not left guessing.
  3. •You are not sure what you feelTime, honest reflection and wise counsel help. But a person cannot wait indefinitely.

Point 3 · When “not yet” is the wrong answer

Slowing down can be a way of doing nothing.

  1. •When you already knowSlowing down will not resolve a directional incompatibility. Lesson 19 is the lesson you need.
  2. •When it is a punishment or a testNot “until you prove yourself”, but two people looking at the same thing.

When “not yet” is wrong (continued)

  1. •When it is a way of keeping someoneHeld in reserve without knowing it. If you are unsure, say so, and let them decide.
  2. •When the relationship keeps cyclingRepeated pauses are evidence too.

Unsafe? Do not slow down an unsafe relationship. There is no question to answer and no safe way to wait: Lesson 6 first.

Point 4 · Slowing down well

Slowing down well

  1. •Say it, and say whyA pause decided privately is not a pause; it is withdrawal. If saying it feels unsafe: no private confrontation; seek support.
  2. •Agree the shape together, or recognise that you cannotWhat continues? What pauses? An honest ending is better than an undefined pause.

Name what you are looking for, and make it observable.

  1. •Something observableYou cannot see a motive; a slowdown waiting to see a heart will never end.
  2. •Keep living ordinary life togetherFriends, errands, small disagreements, tiredness and disappointment.
  3. •Let the evidence emerge naturallyNo testing, provoking, monitoring or reading their messages.

Evidence gathered that way tells you more about the gatherer than the gathered.

Respect the other person’s freedom.

A slowdown is your decision; the response is theirs. If they decide not to wait, they have not done something wrong.

Do not get engaged while a major concern is still being tested.

Engagement is a public promise, not a way to resolve a private uncertainty.

The review

The 90-Day Relationship Review

A practical checkpoint, not a proven test: long enough for more than a first impression, short enough to prevent an indefinite wait.

Set the period to fit the concern, and say what it is.

When the period ends, do the review.

Reflect separately first. Talk together only if both freely wish to, and it is safe. A joint meeting is not required.

Looking forSawNow knowDecide

Continuing: two freely given yeses. Walking away: one person’s no.

Continue · slow down again · walk away

A slowdown that ends in a clear, kind no has done exactly what it was for.

Resolved? Go forward gladly. You spent the time finding out, which is what dating is for.

A word about safety

The review is for relationships where both people can speak freely and neither is afraid.

Do not complete or compare it with a partner you fear. Do not share private plans, passwords, financial information or sources of support.

Fear, coercion, control, intimidation or violence? Do not slow down. Lesson 6, and Lesson 19’s safety guidance.

Where you are right now

Moving faster than you know? Slow down. Waiting without a reason, a shape or an end? Give it one.

“…let your ‘yes’ be yes and your ‘no’ be no…”James 5:12 (ESV)

About truthful speech, not dating. But it is what a slowdown is for: to say one or the other, and mean it.

Bringing it together

  1. A wise “not yet” has a reason, a shape and an end
  2. Right when evidence is missing, the pace has outrun knowledge, or readiness is unequal
  3. Wrong when you already know, when it punishes or keeps someone, or when it is unsafe
  4. Slow down well, review honestly, and decide: two yeses to continue, one no to end

One step now

Write what you would want to know before the next step.

“Before we talk about engagement, I want to see how we handle money together for three months.”

If it is safe, write it privately, or simply think it through before God.

The full 90-Day Relationship Review is below this lesson (optional): a checkpoint, not a test, for relationships where both can speak freely. Nothing is saved. If someone else may access your device or papers, simply think through the questions instead.

Prayer

Keep me from using “not yet” to avoid a decision, and keep me from holding anyone in uncertainty. Help me say yes or no when the time comes, and mean it. In Jesus’ name, amen.
