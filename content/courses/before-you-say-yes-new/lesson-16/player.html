When Good Christians Are Wrong for Each Other

What if we are both good people, but this is not right?

Some relationships have no villain.

Goodness alone does not create compatibility.

This lesson is for relationships without abuse or control. If fear, coercion or control is present, Lesson 6 comes first.

Acts 15 · Antioch

“Let us return and visit the brothers in every city where we proclaimed the word of the Lord, and see how they are.”Acts 15:36 (ESV)

“Now Barnabas wanted to take with them John called Mark. But Paul thought best not to take with them one who had withdrawn from them in Pamphylia and had not gone with them to the work.”Acts 15:37–38 (ESV)

“And there arose a sharp disagreement, so that they separated from each other. Barnabas took Mark with him and sailed away to Cyprus, but Paul chose Silas and departed, having been commended by the brothers to the grace of the Lord.”Acts 15:39–40 (ESV)

Two good men. Two journeys. Luke does not declare which man was right about taking Mark.

Understand the original meaning

παροξυσμός

paroxysmos ✱ · “sharp disagreement”: a sharp, stirring contention

“…let us consider how to stir up one another to love and good works…”Hebrews 10:24 (ESV)

Hebrews uses it positively; Acts describes a painful disagreement that ended in separation.

Explore the background

A disagreement about ministry, not a romantic relationship, and not a model for dating decisions.

“…welcome him” Colossians 4:10 · “…he is very useful to me for ministry.” 2 Timothy 4:11 (ESV)

Scripture does not record a reconciliation between Paul and Barnabas. Faithful people sometimes part ways.

Point 1 · Good is not the same as right

Good is not the same as right.

Goodness is a reason to respect them. It is not, on its own, a reason to marry them.

“Wrong for each other”: this relationship cannot support a marriage both can enter freely and faithfully.

Kindness does not create an obligation.

Not as gratitude, not as charity, not to avoid hurting a good person.

“…she is free to be married to whom she wishes, only in the Lord.”1 Corinthians 7:39 (ESV) About a widow: within Christian faithfulness, marriage is not forced on anyone.

Point 2 · What “wrong for each other” looks like

Several signs together: none a fault, none a verdict on its own.

  1. •1 Different livesA central conviction, or an unexplored preference? Test a claimed calling with humility.
  2. •2 The same person always adaptingSurrendering convictions, relationships or identity, while the other stays unchanged.
  3. •3 Depletes rather than restores, over timeName the source: is outside strain affecting you both?
1 Different lives2 Always one adapting3 Depletes, over time4 Not about incidents5 Less yourself6 Counsel converges7 Lasting reluctance
  1. •4 Disagreements not about incidentsLasting differences in conviction, direction or needs.
  2. •5 Less yourselfWhich part has become quieter? Did they ask for that?
  3. •6 Independent counsel convergesOn something concrete, without comparing notes.
  4. •7 A reluctance honest time has not resolvedNot automatically God’s warning, and not to be pushed down. Understand it.

Be honest in the other direction too.

  1. •Not ordinary doubt“What if I’m wrong?” is the weight of the decision, not a verdict.
  2. •Not fear of commitment, dressed upLeaving every good relationship at the same point? Examine the pattern.
  3. •Not perfectionismWaiting for the absence of all friction means waiting forever.
  1. •Perhaps outside stressGrief, illness or job pressure, reducing both people’s capacity.
  2. •Perhaps a personal struggleIndividual support may be needed before you interpret the pattern.
  3. •Not “needs work”A named difficulty, mutual willingness, and evidence that effort produces change: Lesson 17.

A fundamental incompatibility stays unchanged despite honest effort. Even then, effort alone does not obligate continuation.

Telling the difference is rarely possible alone.

Point 3 · The theology of a good ending

Said plainly:

Dating is not marriage. Engagement is a serious promise, but not yet the marriage covenant.

Before the vows, you are two people discerning, and discernment is allowed to conclude “no”.

God does not require you to marry someone to reward their goodness.

God can work through a relationship without obligating two people to continue it.

A clear, honest, gentle no, given as soon as you know it, is one of the most respectful things you can offer.

Point 4 · When only one of you sees it

If they believe it is right, and you do not:

You are not obliged. Your no does not require a competing certainty.

Do not say “not now” when you mean “not this”. Ambiguity is a slow cruelty.

If you believe it is right, and they do not:

They are not rejecting God. They are using the freedom this lesson describes.

Receive the decision as final. No contact to change their mind.

Grieve it. Do not turn it into a spiritual contest. God’s purposes for you are not smaller because of it.

If you are engaged, and real uncertainty persists:

Do not silence it because plans have advanced. Pause arrangements, speak honestly (unless safety makes that unwise), and seek independent premarital counsel.

A pause is not a cancellation, but a wedding timetable should not decide what you have not resolved.

Coercion, threats or safety concerns: no joint counselling or family mediation. Lessons 6 and 10.

A word about safety

For relationships with no abuse or serious safety concern.

Fear, coercion or control? Do not call it incompatibility. Return to Lesson 6.

Afraid of their reaction? Do not announce an ending alone. Lesson 19’s safety guidance applies.

Where you are right now

The grief with no one to blame? You are taking an important concern seriously. Test it before deciding: Lessons 17, 18 and 19 will help.

Found nothing of yourself here? Be glad. Lesson 17 is for you.

Bringing it together

  1. Good is not the same as right
  2. Look for patterns, not a single sign, and be honest about what it is not
  3. Dating is not marriage; discernment is allowed to conclude no
  4. A clear, gentle answer, given and received without a contest

One step now

Write one thing you know to be true about this relationship, whatever you feel.

“They are kind, and we want different countries.” Or: “We have never tried to solve the thing that worries me.”

If it is safe, write it privately, or simply think it through before God.

Prayer

Lord, I keep looking for someone to blame, and there is no one. They are good, and I am not sure this can be built. Take away the idea that loving you means ignoring what I can plainly see.

If I have reached a decision, give me courage to say it honestly and safely. Hold them, whatever I decide. And if I am wrong about all of this, show me that instead. In Jesus’ name, amen.
