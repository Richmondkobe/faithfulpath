Transcript and Scripture references

1 · How do I test what feels like God's leading?

Welcome back. This is Lesson 13, and the first lesson of Part Four: Discernment.

Part Four is about how to test claims and weigh counsel without giving away your own responsibility. I will picture it as a crossroads with several signposts, each with a voice calling, and the traveller holding the lamp of God's word up to each one.

And we carry one question with us: how do I test what feels like God's leading?

2 · "God told me you're going to be my wife"

"God told me you're going to be my wife." Some of you have said those words. Some have had them said to them. Some have heard them from a pulpit, or from a visiting prophet, or from a mother who had been praying. And nearly every Christian who has dated has faced the quieter version, inside their own head: Is this God? How would I know?

This lesson is about discerning a relationship without turning every feeling into the voice of God, and without dismissing God's guidance altogether. It does not try to settle how God speaks today. What it offers are tests grounded in Scripture that Christians on every side can recognise.

And one thing first: "God told me" must never be used to pressure anyone. If it is being used that way, there is guidance on the page beneath this lesson.

3 · Test everything

Our passage comes from the end of Paul's first letter to the Thessalonians. Paul wrote:

"Do not quench the Spirit. Do not despise prophecies, but test everything; hold fast what is good. Abstain from every form of evil."

4 · Test: dokimazō

The Greek word is dokimazo. You met it in Lesson 7. It means to test or examine something to determine whether it is genuine or approved. Think of metal being tested for its quality: the point is to examine, not merely to assume.

Notice the balance in Paul's words. Not "believe everything", and not "dismiss everything". Test, and then hold fast what is good. Paul's instruction to a church that valued prophecy was to test it.

5 · A church that weighed what it heard

The Thessalonian church was young and eager, and it was sometimes unsettled by claims about the future that had been attributed to Paul. So he tells them neither to quench the Spirit nor to accept every claim.

To the Corinthians, a church that also prized spiritual gifts, he says the same: "Let two or three prophets speak, and let the others weigh what is said." Even in the earliest churches, what was spoken in God's name was weighed by others. Testing is not a lack of faith. It is obedience.

6 · Point 1: What Scripture says about guidance

Our first point is what Scripture says about guidance. Start with what is clear. God has already given a great deal of direction about whom to marry. Marry in the Lord. Look for truth, holiness and good fruit. Do not spiritualise conduct that is deceptive or unsafe. Be wise, be patient, seek counsel, test everything. That is God's revealed will, and it is not hidden. "Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path."

And notice what Scripture does not do. It does not promise that God has selected one specific person and that your task is to detect them. When Paul discusses a Christian widow's freedom to remarry, he says she may marry "whom she wishes, only in the Lord". The instruction is about widows, but it shows a pattern: within Christian faithfulness, a believer makes a genuine choice. Choosing a spouse is not a treasure hunt in which one wrong step means missing "the one".

7 · How God guides

How does God guide, then? Christians have commonly understood him to guide through Scripture-shaped wisdom, prayer, counsel, conscience and providential circumstances. Wisdom he promises to give: "If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach, and it will be given him." Counsel he commends: "in an abundance of counselors there is safety."

And conscience matters, but it must itself be formed and tested. A strong conscience can still be mistaken, fearful, or poorly taught.

8 · Christians differ: how God speaks today

Here, Christians differ. Some believe God guides today through impressions, dreams and prophetic words. Others believe those gifts ceased with the apostles, or should be treated with great caution. This course does not settle that question.

But two things are true on every side. First, Paul's instruction was to test: "test everything; hold fast what is good." Second, no claim that God has spoken, whoever makes it, binds another person's conscience. If God has genuinely directed someone, they remain responsible for presenting it humbly, and the other person remains free to discern.

9 · Point 2: The problem with "God told me"

Our second point is the problem with "God told me" in dating. There is nothing wrong with believing God has led you towards someone. Many good marriages began with that sense. The problem is with how the claim works once it is spoken aloud.

It is not self-authenticating. The claim, on its own, proves nothing, and the other person cannot examine the private experience behind it. It puts pressure on the other person: once God has been invoked, saying no feels like saying no to him. It can baptise a feeling: loneliness, fear and desire can all feel like conviction from the inside, and Jeremiah warns that the heart can deceive us. Sincere people are wrong: sincerity is not accuracy.

And even when sincerely held, it does not become your obligation. You do not need a competing revelation in order to say no. Your own free and considered consent is necessary, and no one else's experience can supply it.

10 · Point 3: How to test a claim (1)

Our third point is how to test a claim. Whether the claim is someone else's or your own, the same tests apply.

Does it conflict with Scripture? God does not speak against his word. A "word" to marry an unbeliever, to go ahead with someone unsafe, or to abandon existing responsibilities is not from him, however it felt. Does it bypass wisdom? If the character evidence is poor, or red flags are present, a claim that overrides them is asking you to trust an impression over a fact. Does it require haste? A claim from God can tolerate time for consent, character and counsel. Urgency created by another person is not divine urgency. Does it require isolation? Some spiritual experiences are rightly private. Isolation from wise evaluation is the concern.

11 · How to test a claim (2)

Does it try to determine someone else's decision? A private impression does not give one Christian authority over another adult's choice. Has it been weighed, and by whom? By more than one mature, independent believer who knows the relationship and is free to disagree? What fruit does it produce, in conduct? Love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control. Does the claim lead towards humility and respect for the other person's freedom, or is it used to pressure, intimidate or punish?

And is the person open to being wrong? This may be the most telling test. A person who knows the difference between God's authority and their own interpretation can say, "This is what I believe God has shown me, and I may be mistaken, and you are free." The test is not sincerity but humility.

12 · When the word does not come true

One further test is the plainest. Deuteronomy gives Israel two tests for prophecy. A prediction that fails was not spoken by God: "if the word does not come to pass or come true, that is a word that the LORD has not spoken." But even a sign that occurs must be rejected if its message leads away from faithfulness to God. So failure is decisive, and fulfilment is not enough.

The setting is Israel's prophets, not modern dating, but the principle applies with care. If someone makes a definite claim that God has promised you will marry them, and that marriage does not happen, their prediction has proved false. And a prediction coming true does not prove it divine, especially if pressure helped produce the outcome. But you do not need to wait for that outcome to reject pressure. Their claim does not override your freedom to say no.

13 · Point 4: Peace and open doors

Our fourth point is three common ways Christians try to discern, each used more confidently than it deserves. First, "I have peace about it." The verse usually cited, "let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts", is in context about the church being called together "in one body", not a private decision technique. Attraction may produce reassurance that feels like peace about an unwise decision. Fear, trauma, or simply the weight of a decision may produce anxiety about a right one. Peace is worth noticing. It is not worth obeying on its own.

Second, "God opened a door." Circumstances are part of providence, and it is right to notice them. But an open door is not a command. Paul describes "a wide door" in Ephesus, with many adversaries, and elsewhere leaves an open door in Troas because he was anxious for his friend Titus. Doors open for many reasons. Wisdom decides which to enter.

14 · Gideon and the fleece

And third, fleeces. Gideon said to God, "behold, I am laying a fleece of wool on the threshing floor. If there is dew on the fleece alone, and it is dry on all the ground, then I shall know that you will save Israel by my hand, as you have said." And then, afraid, he asked again: "let me test just once more." "And God did so that night."

But notice: Gideon had already received God's instruction before he asked. The signs show God's patience with a frightened man's repeated request for reassurance, not a method for choosing a spouse. "If they text me before nine, it's a sign" is not discernment. It is chance dressed as revelation.

15 · "Is there one person?" "What if I miss it?"

And some hard questions that come up often. "Is there one person God has for me?" Scripture does not teach that there is a single soulmate you must find. Looking back, many married Christians see God's hand in how they met. But looking forward, the question Scripture asks is not "Is this the one?" but "Is this wise?"

"What if I miss God's will?" His moral will is stated plainly: "For this is the will of God, your sanctification." His will is that you become like Christ, and that can happen married or single, with this person or another. If you choose wisely and faithfully and it does not work out, you have not fallen out of his purposes. If you choose foolishly and repent, his grace is not smaller than your mistake.

16 · "Am I disobeying God by saying no?"

"They said God told them. Am I disobeying God by saying no?" No. You are not. Their word is not binding on you, and a no given honestly, after prayer and thought, is not rebellion. It may be the most obedient thing you do.

"My pastor said…" "A prophet said…" Leaders can be wrong. A pastor's counsel deserves real weight, and Lesson 14 will say so. But a pastor who tells you whom to marry has stepped beyond counsel. A prophetic word that names a person is exactly what Paul said should be weighed, and you are among those who weigh it.

"I prayed and felt nothing." This is normal, and it is not a sign of God's absence. Most decisions in the Christian life are made by wisdom applied to what is known. Silence in prayer is often an invitation to use the tools God has already given.

17 · "I don't feel led to end it"

"I don't feel led to end it, even though it's harming me." You do not need a special word to leave harm. God has already spoken: "The prudent sees danger and hides himself." Waiting for a feeling of release before leaving a relationship that is unsafe is waiting for something Scripture has already given. And if immediate safety is involved, do not delay while seeking complete spiritual certainty. If leaving could put you in greater danger, seek confidential, independent help to plan your next step safely.

"Should I wait for a sign?" You may pray for wisdom and clarity, and God gives them. But waiting for a sign can sometimes become a way of postponing a difficult decision that Scripture, evidence and wisdom have already made sufficiently clear.

18 · A word about safety

When "God told me" is used to override your judgement, silence your questions, hurry your decision, or make you afraid to say no, Lesson 6's pattern is present, whoever is speaking: partner, parent or leader. The response is the same: take the spiritual language off, look at what is actually being asked, and talk to someone outside the situation.

If a church leader is the one applying the pressure, seek confidential help from an independent safeguarding professional, denominational authority or qualified counsellor outside that leader's influence. If threats, stalking or physical danger are present, Lesson 6's immediate safety guidance applies. Guidance is on the page beneath this lesson.

19 · Your own "God told me", and where you are

Everything above applies to you as much as to anyone else. If you believe God has drawn your attention towards someone, hold it with open hands, and in most cases, do not announce it. Translate it into an ordinary, honest sentence: "I am interested in getting to know you." Let it be tested by Scripture, wisdom, counsel, time, and the other person's freedom. If it is from God, testing will not harm it. If it is not, testing is a mercy.

And where you are right now. If you have used "God told me" to put pressure on someone, acknowledge that it was wrong and release them from that pressure. Where it is appropriate and welcome, you may apologise. But if they have asked for no contact, respect that boundary. Do not use an apology as another reason to approach them. If someone has said it to you, take the claim through the tests, honestly, and then decide on grounds both of you can examine.

And if you are simply trying to work out whether God is leading you, use what he has already given. Scripture. Wisdom. Counsel. Time. Character. You may find that he has been speaking all along, in the ordinary ways, while you were waiting for the extraordinary.

20 · Bringing it together

Let us bring it together. Test everything, and hold fast what is good. No claim that God has spoken proves itself, and none binds another person's conscience. Test any claim, yours or anyone's, by Scripture, wisdom, time, counsel, fruit and humility. And peace, open doors and fleeces are worth noticing, but not worth obeying on their own.

22 · Prayer

Let us pray.

Lord, I do not want to be deceived, and I do not want to deceive myself. Where your name has been used to move me, help me see what is really being asked. Where I have used your name to get what I wanted, forgive me. Teach me to want your will more than I want this to be your will. And give me the wisdom you promised — not a sign or a feeling, but clear wisdom to see what is in front of me and decide honestly. In Jesus' name, amen.
