Lesson 15 · Book chapter 15 · Part 4

Can We Build a Life Together?

Beyond faith, can our lives fit together?

Lesson 2: a shared foundation. This lesson: could you build a house on it?

A shared faith is a foundation. It is not yet a house.

Being equally yoked does not settle whether two people should marry. It makes the question askable.

These conversations are for a relationship where both of you can speak freely. Guidance is on the page beneath this lesson.

Proverbs 24 · the sayings of the wise

“By wisdom a house is built, and by understanding it is established; by knowledge the rooms are filled with all precious and pleasant riches.”Proverbs 24:3–4 (ESV)

Not built by feeling alone: by wisdom, understanding and knowledge.

Understand the original meaning

כְּנֶגְדּוֹ

kenegdô ✱ · “fit for him”: corresponding to him (Genesis 2:18) · in our words, a counterpart, not a copy

“Two are better than one… For if they fall, one will lift up his fellow.”Ecclesiastes 4:9–10 (ESV) Mutual support: they must be able to help one another.

Not “Are we alike?” but “Can we build one life out of two, with the differences we actually have?”

Explore the background

In Proverbs, a “house” is a household: the shared life of a family.

“Prepare your work outside; get everything ready for yourself in the field, and after that build your house.”Proverbs 24:27 (ESV)

“The wisest of women builds her house, but folly with her own hands tears it down.”Proverbs 14:1 (ESV)

Not a modern marriage checklist: wisdom, preparation and conduct in building a household.

Point 1 · Compatibility is not sameness

Two questions: what are the differences? And can you handle differences at all?

The second is often the more important.

Agreement is pleasant, but it is not the foundation. The ability to disagree well is.

How do you know whether you can handle differences?

MutualityHonest disagreementRepair

Present: many ordinary differences become easier. Some major ones still need agreement, and some may remain incompatible.

Absent: even a couple who agree on many things may struggle to build well.

Think of a recent disagreement. Did you find your way back? Not perfection: seen more than once.

Point 2 · Seven areas to look at

  1. •1 Personality and temperamentComplement or grind? Does their energy restore or exhaust you, over time?
  2. •2 Communication and conflictOut loud or in silence, as long as neither reads the other’s way as rejection.
  3. •3 Calling and directionCould two purposes be lived in one house? Sharing or supporting, gladly, for decades?
  1. •4 LocationWhere, and near whom. “I always thought we’d end up back home” belongs before engagement.
  2. •5 Expectations of marriageRoles, money, decisions, time. Preference, or conviction?
  1. •6 ChildrenWhether, how many, when, how raised; if they do not come; children already here. Cannot be split down the middle.
  2. •7 LifestylePace, rest, food, company, solitude. Small until they are daily, and then they are everything.

Point 3 · Sorting the differences

1 · Liveable: part of the texture of your marriage. Temperament, rest, celebrating, affection.

Patience and humour, not resolution.

2 · Needing agreement before engagement: settled substantially, not perfectly, before you commit.

Children · Location · Household roles · Family obligations · Money · The direction of your lives

“We’ll work it out later” is not enough here. Settling is not surrender: an answer both of you can live with gladly.

3 · Incompatible: real, unchangeable, and would require one of you to give up something central.

An incompatibility is not a failure of love. It is information about what a shared life would cost.

Honesty about it now is a kindness to both of you. Lesson 16 is about exactly this.

Attraction moves everything into group 1. Fear moves everything into group 3. Neither is honest.

Use the evidence of earlier lessons, and the counsel of Lesson 14.

“If nothing about this changed for forty years, could I live with it gladly?”

A question to help you think, not a test that decides by itself.

Point 4 · Counting the cost

“For which of you, desiring to build a tower, does not first sit down and count the cost, whether he has enough to complete it?”Luke 14:28 (ESV)

Jesus was speaking of the cost of following him, not of marriage. But the picture of sitting down before you build is exactly right.

Counting the cost: what would each of you be giving up?

Every marriage costs something. Many count it, pay it, and are glad they did.

The danger is not cost. It is uncounted cost.

  1. •What would I be giving up? What would they?
  2. •Have we said so out loud?
  3. •A life we both want, or one quietly agreed to?
Not a premarital courseIs there enough here to justify those deeper conversations?
Not a guaranteeCompatibility is the ground; faithfulness, repair, patience and grace build on it.

Level ground, or a slope?

Alone first, then together. Where do we agree? Where do we differ? Preference or conviction? Anything assumed but never said?

Can you disagree and stay connected?

Afraid to say what you think, or pressured? Stop. Lesson 6 comes first.

Early?Note the areas, watch, and come back.
Moving towards engagement?Third group empty, second short: worth building on. Anything in the third: Lesson 16 is next.
Moving stones out of group 3?Stop, and take it to someone wise.

The reflection is only useful if it is honest.

Bringing it together

  1. A shared faith is a foundation, not yet a house
  2. Compatibility is not sameness: can we handle difference?
  3. Look at the seven areas, and sort: liveable · agree before engagement · incompatible
  4. Count the cost, out loud, before you build

One step now

Put one difference between you into one of the three groups.

“We rest differently. That is liveable.” Or: “We have never talked about where we would live. That needs agreement before engagement.”

If it is safe, write it privately, or simply think it through before God.

The full Life Compatibility Reflection is below this lesson (optional): alone first, then, only if both of you can speak freely, together. Nothing is saved. If someone else may access your device or papers, simply think through the questions instead.

Prayer

Lord, you are the builder of every good house. Give us wisdom to see our differences honestly, and humility to sort them truthfully. Help us count the cost before we build.

Where our lives can fit together, give us joy and patience. Where they cannot, give us courage and kindness. In Jesus’ name, amen.
