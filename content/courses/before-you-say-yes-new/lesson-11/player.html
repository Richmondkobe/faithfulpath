Lesson 11 · Book chapter 11 · Part 3

Money Reveals More Than a Salary

What does the way someone handles money show?

“What do they earn?” tells you less than you think.

Careful, generous, honest
Reckless, secretive

How a person manages what they have, and how honestly they discuss it, often shows more than the amount.

Money is where words and behaviour most often separate.

Luke 16:10–11 · Jesus teaching about money

“One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much, and one who is dishonest in a very little is also dishonest in much. If then you have not been faithful in the unrighteous wealth, who will entrust to you the true riches?”Luke 16:10–11 (ESV)

Understand the original meaning

πιστός

pistos ✱ · “faithful”: trustworthy, reliable

Faithfulness with money: a training ground for faithfulness in greater things.

By application, a reasonable training ground for marriage too.

Explore the background

A difficult parable about a manager facing dismissal, then teaching on faithfulness with money.

“You cannot serve God and money.”Luke 16:13 (ESV)

“The Pharisees, who were lovers of money, heard all these things, and they ridiculed him.”Luke 16:14 (ESV)

Money is not a dirty subject. But it shows what we serve.

Point 1 · What this lesson is not about

Not how much a person has.

Poverty is not a character flaw, and neither is wealth.

James warned against honouring the rich and slighting the poor (James 2:1–5). God does not measure worth by resources.

“And a poor widow came and put in two small copper coins, which make a penny.”Mark 12:42 (ESV)

“…this poor widow has put in more than all…” Jesus noticed what others overlooked.

Just before: a warning against leaders who “devour widows’ houses”. Never use this passage to pressure the poor to give what they need to live.

Dating is not a financial audit.

Earlyattitudes and habits, seen more than heard
Before exclusivityanything that affects safety
Before engagementsubstantial financial truth, from both

What you are watching is character.

Point 2 · Six windows

1Spending

Not how much, but how.

Not judged from one purchase; over time, what they choose.

“Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.” Matthew 6:21

2Debt

Not whether, but what, why, and how it is carried.

“The borrower is the slave of the lender.” Proverbs 22:7

Seeking debt advice is a good sign.

3Generosity

“God loves a cheerful giver.” 2 Corinthians 9:7

Generous, with wisdom and the ability to say no.

Giving that buys affection is not generosity.

4Secrecy

Privacy about details: fine. Secrecy about material facts: not.

Honesty never requires passwords or account access.

Watch for shifting stories, or a false picture of wealth.

5Ambition

Drive is good. What would they sacrifice for money?

“The love of money is a root of all kinds of evils.” 1 Timothy 6:10

“Wealth gained hastily will dwindle, but whoever gathers little by little will increase it.” Proverbs 13:11

6Responsibility

Read fairly: hardship is not bad character.

Honest when they cannot pay, and seeking solutions.

Point 3 · Money and the two of you

Who pays, and does it create obligation? Gifts do not purchase access, affection, sex, commitment or influence.

How do they treat your money? And what are your own patterns?

Supporting family can be honourable: freely acknowledged, sustainable, and known before marriage.

Point 4 · Warning signs

Warning signs and what is beyond warning

Warning signs deserve attention, and if they persist, a serious conversation and outside counsel.

Repeated borrowing, with entitlement or deceptionPressure to lend, co-sign or guaranteeConcealed gambling, or chasing lossesDebts discovered, not disclosedSpending that contradicts stated valuesGifts or money used to controlTreating your money as theirsRefusing to discuss money when it is time

Weigh seriousness, repetition, deception, and the response when challenged.

As a dating safeguard: do not co-sign or guarantee another person’s debt.

A co-signer may become responsible for the whole debt.

Exceptional circumstances? Independent advice first, and only if you could repay it all yourself.

Proverbs 6:1–5: not a universal law about modern lending, but old wisdom about carrying a debt that belongs to someone else.

Financial exploitation is not a warning sign. It is harm.

Any request for money from a romantic interest you know only online is a serious scam warning, whatever the story. Do not send money while you try to find out.

Being deceived does not make someone foolish. Scammers deliberately manipulate trust, affection and vulnerability.

If you have already paid

  1. 1Stop payments and contact
  2. 2Call your bank or payment service at once
  3. 3Keep messages, receipts and profile details
  4. 4Report the profile and the fraud
  5. 5Shared documents or account details? Ask how to prevent identity misuse

“Recovery agents” who promise to get it back for a fee are often a second scam.

Never put money into an investment introduced by an online romantic interest. Check independently, through the regulator.

Financial abuse

Taking money, cards or documents · forcing signatures · credit in your name · withholding essentials · blocking work or study · receipts for everything · dependence, step by step

Earning less, staying home, studying, or receiving agreed support is not abuse. The concern is deliberate restriction.

If you fear their reaction, get confidential advice before making changes they may notice, on a safe device.

What to learn when

Enough to decide whether a shared future is possible, and enough to keep yourself safe.

Where you are right now

Not datinglook honestly at your own money first
Never discussedan ordinary conversation about what money is for
Ordinary concernsseek wise counsel; you are free to slow down or end it

Intimidation, coercion or exploitation: not a normal disagreement. Safety first, and independent support.

You are not foolish. You offered trust, and someone chose to exploit it.

Bringing it together

  1. Faithful in a very little, faithful in much (Luke 16:10)
  2. Not how much, but how: six windows on character
  3. No score-keeping, no leverage, and an honest look at yourself
  4. Ordinary concerns deserve discernment. Exploitation calls for protection and help.

One step now

Write one thing your own spending shows about your heart.

“I spend most freely on things that make me look successful.” · “I am more generous with friends than I realised.”

If it is safe, write it privately, or simply think it through before God.

The full Financial Warning Signs Checklist is below this lesson (optional). Never write account numbers or passwords in it. If someone else may access your device or papers, simply think through the questions instead.

Prayer

Lord, everything I have comes from you. Keep me from loving money, and keep me from fearing its absence. Make me honest, generous, and wise.

Help me see clearly how others handle money, and help me be faithful with what you have given me, in little things and in big things.

Protect me from anyone who would take advantage of my trust. In Jesus’ name, amen.

“Give me neither poverty nor riches.” Proverbs 30:8 (ESV)

May whatever you might one day hold together be held faithfully, in very little and in much.
