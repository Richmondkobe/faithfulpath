Sexual Boundaries, Practically

How do we honour God with our bodies before marriage?

The rule is clear. The practice is where people struggle.

The historic Christian conviction: sexual intercourse and deliberately sexual activity belong within marriage. Different practical lines do not remove the call to sexual integrity.

Under eighteen and feeling pressured or unsafe? Speak with a safe adult or a safeguarding service.

1 Thessalonians 4:3–5 · Paul’s first letter to the Thessalonians

“For this is the will of God, your sanctification: that you abstain from sexual immorality; that each one of you know how to control his own body in holiness and honor, not in the passion of lust like the Gentiles who do not know God.”1 Thessalonians 4:3–5 (ESV)

Understand the original meaning

ἁγιασμός

hagiasmos ✱ · “sanctification”: being made holy, set apart for God

Holiness is first about belonging to God, not only about avoiding something.

Your body is not a problem to be managed. It belongs to the God who made it and redeemed it.

Verse 4 has been understood in more than one way; its central instruction is clear.

Explore the background

Thessalonica: a young church in a busy port city. Many had “turned to God from idols” (1 Thessalonians 1:9).

“…that no one transgress and wrong his brother in this matter.”1 Thessalonians 4:6 (ESV)

God “gives his Holy Spirit to you”: holiness is not a burden carried alone.

Point 1 · What Scripture says

What Scripture says, and what it does not

“Flee from sexual immorality… your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you… You are not your own, for you were bought with a price. So glorify God in your body.”1 Corinthians 6:18–20 (ESV)

“Let marriage be held in honor among all, and let the marriage bed be undefiled.”Hebrews 13:4 (ESV)

Sexual union belongs within marriage: a publicly made, whole-life covenant.

What Scripture does not say

Scripture does not saySex is dirty or shameful
A good gift, for its covenant

Govern desires in holiness: not every desire should be cultivated or acted upon.

Scripture does not sayYour worth depends on your sexual history
Forgiven, fully

Not a lesser Christian, a damaged gift, or a second-class spouse.

Scripture does not sayOne person is responsible for the other’s desire
Each is responsible for themselves

and for not wronging the other.

If you were taught otherwise, you were taught wrongly.

Engagement is a serious promise, but it is not marriage.

“We’re basically married” is not a category Scripture recognises.

Marriage is entering a recognised covenant, not simply intending to.

If sexual activity happened to you without your consent, it was not your sin. It was a wrong done to you.

Help is on the page beneath this lesson.

Point 2 · Why “just don’t” is not enough

A rule with no plan

Willpower in the momentalone, late, tired, unprepared
Wisdom in advancea plan drawn in daylight

Desire is strong, and God made it so. There is no shame in that.

“But put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, to gratify its desires.”Romans 13:14 (ESV)

About sinful desire more broadly; the practical wisdom applies here.

Do not keep arranging situations that make your conviction unnecessarily difficult to keep.

“But he left his garment in her hand and fled and got out of the house.”Genesis 39:12 (ESV)

Persistent pressure and a serious imbalance of power: not ordinary dating temptation.

Sometimes wisdom means leaving the room, rather than staying to prove your strength.

Point 3 · Drawing the line

A framework with three areas, drawn in advance, together

Not one universal line for every form of affection, but a framework.

1Clearly sexual activity

Belongs within marriage.

Not up for negotiation between you.

2Sexually explicit digital

It is sexual activity, even apart.

And it carries permanence.

3Affection

Respect conscience; avoid escalation.

Honour the stricter line of either person.

An involuntary physical response is not a moral failure. What matters is what you deliberately seek or continue.

Six questions

For each of you alone, and then together

  1. 1A clear conscience before God?
  2. 2Deliberately seeking sexual escalation?
  3. 3Can either stop without pressure, guilt or punishment?
  4. 4Respecting what we honestly said?
  5. 5Making wise judgement harder?
  6. 6Am I hiding it?

These questions are for a relationship where both can speak freely. Afraid of your partner’s reaction? Do not use this together. Seek individual support.

Not one person drawing the line for both. Not negotiated in the moment it is tested.

Making no provision

Decided together and in advance, so the moment does not decide for you.

Point 4 · Consent and coercion

Consent and coercion

Everything so far assumes two people who are each freely choosing. Some situations are not that.

clearvoluntaryinformedspecificongoing

Silence, freezing, or giving in after repeated pressure is not freely given consent.

For each occasion. It can always be withdrawn.

A previous yes, a ring, or a plan never removes the right to say no.

Pressure is not persuasion.

Disappointment is not coercion, but using it as a lever is.

Coercion is not a boundary problem to be solved with a better plan. Seek outside help.

If you have been hurt before

If you are a survivor of abuse or assault:

Freezing, going quiet or panicking are not consent, and not your failure.

You decide when and how much to tell. A partner worth having will not press for details.

If telling feels unsafe, that is information about the relationship, not about you.

Point 5 · When the line is crossed

Grace does not erase reality. It lets you face it without condemnation.

First, which of four situations are you in?

1Mutually chosen

A shared line, freely crossed, regretted by both.

2Different convictions

Are your convictions compatible? Was anyone pressed?

3Pressure or coercion

Not a shared lapse. Nothing to confess to the one who pressured you.

Seek help.

4A medical concern

Seek timely advice from a health service.

Medical care is not evidence of weak faith.

After a mutual lapse: five steps

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”1 John 1:9 (ESV)

  1. 1Confess honestlyto God, and truthfully with each other
  2. 2Neither spiral nor shrugforgiveness that is real makes it possible to try again
  3. 3Revise the safeguardswhat did not hold? change it
  4. 4Accept consequenceswisdom, not punishment: perhaps less time alone
  5. 5Do not use marriage as the solutiondesire is a good reason to marry, not to rush an unsuitable marriage

Accountability never becomes public exposure or detailed reporting.

Where you are right now

Crossed again and again? Ask a larger question, with a pastor or counsellor.

If one person consistently pushes, the question is about that person.

Repeated failure is never a reason for repeated shame.

Not in a relationshipknow your own line first
Dating, no planmake one
Crossed the lineyou are not disqualified; begin again

If you recognised pressure in this lesson, put the plan aside and talk to someone safe.

Bringing it together

  1. Holiness is belonging to God; your body is his (1 Thessalonians 4:3)
  2. Not willpower in the moment, but wisdom in advance: make no provision
  3. A line drawn together, the stricter honoured; consent is free and for each occasion
  4. After a mutual lapse: grace, honesty and wiser safeguards. Where there is pressure or coercion: safety and independent help.

One step now

Write one practical way you will “make no provision” this week.

“I will say goodnight by ten.” · “We will spend Saturday where other people are.”

If it is safe, write it privately, or simply think it through before God.

Where I have failed, thank you for your forgiveness. Where I have been hurt, bring healing and safe help. In Jesus’ name, amen.
