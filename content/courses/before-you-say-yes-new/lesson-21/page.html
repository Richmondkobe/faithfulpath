Safety first

Do not complete these questions jointly if you are afraid of the other person, if saying “not yet” would bring threats, punishment, spiritual condemnation or family retaliation, or if your answers could be used against you. If any answer reveals fear, coercion, surveillance or an inability to say “not yet”, stop the exercise. Do not compare answers or confront the other person using the worksheet. Use the safety guidance in Lessons 6, 10 and 19, and seek independent help. Finding Help Where You Live

These are threshold questions. They ask whether you are ready to declare an intention to marry, not whether you are ready to marry. They do not score, and they do not certify a relationship. Two free and informed yeses. One no ends it. One “not yet” postpones it. Neither answer may be negotiated into a yes.

How to answer: separately, in writing, without discussing your answers first. Yes / Not sure / No, with a brief explanation; “Not sure” is an honest answer. If a question cannot be answered safely, or touches information that is not yours to disclose, note that and seek individual guidance.

1. Free intention

  1. Do I want to marry this person? Not “could I”, not “should I”: do I?
  2. Would I still want to if no one else knew we were together and no one was waiting for an answer?
  3. Has either of us ever said yes to something in this relationship because no felt impossible? (If yes, do not proceed until you have examined this independently.)

If I said “not yet”, what would happen? If they said “not yet”, what would I do?

2. What we have faced

  1. Is there a concern from Lessons 15 to 19 that is still open?
  2. Is anything currently being tested that a review has not yet closed?
  3. Is there anything I am hoping engagement will fix?

Is there anything I have decided to “live with” that I have not told them I am living with?

3. What we know

  1. Did what I observed across ordinary life match who they say they are?
  2. Is there any material fact about my history, responsibilities or present circumstances that could reasonably affect their decision, and have I disclosed it at an appropriate level of detail?

Material information means something a reasonable person might consider important when deciding freely whether to move towards marriage: a previous marriage, children or dependants, substantial debt, criminal or immigration matters affecting the shared future, an active addiction, or an existing commitment that would shape married life. It does not mean every private detail, someone else’s confidential information, or a complete account of past trauma. Privacy is legitimate; secrecy about a material fact is not.

4. Spiritual foundation

  1. Do we both personally confess and follow Christ, rather than relying on family, church attendance or a label?
  2. Can we pray, read Scripture and discuss spiritual disagreement without performance or control?
  3. Has either of us used God, Scripture, prophecy or pastoral authority to pressure the other?

5. Direction

  1. Do our two pictures of married life (where, work, family, children, the place of God) broadly match?
  2. Is there a directional difference we have agreed to “sort out later”?

6. Counsel

  1. Have we asked wise, independent people, separately, whether there is anything we should look at before engagement?
  2. Have we dismissed a credible, concrete concern merely because it was inconvenient?

Is there anyone we have avoided asking?

7. Pressure

  1. Have we attached engagement to an artificial deadline?
  2. Have deposits, announcements or family plans made us feel unable to reconsider?

Which pressure on me is strongest: age or time, fear that no one else will come, sexual boundaries, the length of the relationship, the other person’s readiness, family, church? As far as I can separate my desire from these pressures, what reasons for engagement remain?

8. The relationship itself

  1. Can I raise a hard question, disagree or say “not yet” without retaliation, prolonged withdrawal, intimidation, contempt or the misuse of spiritual authority?
  2. Do I like them, not only love them, and enjoy their company in ordinary life?
  3. Can I keep my conscience, voice, friendships and appropriate independence?

When I imagine the ordinary marriage rather than the wedding, what do I feel, and what is producing that response?

9. Readiness for the engagement itself

  1. Are we both willing to complete thorough premarital preparation?
  2. Are we willing to delay setting a wedding date until the major premarital conversations have begun?
  3. If engagement reveals a serious concern, are we both willing to pause?
  4. Do we understand that engagement permits neither sexual entitlement nor greater control over the other person?

10. The decision. Yes: I freely want to move towards engagement · Not yet: I need further information, time or resolution · No: I do not intend to marry this person. Then: what evidence supports my answer? What pressure makes it hard to say? If “not yet”, what specifically is missing, and when will I review it? If “no”, am I prepared to say so clearly rather than asking for an indefinite wait? If either answer is no, do not become engaged. If either answer is not yet, do not become engaged yet.

  1. My answer, and why, in one or two sentences:

Comparing your answers (only if both can speak freely). Begin where your answers differ, but do not ignore matching answers that are vague or based on assumption. If the differences are about direction or faith, return to Lessons 2, 15 and 16; about safety or freedom, Lesson 6; about pace or readiness, Lesson 18. If either person answers no, receive it as final. If either answers not yet, name what remains and return to the relevant lesson without setting an engagement date. This worksheet does not certify the relationship; it helps you approach the threshold with your eyes open.

Transcript and Scripture references

1 · How do we know we are near the threshold?

Welcome back. This is Lesson 21, the closing lesson of this course.

The book has been about dating, and it has deliberately stopped short of engagement. But some of you will reach the end in a relationship that has been examined carefully, and is now moving towards a different question: not "should we continue?", but "are we ready to move intentionally towards marriage?" Looking back, you can see the whole road. I will picture two walkers at a threshold: close to it, and not yet through it.

And we carry one question with us: how do we know we are near the threshold?

2 · A threshold, not a finish line

This short lesson is about the threshold of engagement: how to recognise when you have arrived at it, and when you have not. It is not a premarital guide. The conversations that belong to engagement, about money, family, faith, sex, conflict and the shape of marriage, are the work of the course Talk Before You Marry.

And engagement is not a finish line. It is a threshold, with a road on the other side. If you are not in a relationship at all, or not near this point, this lesson is still for you. It shows what you are walking towards, so you can recognise it when, and if, you arrive.

3 · Leave and hold fast

Our passage is the one we met in Lesson 10, the foundation of marriage in Scripture:

"Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and they shall become one flesh."

4 · A covenant, and a promise to enter it

The verse describes three movements. You met the first two words in Lesson 10: to leave, a change of first loyalty; and to hold fast, to cling, to stay joined. Then, one flesh: a new household, a shared life, formed freely by two people before God.

That is why this verse is the foundation for this lesson. Genesis 2:24 describes the leaving, holding fast and becoming one flesh that belong to marriage. Scripture also speaks of marriage as a covenant, as in Malachi 2:14. Engagement is not that covenant. It is a serious promise to enter it. So the question at the threshold is not whether you are in love. It is whether you are both ready, freely, to promise that.

5 · Counting the cost, and a free answer

Jesus told a short parable about someone desiring to build a tower, who does not "first sit down and count the cost". He was speaking about the cost of discipleship, not about marriage, and I use it here only as an illustration. But the picture fits: a serious commitment deserves the time to sit down and consider it before you begin.

And in Genesis 24, Rebekah's family asks her: "Will you go with this man?" She said, "I will go." In its setting, the marriage had largely been arranged, and the question was whether she would go at once. Scripture gives us no modern courtship system. But even in that very different world, the story records her own answer. A yes to marriage is meant to be given, not taken.

6 · Point 1: What engagement is

Our first point is what engagement is. It is a mutually declared intention to marry. It is serious, but it is not yet marriage. The marriage covenant is ordinarily entered through vows, recognised by the couple's church, community and the law.

So, first, engagement is not a light thing. Two people who become engaged have declared to each other their intention to marry, and that commitment may also carry expectations within their families, church and wider community. Ending an engagement can carry emotional, financial and communal costs. So engagement should not be entered merely to discover whether you are interested in marriage. Dating should have established a genuine, mutual intention to marry. Engagement is then a focused season of preparation, counsel and deeper conversation.

7 · Not irreversible

And second, engagement is not irreversible. Important new information may still arise during that season, and that is exactly why it remains breakable. If you are engaged and have recognised a substantial concern, do not silence it because plans have advanced, and do not let a wedding timetable decide what the two of you have not resolved.

An engagement can be paused, and it can be ended, and either is far better than a marriage entered because the invitations were already printed. If that is where you are, Lesson 16 is for you, and where there is fear, the safety guidance in Lessons 6, 10 and 19.

8 · Point 2: The essentials (1)

Our second point is what is essential before engagement. These are not signs. They are conditions, and if any is missing, engagement is not the next step, whatever else is true.

Both people freely intend marriage. Not one of you wanting it and the other agreeing. Not one of you wanting it and the other unable to say no. Lesson 18 said that continuing requires two freely given yeses; engagement requires the same, with more weight. A reluctant yes is not made free merely because it is eventually spoken.

And neither person is being pressured or frightened. If saying "not yet" would bring threats, punishment, spiritual condemnation or family retaliation, do not complete the engagement questions jointly. Use the safety guidance in Lessons 6, 10 and 19, and seek independent help. Pressure does not need to be physical to remove consent.

9 · The essentials (2)

No known directional incompatibility is being ignored. If Lessons 15 or 16 found something you want differently in your lives, engagement does not resolve it. It only makes it public.

No serious concern is currently being tested. Lessons 17 and 18 were clear: do not become engaged while a significant difficulty is under review. If you are in a slowdown, finish it. If a serious pattern has only recently been acknowledged, allow time to see responsibility, appropriate help and sustained change. Do not require perfection; look for honesty, ownership and evidence that the concern is being managed well.

And both of you understand what engagement is: a season of preparation for marriage, not an experiment with the idea of it.

10 · Point 3: Signs you may be near (1)

Our third point is the signs that you may be near the threshold. With the essentials in place, these are descriptions of what the threshold usually looks like, not a scorecard.

The important questions have been faced. You have used the tools that fitted your relationship, not necessarily every one. You are not moving towards engagement to silence a known concern, force the relationship forward, or find out whether you want marriage.

You know each other across ordinary life: some combination of tiredness, disappointment, disagreement, family time, money decisions, disrupted plans and everyday responsibility. You do not need to manufacture tests. The question is whether enough ordinary life has been shared to support more than an idealised impression. And words and observed character broadly agree. What each of you says about yourselves, and what the other has seen over time, describe the same person.

11 · Signs you may be near (2)

Your relationship is known, not hidden. Wise, independent people have had enough access to observe it, and permission to speak. Listen carefully to concrete concerns rather than counting supporters and opponents. Their counsel informs your discernment; it does not replace the free decision of the two people marrying.

You have discussed the shape of married life: where, with what work, what shape of family, what place for God. And the picture is one you both recognise and want. If most of your conversations about the future are about the ceremony, you are further from the threshold than the excitement suggests.

And the relationship can tolerate difficult questions. Either of you can raise a concern, ask for time, or say "I'm not sure about that" without punishment, sulking or a spiritual rebuke. If you cannot, go back to the essentials.

12 · Point 4: The pressures (1)

Our fourth point is the pressures that create a premature engagement. Every pressure this course has named shows up again at this threshold, and some show up harder.

Family. Parents who want grandchildren, or think you have dated long enough. Their voice belongs in counsel. It does not belong in the decision, and where family pressure becomes coercion, Lessons 10 and 19 apply, and engagement is not the way to relieve it. Church. Congregations love a wedding. If your church has been planning the wedding since your third month together, the enthusiasm is kind, and it is not discernment.

And age and time. Lesson 20 gave a whole lesson to this fear. Fear of time is not a reason to propose. It is a reason to be honest about time with the person you are with, and to decide with the fear separated from the evidence.

13 · The pressures (2)

Sexual pressure. Sexual desire is a legitimate part of wanting marriage, and Scripture does not treat it as shameful. But it is not evidence that this relationship is ready. Engagement is not sexual permission, and a rushed wedding does not repair weak character, incompatibility or an unsafe relationship. If temptation is the main reason things are accelerating, bring it into the open, strengthen the boundaries of Lesson 9, and ask whether you would still call the relationship ready if the pressure were removed.

Momentum. The relationship has gone on for years, everyone assumes, and proposing feels like the only way to justify the time. A long relationship is not owed an engagement. And the other person. Readiness is allowed to be unequal, but your readiness is not their obligation. If you are the one who is ready, say so, once, and let them answer freely. If you are not, say so too, and do not let their disappointment become your decision.

14 · Point 5: Approaching the decision (1)

Our fifth point is how to approach the decision. Talk about it before you decide it. A proposal, or the formal step your culture uses, should not be the first time either person is asked to consider marriage. The manner and timing may contain a surprise. The underlying question should not. Two people who have never talked about whether they want to marry each other are not ready to decide it.

Bring it to counsel. Tell the people you trust that you are thinking about it, and ask them directly: is there anything you have seen that we should look at before we do this? Ask separately, so neither of you answers for the other. If something comes back, look at it. The absence of a concern is reassuring, but it is not a guarantee. Counsel can contribute what it observes; it cannot certify a future marriage.

15 · Approaching the decision (2)

Work through the questions. The Questions Before Engagement, below this lesson, are threshold questions, not the premarital conversations of Talk Before You Marry. They do not score, and they do not certify. Do them before the ring, or its equivalent, not after.

And decide, both of you, and say it plainly. When you have talked, taken counsel and worked through the questions, decide together and say so in words. Then the proposal, however you choose to do it, is a celebration of a decision already made, rather than the decision itself. That is what a good engagement looks like: not a leap, but a step you can both see the ground under.

16 · Questions Before Engagement

The Questions Before Engagement ask whether you are ready to declare an intention to marry, not whether you are ready to marry. They cover free intention, what you have faced, what you know about each other, your spiritual foundation, the direction of your lives, counsel, pressure, the relationship itself, readiness, and the decision.

And they rest on one sentence: two free and informed yeses; one no ends it; one not yet postpones it. Neither answer may be negotiated into a yes.

Answer them alone first, in writing, before you compare. And do not complete them jointly if either of you is afraid, if saying "not yet" would bring threats, punishment or retaliation, or if your answers could be used against you. Then the questions stop, and the safety guidance comes first.

17 · And then

If you become engaged, the work is not over; it changes. Talk Before You Marry is built for the conversations that belong to engagement: money, family, faith, sex, conflict, children, the shape of the marriage itself. Take it with you. And seek thoughtful, competent premarital preparation where it is safely available, from someone who will ask difficult questions rather than only prepare a ceremony. Where fear, coercion or abuse is present, seek individual specialist help rather than ordinary joint counselling.

The freedom this course has tried to give you, to say no, does not disappear at engagement. It becomes the freedom to say yes with your eyes open, and to keep them open.

18 · If you are not near it

And if you have reached this lesson and realised that you are not near the threshold, that an essential is missing, that a concern is still open, that one of you is not sure, that the push is coming from outside, that is not a failure. It is exactly the kind of seeing this course has asked of you from the first lesson.

Go back to the lesson that fits. Do not let urgency make the decision for you. Lesson 18's proverb still holds: "Desire without knowledge is not good, and whoever makes haste with his feet misses his way." Take the time the unresolved question requires, give the other person an honest horizon, and do not move forward until both of you can do so freely.

19 · The whole road

At the beginning of this course, we asked a question: why do you want a relationship? Everything since has assumed the answer was a good one: because you want to share a life with someone, before God, in a way that is good for you both.

You know now what you are looking for, and what you will not accept. You know the difference between attraction and discernment, between a good person and a right relationship, and how to continue, slow down or walk away with wisdom. None of that depends on marriage coming. It depends on being someone who can see clearly and act kindly, whatever comes. And it began, and ends, with this: before you say yes to anyone, you must know that you are free to say no.

20 · Bringing it together

Let us bring it together. Engagement is a promise to enter the covenant of marriage: serious, and still breakable. Before it, five conditions: free intention on both sides, no fear or pressure, no ignored incompatibility, no concern still under review, and a shared understanding of what engagement is. Notice the signs that you may be near, and name the pressures that push you there early. Then talk, take counsel, work through the questions, and decide plainly, together. Two free and informed yeses; one no ends it; one not yet postpones it.

21 · One step now

Here is one step for today. Write the one question you most need answered before engagement. For example: "Have we really talked about where we would live, and whether we want children?" Or, if you are not in a relationship: "What would I need to see before I could say yes freely?"

If it is safe, write it privately, or simply think it through before God.

22 · Prayer

Let us pray.

Lord, thank you for every step of this journey. Thank you for truth, even when it was hard to hear, and for grace, even when I needed it most. Go with me into whatever comes next. If I marry, help me build a home founded on you. If I do not, help me live a full and faithful life with you. And in every relationship, help me love with honesty, freedom, and care. In Jesus' name, amen.
