Transcript and Scripture references

1 · When is a spiritual explanation hiding a warning?

Welcome back. This is Lesson 6, and we are in Part Two of the course, Choosing Wisely.

In Lesson 5 we learned to look at character patiently, through nine windows, over time. In this lesson we look at something harder: the patterns that warn us, and the way Christian language can sometimes hide them. I will picture these warnings as small cloth flags along the path. Not alarms. Just flags, telling us to slow down and look.

And we carry one question with us: when is a spiritual explanation hiding a real warning?

2 · Easier to see from the outside

Here is something many of us have noticed. Warning signs can appear clearer in someone else's relationship than in our own.

A friend describes a boyfriend who checks her phone, a girlfriend who has cut him off from his family, a fiancé who is always sorry and never different, and we can see it.

In our own relationships it is harder. From the outside we are less affected by attraction, hope, fear and spiritual pressure. On the inside, all of those are working on us at once. That is why we need Scripture to help us see.

3 · The prudent sees danger

Our passage is one verse, from the book of Proverbs. The Scripture readings in this course are from the English Standard Version. Proverbs chapter 22, verse 3:

"The prudent sees danger and hides himself, but the simple go on and suffer for it."

Notice what the prudent person does not do. He does not panic. He does not pretend the danger is not there. He sees it, and he acts on what he sees.

4 · Prudent

The word translated prudent is the Hebrew ʿārûm. It describes someone who is shrewd or sensible: a person who sees ahead, and acts on what he sees.

In Proverbs it is a virtue. In passages such as Proverbs 14:15 and 22:3, the prudent is contrasted with the simple, the person who believes whatever he is told. Proverbs 14 puts it this way: "The simple believes everything, but the prudent gives thought to his steps."

Interestingly, the same word is used of the serpent in Genesis 3, where it is translated "crafty". So the word itself is about shrewdness; what matters is how it is used. Here it is a virtue: shrewdness in the service of what is good, the wisdom to see danger coming and step aside.

5 · A book that trains the young

Now some background. The book of Proverbs tells us why it was written. Proverbs chapter 1 says it was given "to give prudence to the simple, knowledge and discretion to the youth".

In Proverbs, simple does not mean a lack of intelligence. It means inexperienced: open to being led anywhere. And the good news of Proverbs is that prudence can be learned. A young or trusting person can be trained to see ahead.

That is what this lesson is for. Not to make you suspicious of everyone, but to help you see ahead, the way Proverbs teaches.

6 · The disguises

Here is the particular difficulty this lesson exists to name. The language of faith can be used to turn a warning sign into a virtue.

A rushed engagement becomes "God's timing". Control becomes "spiritual leadership". Isolation becomes "guarding what God has given us". Secrecy becomes "keeping it between us and the Lord". Jealousy becomes "protection".

None of these reframings changes what the behaviour is. Whether they are used deliberately or not, they make it harder for the other person to object, to ask questions, or to slow the relationship down.

7 · Testing is not unbelief

Some Christians feel that questioning anything said in God's name is a lack of faith. Scripture says the opposite.

Paul told the Thessalonians, "test everything; hold fast what is good." John told his readers to "test the spirits to see whether they are from God." And Luke commends the Bereans, because they received the word eagerly, "examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so", even when the teacher was the apostle Paul.

Testing is not unbelief. It is what believers are told to do.

8 · Point 1: Three categories

Our first point. Not every concern carries the same weight, and it helps to keep three categories apart.

Compatibility differences are matters of fit: faith practice, family expectations, money habits, direction in life. They call for honest conversation, and we will look at them in later lessons.

Concerning patterns are behaviours that become serious through repetition. They call for a slower pace, clearer boundaries and an outside view.

And abuse or danger is serious the first time. It calls for safety and help, not patient observation. Wisdom considers both pattern and severity.

9 · A flag is not a flaw

Before we look at the patterns, one distinction. A red flag is not a flaw. Everyone has flaws, and Lesson 5 was about watching character patiently.

A red flag is a behaviour or pattern that gives you good reason to question whether a relationship is safe, honest or respectful. Some concerns become clear through repetition. Others are serious the first time. A red flag is not simply an ordinary imperfection.

The response to a flaw is patience and honesty. The response to a red flag is boundaries, outside help, and sometimes the end of the relationship.

10 · Point 2: Spiritual manipulation

Our second point: seven patterns, and the spiritual disguises they wear.

The first is spiritual manipulation. It is the master pattern, because it is the disguise the others most often wear. It means using God, Scripture, prophecy or spiritual authority to end a disagreement, override your judgement, or place a person beyond question.

You will recognise its shape: "If you really trusted God, you wouldn't question me." "The devil is trying to separate us." Each one takes an ordinary question, like "Is this too fast?", and replaces it with a spiritual question you cannot win.

Two things are true. A genuine word from God does not need you to stop thinking in order to receive it. And Christians understand the roles in Ephesians 5 in different ways, but the passage addresses marriage, not dating, and it never permits coercion. The command to husbands is to love "as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her". That is self-giving love, never a licence to control.

11 · Jealousy and rush

The second pattern is jealousy disguised as protection. Checking your phone. Objecting to your friends. Needing to approve your plans. The disguise is care: "I'm just protecting us."

Genuine care can raise a concern. The difference is what happens to your freedom. Care helps your life flourish; control makes your life revolve around the controller. If you have fewer people, fewer places and fewer freedoms than you had before the relationship, that is not protection.

The third is rushed commitment. Declarations of love within days. Pressure towards engagement far ahead of what you actually know. The disguise is "God's timing", or "when you know, you know." Speed serves anyone who does not want to be seen clearly. Scripture's wisdom is unhurried: "whoever makes haste with his feet misses his way."

Be fair here. A shorter courtship is not automatically a warning. The warning lies in pressure, and in punishment when you ask for time.

12 · Secrecy and pushed limits

The fourth is secrecy. Being asked to keep the relationship hidden from your parents, your pastor, your friends. Stories that change. The disguise is "it's between us and God", or "people wouldn't understand".

Privacy protects appropriate personal space. And sometimes keeping distance from an unsafe or controlling relative or leader is wise. The warning is enforced secrecy and isolation: concealing what you need to know, or cutting you off from safe people. Here is a simple principle: if someone needs the relationship to be invisible to the people who love you, ask what would become visible if it were not.

The fifth is repeated boundary violations. You say no, and it is treated as the start of a negotiation. A limit is pushed, apologised for, and pushed again. The disguise is passion: "I just love you so much." But respect shows in how a person responds to your limit. An apology without changed behaviour does not restore safety.

13 · No one can speak into it

The sixth is a lack of accountability. They are hostile when you seek counsel. They have cut off everyone who ever raised a concern about them. The disguise is maturity, or "I answer to God."

The concern is not an unusual life story; many good people have moved, or lost a church, or come from a hard family. The concern is a pattern of separating you from everyone who could see the relationship clearly. Isolation is the environment in which every other pattern grows.

And the seventh is a refusal to accept correction. They are never wrong about the specific thing you raised. Their apologies contain no change. And after a while you notice that you are the one who always ends up apologising.

Disagreement is not refusal of correction; a person may hear you and still honestly see it differently. The warning is a person who cannot listen, take responsibility, or change, because a person who turns every correction back onto you is teaching you to stop raising things.

14 · Serious the first time

Some of the behaviours we have discussed are warnings of what may happen next. Others may already be causing harm. And some actions are serious the very first time.

Violence of any kind. Threats. Sexual pressure or force. Stalking, tracking or intimidation. These are not warning signs to watch patiently, and they are not differences to work on. They are reasons to seek safety and help, and they are serious the first time.

If anything in this lesson touches your situation, there is guidance on the page beneath this lesson about where to find help where you live.

15 · Point 3: True teachings, twisted

Our third point: why warning signs can be hard for Christians to name. Several good Christian teachings can be bent in ways that make them harder to see. In every case, the teaching is true and the distortion is not.

Forgiveness. Jesus told Peter to forgive "seventy-seven times". But forgiveness does not automatically restore trust or require you to remain where you are being harmed.

Not judging. In the same sermon where Jesus warns against judging, he says, "Thus you will recognize them by their fruits." You can evaluate fruit without claiming to know someone's heart.

Not gossiping. Seeking counsel about your own life is not gossip. "In an abundance of counselors there is safety."

Suffering. God does use hardship to form us. But he does not ask you to volunteer for mistreatment in order to be formed.

And grace. Grace does not entitle anyone to another opportunity to harm you. Repentance may be real, but trust is rebuilt through safety, sustained change and accountability. The teaching is true. The distortion is not.

16 · Love and wisdom belong together

So let me put it simply, as three truths to carry.

Faith does not require us to ignore what is true. Forgiveness does not make repeated wrongdoing harmless. And love and wisdom belong together.

17 · Beware of him yourself

Paul himself shows us this balance. Near the end of his life, writing to Timothy, he says: "Alexander the coppersmith did me great harm; the Lord will repay him according to his deeds. Beware of him yourself."

Notice what the passage shows us. Paul entrusted repayment to the Lord: "the Lord will repay him according to his deeds." And he still named the harm plainly, and warned Timothy to be careful.

Wariness is not unforgiveness. Paul shows us that a mature Christian can do both: entrust repayment to God, and protect those who could be hurt.

18 · Point 4: What to do when you see a flag

Our fourth point: what to do when you recognise a flag. Five steps.

First, consider severity and pattern together. An isolated impatient remark is not necessarily a red flag. But a threat, an act of violence or sexual coercion is serious even once. Wisdom pays attention to both what happened and whether it keeps happening.

Second, take the spiritual language off. Think about what was said, or write it down only if it is safe, and remove the reference to God. What is actually being asked of you? "If you trusted God you wouldn't question me" becomes "Don't question me."

Third, talk to someone outside the relationship, someone wise and trustworthy. Not because they are automatically right, but because they can see the pattern without being inside it.

Fourth, notice whether raising it is possible. In a safe relationship you can say, "This bothered me," and be heard. If you are afraid to raise a concern, the fear is telling you something.

And fifth, if safety is involved, act on safety first.

19 · Turning the list on yourself

Before we finish, read the seven patterns once more, honestly, about yourself. Have you ever used God's name to win an argument? Called jealousy protection? Rushed someone? Pushed a limit and apologised and pushed again?

Two cautions. Do not use this to blame yourself for another person's harm; reacting imperfectly under pressure is not the same as a pattern of control. And do not mistake recognition for repentance. Recognition is only a beginning. Change is what matters.

20 · Where you are right now

Let us bring it together. Three categories: differences to talk about, patterns to watch, and danger that is serious the first time. Seven patterns, and the spiritual disguises they wear. Good teachings that can be twisted. And five steps when you see a flag.

If you recognised no warning signs, continue getting to know the person without becoming suspicious. If you recognised a concerning pattern, slow down and seek a trustworthy outside perspective. And if you recognised abuse, threats or danger, even once, put your safety first and seek appropriate help. You do not need to wait for several warning signs before taking a serious concern seriously.

Wherever you are, you are not foolish, and you are not alone. Many wise and faithful people have been where you are. The first step is to say what you have seen to one person you trust.

21 · One step now

Here is one step for today. Take one phrase you have heard in a relationship, perhaps "God's timing" or "I'm protecting us", and ask: what is the behaviour without the spiritual language?

You can simply think it through before God. If you write anything down, remember that no note is private on a phone or computer that someone else can see. The full Red Flag Checklist is below this lesson if you want to go further.

22 · Prayer

Let us pray.

Lord, give me the courage to see things as they really are. Where I have been explaining things away, help me to see clearly. Give me courage to speak to one safe person who can help me. Where I have been told that seeing clearly is doubting you, show me that it is not. If I am in danger, lead me to help, and go with me. Amen.
