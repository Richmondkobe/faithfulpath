When You’re Afraid You’ll Never Find Anyone Else

What if I never marry?

Taking the fear seriously

One of the most ordinary fears a person can have, and not a spiritual failure.

God is not disappointed in you for having it. What matters is what you let it do.

If this fear has become hopelessness, or thoughts of not wanting to live, please speak with someone today.

Psalm 13 · a psalm of David · read slowly

How long, O LORD? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me? How long must I take counsel in my soul and have sorrow in my heart all the day? How long shall my enemy be exalted over me?Psalm 13:1–2 (ESV)

Four times: how long.

Psalm 13 · read slowly

Consider and answer me, O LORD my God; light up my eyes, lest I sleep the sleep of death, lest my enemy say, ‘I have prevailed over him,’ lest my foes rejoice because I am shaken.Psalm 13:3–4 (ESV)

He does not hide that he is shaken. He brings it to God.

Psalm 13 · the turn

But I have trusted in your steadfast love; my heart shall rejoice in your salvation. I will sing to the LORD, because he has dealt bountifully with me.Psalm 13:5–6 (ESV)

Nothing outside has changed. The prayer moves from anguish towards remembered trust.

Understand the original meaning

חֶסֶד

ḥesed ✱ · “steadfast love”: God’s faithful, enduring love towards his people

Not merely a passing feeling: his loyalty and faithfulness.

David’s confidence rests on God’s character, even before his circumstances have changed.

Explore the background

“To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David.”

A personal lament, preserved among the songs of God’s people, so that others could bring their sorrow before the Lord.

Honest prayer is not a lack of faith. It is in the Bible because God can bear it.

Point 1 · What the fear does, and what it is made of

Fear is an unreliable adviser.

  1. •It lowers the barThe list is edited, one non-negotiable at a time.
  2. •It spiritualises stayingFear is very good at speaking in a religious voice.
  3. •It hurriesThe risk you cannot afford is marrying in a hurry.

What the fear does (continued)

It makes you the one adapting. It makes you unable to hear a no. It makes you invisible to yourself.

Recognise it, separate it from the evidence, and refuse to let it cast the deciding vote.

Several fears wearing one coat

lonelinessbeing unwantedthe life I hoped fortimewhat others thinkwhat it says about God

The fears have different answers. Which is yours?

Point 2 · What Scripture does and does not promise

What Scripture does and does not promise

God has not promised you a spouse.

Psalm 37:4 and Jeremiah 29:11 are not guarantees about marriage. God keeps his promises, but it matters what he promised.

Singleness is not a waiting room.

“I wish that all were as I myself am. But each has his own gift from God, one of one kind and one of another.”1 Corinthians 7:7 (ESV)

Jesus was unmarried: marital status cannot measure a complete or faithful life. Some forgo marriage “for the sake of the kingdom of heaven” (Matthew 19:12).

You are allowed to want marriage, and to grieve if it does not come.

Whether marriage comes or not, it does not determine God’s goodness towards you. He is not withholding a promise. He never made it.

“…for he has said, ‘I will never leave you nor forsake you.’”Hebrews 13:5 (ESV)

“…nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord.”Romans 8:38–39 (ESV)

Honesty about time

“There’s someone for everyone” is not true, and you know it. If children and age are a concern: individual medical advice.

You may marry. You may not. If you do not, your life will still be a life, and it can be a good one. Holding both is the task.

Fear’s arithmetic

“Some marriage is better than none.” It is wrong.

A bad marriage is not a lesser version of a good one. It is a different thing, and often worse than singleness.

A frightened yes to the wrong person does not solve the fear. It gives it a house to live in.

Point 3 · For the reader who was told no

I have not forgotten you.

The fear says: you will not find this again, and it was your fault. Both may feel true. Neither is a reason to act.

One person’s decision does not determine your worth or predict your future. The fear is guessing in the dark.

Point 4 · What to do with the fear

What to do with the fear

  1. •Name it, out loud, to someone“I am afraid I will never find anyone, and I think it is affecting my decisions.”
  2. •Bring it to God without editing itPray Psalm 13. He can bear it.
  3. •Do not let it make the decisionAdvise yourself as you would someone you love who was not afraid this was their last chance.

Build the life the fear says is impossible.

Deep friendships · a church you are known in · work that matters · a home that is yours · a table people come to

Not a consolation prize while you wait. It is real in itself.

Grieve the life you expected, if you need to. Recurrence does not mean you have failed to grieve.

If the fear is bigger than this

Constant fear, or feeling worthless without a partner? Talk to a qualified counsellor.

Hopelessness, or thoughts of not wanting to live? Seek crisis support now, and tell someone you trust.

Before you say yes to anyone, you must know that you are free to say no. Carry that freedom with you, and go and live.

Bringing it together

  1. The fear is ordinary, not a failure: name what it is made of
  2. God has not promised you a spouse; he has promised never to leave you
  3. Do not let the fear cast the deciding vote, or turn a no into a campaign
  4. Name it, pray it unedited, and build the life you have now

One step now

Write one thing you are building in your life now.

“I am inviting two friends for a meal once a month,” or “I am learning to be known at church, not just to attend.”

If it is safe, write it privately, or simply think it through before God.

Prayer

Lord, I am afraid there will be no one, and I have been letting that fear choose for me. Take the panic out of my hands. Help me desire a faithful life more than I fear a different future.

Help me tell the truth about what I can see, even when it costs me something I have been holding tightly. I do not know what is coming. You have not promised me marriage. But you have promised never to leave me. Help me trust and live in that promise today. In Jesus’ name, amen.
