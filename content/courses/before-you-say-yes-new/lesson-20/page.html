Transcript and Scripture references

1 · What if I never marry?

Welcome back. This is Lesson 20, the last lesson in Part Five, and the last chapter of the book.

Every part of this course has, at some point, pointed here. Lesson 17 said: do not continue because you are afraid there will be no one else. Lesson 19 said it again. I have kept saying that this fear is not a reason. Today I want to say what it is. I will picture a single walker on the path, lamp in hand, not alone, walking towards a home with a table and an open door.

And we carry one question with us: what if I never marry?

2 · Taking the fear seriously

I want to start by taking this fear seriously, because it is usually not taken seriously. Single Christians are told to wait, to trust, to focus on God, and then quietly judged when the waiting turns into fear. But the fear of never finding anyone is one of the most ordinary fears a person can have. It is what it feels like to want something good, to have no control over whether it comes, and to watch time pass.

So let me say it plainly: that fear is real, it is common, and God is not disappointed in you for having it. What matters is not whether you feel it, but what you let it do.

And if this fear has become hopelessness, or thoughts of not wanting to live, please speak with someone today. There is help on the page beneath this lesson.

3 · How long?

Our passage is Psalm 13, a psalm of David. It has only six verses, and we are going to read it slowly.

"How long, O LORD? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me? How long must I take counsel in my soul and have sorrow in my heart all the day? How long shall my enemy be exalted over me?"

Four times: how long. David says to God what a polite believer might never dare to say.

4 · Answer me

Then he asks:

"Consider and answer me, O LORD my God; light up my eyes, lest I sleep the sleep of death, lest my enemy say, 'I have prevailed over him,' lest my foes rejoice because I am shaken."

He does not hide that he is shaken. He brings it to God.

5 · But I have trusted

And then the psalm turns:

"But I have trusted in your steadfast love; my heart shall rejoice in your salvation. I will sing to the LORD, because he has dealt bountifully with me."

Notice what the psalm does not tell us. It does not say that anything outside David changed between verse two and verse five. What changes is his hold on what he knows about God. The prayer moves from anguish towards remembered trust.

6 · Steadfast love: ḥesed

The word translated "steadfast love" is the Hebrew hesed. Here it speaks of God's faithful, enduring love towards his people. It is not merely a passing feeling. It expresses his loyalty and faithfulness.

David's confidence rests on God's character, even before his circumstances have changed.

7 · A lament among the songs of God's people

The heading says, "To the choirmaster. A Psalm of David." This personal lament was preserved among the songs of God's people, so that others could also bring their sorrow before the Lord. God's people were not told to keep their complaints private and their worship cheerful. They were given words for both.

Many of the psalms are like this: how long, why have you forgotten me, will you hide your face forever? Honest prayer is not a lack of faith. It is in the Bible because God can bear it, and because his people need it. If you are afraid you will never marry, this psalm is for you.

8 · Point 1: What the fear does (1)

Our first point is what the fear does, and what it is made of. Fear is an unreliable adviser. Sometimes it hurries you; sometimes it paralyses you. Either way, it makes avoiding loss more important than seeing clearly.

It lowers the bar. A red flag that would have ended a relationship two years ago becomes something you can live with. The list you made in Lesson 3 is edited, one non-negotiable at a time, until it describes whoever is in front of you.

It spiritualises staying. "God has been so good to bring me this person", said of a relationship that, on any honest reading, is not good. Fear is very good at speaking in a religious voice. And it hurries. Lesson 18's "not yet" feels to a frightened person like a risk they cannot afford, when the risk they cannot afford is marrying in a hurry.

9 · What the fear does (2)

It makes you the one adapting. If you believe this is your last chance, you will surrender convictions, friendships and pieces of yourself to keep it, and call that love. It makes you unable to hear a no. For the person who was told no, fear turns grief into a campaign: they will come round; God will change their heart.

And it makes you invisible to yourself. A person afraid of being alone stops asking what they want, what they are called to, who they are becoming, and asks only whether they are wanted.

If you recognise yourself in any of these, that recognition is the beginning of taking the fear out of the driving seat. You do not have to make the fear disappear before you can decide well. But you do need to recognise it, separate it from the evidence, and refuse to let it cast the deciding vote.

10 · Several fears in one coat

It helps to know what you are actually afraid of, because "never finding anyone" is usually several fears wearing one coat. For some, it is loneliness: an empty house for the rest of your life. For some it is being unwanted, the belief that no one would choose you. For some it is missing the life you deeply hoped for: marriage, children, a family of your own. For some it is time: an age, a number, a closing window. For some it is what other people think: the family that asks at every gathering, the culture that treats an unmarried adult as unfinished.

And for many it is the fear of what singleness would say about God. If he is good, and I have asked for this, and it has not come, what does that mean? The fears have different answers, and that last one is answered by what Scripture actually says.

11 · Point 2: What Scripture does not promise

Our second point is what Scripture does and does not promise. And here is the thing Christian singles are often not told, or are told so gently that they do not hear it: God has not promised you a spouse.

There is no verse. There is no promise that every believer who wants to marry will. "Delight yourself in the Lord, and he will give you the desires of your heart" is a psalm about the righteous being vindicated in a world where the wicked prosper. It has been used to break the hearts of people who took it as a guarantee about marriage. "I know the plans I have for you" was spoken to exiles about the return of a nation. God does keep his promises. But it matters what he actually promised.

12 · Singleness is not a waiting room

What Scripture does say about singleness is more surprising than most churches let on. Paul, in a chapter largely about marriage, writes: "I wish that all were as I myself am. But each has his own gift from God, one of one kind and one of another." He says the unmarried person can give the Lord undivided attention. He defends marriage in the same chapter. But he refuses to treat singleness as a lesser state, or a waiting room.

Jesus himself was unmarried, so marital status cannot be the measure of a complete or faithful human life. And he spoke of some who would forgo marriage "for the sake of the kingdom of heaven". That does not describe every person who longs to marry and remains single. But it does show that marriage is not the only form a life devoted to God may take.

13 · What God has promised

That does not mean you have to want singleness, or pretend to. You are allowed to want marriage. You are allowed to grieve if it does not come. But whether marriage comes or not, it does not determine God's goodness towards you. His faithfulness is not measured by whether this particular longing is fulfilled. He is not withholding a promise. He never made it.

What he has promised is this: "for he has said, 'I will never leave you nor forsake you.'" And: "neither death nor life, nor angels nor rulers, nor things present nor things to come, nor powers, nor height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord."

Those promises do not make an unmet longing painless. They mean that an unmet longing is not proof that God has withdrawn his love.

14 · Honesty about time

I do not want to be one of the pastors who says "there's someone for everyone" and moves on, because it is not true, and you know it is not. Some people who want to marry do not. Age changes the picture for many people. And if biological children are part of what you hope for, age may place real limits on some options. That should be neither denied nor used to frighten you into marriage; if it is a real concern, seek individual medical advice, so your decisions rest on your own circumstances.

So here is the honest version. You may marry. Many people who feared they never would did, sometimes late, sometimes after they had stopped expecting it. But you may not, and if you do not, your life will still be a life, and it can be a good one. Those are both true, and holding both is the task.

15 · Fear's arithmetic

What honesty about time does not mean is that the odds justify lowering the bar. The possibility that you may not marry is not an argument for marrying the wrong person. That is fear's arithmetic: some marriage is better than none. It is wrong.

Everything in this course, the red flags, the compatibilities, the good people who are wrong for each other, is evidence that a bad marriage is not a lesser version of a good one. It is a different thing, and often a worse one than singleness. A frightened yes to the wrong person does not solve the fear. It gives it a house to live in.

16 · Point 3: For the one who was told no

Our third point is for the reader who was told no. Lesson 16 sent you here, and I have not forgotten you. You were in a relationship that you believed was right, and the other person ended it. The fear is now sitting where the relationship was, and it is telling you two things: that you will not find this again, and that if you had done something differently you would still have it.

Both may feel true. Neither is a reason to act. Do not pursue them, pressure them, or keep contact for the hidden purpose of reversing their decision. What you have lost is real, and it is allowed to hurt, and the way through it is through, not around.

And when the fear says "no one else", answer it with what you know. One person's decision does not determine your worth or predict your future. You may have things to learn, but learning is not the same as accepting a verdict that you are unlovable. You do not know what comes next, and neither does the fear. It is guessing in the dark.

17 · Point 4: What to do with the fear

Our fourth point is what to do with the fear. Name it, out loud, to someone. A fear that is never said grows in the dark. Tell a trusted friend, your pastor, a counsellor: "I am afraid I will never find anyone, and I think it is affecting my decisions." Many will say, me too.

Bring it to God without editing it. Pray Psalm 13. God does not require you to perform confidence before him. Say what you are afraid of. Say what you are angry about, if you are angry. He can bear it.

And do not let it make the decision. If you are afraid to end a relationship because of what comes after, the fear is not an argument for the relationship. Answer the earlier lessons' questions as though you were advising someone you love, who shared your convictions, but whose judgement was not controlled by the belief that this was their last chance. Then act on the answers. The fear may still be there. It just will not be the one deciding.

18 · Build the life

Build the life the fear says is impossible. Fear of loneliness is answered, in part, by connection you can build now. Deep friendships. A church you are known in, not just attend. Work or service that matters. A home that is yours and not a holding pattern. Hospitality: a table people come to. This is not a consolation prize while you wait for the real thing. It is real in itself. And if marriage comes, it will enter a life that already has purpose and belonging.

And grieve the life you expected, if you need to. Some people have carried an expectation since childhood, of marriage by a certain age, of a certain shape of family, and the fear is really grief that the expectation is dying. Give it room. It may return at particular ages, at weddings or anniversaries. Recurrence does not mean you have failed to grieve faithfully.

19 · If it is bigger than this, and where the book ends

Get help if the fear is bigger than this lesson. If it is constant, if it affects your sleep or your work or your other relationships, if it makes you feel worthless without a partner, talk to a qualified counsellor. And if fear has become hopelessness, or you find yourself thinking about harming yourself or not wanting to live, seek immediate crisis or emergency support in your country, and tell someone you trust now. Do not wait for a routine appointment.

This is the last chapter of the book. You know how to continue with wisdom, how to slow down, and how to end well, and safely. None of this depends on marriage coming. It depends on your being the kind of person who can see clearly and act kindly, whatever comes. Before you say yes to anyone, you must know that you are free to say no. Carry that freedom with you, and go and live.

20 · Bringing it together

Let us bring it together. The fear of never finding anyone is ordinary, not a spiritual failure, and it is usually several fears wearing one coat. God has not promised you a spouse; he has promised never to leave you. Do not let the fear cast the deciding vote, and do not let it turn a no into a campaign. Name it to someone, pray it to God without editing it, and build the life you have now. David's "how long" became "but I have trusted", before anything had changed.

21 · One step now

Here is one step for today. Write one thing you are building in your life now. For example: "I am inviting two friends for a meal once a month," or "I am learning to be known at church, not just to attend."

If it is safe, write it privately, or simply think it through before God.

22 · Prayer

Let us pray.

Lord, I am afraid there will be no one, and I have been letting that fear choose for me. Take the panic out of my hands. Help me desire a faithful life more than I fear a different future. Help me tell the truth about what I can see, even when it costs me something I have been holding tightly. I do not know what is coming. You have not promised me marriage. But you have promised never to leave me. Help me trust and live in that promise today. In Jesus' name, amen.
