Character Before Charisma

How do I see who someone really is?

Charismawhat a person can present
Characterwhat a person actually is

Genuine character and polished presentation can be difficult to tell apart at first.

“And Absalom used to rise early and stand beside the way of the gate.”2 Samuel 15:2 (ESV)

“See, your claims are good and right, but there is no man designated by the king to hear you.”2 Samuel 15:3 (ESV)

“Oh that I were judge in the land!”2 Samuel 15:4 (ESV)

“And whenever a man came near to pay homage to him, he would put out his hand and take hold of him and kiss him.”

“So Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel.”

2 Samuel 15:5–6 (ESV)

Understand the original meaning

“stole the hearts”

וַיְגַנֵּב … אֶת־לֵב

gānaḇto steal
lēḇheart: thinking and choosing (Lesson 3)
“And Jacob tricked Laban the Aramean” ESV footnote: Hebrew stole the heart of · Genesis 31:20

Not simply liked. Their loyalty was won by deception.

Explore the background

His warmth at the gate had been strategic: sympathy and personal warmth, used to build loyalty while preparing to seize power.

a means of influence
genuine care

Not a profile of every charismatic person. Charm itself is not sinful.

Time and conduct reveal the difference.

Point 1 · Charisma and character

confidencecharmeloquencepresencehumourspiritual fluency

Neither proof of character, nor evidence against it.

Character: a settled pattern of conduct, over time, especially when behaving well is unobserved, inconvenient or costly.

“A healthy tree cannot bear bad fruit, nor can a diseased tree bear good fruit.”Matthew 7:18 (ESV)

Not that faithful people never sin, but that persistent fruit reveals the pattern.

“They profess to know God, but they deny him by their works.”Titus 1:16 (ESV)

“One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much, and one who is dishonest in a very little is also dishonest in much.”Luke 16:10 (ESV)

Character is visible in small things.

a small lie
a small unkindness
a small refusal

Ordinary moments are revealing: they are not moments to impress.

How the New Testament assesses character

householdnot quarrelsomemoneyreputation

Church leadership, not marriage. Not a dating checklist, but the method is instructive.

Scripture does not ask whether a person is impressive. It asks what they have consistently done.

Point 2 · Nine windows into character

These windows help us see character over time.

Many observations need context, but serious harm or danger can be decisive even the first time.

Windows 1 and 2

1Family relationships

Honesty, wisdom and appropriate boundaries

Where there has been abuse, closeness is not required.

2People they have nothing to gain from

Courtesy is not conclusive; repeated contempt is significant.

Quietness or awkwardness is not contempt.

Windows 3 and 4

3How they speak about former partners

Ordinary conflict: can they recognise their own part?

Where they were harmed, do not demand they share the blame.

Consistent facts · privacy · a pattern in the story?

4Authority and accountability

Honest disagreement, without deception, retaliation or contempt

Healthy character sometimes includes leaving.

Ask what happened.

Windows 5 and 6

5Money

Lesson 11 is given over to it.

Where words and behaviour most often separate

Careful with their own money, careless with yours?

6Disappointment

gracioussulkingpunishingraging

Especially when you are the cause, and when you have said a reasonable “no”.

Window 7

7Correction

Can they listen, consider, and return to it?

Or: defence, retaliation, an argument about how it was said

“Let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger.”James 1:19 (ESV)

A person who cannot be corrected cannot grow.

A fairness note for windows 6 and 7

Some people need time. That may reflect temperament, anxiety or past hurt, not manipulation.

Do they return to the conversation, or use withdrawal to control?

Windows 8 and 9

8Small dishonesties

White lies · cut corners · small promises kept?

Where Luke 16:10 does its work

9How they speak about other people

Contempt, mockery, suspicion, or a basic charity?

How they speak of others is a guide to how they will one day speak of you.

How to look through the windows

1Watching, not testing. Let ordinary life open the windows. Some may stay closed for good reasons.
2Patterns, but severity matters. Some things are serious the first time: seek safety and help.
3Evidence, not impressions.
“He seems kind.”an impression
Stayed calm when the taxi driver got lost, and thanked him.evidence · if it is safe, a private note

The response to failure

Nobody passes all nine windows cleanly.

Character

failsnoticesowns itrepairs it

Charisma without character

failsexplainsdeflects“noticing was the problem”

In ordinary failures, the response and lasting change reveal a great deal.

Some actions are serious enough that an apology does not make a relationship safe.

Point 3 · Integrity: the master window

In public
In private

Do public and private conduct match? Do their words match their behaviour?

Not identical behaviour everywhere: everyone has more energy for a platform than for a kitchen.

Public virtues not contradicted by private cruelty, dishonesty or contempt.

“The crucible is for silver, and the furnace is for gold, and a man is tested by his praise.”Proverbs 27:21 (ESV)

Praise can test character. Notice how someone handles admiration, recognition and attention.

Point 4 · Your own bias

Overvaluing charismaconfidence and visibility weigh too much
Suspicious of charismagifts treated as danger

Charisma is not a red flag. It is simply not a green one.

They may have both gifts and goodness. Make sure you have looked for the second.

If a window has already shown cruelty, contempt, intimidation, deception, or a person you are afraid to disappoint: do not keep observing. Talk to someone you trust now. Help is on the page beneath this lesson.

Where you are right now

Bringing it together

  1. Charisma is what a person can present; character is what they are
  2. Faithful in a very little: character shows in small things
  3. Nine windows, and the response to failure
  4. Integrity is the master window; know your own bias

One step now

Choose one of the nine windows you have not yet looked through.

Watch, do not test. Let ordinary life open it.

Not dating? Choose one window, and look through it at yourself.
