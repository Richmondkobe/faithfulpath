Boundaries Without Shame

Is it unloving to have limits?

“Boundaries”: two uneasy reactions

“It sounds selfish, or cold.” · “It is used as a weapon.”

Physical boundaries: Lesson 9

Shame stops Christians having boundaries, and shame is used against them when they do.

Matthew 5:37 · the Sermon on the Mount

“Let what you say be simply ‘Yes’ or ‘No’; anything more than this comes from evil.”Matthew 5:37 (ESV)

Understand the original meaning

ναὶ ναί, οὒ οὔ

nai nai, ou ou · literally “yes, yes; no, no”

Speech that can be trusted as it is.

Explore the background

About truthful speech and unnecessary oaths: not first about dating, and not a rule about boundaries.

“No.”plain, and easy to know
“Well… maybe… I suppose…”harder to know, often resentful

By application: a plain yes and no is easier to love.

Point 1 · What a boundary is

A personal boundary identifies what you will take part in, what access you will give, and what you will do when a legitimate limit is crossed.

It does not guarantee another person’s compliance. It concerns your own choices.

One word, four different things

Boundary
Boundary

“I will end the call if I am being shouted at.”

Request
Request

“Would you put your phone away while we have dinner?”

Agreement
Agreement

“We agree not to post photographs of each other without asking.”

Control
Control

“You must give me your passwords.”

Not every request is control. The question: are both still free to discuss and decline, without pressure or punishment?

Three things a boundary is not

Not a wall: a line that lets the right things in at the right pace.

Not a test: you set it because it is true.

A consequenceprotects participation or safety
A punishmentto make someone suffer or submit

Taking space is not punishment. Withholding affection to force submission is manipulation.

Boundaries run both ways.

Sometimes two legitimate boundaries reveal that two people are not compatible.

If only one person may say “this far and no further”, it is a relationship with a ruler.

Point 2 · Why boundaries are not shameful

Some think a loving person has no limits. Scripture does not teach this.

“But he would withdraw to desolate places and pray.”Luke 5:16 (ESV)

Faithful love and service can include time away from other people’s demands.

“Bear one another’s burdens.”Galatians 6:2
“Each will have to bear his own load.”Galatians 6:5

Two different Greek words: we should not make them carry more than the passage allows.

Care for one another · take responsibility for your own conduct

“A man without self-control is like a city broken into and left without walls.”Proverbs 25:28 (ESV)

About self-control, with care: not fortresses, but protection for what is valuable.

You can only give freely what you could have withheld.

A gift that could not have been refused is not a gift.

Boundaries are what make intimacy mean something.

Point 3 · Six kinds of boundary

EmotionalSpiritualDigitalFinancialTimeRelational

A map, not a rulebook: the right lines differ between people and cultures.

1Emotional

“Do not stir up or awaken love until it pleases.” (Song of Songs 2:7) Poetry, not a timetable.

Closeness at the pace of trust.

2Spiritual

Your walk with God remains yours.

Neither holds spiritual authority over the other.

EmotionalSpiritualDigitalFinancialTimeRelational
3Digital

A partner is not entitled to your accounts.

No pressure for intimate images; no sharing without consent.

If monitored, do not simply change passwords: get qualified advice (Lesson 6).

4Financial

Shared accounts and debts: usually “not yet”.

Gifts do not purchase affection, access or commitment.

Be cautious about money requests from anyone not met in person.

EmotionalSpiritualDigitalFinancialTimeRelational
5Time

Work · sleep · church · friends · family · rest

One part of a full life, not the whole of a shrinking one.

6Relational

Privacy protects personal space. Secrecy hides what the other needs to know.

Fair in both directions: no control by intimidation or monitoring.

How to set a boundary

  1. 1Know it where you canyou may also name it later
  2. 2State it plainlynot as an accusation, not as an apology
  3. 3Say what you will do“I’ll say so and change the subject”
  4. 4Follow through where you can; get help where you cannotdifficulty is not your fault
  5. 5Watch the responseacceptance is a green flag; pressure is not

“I don’t lend money in a relationship” is enough.

Point 4 · When a boundary is crossed

When a boundary is crossed

  1. 1Name it, calmly and specifically“…last night you called at one.” Not “you never respect me.”
  2. 2Consider the crossing and the responsemisunderstanding · lapse · pattern; severity matters
  3. 3Allow honest disagreementit may show incompatibility, not wrongdoing
  4. 4Act on what you said, if you safely cannot punishment: being who you said you were

Coercion, intimidation or control is no longer a boundary conversation. Boundaries are not a technique for making an unsafe person behave better. Seek help. Guidance is on the page beneath this lesson.

Shame, in three directions

For having themlimits are how finite people love well
Used against youthat behaviour is theirs to answer for
At yourself“No condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.” (Romans 8:1)

Draw the line again, and start from where you are.

If you crossed someone else’s line: grace removes condemnation without removing responsibility.

Where you are right now

Not yet in a relationshipknow your lines before someone tests them
Dating, never discussed ithave the conversation: awkward for ten minutes
A line already crosseddraw it where you are now; say so plainly

If speaking about the boundary could put you at risk, do not confront the person simply to complete this step. Seek independent support and decide what is safe.

Bringing it together

  1. A plain yes and a plain no can be trusted (Matthew 5:37)
  2. A boundary is about your own choices: not a wall, a test or a punishment
  3. Six kinds of boundary, set plainly and kept with help
  4. No shame for having limits, or for starting again

One step now

Write one boundary you already keep, in one sentence.

“I don’t lend money in a relationship.” · “I keep Sunday mornings for church.”

If it is safe, write it privately, or simply think it through before God.
