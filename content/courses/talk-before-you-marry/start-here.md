## Why this course exists

In my work as a pastor and relationship counsellor, I have sat with many couples: some before the wedding, some years into marriage, and some when trust had been badly damaged.

After enough of those conversations, you start to notice something. Many couples talk constantly about work, family, plans and faith. Yet they can struggle to discuss the subjects that feel most threatening. Being able to talk easily is not the same as being able to speak honestly when something important is at stake.

Unspoken concerns do not disappear. They can become recurring sources of disappointment, resentment and distance.

So this course has a narrow purpose. It is not about romance, and it is not about the wedding. It is about helping two people who can talk easily about most things learn to speak honestly about the things that are hard.

Marriage is a covenant, entered in truth, love, wisdom, faithfulness and mutual honour. These conversations help you bring your real expectations into the light before you make that covenant, while you are still free to decide.

The goal is not to become a couple who agrees about everything. It is to become a couple who can speak the truth in love, listen with humility and face important differences faithfully.

*Pastor Richmond Kobe, Faithful Path Community*

## Who it is for

- Engaged Christian couples preparing for marriage. It assumes you both take your faith seriously, and it turns to Scripture where Scripture speaks to what you are deciding.
- Couples in a serious relationship who are deciding whether to marry. What you find may help you decide, in either direction.
- Married couples who want to revisit these conversations. Adapt the questions to where you are now.
- Pastors and counsellors who want a structure for premarital sessions. A separate facilitator guide is available.

## What it is not

- It is not a test. There is no score to pass, and finishing does not certify that you are ready, or not ready, to marry.
- It is not a replacement for personal pastoral care, premarital counselling, or qualified help when these are needed.
- It cannot tell whether your relationship is safe. If you are afraid, read the section on safety below before you go any further.

## How the route works

There are **fourteen lessons**, in a deliberate order. Each one depends on the one before it, so please do not skip ahead to the rooms because they look like the practical part.

- **Lessons 1 to 5** build the ability to notice what you avoid, build a relationship in which honesty can be heard, know your own mind, raise something difficult and listen well when your partner speaks.
- **Lessons 6 to 14** spend that ability in nine “rooms”: money, sex, family and boundaries, children, faith and church, roles, past relationships, what you are marrying into, and conflict itself.

**One lesson a week is the suggested pace, not a deadline.** All lessons are open to you. Follow the course in order, but do not postpone an urgent concern simply because its room comes later. Address it carefully, and seek appropriate help where needed. Repeat a week whenever a conversation needs more time.

Each lesson is anchored in Scripture. We read the passage, understand it in context, draw out its biblical truth for marriage, see that truth in an ordinary story and then put it into practice. Where a Greek or Hebrew word genuinely clarifies the passage, we explain it in plain language.

Each lesson has one practice: usually one conversation, sometimes preparation on your own first. Room conversations take about 45 to 60 minutes. Some preparation exercises take longer. The Private Inventory is completed over several sittings, and Lessons 4 and 5 may take about an hour. Each lesson will tell you what to expect before you begin.

You may stop at any time. Stopping for today is a good way to end.

## How to use it

- **Watch, listen or read.** Every lesson has a recording you can watch or listen to, and a full written transcript. Worksheets can be downloaded.
- **Each of you uses your own account.** Your private preparation belongs to you. Doing a conversation together does not give either of you a right to see the other’s private notes.
- **Do the alone parts alone.** Private preparation gives each of you room to think without watching the other person’s reaction. It can help you recognise what you actually believe before you begin the shared conversation.
- **Use two notebooks, or two copies of each worksheet.** Several exercises ask you to write separately before you compare.
- **Choose a time when neither of you is tired or rushed.** Take breaks. Adapt the exercises to your language, energy and circumstances. Neither speed nor eloquence is a measure of honesty.
- **If you are apart,** choose a private video or phone call, agree how to pause, and check that neither of you is being overheard. Do not record personal conversations unless both of you freely agree.
- **Expect to learn something.** You may find an important difference, or find that an apparent agreement needs more detail. Neither is a verdict on your relationship. Both deserve honest attention.

## Privacy

- **The site stores:** your account details (your email address and a securely stored password) and your lesson-completion progress (which lessons you have marked complete).
- **The site does not store:** answers to reflection questions. The lessons have no place to type answers. The reflection questions are for prayer and thought.
- **Your worksheets:** you download them and complete them on your own device or on paper. They are not uploaded to the course. They stay with you unless you choose to share them.
- **Payments:** payments are handled by Stripe. The site does not store your card details.
- **Tracking:** the site does not use analytics or advertising trackers. Like most websites, its hosting service keeps basic technical records, such as the time of a visit and the IP address, to keep the site working and secure, and keeps them only for a limited time.
- **Who can see your information:** each partner has a separate account, and your partner cannot see your account or your progress. Only the course author, who runs the site, can technically access account and completion information, and uses it only to run the course and give support.
- **Store private notes securely.** If writing something down would not feel safe, you may hold the reflection privately in thought or prayer instead.

## Safety and support

Some conversations may feel awkward, tiring or emotional. You may pause, take a break or return another day.

Some situations, however, are different. If you are afraid of your partner’s response, or you do not feel free to speak honestly, pause the joint exercises and seek confidential help from someone trained to support you. If you are in immediate danger, contact the emergency service where you are, if you can safely do so.

The full guidance, and where to find help in your country, is on the Safety and Support page. Every lesson links back to it.
