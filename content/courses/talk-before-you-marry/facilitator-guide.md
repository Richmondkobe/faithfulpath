## What this guide is for

*Talk Before You Marry* is a fourteen-lesson course that helps an engaged or seriously courting couple tell each other the truth about the things that are hard to say, before they make a covenant. Each lesson is a short recording with a Scripture, a private preparation and a couple’s conversation.

You do not need to run the course for the couple. The course is designed so that a couple can work through it on their own. Your role is to accompany them: to listen, to help them make sense of what they find, and to notice when something needs more than the course can give.

## What the course is not

- It is not a test. There is no score, and no certificate of readiness. Finishing does not mean a couple is ready or not ready to marry.
- It is not a replacement for personal pastoral care, premarital counselling or qualified help.
- It does not decide for the couple. Engagement is a time for discernment, and nothing in the course promises that the couple will proceed.
- It is not safe for every couple. Some couples should not do the joint exercises. See the next section.

## Before you begin: meet each person alone

Before you start, meet each partner separately for a short time, even if the couple has come together. This is not an interrogation, and you do not need to tell them why. You are offering each of them a private moment.

Ask each of them, in your own words:

- What are you hoping this preparation will do for you?
- Is there anything you are worried about, or anything you would find hard to say in front of your partner?
- Do you feel free to say no, to disagree and to be honest with each other?
- Is there anything that makes you afraid of how your partner will respond?

If either person describes fear, control, threats, monitoring, pressure to have sex, or a sense that they cannot refuse or leave, **do not start the joint exercises.** Do not confront the other partner, and do not arrange a joint conversation about it, because either can raise the danger. Offer the person private help and point them to specialist support. Abuse is not a communication problem that two people share equally.

If you are unsure, go slowly and ask again in a few weeks.

## The shape of the course

- **Start Here.** The couple read the introduction and safety guidance, and acknowledge it before beginning.
- **Lessons 1 to 5: the foundations.** They name what they are afraid to discuss, agree rules for hard conversations, do private work, and learn to say and to hear the hard thing. The first real conversation is held after Lesson 5.
- **Lessons 6 to 13: the rooms.** Money, sex, family, children, faith, roles, past relationships and what each is bringing into the marriage. Each has a private preparation, then a conversation of 45 to 60 minutes.
- **Lesson 14: conflict itself.** They write a Conflict Covenant and consider what they have learned.

Each lesson takes about 13 minutes to watch or listen to, plus the preparation and conversation.

## Suggested pace

Most couples do best with one lesson a week or a fortnight. The conversations, not the recordings, take the time. Do not hurry a couple who need longer. If they stop partway, they can return without losing anything.

A simple pattern for your own meetings, if you are meeting them:

1. Ask what they did since you last met, and what they learned. Do not ask them to report what was said in their private conversations.
2. Ask what was hard, and what they would like to talk through with you.
3. Look together at any difference they found, and what kind it is: a preference they can negotiate, a conviction that needs mutual understanding and respect, or an incompatibility that neither can honestly live with.
4. Close in prayer if they wish.

## The lessons that need particular care

**Lesson 3 (private inventory).** Nothing written is stored on the site, and the couple should not be asked to share it. If someone tells you what they wrote, listen, and do not ask for more than they offer.

**Lesson 6 (money).** Look for financial control: one partner who decides everything, withholds information, or does not allow the other access to their own money.

**Lesson 7 (sex).** The conversation uses words only. It is never a way of testing compatibility. If either person has experienced sexual abuse or coercion, do not press for details, and offer private, specialist help.

**Lesson 11 (roles).** The course does not declare complementarian or egalitarian marriage the winner. Represent both positions fairly and help the couple see what their words mean in ordinary life. Do not let anyone use “roles” or Scripture to silence a partner.

**Lesson 12 (past relationships).** Separate what a person chose from what was done to them. A person who has been abused has nothing to confess. They control whether, when and how they tell that story. If a partner is demanding a full account, treat that as its own issue.

**Lesson 14 (conflict).** Abuse is not a shared communication loop, and the language of “our cycle” must never be used to assign equal blame for abusive conduct.

## When someone tells you something

- Listen, and thank them. Do not promise to keep secrets you cannot keep.
- Know the limits of your own confidentiality before you begin, and tell each partner about them at the start, including any legal duty to report, which differs by country and by your role. Check this in your own jurisdiction.
- Do not confront the other partner on someone’s behalf.
- Where there is fear or harm, help the person reach specialist support, and a private safety plan comes before any joint work.
- Where a person is thinking of harming themselves, help them reach emergency or crisis support at once.

## What not to do

- Do not declare that the couple is ready or not ready to marry, and do not treat the course as a pass or fail.
- Do not pressure them to continue, or to end the engagement.
- Do not take sides on a disagreement of conviction, such as roles, and do not use your authority to settle something they need to work out.
- Do not ask to see their private worksheets, and do not keep copies.
- Do not hold a joint meeting about a matter one of them has told you privately, without their agreement.
- Do not treat asking for help as a sign that the relationship has failed.

## When to bring in someone else

Suggest more help when the same issue keeps returning with no progress; when conversations reliably explode or shut down; when there is serious distrust or concealment; when there is a significant difference over children, faith, money, roles or family that neither can move; when one of them is afraid to speak honestly; or when a matter needs a healthcare professional, a lawyer, a financial adviser or an addiction or mental-health service. Where there is fear or abuse, seek individual specialist advice before considering joint counselling.

## What the site stores

The site stores each person’s account and their lesson-completion progress. It does not store their worksheets or answers, and one partner cannot see the other’s account or progress. As facilitator, you do not have access to their information on the site unless they choose to share it with you.
