## If you are in immediate danger

Contact the emergency service where you are, if you can safely do so. If you cannot speak, or a call would be unsafe, use whatever way is safest for you to signal that you need help.

## When a conversation is only hard

Most difficulty in these lessons is ordinary. A conversation can feel awkward, tiring or emotional and still be a healthy one. You may:

- Pause, take a break, and agree when you will return.
- Stop for the day. Nothing in the course is lost.
- Go back over a lesson, or slow down to one lesson a fortnight.
- Ask a trusted pastor, mentor or qualified counsellor to help you think it through.

## When something is more than difficult

Please stop the joint exercises and seek help from someone trained to support you, if any of these is true:

- You are afraid of how your partner will respond to something you want to say.
- You do not feel free to say no, to disagree, or to speak honestly.
- You are threatened, insulted, followed, monitored or controlled, or your money, phone, movements or friendships are controlled.
- Anger, jealousy or "concern" is used to make you change what you say or do.
- You have been pressured, forced or frightened in a sexual way.
- Your partner uses Scripture, "roles" or your faith to silence you or to make you feel that refusing or leaving would be sinful.
- You have been hurt, or you are afraid you will be.

You do not need to choose a label or make a final decision today. These signs are enough reason to pause and seek private specialist advice, because a joint conversation is not the safest next step. Abuse is not a communication problem that two people share equally, and it is never the fault of the person who is being harmed.

If you are the one who is causing fear or harm to your partner, please stop, and seek a specialist behaviour-change programme for people who use abuse or coercive control. Anger management or couples counselling are not substitutes, and a pastor can support you but this needs specialist help.

## Safer ways to use this site

If someone else may see your phone, computer or accounts:

- Use a device and account the other person cannot access, if you can safely do so, such as a library or a trusted friend's device.
- Private browsing may leave less information on the device after you close it, but it cannot hide activity from every form of monitoring.
- Sign out when you finish. On a public or shared device, do not save passwords or downloads.
- Keep worksheets on paper somewhere private, or do not write sensitive things down at all. You may hold the reflections in thought or prayer.
- You can stop, or return later, without explaining to anyone.

## What kind of help

Different situations need different help. You may need more than one.

- **A pastor or mentor** can offer spiritual care and help you think and pray. If there is fear, coercion or harm, speak to someone privately who understands abuse and will not arrange a joint conversation or contact your partner without your agreement.
- **A qualified counsellor or therapist** for ordinary conflict, communication, grief or anxiety. Ask about their training, how they handle confidentiality, and how they assess safety. Where there is fear, coercion or abuse, seek individual specialist advice before considering joint counselling.
- **A specialist domestic-abuse or sexual-violence service** if there is abuse, coercion, financial control or fear. These services can talk with you confidentially and help you plan, and you do not have to leave or press charges to use them. Financial control is a form of abuse and may also need legal support.
- **A doctor or healthcare professional** for your physical or sexual health, and for advice about health information that may be important to discuss before marriage.
- **A lawyer or legal adviser** for custody, support, immigration, property, debts or a previous marriage.
- **A financial adviser or debt-advice service** for debt or financial planning, where there is no fear or control.
- **An addiction or mental-health service** for addiction, or for thoughts of harming yourself. If you are thinking of harming yourself, please contact emergency or crisis support where you are.

## If someone tells you they are afraid

Listen and believe them. Do not tell them to work it out, to pray harder or to be more forgiving. Do not confront the person they fear on their behalf, because that can increase the danger. Ask what would help, and help them reach specialist support.

## You are not alone

God is near to those who are afraid. He does not require you to endure harm in order to preserve an engagement, a marriage or participation in this course. Seeking safety and wise help is not a failure of faith.
