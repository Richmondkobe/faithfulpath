A Sustainable Rhythm of Life With God

I have learned so much, but my life is busy and tiring. How do I keep walking with God for the long term, not just in bursts of enthusiasm?

About 15 minutesMark 1:35 and Acts 2:42My Growth: Keep a rhythm

In this lesson

You will learn

  • What Jesus' early prayer in Mark 1:35 shows, and what it does not require
  • What the first believers "devoted themselves" to in Acts 2:42
  • The four parts: Time Alone With God, Life Together, Rest as Trust, A Rhythm That Fits
  • How to write a simple rhythm for your season of life, kept with grace
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · For the long term

Welcome to Lesson 12, the final lesson of Grow. In Begin, you took your first steps in reading the Bible, praying and belonging to a church. In Establish, you learned to read Scripture with four questions, and to pray, worship and fast. And in Lesson 1 of Grow, you saw that grace trains us. This last lesson gathers them into something simple enough to keep: a rhythm of life with God.

Many believers know the pattern: a season of fresh enthusiasm, then busyness, tiredness and drifting, then guilt, then another fresh start. Work, family, illness and worry crowd in. Over the years, it can be hard to keep walking steadily with God.

So here is our question: I have learned so much, but my life is busy and tiring. How do I keep walking with God for the long term, not just in bursts of enthusiasm?

2 · Very early in the morning

Our first passage is Mark chapter 1, verse 35: "And rising very early in the morning, while it was still dark, he departed and went out to a desolate place, and there he prayed."

Use the first question: what does it say? Notice what Jesus did in the middle of a very busy time: he went to be alone with his Father, and he prayed.

3 · They devoted themselves

Our second passage is Acts chapter 2, verse 42: "And they devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers."

Four practices: teaching, fellowship, the breaking of bread, and prayer.

4 · Devoted = proskartereō

Our Greek word today is proskartereō, translated "devoted themselves". It means to continue in something: to keep at it steadily.

Luke uses the same word in Acts 1, where the disciples were "devoting themselves to prayer", and Paul uses it in Romans 12: "be constant in prayer." It describes steady, ongoing commitment, not occasional bursts of enthusiasm.

5 · After a long day in Capernaum

Now the background. The day before, Jesus had taught, healed and cast out demons late into the evening. His prayer was not simply something added after the work was finished. Time with the Father remained central while the demands continued around him.

Now use the second question: what did it mean to the people who first heard it? When the disciples found him and said, "Everyone is looking for you", he answered, "Let us go on to the next towns." It is reasonable to see that time with the Father shaped his priorities, not only his feelings. And this verse describes Jesus' practice. It does not set a required hour, length or daily formula for every believer.

6 · In the temple and in homes

Acts 2 describes the first believers just after Pentecost. "Day by day, attending the temple together and breaking bread in their homes," they kept these practices together. Teaching, fellowship, the breaking of bread and prayer became a simple shared rhythm.

Acts describes a pattern to learn from, not a rigid template for every detail. But it shows that faith grew through steady practices, kept together.

7 · The short answer

So, how do I keep walking with God for the long term? Here is the short answer.

Long-term faithfulness grows from small, steady patterns: time alone with God, life together with his people, rest that trusts him, and a rhythm that fits your season of life, kept with grace.

That gives us four parts: Time Alone With God; Life Together; Rest as Trust; and A Rhythm That Fits.

8 · Part 1: Time Alone With God

The first part: Time Alone With God.

Even when crowds pressed in, Jesus "would withdraw to desolate places and pray." Scripture and prayer are important ways we live out our continuing relationship with Christ. Jesus said, "I am the vine; you are the branches. Whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing."

This time does not have to be early, long or silent. It may be a few minutes with a Bible passage, an audio Bible on a journey, or a short prayer between tasks. Daniel prayed three times a day. The psalmist prayed "evening and morning and at noon". What matters is not the length, but returning regularly to the God who loves us.

9 · Part 2: Life Together

The second part: Life Together.

Christian rhythm is not only private. Regular worship, the Lord's Supper, fellowship in a small group, encouragement and service sustain faith over time. "Let us consider how to stir up one another to love and good works, not neglecting to meet together… but encouraging one another."

God ordinarily sustains his people in fellowship with other believers. When our own faith feels weak, others pray for us; when theirs is weak, we pray for them. "Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly, teaching and admonishing one another in all wisdom."

10 · Part 3: Rest as Trust

The third part: Rest as Trust.

God commanded his people to rest one day in seven, and Jesus said, "The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath." As you saw in Lesson 8, Christians differ about how the Sabbath command applies today, but all can learn from its gift.

When his disciples were tired, Jesus said, "Come away by yourselves to a desolate place and rest a while." And the psalm says, "It is in vain that you rise up early and go late to rest, eating the bread of anxious toil; for he gives to his beloved sleep."

Rest is not laziness. Rest can be an act of trust: we stop because we are creatures, not God, and the world remains in his hands. Sleep, enjoyment, friendship, creation and time with family are part of a healthy life with God.

11 · Part 4: A Rhythm That Fits

The fourth part: A Rhythm That Fits.

"For everything there is a season." A rhythm of life is a simple pattern of daily, weekly and occasional practices that help us stay close to God. It is like a trellis that supports a growing plant, not a cage. It should fit your season of life. A parent of young children, a shift worker, a carer, a student, a person living with illness, or someone in a place where Christians cannot meet openly will each need a different shape.

A simple rhythm might include: daily, a few minutes in Scripture and prayer; weekly, worship with your church, time with other believers, a time of rest, and an ordinary way of loving someone; and occasionally, something that helps you go deeper, such as meeting a mentor, a longer time of prayer, or fasting where it is safe.

Christians differ on how structured such a rhythm should be, and on keeping the church year. You will find both under Go deeper.

12 · Grace, not grading

Jesus invites the weary to come to him and take his yoke: "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." A rhythm of life should help us live as his disciples. It must not become a new law, or a measure of God's love.

As you saw in Lesson 1, grace trains us; it does not grade us. When your rhythm breaks down, simply begin again.

13 · Sunita's story

Let me tell you about Sunita. Her story is a composite, drawn from the experiences of many people.

Sunita works long shifts in a garment factory in Kathmandu, Nepal, and has three children. She came to faith four years ago and loves her church, which meets on Saturdays. For a while she tried to copy her pastor's habit of praying for an hour at five in the morning, but she kept falling asleep and felt like a failure. She had also agreed to help with three church ministries, and was rarely home on her day off.

Her small group leader, Asha, read Mark 1:35 and Mark 6:31 with her. "Jesus prayed alone, and he also told his tired disciples to rest," Asha said. "Let us make a rhythm that fits your life, not someone else's."

Together they wrote a simple plan. Each day: listen to a short Bible passage on her phone during the bus ride, and pray the pattern of the Lord's Prayer before sleeping. Each week: worship with her church on Saturday morning, eat and rest with her family on Saturday afternoon, and join her small group on Wednesday evening when her shifts allow. Each month: meet Asha for tea and prayer. Sunita also stepped back, kindly and with notice, from two of her three church roles.

Some weeks the factory adds extra shifts and her rhythm shrinks to a few short prayers, and that is enough. When she misses a day, she now simply begins again. She says her relationship with God no longer feels like a test she keeps failing.

14 · What this lesson is not saying

Here are five common mistakes.

"Real Christians pray for an hour every morning." No. No schedule, time of day or length of prayer is the spiritual standard.

"If I miss a day, God is disappointed with me." No. A rhythm is a gift, not a measure of God's love; simply begin again.

"More church activities mean more spiritual growth." No. Rest, family, work and health matter to God too.

"Rest is laziness." No. Rest is trusting God to hold the world while we stop.

And "everyone's rhythm should look the same." No. A rhythm should fit your season and circumstances.

15 · This week

This week, your assignment is to write a simple rhythm for this season of your life, and try it for one week.

First, read Mark 1:35 and Acts 2:42. Second, write one short line for each of the four parts, in your own words. You can use the book to help.

Third, if you wish, write your rhythm. Daily: one small way to meet God in Scripture and prayer. Weekly: worship with your church, one connection with other believers, one time of rest, and one ordinary way of loving someone already in your life. Occasionally: something that helps you go deeper. These may overlap; do not add another activity just to complete the list. Keep it small enough to keep on hard weeks. If your health, work or safety limits what is possible, a smaller rhythm is faithful.

Fourth, try your rhythm for one week.

16 · Looking back over Grow

Before we finish, look back over the road you have travelled. In Grow you have seen grace that trains us, a renewed mind, the fruit of the Spirit, and honest emotions brought to God. You have learned about forgiveness and boundaries, loving others, honouring God with your body, work and money, words and peacemaking, guidance, and suffering. Now you have a simple rhythm to keep walking.

When you are ready, use the reflection called My Growth in Following Jesus at the end of the course. It uses the six Grow areas as reflection prompts, not a score. And remember: this is about becoming like Jesus over time, by grace. It is not a ladder to climb to earn God's love.

17 · Remember

Let us bring it together. We spend time alone with God, share life with his people, rest as an act of trust, and keep a rhythm that fits, beginning again whenever it breaks down.

Time alone with God: Scripture and prayer help us remain attentive to Christ, the vine. Life together: worship, fellowship, the Lord's Supper and prayer with others sustain faith over the years. Rest as trust: sleep and rest are gifts; we are creatures, and the world remains in God's hands. And a rhythm that fits: a trellis, not a cage, shaped for your season, and never a measure of God's love.

18 · Prayer

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you for walking with me through Grow. Thank you that your grace saves me and trains me. Keep me close to you, the vine, day by day. Give me your people to walk with, and give me rest that trusts you. Shape a rhythm that fits my life in this season, and when it breaks down, help me simply begin again. Keep me following you for the long road ahead. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Pause and seek help

A rhythm should give life, not drain it: if you are persistently exhausted, sleeping badly, constantly anxious or unable to rest, take this seriously and consider speaking with a doctor or other qualified health professional. If a church or leader expects so many meetings or roles that your health, family or work suffer, or treats saying no as unfaithfulness, seek advice from a trustworthy person outside that situation (Support Page: I am being controlled through prophecy, spiritual language, money or church authority). If you find yourself anxiously checking whether you have prayed or read enough, or feel that God is angry when you miss a day, return to grace, keep your rhythm small, and seek patient pastoral care and, where appropriate, qualified help. Where meeting other believers is dangerous, your safety matters to God: seek experienced local advice about safe ways to connect, and remember that a quiet rhythm of prayer and Scripture is faithful (Support Page: Safety and Support: Start Here).

Optional

Go deeper

Structured rhythms and spontaneous devotion. Some Christians keep fixed times of prayer, use written prayers, and follow a written pattern for their life with God (they often point to Daniel 6:10, Psalm 55:17 and Acts 3:1). Others place more emphasis on spontaneous prayer and responding to the Spirit throughout the day (they often point to Romans 8:26, Ephesians 6:18 and 1 Thessalonians 5:17). Many combine both. Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that regular time with God and his people helps believers grow, and that any pattern should serve love for God, not replace it.

The church year. The New Testament does not give the church a required yearly Christian calendar. Many churches nevertheless use seasons such as Advent, Lent, Easter and Pentecost to remember the story of Christ each year. Others choose not to, stressing Christian freedom (they often point to Romans 14:5–6 and Colossians 2:16–17). Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that remembering God's saving acts regularly is good, and that no believer should judge another over such practices. If it would help, ask a trustworthy church leader how your church approaches these matters, and compare what you hear with Scripture.

If you are still exploring. Everyone lives by some kind of rhythm, chosen or not. This lesson describes the simple patterns that help Christians stay close to God over many years.
