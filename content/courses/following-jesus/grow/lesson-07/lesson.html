Honouring God With Your Body and Relationships

My culture, my friends and my own desires pull me in different directions about sex, dating and marriage. Does God care about my body? And is there hope for someone with my past?

About 16 minutes1 Corinthians 6:12–20 · 7:7–9 · 1 Thessalonians 4:3–8My Growth: Grow in relationships

In this lesson

You will learn

  • Why the body matters to God, from 1 Corinthians 6:19–20
  • What "temple" means, and why Paul used it in Corinth
  • The four parts: Bought With a Price, Wholly His, Single or Married, Holy and Self-Controlled, Grace, Help and Safety
  • Why no past is beyond God's grace, and why people who have been harmed are never to blame
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Competing messages

Welcome to Lesson 7 of Grow. In Establish, you saw that every person is made in God's image. In Lesson 3 of Grow, you learned to make every effort as the Spirit grows his fruit. Now we bring that teaching to the body, to singleness and marriage, and to sexuality. And this lesson begins and ends with grace.

Few areas of life carry as many competing messages. Advertising, entertainment and social media say one thing. Family and culture may say another. Our own desires pull in yet another direction. And many believers carry regret about the past, or hurt from what others have done to them.

So here is our question: my culture, my friends and my own desires pull me in different directions about sex, dating and marriage. Does God care about my body? And is there hope for someone with my past?

2 · You are not your own

Our first passage is 1 Corinthians chapter 6, verses 19 and 20: "Or do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God? You are not your own, for you were bought with a price. So glorify God in your body."

Use the first question: what does it say? Notice that Paul's appeal rests on belonging and grace: you were bought with a price. It does not rest on fear, or on disgust about the body.

3 · Two more passages

Two shorter passages complete the picture. In 1 Corinthians 7, Paul honours both singleness and marriage: "each has his own gift from God, one of one kind and one of another."

And in 1 Thessalonians 4, he writes: "For this is the will of God, your sanctification: that you abstain from sexual immorality; that each one of you know how to control his own body in holiness and honor." Both passages show that sexuality is part of discipleship, not a private area outside it.

4 · Temple = naos

Our Greek word today is naos, translated "temple". It refers to the temple or sanctuary, especially the sacred dwelling place. Paul uses this word when he says that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit.

Corinth was a city full of temples. Into that city Paul writes that the believer's body is the Spirit's dwelling place. Paul's point is striking: by his Spirit, God dwells in his people. That is why what we do with our bodies matters to him.

5 · Corinth and its slogans

Now the background. Corinth was a busy port city with many temples. In this chapter, Paul appears to quote sayings that were used among the Corinthians: "All things are lawful for me", and "Food is meant for the stomach". Some were apparently using ideas like these to separate what they did with their bodies from their spiritual life.

Now use the second question: what did it mean to the people who first heard it? Paul answers that the body matters to God. It is "for the Lord". It will be raised. It is joined to Christ. And it is the Spirit's temple.

6 · Paul and the new believers

Paul wrote 1 Corinthians 7 in answer to questions the church had sent him. Paul was himself single, yet he does not rank believers by whether they are married. He honours both singleness and marriage as gifts.

First Thessalonians was written to relatively new believers learning to follow Christ within the sexual culture around them. Paul says that no one should "transgress and wrong his brother in this matter." Notice that sexual sin is not only private: it wrongs other people.

7 · The short answer

So, does God care about my body? Here is the short answer.

Our bodies belong to the Lord, who made them, bought them and will raise them. Single or married, we are wholly his. He calls us to sexual holiness and self-control. And his grace meets us in our past, while those who are struggling or harmed deserve help and safety.

That gives us four parts: Bought With a Price; Wholly His, Single or Married; Holy and Self-Controlled; and Grace, Help and Safety.

8 · Part 1: Bought With a Price

The first part: Bought With a Price.

God made the human body and called his creation "very good". The psalmist prays, "I praise you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made." The body is not shameful. Nor is it a god to be worshipped.

Your body belongs to the Lord. He made it, he bought it with the price of Christ's death, and he lives in it by his Spirit. God cares about our bodies so much that he will raise them: "God raised the Lord and will also raise us up by his power."

Honouring God with our bodies includes how we use our sexuality. It also includes how we care for our health and rest, and how we treat other people's bodies with respect. As Lesson 2 taught us, we present our bodies "as a living sacrifice".

9 · Part 2: Wholly His, Single or Married

The second part: Wholly His, Single or Married.

Jesus was single. Paul honours both marriage and singleness. Single people can give "undivided devotion to the Lord", and as Lesson 6 showed, they belong fully in God's family. This does not mean every single person chose singleness, finds it easy, or expects it to be lifelong. Whether singleness is chosen, unexpected, temporary or lifelong, a single believer is complete in Christ.

Marriage, as Jesus taught from Genesis, is a lifelong covenant between a man and a woman. "What therefore God has joined together, let not man separate."

Christians differ on divorce and remarriage, and on roles in marriage. You will find both under Go deeper on the lesson page.

10 · A good gift, freely given

Sexual intimacy is God's good gift within marriage. "Let marriage be held in honor among all, and let the marriage bed be undefiled." The Song of Songs celebrates the power and beauty of faithful love: "love is strong as death".

In marriage, husband and wife belong to each other with mutual love and care. This never gives either one a right to demand, force or pressure the other. Intimacy must be freely agreed to by both spouses. Agreement cannot be gained through fear, threats, guilt, spiritual pressure or control. Paul's teaching on mutual care must never be used to demand intimacy, or to blame a spouse who refuses unwanted contact.

11 · Part 3: Holy and Self-Controlled

The third part: Holy and Self-Controlled.

"This is the will of God, your sanctification: that you abstain from sexual immorality." Sexual immorality includes sex outside marriage and unfaithfulness within it. Jesus showed that it begins in the heart and the eyes: "everyone who looks at a woman with lustful intent has already committed adultery with her in his heart." This includes pornography.

Scripture's call is practical. "Flee from sexual immorality." And, "So flee youthful passions and pursue righteousness, faith, love, and peace, along with those who call on the Lord from a pure heart." Notice both parts: we flee from what harms, and we pursue what is good, together with other believers.

Self-control is part of the fruit of the Spirit. It grows through wise choices about relationships, situations and devices, and through honest friendship. And remember: marriage does not by itself solve loneliness or temptation, and singleness does not mean a life without love.

12 · Part 4: Grace, Help and Safety

The fourth part: Grace, Help and Safety.

After listing many sins, including sexual sins, Paul writes, "And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God." No one's past is beyond God's grace.

Jesus welcomed and forgave a woman known in her town as a sinner. While others judged her, he said, "Your faith has saved you; go in peace." And, "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins."

Grace also means that people who have been abused, assaulted or pressured are not guilty for what was done to them. "The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit." Those who are struggling or have been harmed deserve help, safety and care, not shame. Seeking help is not a lack of faith. If any part of this lesson has touched something painful, please read the note on the lesson page.

13 · Marta's story

Let me tell you about Marta. Her story is a composite, drawn from the experiences of many people.

Marta is a graphic designer in Warsaw, Poland. She is thirty-one and single. At every family gathering, her aunts ask when she will marry. Last year she began dating a colleague who was kind at first, but soon pressured her to move in with him. When she said no, he called her old-fashioned and stopped speaking to her. Marta ended the relationship, but she felt lonely, embarrassed and unsure whether she had done the right thing. She also carried regret about a relationship in her early twenties.

She talked with her small group leader, Ewa, who read 1 Corinthians 6 and 7 with her. "Your body belongs to the Lord, and so does your future," Ewa said. "You are not less of a person because you are single, and you were right to say no to pressure. As for the past, 1 Corinthians 6:11 is for you too: you have been washed."

Ewa also noticed that Marta was isolating herself, and invited her to a church community group and to regular Sunday lunches. In time, after the church's usual preparation and safeguarding process, Marta began helping with an adult Bible study.

Marta is still single, and still finds family gatherings difficult, but she has learned to answer her aunts kindly and briefly. She has deeper friendships than before, serves gladly, and remains open to marriage if God leads her to a faithful husband. Many single Christians face pressure from culture, family and their own desires, and need to hear that they are wholly God's and fully part of his family.

14 · What this lesson is not saying

Here are six common mistakes.

"The body is shameful or unspiritual." No. God made it good, bought it and will raise it.

"Single people are incomplete." No. A single believer is complete in Christ and fully part of God's family.

"Marriage solves loneliness or temptation." No. Marriage is a gift, not a cure.

"A spouse is entitled to intimacy." No. Intimacy in marriage is always freely and mutually agreed.

"My past puts me beyond God's grace." No. "And such were some of you. But you were washed."

And "victims of abuse or assault share the blame." No. The person harmed is never at fault.

16 · Remember

Let us bring it together. Our bodies belong to the Lord; single or married, we are wholly his; we live with sexual holiness and self-control; and his grace, help and safety are for everyone.

Bought with a price: the body is good, belongs to the Lord and will be raised, so glorify God in your body. Wholly his, single or married: God honours both, marriage is a lifelong covenant, and intimacy is always freely agreed. Holy and self-controlled: flee sexual immorality, including in the heart and eyes, and pursue what is good with others. And grace, help and safety: no past is beyond grace, and those harmed are not to blame and deserve help and safety.

17 · Prayer

Let us pray. Lord, thank you that you made my body and called it good, and that you bought me with the price of your Son. I am not my own; I am yours. Help me to glorify you in my body, single or married. Where I have sinned, forgive and cleanse me. Where I have been hurt, meet me with your comfort and healing. Lead me in holiness and in safety, with your people around me. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Pause and seek help

Sexual abuse, assault and coercion are never the fault of the person harmed, whoever did it, including a spouse or a church leader; being married gives no one the right to force or pressure sex, and sharing or threatening to share intimate images without consent is also abuse (Support Page: I am in danger or being abused). Any concern about a child or vulnerable adult must be reported promptly according to local law and safeguarding procedure. If a church leader makes sexual comments or advances towards someone in their care, asks for secrecy, or uses God or ministry to create pressure, this is an abuse of power, and help should be sought outside that leader's circle (Support Page: I am being controlled through prophecy, spiritual language, money or church authority). Compulsive patterns with pornography or sexual behaviour, or concerns about your body, weight or eating that affect your health, often need specialist help as well as spiritual care; seeking help is not a lack of faith.

Optional

Go deeper

Divorce and remarriage. Some Christians believe divorce is permitted only for sexual unfaithfulness or desertion by an unbelieving spouse, with remarriage possible in those cases (they often point to Matthew 19:9 and 1 Corinthians 7:15). Others believe that serious, persistent breaking of the marriage covenant, such as abuse or severe neglect, can also be grounds for divorce (they often point to Exodus 21:10–11 and Malachi 2:14–16). Others believe remarriage after divorce is not permitted while a former spouse is living (they often point to Mark 10:11–12 and Romans 7:2–3). Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that God values faithful marriage and that divorced and remarried people belong fully in the church. No one is ever required to remain in danger: separating for safety is right, whatever view of divorce a church holds, and it is a different question from deciding whether a marriage has ended.

Roles in marriage. Some Christians believe that husbands are called to loving, servant leadership, and wives to respect and support that leadership (they often point to Ephesians 5:22–33, Colossians 3:18–19 and 1 Peter 3:1–7). Others believe that husbands and wives share leadership equally in mutual submission (they often point to Ephesians 5:21 and 1 Corinthians 7:3–5). Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that husbands and wives are equal in dignity before God and that each is called to sacrificial love. No understanding of marriage roles permits coercion, intimidation, violence, sexual entitlement or the silencing of abuse. If it would help, ask a trustworthy church leader how your church approaches these matters, and compare what you hear with Scripture.
