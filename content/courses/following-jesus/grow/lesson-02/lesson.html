Renewing Your Mind With Truth

My mind is full of noise: my phone, the news, other people's opinions and my own worries. How does God change the way I think?

About 16 minutesRomans 12:1–2 and Philippians 4:8My Growth: Grow by grace

In this lesson

You will learn

  • How renewal begins with God's mercy, from Romans 12:1–2
  • What Paul means by "be transformed", and who does the transforming
  • The four parts: Offered to God, Not Conformed, Tested by Truth, Filled With Good
  • How to test a belief, bring a feeling to God, and treat an unwanted thought
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · A mind full of noise

Welcome to Lesson 2 of Grow. In Lesson 1, you saw that we walk by the Spirit, and that the Spirit changes us over time through ordinary, repeated means. In Establish, you learned to ask four questions of a Bible passage. Now we are going one step further. One of the main places God changes us is in the way we think.

Most of us carry a constant stream of input: messages, videos, headlines, advertisements, other people's opinions, and our own repeating worries. Much of it we never chose. Yet over time it shapes what we value, what we fear and what we believe about ourselves.

So here is our question: my mind is full of noise. My phone, the news, other people's opinions and my own worries. How does God change the way I think?

2 · Be transformed

Our first passage is Romans chapter 12, verses 1 and 2: "I appeal to you therefore, brothers, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God, which is your spiritual worship. Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind, that by testing you may discern what is the will of God, what is good and acceptable and perfect."

Let us use the first question from Establish: what does it say? Notice where Paul begins. Not with pressure, but with "the mercies of God". And notice the two directions in verse 2: do not be conformed, but be transformed.

3 · Think about these things

Our second passage is Philippians chapter 4, verse 8: "Finally, brothers, whatever is true, whatever is honorable, whatever is just, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely, whatever is commendable, if there is any excellence, if there is anything worthy of praise, think about these things."

Paul gives us a list, and then a simple command: think about these things. What we give our attention to matters.

4 · Transformed = metamorphoō

Our Greek word today is the one translated "be transformed" in Romans 12:2: metamorphoō. It means to be changed in form.

Paul uses the same word in 2 Corinthians 3:18, which we read in Lesson 1: "we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image."

And notice the form Paul uses in Romans 12. The command is passive: "be transformed". We do not manufacture Christian transformation by willpower. As Paul says in 2 Corinthians 3:18, the Lord is at work changing his people, as they respond to his mercy and learn to think and live differently.

5 · Therefore

Now the background. Romans 12 begins with the word "therefore". That word connects these verses to everything Paul has written before. For eleven chapters he has described God's mercy in Christ, for Jews and Gentiles alike. Only then does he say, "I appeal to you therefore."

Now use our second question: what did it mean to the people who first heard it? The first readers lived in Rome, surrounded by temples and familiar with the language and practice of sacrifice. Paul calls believers to present themselves to God as a living sacrifice. And he goes straight on to show what a renewed mind looks like: humility, love, service and peace with others. A renewed mind is not only private. It changes how we treat people.

6 · Written from prison

Paul wrote Philippians from prison. Just before our verse, he urged believers not to be anxious about anything, "but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God." And he promised that "the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus."

So Paul is not telling people to ignore real problems or to think only happy thoughts. He is calling them to fix their attention on what is true and good. And the peace he promises comes from "the God of peace", not from positive thinking alone.

7 · The short answer

So, how does God change the way we think? Here is the short answer.

Renewing the mind begins with God's mercy. We offer ourselves, including our thinking, to God. We notice what is shaping us. We test thoughts and messages by his truth. And we fill our minds with what is good.

That gives us four parts: Offered to God, Not Conformed, Tested by Truth and Filled With Good.

8 · Part 1: Offered to God

The first part: Offered to God.

Renewing the mind begins with mercy, not pressure. Paul has spent eleven chapters describing God's grace before he says, "by the mercies of God, present your bodies as a living sacrifice."

Notice that he says "bodies". God wants the whole person: what we do, where we go, what we watch, and how we think. Paul calls this "spiritual worship". Offering our minds to God is not a grim duty. It is a grateful response to a God who has already given us everything in Christ.

9 · Part 2: Not Conformed

The second part: Not Conformed. Paul says, "Do not be conformed to this world." Every culture, including church culture, presses our thinking into its shape. It tells us what to value, what to fear, what to desire and what to believe about ourselves.

Today much of that pressure comes through screens: algorithms that learn what holds our attention, advertising that makes us discontent, and constant comparison with other people's lives. Many people also carry messages from their past, such as "You are a failure" or "You will never be enough."

But not everything in human culture is evil. People made in God's image can produce things that are beautiful, useful and true, even while every culture also contains what sin has distorted. Christians learn to recognise both. Christians also differ on how much to engage with popular culture, and you will find that question under Go deeper on the lesson page.

The first step is simply noticing what is shaping us. "Keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life."

10 · Part 3: Tested by Truth

The third part: Tested by Truth. Paul says, "be transformed by the renewal of your mind, that by testing you may discern what is the will of God." Jesus said, "If you abide in my word, you are truly my disciples, and you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free." And he prayed, "Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth."

Paul also speaks of taking "every thought captive to obey Christ". In context, he is speaking first about "arguments and every lofty opinion raised against the knowledge of God". This verse is not a call to monitor, confess or fight every unwanted thought. We apply it by testing beliefs and messages against Christ.

11 · What kind of thought is this?

A simple approach is first to ask what kind of experience this is.

A belief or message, like "I am worthless", or "God has given up on me", or a claim in the news, can be tested for truth against Scripture.

A feeling, like sadness, fear or anger, is not true or false. Bring it honestly to God: "Search me, O God, and know my heart! Try me and know my thoughts!"

And an unwanted intrusive thought is not a confession or a hidden desire. It does not need to be argued with or confessed. If thoughts like these are persistent or distressing, please talk to someone you trust; you will find more on the lesson page.

The same testing applies to news, rumours and online claims. "The simple believes everything, but the prudent gives thought to his steps." Before you believe or share a claim, ask: Who says this? Is it true? What does the other side say? Paul's rule is simple: "test everything; hold fast what is good."

12 · Part 4: Filled With Good

The fourth part: Filled With Good. Renewal is not only removing harmful input. It is filling the mind with what is good. "Think about these things."

Scripture read, memorised and meditated on shapes us over time. The person who meditates on God's law "day and night" is "like a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in its season." "I have stored up your word in my heart, that I might not sin against you."

God also uses worship, wise friends, creation, good books and honest prayer. "Set your minds on things that are above", Paul says, because our life is now "hidden with Christ in God." That does not mean ignoring our responsibilities on earth. It means a Christ-centred mind that shapes our character, our relationships and our work. Christians practise meditation in different ways, and you will find that under Go deeper too.

Small, steady habits work better than dramatic resolutions. A psalm before sleep, one verse memorised each week, or a walk without a screen can, over time, make room for God's truth.

13 · Jonas's story

Let me tell you about Jonas. His story is a composite, drawn from the experiences of many people.

Jonas is a logistics apprentice in Hamburg, Germany. He came to faith at university and reads his Bible most mornings. But every night he scrolls for hours: angry videos, news alerts, and friends' photos of holidays and new cars. He falls asleep late, wakes tired, and carries a repeating thought: "You are falling behind. You will never amount to anything."

His home group leader, Katrin, read Romans 12 and Philippians 4 with him. "Let us examine that message," she said. "Is it true? What does God say about you?" Together they wrote down a few truths from Scripture that Jonas could return to, such as Romans 8:1, "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus", and Ephesians 2:10, "For we are his workmanship."

Jonas decided to charge his phone outside his bedroom, turn off most notifications, and unfollow accounts that left him angry or envious. He replaced some evening scrolling with a short psalm and a walk. Katrin also noticed how anxious he seemed, and gently suggested he talk to his doctor about his sleep and worry, which he did.

Change came gradually. Some nights Jonas still scrolls too long, and the old message still returns on hard days. But he now notices it sooner, names it, and turns to what God says. He sleeps better, and he meets a friend from church each week to read Scripture together. Many believers do not realise how much their daily input shapes their thoughts, until they begin to test it.

14 · What renewing the mind is not

Here are five common mistakes about renewing the mind.

"Renewing your mind means positive thinking." No. It means offering your mind to God and testing it by his truth.

"Enough Bible reading will cure mental illness." No. Scripture and prayer can go alongside medical and professional care.

"Every unwanted thought is a sin." No. Intrusive thoughts are not chosen convictions or confessions.

"A believer should never feel sad, anxious or afraid." No. Feelings are brought honestly to God, not denied.

And "Christians should withdraw from the world in fear." No. We guard our hearts, and we love the people around us.

15 · This week

This week, your assignment is to test one message by God's truth, and to make room for what is good.

First, read Romans 12:1 and 2, and Philippians 4:8. Use the four questions from Establish on Romans 12:1 and 2. Second, write one short line for each of the four parts, in your own words. You can use the book to help.

16 · Remember

Let us bring it together. In response to God's mercy, we offer him our minds, notice what shapes us, test it by his truth, and fill our minds with what is good.

Offered to God: renewal begins with mercy, and we give God our whole selves as worship. Not conformed: we notice what is pressing our thinking into its shape. Tested by truth: we test beliefs and claims by Scripture, we bring our feelings to God, and we do not treat intrusive thoughts as confessions. And filled with good: we fill our minds with Scripture, worship, wise friends and what is true and good.

17 · Prayer

Let us pray. Father, thank you for your mercies to us in Jesus. I offer you my whole self today, including my thinking. Show me what is shaping me, and teach me to test it by your truth. Fill my mind with whatever is true, good and worthy of praise. And guard my heart and my mind in Christ Jesus. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Pause and seek help

Unwanted or intrusive thoughts are not sins to confess, and having one does not mean you want it or will act on it. Persistent low mood, constant anxiety, panic or distressing thoughts can be signs of a health condition; talk with a trusted person and a doctor or counsellor, alongside prayer and Scripture. If you are thinking about ending your life or cannot keep yourself safe, seek urgent help now and stay with a safe person (Support Page: I may harm myself or someone else, or I cannot stay safe). If someone online is pressuring you, asking for images or demanding money, do not pay or send anything more; report it and tell a trusted person (Support Page: Safety and Support: Start Here).

Optional

Go deeper

How much to engage with the surrounding culture. Some Christians emphasise separation, avoiding much of popular media and entertainment to guard their minds (they often point to 2 Corinthians 6:17 and Psalm 101:3). Others emphasise wise engagement, understanding culture in order to live and speak faithfully within it (they often point to John 17:15–18, Acts 17:22–28 and 1 Corinthians 5:9–10). Many hold both together. Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that believers should guard their hearts, avoid what leads them into sin, and love the people around them.

Forms of Christian meditation. Many Christians meditate by slowly reading, repeating and memorising Scripture (they often point to Joshua 1:8 and Psalm 1:2). Some also practise quieter forms of prayer, such as slow, prayerful reading of a short passage, or resting silently before God (they often point to Psalm 62:1 and Psalm 131). Some are cautious about practices detached from Christian belief or that aim at an empty mind, and stress meditation that directs attention to God and his word (they often point to Colossians 3:16). Across historic Christian traditions, Christian meditation focuses on God and his word.
