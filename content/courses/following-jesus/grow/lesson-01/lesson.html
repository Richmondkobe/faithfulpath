Grace-Fuelled Growth

I know I am saved by grace. So why should I try hard to change? And what do I do when I keep failing?

About 18 minutesTitus 2:11–14 and Philippians 2:12–13My Growth: Grow by grace

In this lesson

You will learn

  • How grace not only forgives us but also trains us, from Titus 2:11–14
  • What it means to "work out" your salvation, and why God's work comes first (Philippians 2:12–13)
  • The four parts: Trained by Grace, Work It Out, Walk by the Spirit, Begin Again
  • How to begin again after failure, without fear and without pretending
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Why try hard to change?

Welcome to Grow, the third course in Following Jesus. In Begin, you learned to obey from grace. In Establish, you saw that every believer is being made holy. Now we are going one step further. In this course we ask a more personal question: how does Jesus reshape my character, my relationships, my decisions and my ordinary life?

And we begin where all Christian growth begins: with grace.

Many believers feel caught between two fears. If they try hard to change, they worry that they are trying to earn God's love. If they relax, they worry that nothing will ever change. And when they fail again, especially in the same area, they wonder whether their faith was ever real.

So here is our question: I know I am saved by grace. So why should I try hard to change? And what do I do when I keep failing?

2 · Grace that trains

Our first passage is Titus chapter 2, verses 11 to 14. Listen carefully: "For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people, training us to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions, and to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives in the present age, waiting for our blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ, who gave himself for us to redeem us from all lawlessness and to purify for himself a people for his own possession who are zealous for good works."

Let us use the first question from Establish: what does it say? Notice what grace does here. Grace brings salvation, and grace is also training us. Grace is not only something that happened to us once. It is something that teaches us now.

3 · God works in you

Our second passage is Philippians chapter 2, verses 12 and 13: "Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, so now, not only as in my presence but much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure."

Look at the little word "for". Work out your salvation, for it is God who works in you. Verse 13 is the foundation of verse 12. We work because God is already at work. He even gives us the desire, the will, as well as the strength to do what pleases him.

4 · Training = paideuō

Our Greek word today is the one translated "training" in Titus 2:12: paideuō. It can describe training, teaching or discipline, including the way a child is brought up.

The same word appears in Hebrews 12, where God disciplines the children he loves: "For the Lord disciplines the one he loves." So grace is not a strict examiner waiting for us to fail. Think instead of a loving parent, patiently training a child as they grow. And Hebrews says that God's discipline is "for our good."

Grace does not only forgive us. Grace trains us to grow.

5 · Titus in Crete

Now the background. Paul had left his co-worker Titus on the island of Crete. In chapter 1, verse 5, he says why: "so that you might put what remained into order, and appoint elders in every town." These were young churches, and many of the believers were new to the faith. Paul's letter teaches them how the gospel shapes everyday life.

Now use our second question: what did it mean to the people who first heard it? Notice the three times in our passage. Grace has appeared in the past, when Jesus first came. Grace is training us in the present age. And we are waiting for our blessed hope, Christ's return, in the future. Growth sits right in the middle of God's whole saving plan. It is not an extra for especially keen believers.

6 · Philippians: a letter from prison

Our second passage comes from a letter Paul wrote from prison to the church in Philippi, a church he loved deeply. That is why he calls them "my beloved".

Just before our verses, Paul describes the humility of Christ, who "emptied himself" and became obedient to the point of death. Then he says, "Therefore… work out your own salvation." In other words, live out the humble mind of Christ. And "your own salvation" is not only about each person on their own. Paul is also speaking to the whole church about how they live together.

So Paul is not telling believers to earn God's acceptance. He is telling people who already belong to Christ to take their growth seriously.

7 · The short answer

So, why should we try hard to change, and what do we do when we fail? Here is the short answer.

The grace that saves us also trains us. God works in us, so we work out what he has given. The Spirit changes us over time. And when we fail, grace meets us again.

That gives us four parts: Trained by Grace, Work It Out, Walk by the Spirit and Begin Again.

8 · Part 1: Trained by Grace

The first part: Trained by Grace.

We often think of grace only as forgiveness. It is that, gloriously. But Titus 2 says grace is also training us. Grace does not only cancel our guilt; it teaches us and changes us.

Paul puts both together in Ephesians 2: "For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works."

Notice the order. We are saved, not as a result of works, and we are created for good works. The order matters. Growth is a response to God's love, never a way to earn it. As you saw in Establish, good works are the fruit of salvation, not its price.

9 · Part 2: Work It Out

The second part: Work It Out.

Paul does not tell us to work for our salvation. He tells us to work out what God has already given. Think of a gift of land. You do not work to buy it; it is already yours. But you work the land, so that it bears fruit.

And "fear and trembling" does not mean anxious fear that God is waiting to reject you. It describes a humble, reverent seriousness before God, as we live out what he is working in us.

Our effort is real. Peter says, "make every effort to supplement your faith with virtue." Hebrews calls us to "run with endurance the race that is set before us, looking to Jesus." But God's work underneath our effort is always greater. Paul could say, "I worked harder than any of them, though it was not I, but the grace of God that is with me." Paul worked hard, and he knew that grace was doing the work. Both are true.

10 · Part 3: Walk by the Spirit

The third part: Walk by the Spirit.

Change does not come by willpower alone. Paul says, "Walk by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the flesh." And as we keep looking to Christ, the Spirit changes us: "we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another."

The Spirit often forms us through ordinary, repeated means: Scripture, read and obeyed; prayer and worship; the church, where believers encourage and correct one another; obedience in small things, day after day; and sometimes hardship. God "disciplines us for our good, that we may share his holiness." That does not mean every hardship is a punishment from God. It means God can use even hard seasons to shape us. And it never means you should remain in an unsafe or abusive situation.

And growth is usually slow and uneven, more like a plant than a light switch. You may not notice it day by day. But over months and years, the Spirit produces "love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control."

11 · Part 4: Begin Again

The fourth part: Begin Again.

Every believer still sins. John says, "If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves." But he goes on: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness." And "if anyone does sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous." God's mercies "never come to an end; they are new every morning."

Think of Peter. He denied Jesus three times. But Jesus had already prayed for him: "when you have turned again, strengthen your brothers." After the resurrection, Jesus restored Peter by a fire on the beach and gave him work to do: "Feed my sheep."

Failure is not the end of the Christian life. It is a place where grace meets us again. Beginning again means confessing honestly to God, receiving his forgiveness, making things right where we can, and taking the next small step, often with help.

Beginning again does not erase debts, repair trust immediately or remove the consequences of what we have done. But grace gives us the courage to face those responsibilities honestly.

12 · Christians differ

Christians are not all agreed on how our effort and our surrender work together.

Some Christians emphasise surrender and trust. We stop striving in our own strength and yield to Christ, who lives his life through us. They often point to Galatians 2:20, "It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me", and to Jesus' words, "apart from me you can do nothing."

Others emphasise disciplined effort empowered by grace. We actively put sin to death by the Spirit and train ourselves for godliness. They often point to Romans 8:13 and to 1 Timothy 4:7, "train yourself for godliness." Many Christians hold both together, as Philippians 2 does.

Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that growth depends on God's grace, and that believers are called to respond actively. You will find another question, about how to read Romans chapter 7, under Go deeper on the lesson page.

13 · Andrei's story

Let me tell you about Andrei. His story is a composite, drawn from the experiences of many people.

Andrei is a software developer in Cluj-Napoca, Romania. Before he came to Christ, he gambled online most nights and built up serious debt. After he became a Christian, he deleted the apps, told his wife, and went six months without gambling. Then, after a stressful week, he placed one bet, then several. The next morning he felt sick with shame. "I am a fake," he thought. "Real Christians do not do this." He stopped going to his home group.

His mentor, an older man named Mihai, invited him for coffee and read 1 John chapter 1 with him. "You are not the first believer to fall," Mihai said. "Peter fell, and Jesus restored him. Confess it to God and receive his forgiveness. Then let us take sensible steps together."

Andrei confessed to God and gave his wife a truthful account of the gambling and what it had done to their finances. She was free to take time, seek her own support and set financial boundaries. She was not responsible for monitoring Andrei or keeping him from gambling. Andrei contacted a gambling support service, met a debt adviser, installed blocking tools and joined a recovery group. "Grace does not mean you will never struggle again," Mihai said. "Grace means you do not have to struggle alone."

There was no dramatic victory. Andrei has gone several months without gambling, though the temptation still comes on hard days. He is back in his home group, and each morning he prays Titus 2:11 and 12, asking God's grace to train him. Many believers fear that one fall proves their faith was never real. Often a fall is where they learn to depend on grace, and on wise help.

14 · What growth is not

Here are five common mistakes about growth.

"Growing earns God's love, and failing loses it." No. Growth is a response to God's love, never its price.

"Grace means effort does not matter." No. Grace trains us, and we work it out.

"I can change by willpower alone." No. We walk by the Spirit, through the ordinary means God gives.

"Real Christians do not fall." No. Every believer still sins, and confession and forgiveness are for believers.

And "Needing a doctor, counsellor or recovery group shows weak faith." No. Some struggles need wise help as well as prayer. Seeking help is wise, not faithless.

15 · This week

This week, your assignment is to let God's grace train you, one small step at a time.

First, read Titus 2:11 to 14 and Philippians 2:12 and 13. Second, write one short line for each of the four parts, in your own words. You can use the book to help.

Third, think of one area where you would like to grow, and one small, realistic step you could take with God's help this week. Just one. Please do not make a long list of your failures. This is about grace, not about a scorecard.

Fourth, each morning, pray Titus 2:11 and 12 for yourself: ask God's grace to train you today.

And fifth, follow the seven-day reading plan below this lesson. If you miss a day, simply begin again the next day. That too is grace. This assignment belongs to the first My Growth area: grow by grace.

16 · Remember

Let us bring it together. The grace that saves us also trains us. We work out what God works in. The Spirit changes us over time. And when we fall, grace meets us again.

Trained by grace: grace forgives and teaches, and growth responds to God's love; it never earns it. Work it out: our effort is real, and God's work beneath it is greater. Walk by the Spirit: the Spirit changes us through ordinary means, and growth is slow and uneven. And begin again: confess, receive forgiveness, make things right where you can, and take the next small step.

17 · Prayer

Let us pray. Father, thank you that your grace has appeared in Jesus, bringing salvation. Let your grace train me today. Thank you that you are at work in me, giving me both the will and the strength to do what pleases you. Holy Spirit, keep changing me to be more like Jesus, slowly and surely. And when I fall, help me not to hide from you, but to come back to you quickly and begin again. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Pause and seek help

Some habits keep returning despite sincere repentance, such as addiction to alcohol, drugs or gambling, or compulsive sexual behaviour. They often need a doctor, counsellor or recovery group as well as prayer, and seeking that help is not a lack of faith (Life Guide: Addiction and Controlling Habits). Healthy accountability is voluntary and respectful; if it has become control, see the Support Page "I am being controlled through prophecy, spiritual language, money or church authority". Where a failure has harmed someone else, God's forgiveness does not remove the responsibility to make things right and to protect others; if someone is at risk, safety comes first.

Optional

Go deeper

How effort and surrender work together. Some Christians emphasise surrender and trust: we stop striving in our own strength and yield to Christ, who lives his life through us (they often point to Galatians 2:20 and John 15:4–5). Others emphasise disciplined effort empowered by grace: we actively put sin to death and train ourselves for godliness (they often point to Romans 8:13, 1 Timothy 4:7–8 and 2 Peter 1:5–7). Many hold both together, as Philippians 2:12–13 does. Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that growth depends on God's grace and that believers are called to respond actively.

How to read Romans 7. In Romans 7:14–25, Paul describes doing what he hates. Some Christians read this as the ongoing struggle of a believer who still lives with sin (they often point to 7:22, "I delight in the law of God," and 7:25). Others read it as Paul describing life under the law without the Spirit's power, which Romans 8 answers (they often point to 7:14, "sold under sin," and 8:2–4). Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that Romans 8 is the believer's hope: "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" (8:1), and the Spirit gives real power to change.

If you are still exploring. You may wonder whether Christians are simply people trying hard to be good. This lesson says something different: Christian change begins with a gift, not an effort, and failure does not end it.
