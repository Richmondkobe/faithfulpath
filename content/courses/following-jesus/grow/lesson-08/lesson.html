Work, Money, Generosity and Everyday Faithfulness

Most of my week is spent working and worrying about money. Does God care about my job and my bank account, or only about church?

About 16 minutesColossians 3:23–24 and 1 Timothy 6:6–10, 17–19My Growth: Grow in everyday life

In this lesson

You will learn

  • How all honest work can be done "as for the Lord", from Colossians 3:23–24
  • What Paul means by "contentment" in 1 Timothy 6:6, and why the love of money is so dangerous
  • The four parts: Work as for the Lord, Honest in All Things, Content, Not Greedy, Generous and Faithful
  • How to be generous realistically, without debt or neglecting what your household needs
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Monday to Saturday

Welcome to Lesson 8 of Grow. In Establish, you saw that we live now under Christ's rule, and that nothing done for him is wasted. You also saw that every member builds up the church, including through giving. Now we bring Christ's lordship into the workplace and the wallet.

Most of our waking hours are spent working: in jobs, businesses, fields, homes, classrooms and care. And much of our worry is about money: rent, food, school fees, debt and the future. If following Jesus only touched Sunday, it would miss most of our lives.

So here is our question: most of my week is spent working and worrying about money. Does God care about my job and my bank account, or only about church?

2 · As for the Lord

Our first passage is Colossians chapter 3, verses 23 and 24: "Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men, knowing that from the Lord you will receive the inheritance as your reward. You are serving the Lord Christ."

Use the first question: what does it say? Notice the word "whatever". And notice who is really being served: "the Lord Christ".

3 · Godliness with contentment

Our second passage is 1 Timothy chapter 6: "But godliness with contentment is great gain, for we brought nothing into the world, and we cannot take anything out of the world… For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evils."

And to those who are rich, Paul says they are "to do good, to be rich in good works, to be generous and ready to share." Notice that the problem is not money itself, but the love of money.

4 · Contentment = autarkeia

Our Greek word today is autarkeia, translated "contentment". It can carry the idea of having enough, or being content with what one has.

Self-sufficiency was valued by some thinkers in Paul's world. But Paul's contentment is not proud independence. In Philippians 4 he says, "I have learned in whatever situation I am to be content," and then, "I can do all things through him who strengthens me." Christian contentment rests on Christ.

5 · Work seen by the Lord

Now the background. Paul first wrote these words to enslaved people, in a society where slavery was an established part of social and economic life. He reminds enslaved believers that they belong to Christ. And he tells masters that they too are accountable to "a Master in heaven". Elsewhere, Paul encourages enslaved people to gain their freedom if they can.

These passages must never be used to justify slavery, forced labour, abusive employment, or demands that exploited workers submit silently. Now use the second question: what did it mean to the people who first heard it? Even work done in hard circumstances can be offered to Christ, who sees the faithfulness of his people. That never makes exploitation acceptable.

6 · Godliness is not a means of gain

Timothy was in Ephesus, a wealthy trading city. Paul warns him against teachers who imagined "that godliness is a means of gain". This is a direct warning against any teaching that faith is a way to get rich.

Notice that Paul speaks to two groups: those who want to be rich, and those who already are. Both are called to trust God rather than wealth, and the rich are called to generosity.

7 · The short answer

So, does God care about my job and my bank account? Here is the short answer.

Work is part of God's good design, and all honest work can be done for the Lord. Believers work with integrity and refuse corruption. They learn contentment in a money-focused world. And they give generously and manage what God entrusts to them faithfully.

That gives us four parts: Work as for the Lord; Honest in All Things; Content, Not Greedy; and Generous and Faithful.

8 · Part 1: Work as for the Lord

The first part: Work as for the Lord.

Work was part of God's good design before sin entered the world. God placed the first humans in the garden "to work it and keep it". Work became harder after the Fall, but it is not a curse in itself.

Paid and unpaid work, from farming and factory shifts to caring for family and studying, can honour God. "You are serving the Lord Christ."

Caring for the earth God made is part of this calling, because "the earth is the LORD's and the fullness thereof." Humanity's rule over creation is accountable stewardship under the God who owns the earth. It is not permission to waste, destroy or exploit creation, without regard for our neighbours and for those who come after us.

9 · Rest, and your worth

God built rhythms of work and rest into Israel's life, commanding the Sabbath: "Six days you shall labor, and do all your work, but the seventh day is a Sabbath to the LORD your God." Rest is a gift, not laziness. Christians differ about how to keep a day of rest today, and you will find that under Go deeper.

But all Christians can agree on this: work must never become our identity or our god. People who are unemployed, retired, sick or disabled are no less valuable to God.

10 · Part 2: Honest in All Things

The second part: Honest in All Things.

"A false balance is an abomination to the LORD, but a just weight is his delight." God cares about honest scales, honest records and honest dealings. When tax collectors asked John the Baptist what to do, he said, "Collect no more than you are authorized to do." To soldiers he said, "Do not extort money from anyone by threats or by false accusation."

So believers refuse bribes, because "a bribe blinds the clear-sighted". They do not cheat employers, customers or governments. They pay what they owe: "Pay to all what is owed to them." And those with authority over workers are called to act "justly and fairly". Scripture condemns employers who hold back wages.

Integrity may cost something. But it reflects the character of God, and it is a quiet witness where corruption is often normal. Where speaking up could be dangerous, choosing a careful, safe way to act with integrity is not cowardice.

11 · Part 3: Content, Not Greedy

The third part: Content, Not Greedy.

Jesus warned, "Be on your guard against all covetousness, for one's life does not consist in the abundance of his possessions." And, "You cannot serve God and money."

Contentment is learned, and it rests on God's presence: "Keep your life free from love of money, and be content with what you have, for he has said, 'I will never leave you nor forsake you.'"

But contentment does not mean ignoring real needs or accepting injustice. It is not a command to stay silent about stolen wages, unsafe work, discrimination or exploitation. Employers and church leaders must never use teaching about contentment to stop workers from seeking fair treatment.

Practical wisdom helps. Budgeting, saving where possible, and being cautious about unnecessary debt can be part of that wisdom. Proverbs observes, "the borrower is the slave of the lender." Debt can restrict our freedom, although sometimes people fall into debt through hardship rather than irresponsible choices.

12 · Part 4: Generous and Faithful

The fourth part: Generous and Faithful.

Giving is a joyful response to God's generosity. "Each one must give as he has decided in his heart, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver."

A simple generosity plan might include regular giving to your local church and to people in need, in proportion to what you have, alongside providing for your own household. But giving must never be funded by new debt, or by neglecting essential food, housing, medicine, safety, child support, taxes or agreed debt repayments. In a financial crisis, the faithful step may be to receive help, steady the household, and be generous in other ways, such as time, skills and hospitality.

Christians differ on how to balance saving and planning with generous giving, and you will find that under Go deeper. And faithfulness begins small: "One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much."

13 · Josh's story

Let me tell you about Josh. His story is a composite, drawn from the experiences of many people.

Josh is a warehouse supervisor in Winnipeg, Canada. He and his wife, Kayla, have two young children and growing credit-card debt from car repairs, online shopping and a holiday they could not afford. One week, Josh's manager asked him to record extra hours for a friend who had not worked them. "Everyone does it," the manager said. Josh felt trapped: he needed the job, and he was already anxious about money.

His home group studied Colossians 3 and 1 Timothy 6 that week. Josh talked privately with an older member, Gordon, who said, "Integrity matters, but you can be wise and respectful about how you say no." Josh told his manager calmly that he could not sign for hours that were not worked, and offered to help the friend find extra shifts instead. The manager was annoyed, but did not press further. Josh kept a factual note of what he had been asked to do, checked his workplace's reporting policy, and sought confidential advice about his rights before considering any further step.

Gordon also suggested that Josh and Kayla meet a free, accountable credit counselling service. Together they made a budget and a plan to repay their debt. They worked out what they could safely afford while repaying it. For the time being, some of their generosity was through time and practical help; when their budget allowed, they also set aside a small amount to give.

There was no sudden wealth. The debt is shrinking slowly. Josh and Kayla still feel the pull to spend, but they now talk about purchases together, and give thanks for what they have. Integrity, contentment and generosity usually grow through small, practical decisions in ordinary weeks.

14 · What this lesson is not saying

Here are six common mistakes.

"Only church work really matters to God." No. All honest work can be done for the Lord.

"Faith and giving will make you rich or healthy." No. Paul warns against those who think godliness is a means of gain.

"Being poor, unemployed or in debt means God is displeased with you." No. Your value to God does not depend on money or work.

"Contentment means accepting unfair wages or unsafe work." No. Contentment is not silence about injustice.

"Generosity means giving more than you can afford." No. Giving is cheerful, realistic, and never funded by neglecting essentials.

And "one economic or political system is the Christian one." No. Scripture gives principles of honesty, justice and generosity, not a party programme.

15 · This week

This week, your assignment is to offer your work to God, and be generous in a way that is safe.

First, read Colossians 3:23 and 24, and 1 Timothy 6:6 to 10 and 17 to 19. Second, write one short line for each of the four parts, in your own words. You can use the book to help.

Third, each day, offer your work or daily tasks to God in a short prayer.

Fourth, if you can give safely, make a simple and realistic generosity plan: where you will give, how often, and an amount or proportion you can keep, even if it is small. If money is not available at present, choose another way to be generous, such as time, encouragement, hospitality, prayer or sharing a skill. If you are in serious debt or hardship, put stability and good advice first. Keep your plan private; no one should ask you about amounts.

And fifth, follow the seven-day reading plan below this lesson. If you miss a day, simply begin again the next day. This assignment belongs to the My Growth area called grow in everyday life.

16 · Remember

Let us bring it together. We work as for the Lord, stay honest in all things, learn contentment, and give generously and faithfully.

Work as for the Lord: all honest work, paid or unpaid, can serve Christ; care for creation; rest is a gift; and your worth is not your work. Honest in all things: no cheating, no bribes, fair wages, and pay what you owe. Content, not greedy: trust God's care, plan wisely, avoid unnecessary debt, and remember that contentment is not silence about injustice. And generous and faithful: give cheerfully and realistically, never at the cost of essentials, and be faithful in little things.

17 · Prayer

Let us pray. Lord Christ, I offer you my work today, seen or unseen, paid or unpaid. Keep me honest in all things. Teach me to be content in you, and free me from the love of money and from fear about tomorrow. Make me generous and faithful with what you have entrusted to me, and wise in caring for those who depend on me. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Pause and seek help

If you face serious debt, threats from lenders or gambling to escape debt, seek help from a trustworthy, accountable debt-advice service before giving, and do not borrow from unlicensed lenders; if a scheme promises guaranteed or unusually high returns, pressures you to act quickly or asks for secrecy, pause and seek independent advice, and do not send more money to recover what you have lost. Financial control can be abuse: if someone takes your wages or documents, stops you working, forces you to take loans, or you cannot leave a job, seek specialist help and do not confront the suspected exploiter yourself (Support Page: I am in danger or being abused). If a church or ministry promises blessing in return for money or pressures you to give, see the Support Page: I am being controlled through prophecy, spiritual language, money or church authority. Where refusing a bribe or reporting corruption could be dangerous, seek wise counsel and consider your and your family's safety first.

Optional

Go deeper

Saving and planning, and generous giving. Some Christians emphasise wise saving, insurance and planning for the future as faithful stewardship (they often point to Proverbs 6:6–8, Proverbs 21:20 and 1 Timothy 5:8). Others emphasise giving generously and living simply, warning against storing up wealth (they often point to Luke 12:16–21, Luke 12:33 and Matthew 6:19–21). Many hold both together. Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that money must not become our security or our master, and that believers should care for their dependants and for the poor.

How to keep a day of rest. Some Christians keep Saturday as the seventh-day Sabbath (they often point to Exodus 20:8–11 and Mark 2:27). Many gather on Sunday as the Lord's Day and set it apart for worship and rest (they often point to Acts 20:7 and Revelation 1:10). Others keep a regular rhythm of worship and rest without treating one day as binding (they often point to Romans 14:5–6 and Colossians 2:16–17). Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that people are not machines, that worship matters, and that rest is a gift, not laziness. Essential care, emergency work and unavoidable shift work should never become a cause of shame. If it would help, ask a trustworthy church leader how your church approaches these matters, and compare what you hear with Scripture.
