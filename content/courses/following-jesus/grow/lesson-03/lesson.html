Developing the Character and Fruit of Christ

I have been a Christian for a while, but I still lose my temper, worry and think mainly about myself. How does God actually change my character?

You will learn

  • What God is making us into, from Galatians 5:16–25
  • Why Paul speaks of the "fruit" of the Spirit, and how it grows
  • The four parts: Like Jesus, The Spirit's Fruit, Make Every Effort, Stand Firm
  • How to make a simple one-focus plan for the situations that test you
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Will I ever change?

Welcome to Lesson 3 of Grow. In Establish, you met the fruit of character that the Holy Spirit grows in every believer. In Lesson 1, you saw that we walk by the Spirit, and in Lesson 2, that God renews our minds. Now we are going one step further, and looking at character itself.

Many believers are disappointed with themselves. They trust Christ, they pray and read the Bible, and yet the same impatience, worry or selfishness keeps showing up, often with the people closest to them. They wonder whether real change is possible.

So here is our question: I have been a Christian for a while, but I still lose my temper, worry and think mainly about myself. How does God actually change my character?

2 · The fruit of the Spirit

Our first passage is Galatians chapter 5, verses 16 to 25. Here is the heart of it, verses 22 and 23: "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control; against such things there is no law."

And verse 25 sums it up: "If we live by the Spirit, let us also keep in step with the Spirit."

Let us use the first question from Establish: what does it say? Notice who produces this fruit. It is called the fruit of the Spirit.

3 · Make every effort

Our second passage is 2 Peter chapter 1. Peter begins with what God has already given: "His divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness." Only then does he call for effort.

"For this very reason, make every effort to supplement your faith with virtue, and virtue with knowledge, and knowledge with self-control, and self-control with steadfastness, and steadfastness with godliness, and godliness with brotherly affection, and brotherly affection with love."

Notice the order again. First the gift, then the effort. And notice that the list builds towards love.

4 · Fruit = karpos

Our Greek word today is the one translated "fruit" in Galatians 5:22: karpos. It is the ordinary word for fruit, what a plant produces.

Notice that Paul does not say "the fruits of the Spirit". He says "the fruit of the Spirit", in the singular. And he sets it against the "works of the flesh", in the plural.

Paul presents these qualities together, not as nine separate achievements for us to collect, but as the fruit the one Spirit produces in a life. And fruit is grown, not manufactured.

5 · The churches in Galatia

Now the background. Paul was writing to churches in Galatia. Some teachers were pressuring the Galatian believers to add circumcision and observance of the law to their life in Christ. Paul points them instead to life by the Spirit, who produces real change.

Now use our second question: what did it mean to the people who first heard it? Just before our verses, Paul lists the works of the flesh, including "enmity, strife, jealousy, fits of anger, rivalries". Many of those are about how people treat one another. Then he describes the fruit of the Spirit. It describes a whole life shaped by the Spirit, especially in how we treat others. It is not a checklist for scoring ourselves.

6 · Built on what God has given

Peter's letter has the same order. Everything begins with what God has already given us in Christ. The chain of qualities is built on that foundation, and it ends in love.

And verse 8 gives the purpose: these qualities keep believers from being "ineffective or unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ." As in Lesson 1, effort is a grateful response to grace, not a way of earning it.

7 · The short answer

So, how does God actually change our character? Here is the short answer.

God is making his children like Jesus. The Holy Spirit grows his fruit in us. We respond by making every effort in the situations that test us. And in Christ's strength, we stand firm against temptation and evil, without fear.

That gives us four parts: Like Jesus, The Spirit's Fruit, Make Every Effort and Stand Firm.

8 · Part 1: Like Jesus

The first part: Like Jesus.

God's purpose is that his children be "conformed to the image of his Son." The goal of Christian growth is not a better reputation or a stronger personality. It is the character of Jesus.

What is he like? Jesus described himself as "gentle and lowly in heart." On the night before he died, he washed his disciples' feet and said, "I have given you an example, that you also should do just as I have done to you."

Character is who we are in ordinary moments, especially when no one is watching: in traffic, at the end of a long day, with our family. And Christlikeness is not simply being agreeable. Jesus was gentle and humble, but he was also courageous, truthful and willing to confront injustice. Gentleness is strength governed by love, not silence in the presence of wrongdoing.

9 · Part 2: The Spirit's Fruit

The second part: The Spirit's Fruit.

These Christlike qualities belong together in a life increasingly shaped by the Spirit. And fruit is different from personality. A quiet person and a lively person can both be patient and kind. God does not make everyone the same. He makes each of us more like Jesus.

Fruit grows slowly, especially as we learn obedience in relationships and difficult circumstances. Scripture says God can use trials this way: "suffering produces endurance, and endurance produces character, and character produces hope." That does not mean every hardship was sent as a lesson, or that harmful situations should simply be endured.

And as you saw in Establish, fruit is a reliable sign of maturity, but never an anxious test of whether you are saved.

10 · Part 3: Make Every Effort

The third part: Make Every Effort. If God grows the fruit, what do we do? Peter answers: "make every effort." Grace gives the power. Believers respond with real effort.

One practical way is to put off an old pattern and put on its Christlike opposite. Paul tells believers "to put off your old self" and "to put on the new self, created after the likeness of God in true righteousness and holiness." Then he makes it specific. Put off lying, and speak the truth. Put off stealing, and work honestly and share with those in need. Put off harmful talk, and speak what builds others up.

In Colossians, Paul lists what to put on: "compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience", and "above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony."

11 · Where growth happens

It helps to be specific. Rather than resolving, "I will be more patient", it is better to choose one situation where patience is tested, one small practice, such as a pause and a short prayer before responding, and one trusted person to pray with you.

Character often grows in exactly the situations that test it. The traffic jam, the difficult colleague and the tired evening at home are not interruptions to spiritual growth. They are where it happens.

12 · Part 4: Stand Firm

The fourth part: Stand Firm. Growth involves a struggle. "The desires of the flesh are against the Spirit." And believers also face real spiritual opposition: "we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against… the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly places."

But we do not fight from fear. Christ has already won. On the cross, "he disarmed the rulers and authorities and put them to open shame." And "he who is in you is greater than he who is in the world."

So Paul says, "Be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might. Put on the whole armor of God, that you may be able to stand." The armour is truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, the word of God and prayer. Notice what it is made of: gospel truth, Christ-shaped character and ordinary dependence on God, not secret formulas or special techniques.

Jesus himself resisted temptation by trusting and quoting Scripture: "It is written." James says, "Resist the devil, and he will flee from you." And God "will not let you be tempted beyond your ability, but with the temptation he will also provide the way of escape."

Not every struggle is caused by evil spirits. Many come from our own desires, from tiredness or illness, or from other people. Believers stay alert, but they do not live in fear. Christians differ in how they approach spiritual warfare, and you will find that under Go deeper on the lesson page.

13 · Liam's story

Let me tell you about Liam. His story is a composite, drawn from the experiences of many people.

Liam drives a city bus in Brisbane, Australia. He has followed Jesus for three years, but his temper is short. He snaps at passengers who are slow to pay, and at home he often raises his voice at his teenage son over small things. One evening, after shouting about a pair of shoes left by the door, he saw his son's face and felt ashamed. Later that night a familiar thought came: "You will never change. You are a fake Christian."

At his men's group, Liam asked an older friend, Peter, for help. They read Galatians 5 together. "Let us test that thought," Peter said. "The Spirit is growing fruit in you, slowly. Choose one aspect of the fruit to focus on, and practise responding differently, with God's help."

Liam chose patience. Together they made a simple plan: one situation, the first hour of his shift and arriving home; one practice, a short pause and a prayer from Psalm 103: "The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger"; and one person to pray with, Peter, once a week.

Liam also apologised to his son without excuses, and asked him what would help him feel safer when tension rises. He did not demand immediate forgiveness, and he accepted that rebuilding trust would take time. Peter added, "You are responsible for your own growth; your son is not responsible for keeping you calm. If your anger ever becomes threatening or frightening, I will help you find specialist support."

Progress has been slow. Liam still loses his temper sometimes, but less often, and he is learning to stop, step away and calm down before he speaks. His son recently said, "You've been calmer lately." Character grows slowly, often in exactly the situations that reveal our need.

14 · What this lesson is not saying

Here are five common mistakes about character.

"The fruit of the Spirit is a checklist to score yourself." No. It describes a whole life shaped by the Spirit, grown over time.

"If I lack some fruit, I am not saved." No. Fruit is a sign of maturity, not an anxious test of salvation.

"Christlike means quiet and polite." No. Jesus was gentle, and also courageous and truthful.

"God will change me without my effort." No. Grace gives the power, and we make every effort.

And "Patience and gentleness mean putting up with abuse." No. Christlike character never requires staying in harm. If you are being harmed, seeking safety and help is right.

16 · Remember

Let us bring it together. God is making his children like Jesus. The Spirit grows his fruit. We make every effort. And in Christ's strength, we stand firm.

Like Jesus: the goal is Christlike character, and gentleness is strength governed by love. The Spirit's fruit: these qualities belong together and grow slowly, and they are never an anxious test of salvation. Make every effort: put off old patterns and put on Christlike ones, in specific situations, with help. And stand firm: Christ has defeated evil, so we resist temptation with truth, Scripture and prayer, without fear.

17 · Prayer

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you are gentle and lowly in heart, and that you have given us an example. Make me more like you. Holy Spirit, grow your fruit in me, especially in the places where I am tested. Give me grace to make every effort, and strength to stand firm in you, without fear. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Pause and seek help

Prayer for deliverance must never involve holding, restraining or striking people, shaming them, demanding payment, or telling anyone to stop medical treatment. Consent can be withdrawn at any time. If a person asks for prayer to stop, it must stop immediately. Christlike character never requires staying in harm: patience does not mean tolerating abuse. If this is happening to you or someone else, speak with a trustworthy person outside the situation (Support Pages: I am being controlled through prophecy, spiritual language, money or church authority · I am in danger or being abused).

Optional

Go deeper

Approaches to spiritual warfare and deliverance. Many churches pray with authority in Jesus' name for people troubled by evil spirits, and some also pray against spiritual forces over places (they often point to Mark 1:21–28, Luke 10:17–20 and Acts 16:16–18; some also apply Matthew 18:18, though this use is debated). Others emphasise ordinary means of standing firm, such as truth, prayer, repentance, Scripture and the church's care, and are cautious about naming or addressing particular spirits (they often point to Ephesians 6:10–18, James 4:7 and 1 Peter 5:8–9). Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that Christ has defeated evil powers (Colossians 2:15), that believers need not live in fear, and that spiritual conflict never excuses ignoring medical, psychological or social causes of suffering.

Whether believers can be troubled by evil spirits. Some Christians believe that a believer, though belonging to Christ, may still be oppressed or harassed by evil spirits in some way (they often point to Ephesians 4:27 and 2 Corinthians 12:7). Others believe that a person in whom the Holy Spirit lives cannot be inhabited or controlled by an evil spirit, though believers still face temptation and attack from outside (they often point to 1 John 4:4 and 5:18). None of these passages answers the question in exactly the terms used today. Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that believers belong to Christ, that he is stronger than every evil power, and that Christians need not live in fear.
