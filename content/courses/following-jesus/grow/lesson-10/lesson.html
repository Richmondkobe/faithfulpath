Guidance, Decisions and Testing “God Told Me”

I have a big decision to make, and someone at church says God told them what I should do. How do I know God's will? How do I know if a word is really from God?

About 16 minutesProverbs 3:5–6 · Acts 15:28 · 1 John 4:1My Growth: Grow in everyday life

In this lesson

You will learn

  • What it means to trust the Lord and not lean on your own understanding, from Proverbs 3:5–6
  • How the early church reached a decision together in Acts 15, and why John tells us to "test the spirits"
  • The four parts: Trust the Lord, Walk in His Word, Seek Wise Counsel, Test and Take a Step
  • Why God has not hidden one "perfect" choice that you might miss, and why no one may control you with "God told me"
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · A big decision

Welcome to Lesson 10 of Grow. In Establish, you learned to welcome the Spirit's gifts and to test them. In Lesson 2 of Grow, you learned to test messages by truth. Now we bring those together for one of the most practical questions in the Christian life: how does God guide us?

Big decisions can feel heavy: a job, a move, a relationship, a course of study, care for a parent. Many believers worry that God has one hidden answer, and that they might miss it. And sometimes another person says, "God told me what you should do," which can bring comfort, confusion or pressure.

So here is our question: I have a big decision to make, and someone at church says God told them what I should do. How do I know God's will? How do I know if a word is really from God?

2 · Trust in the LORD

Our first passage is Proverbs chapter 3, verses 5 and 6: "Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make straight your paths."

Use the first question: what does it say? Notice that the promise is about the Lord leading us, as we trust and acknowledge him in all our ways.

3 · Good to the Spirit and to us · Test the spirits

Two more short passages. After the early church met to make a difficult decision, they wrote: "For it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us."

And John writes: "Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God, for many false prophets have gone out into the world."

4 · Test = dokimazō

Our Greek word today is dokimazō, translated "test". It means to examine or test something in order to recognise what is genuine or approved.

You have met this word before. In Lesson 2, Romans 12:2 said, "by testing you may discern what is the will of God." Testing is not unbelief. It is obedience. And it is part of discerning God's will.

5 · Proverbs: a father's wisdom

Now the background. Proverbs is written as a father's instruction to his son. It is wisdom literature. It describes how life generally works under God, not guarantees for every situation.

Now use the second question: what did it mean to the people who first heard it? "Do not lean on your own understanding" does not mean refusing to think. Proverbs repeatedly praises careful thought and planning. It means not trusting our own understanding above God. And "he will make straight your paths" is a promise of God's faithful leading, not a promise that life will be easy.

6 · The council and the test

In Acts 15, the early church disagreed about whether Gentile believers must keep the law of Moses. The apostles and elders met, heard what God had done among the Gentiles, debated, and listened as James applied Scripture. This was an authoritative decision by the apostles, not a step-by-step formula for every personal choice. But it shows valuable patterns: careful listening, honest discussion, reading experience in the light of Scripture, and no single person claiming unquestionable private authority.

John wrote to churches troubled by teachers who had left them and who denied that Jesus had come in the flesh. So his test is first about the truth concerning Christ. And because "he who is in you is greater than he who is in the world", testing can be done with confidence rather than fear.

7 · The short answer

So, how do I know God's will? Here is the short answer.

God guides his people as they trust him, walk in his word, seek wise counsel, and test what they hear before taking a humble step.

That gives us four parts: Trust the Lord; Walk in His Word; Seek Wise Counsel; and Test and Take a Step.

8 · Part 1: Trust the Lord

The first part: Trust the Lord.

Guidance begins with relationship, not technique. God is a good Father, and "all who are led by the Spirit of God are sons of God." In context, that verse describes a whole life led by the Spirit. It is not a promise that every personal decision will receive a private message.

God often guides more than he explains. "The heart of man plans his way, but the LORD establishes his steps." He can be trusted even when the way is unclear.

And he invites us to ask: "If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach, and it will be given him."

9 · Part 2: Walk in His Word

The second part: Walk in His Word.

"Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path." Much of God's will is already clear in Scripture: to trust Christ, to grow in holiness, to love others, to give thanks and to pray. "Rejoice always, pray without ceasing, give thanks in all circumstances; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you."

Many decisions, such as which job to take or where to live, may involve several options that are consistent with God's revealed will. We should not assume that God has hidden one perfect choice that we must discover, or else ruin his plan. Within what he has made clear, he gives us real freedom and wisdom, while remaining free to guide or redirect us as he chooses.

And Scripture sets the boundaries of faithful decisions. God will not guide anyone to deny Christ, or to do what Scripture forbids. Paul warned that even "an angel from heaven" must not be believed if the message contradicts the gospel.

10 · Part 3: Seek Wise Counsel

The third part: Seek Wise Counsel.

"Without counsel plans fail, but with many advisers they succeed." In Acts 15, the church reached its decision together, and described it as "it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us". That expressed confidence with humility. God often uses the wisdom of other believers to guide us, so important decisions should not normally be made in proud isolation.

Seek counsel from a small number of suitable people, not from everyone who offers an opinion. Wise counsel may include mature believers, church leaders, family where appropriate, and people with relevant knowledge. Counsel informs a decision. It does not take the decision away from the person who must make it.

11 · Part 4: Test and Take a Step

The fourth part: Test and Take a Step.

"Do not despise prophecies, but test everything; hold fast what is good." Prophecies, impressions, dreams and claims that "God told me" are to be weighed, not simply obeyed. A claimed word must not contradict Scripture; it should honour Christ and be consistent with the Spirit's character. It must never authorise sin, coercion, abuse or deception. Spiritual language must never be used to remove a person's consent or responsible judgement.

It helps to ask three separate questions. Is it consistent with Scripture and the truth about Jesus Christ? Is it wise in these circumstances? And is there good reason to believe this is truly from God, rather than a human impression? A decision may be wise without certainty that God privately revealed it.

12 · Decide humbly, keep walking

After praying, reading Scripture, thinking carefully and seeking counsel, believers make a decision and take a step, holding their plans humbly: "If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that." God can redirect us along the way. Paul's plans were twice blocked by the Spirit before a vision led him to Macedonia.

Feelings of peace may accompany a decision, but they are not the only or final test. Once you have enough information, make the best faithful decision you can, without reopening it again and again just to reach complete certainty. New facts may be a reason to reconsider. Anxiety alone does not mean the decision was wrong.

13 · Christians differ: how God speaks today

Christians are not all agreed about how God speaks today. Many Christians expect the Spirit to guide through prophecy, impressions, dreams and visions, as well as through Scripture. They often point to Acts 2, Acts 16 and 1 Corinthians 14.

Others emphasise guidance through Scripture, wisdom and God's ordering of circumstances, and are cautious about treating impressions as messages from God. They often point to Hebrews 1 and 2 Timothy 3.

Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that claimed guidance must not contradict Scripture, that the Spirit leads God's people, and that no claimed word from God may be used to control another person. Christians also differ about asking God for signs, and you will find that under Go deeper.

14 · Mariam's story

Let me tell you about Mariam. Her story is a composite, drawn from the experiences of many people.

Mariam is a pharmacist in Cairo, Egypt. She was offered a well-paid job in another country. Her father is ageing, and her brother lives nearby, but she is the one who usually takes their father to medical appointments. At a prayer meeting, a woman Mariam barely knew told her, "God showed me you must not leave Egypt, because the man you will marry is here." Mariam went home confused and anxious.

She met her pastor, who read Proverbs 3 and 1 John 4 with her. "Thank God for anyone who prays for you," he said, "but that word cannot decide your marriage or your career for you. Let us weigh it, and then think this through carefully." Together they listed what Scripture made clear: honour her father, work faithfully, stay connected to a church. Both options could fit within those.

Mariam prayed for wisdom, talked honestly with her father and brother, asked a colleague about the job and about churches in the new city, and gave herself a month to decide. With her father's agreement, the family discussed what care he needed. Her brother agreed to take over most of the appointments, and they planned regular video calls and visits.

Mariam accepted the job, found a church in her new city, and visits home when she can. Some days she wonders whether she chose rightly, but she knows she sought God honestly and acted with wisdom and love. She no longer fears that she missed God's will by not following the woman's word.

15 · What this lesson is not saying

Here are six common mistakes.

"God has one hidden perfect choice that I might miss forever." No. Within his clear will, God gives freedom and wisdom.

"The Holy Spirit does not guide today." No. The Spirit leads God's people; claimed words are welcomed and tested.

"Every impression or prophecy is God's voice." No. Every claimed word is weighed by Scripture and wisdom.

"Peaceful feelings, open doors or coincidences prove God's will." No. They may help, but they are not the final test.

"If someone says God told them, I must obey." No. No claimed word may replace your consent or judgement.

And "I should tell others what God wants them to do." No. Pray, listen and ask good questions, and let them decide.

17 · Remember

Let us bring it together. We trust the Lord, walk in his word, seek wise counsel, and test what we hear before taking a humble step.

Trust the Lord: guidance begins with relationship; ask God for wisdom, and trust him when the way is unclear. Walk in his word: much of God's will is clear, and within it, God gives freedom and wisdom. Seek wise counsel: decide with a few wise people; counsel informs, but does not take, the decision. And test and take a step: weigh every claimed word, then decide humbly, and let God redirect you if needed.

18 · Prayer

Let us pray. Father, thank you that you lead your children. I trust you with all my heart, and I do not want to lean on my own understanding. You have promised wisdom to those who ask, so I ask you now. Let your word be a lamp to my feet. Give me wise counsel. Help me to test what I hear, to take a humble step, and to rest in your care, knowing that you can redirect me along the way. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Pause and seek help

A claim that "God told me" can be misused to control: no prophecy may decide whom you marry, how much you give, whether you leave your job or home, or whether you stop medical treatment, and a "prophecy" about pregnancy, illness or health is never a diagnosis or guarantee; you may say no, ask for time and seek counsel, outside that leader's authority if the claim comes from a leader (Support Page: I am being controlled through prophecy, spiritual language, money or church authority). If someone demands an immediate decision "because God says so", treat that urgency itself as a warning sign. If unusual or distressing experiences are affecting your sleep, judgement, daily functioning or safety, seek prompt medical help as well as appropriate pastoral support; if you hear commands to harm yourself or someone else, or cannot stay safe, seek urgent help (Support Page: I may harm myself or someone else, or I cannot stay safe). Seeking medical help is not a lack of faith. Where safety, health or a legal deadline needs prompt action, seek qualified help rather than waiting for perfect clarity.

Optional

Go deeper

Asking God for signs. Some Christians sometimes ask God for a specific sign to confirm a decision, as Gideon did with the fleece (they often point to Judges 6:36–40). Others caution against making requested signs a normal way of seeking guidance. They note that Gideon had already received God's command before asking for the fleece, and that Scripture warns against putting God to the test (they often point to Matthew 4:7). Jesus also rebuked unbelieving demands for signs that refused what God had already shown (Matthew 12:39). Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that God is free to guide as he chooses, and that signs must never replace Scripture, wisdom and counsel. If it would help, ask a trustworthy church leader how your church approaches these matters, and compare what you hear with Scripture.

If you are still exploring. Everyone makes decisions, and many people wonder whether God guides at all. This lesson explains how Christians seek God's guidance through Scripture, prayer and wise counsel. You are welcome to try these steps with any decision you face. No one will ask you about your decisions or to say publicly whether you have become a Christian.
