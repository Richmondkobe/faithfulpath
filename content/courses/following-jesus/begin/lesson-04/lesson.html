Following Jesus as Lord

If I am saved by grace, does it matter how I live? And what if following Jesus causes trouble with my family?

Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Two honest questions

Welcome to Lesson 4. In Lesson 3, we saw what God does in a person who receives Christ: he gives new life from above. Today we ask what that new life looks like, day by day.

In Lesson 2, we learned that God's welcome cannot be earned. That is wonderful news. But it raises an honest question: if salvation is a free gift, does it matter how I live afterwards? Can I simply believe and carry on as before?

For many people there is a second, more painful question. What if following Jesus causes trouble at home? What if my family or community sees my new faith as betrayal? This lesson takes both questions seriously.

2 · "Who do you say that I am?"

Our passage is Luke chapter 9, verses 18 to 26. Jesus is praying alone, and his disciples are with him. He asks them a question: "Who do the crowds say that I am?" They give the popular answers: some say John the Baptist, others say Elijah, others say one of the prophets of long ago.

Then Jesus makes it personal. Verse 20: "But who do you say that I am?" And Peter answered, "The Christ of God."

Peter is right. Remember Lesson 1: Christ is not a surname. It means God's anointed King, the one God had promised. Peter has seen who Jesus really is.

3 · The King who must suffer

But straight away, Jesus says something the disciples did not expect. He tells them that he "must suffer many things… and be killed, and on the third day be raised." The promised King would fulfil his mission through suffering, death and resurrection.

And then he turns to everyone listening. Verses 23 and 24: "If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me. For whoever would save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for my sake will save it."

Notice the order. First, Peter confesses who Jesus is. Then Jesus explains what following him means. Knowing who Jesus is and following him cannot be separated.

4 · "Take up his cross daily"

When Jesus told people to take up their cross, his first hearers did not picture jewellery or an ordinary personal difficulty. Under Roman rule, the cross was an instrument of execution. A person carrying a cross was on the way to die; their life was no longer their own. Jesus was describing the radical cost of following him.

To deny yourself does not mean hating yourself or rejecting every good thing. It means saying no to self as the one in charge, and yes to Jesus. And Jesus promises that whoever loses their life for his sake will truly find it.

And notice one small word. Matthew and Mark also record this saying, but only Luke includes the word "daily." Following Jesus is not only one big decision at the beginning. It is a daily willingness to put him first, whatever it costs.

This does not mean looking for suffering for its own sake. It means that, each day, his will comes before our own, even when that is costly.

5 · Lord = kyrios

One of the earliest and simplest Christian confessions is just three words in English: "Jesus is Lord." The Greek word for Lord is kyrios. In everyday speech it could simply mean "sir," or "master" of a household. But in the Greek Old Testament, kyrios was regularly used where the Hebrew text has God's covenant name, represented in many English Bibles as LORD.

Now listen to Paul in Romans 10, verse 9: "…if you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved." And just a few verses later, Paul quotes the prophet Joel: "For 'everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.'" In Joel, that name is the LORD himself. Paul applies those words to Jesus.

So when Christians say "Jesus is Lord," they are confessing far more than respect for a teacher. Paul speaks of Jesus in language that Scripture uses for the LORD himself. Jesus is the risen Lord and King. That is the only Greek word for today: kyrios, Lord.

6 · The short answer

So, does it matter how we live? Here is the short answer. Yes, it matters how we live, and yes, following Jesus can be costly. But neither of these turns salvation into something we earn.

Jesus is not only the one who saves us; he is the risen King. We follow him because we have been loved and welcomed, not to earn his love. Following him may cost us, so we count the cost, and we show our allegiance with both courage and wisdom.

The rest of this lesson unpacks that answer in four parts: Jesus is Lord, count the cost, obey from grace, and show your allegiance.

7 · Truth 1: Jesus Is Lord

The first part: Jesus is Lord. To call Jesus Lord is to say that he is the rightful King of the world and of my life. After his resurrection, Jesus said, "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me." His rule is not limited to Sundays or to religious activities. It reaches our work, our money, our relationships, our words and our plans.

Some people imagine that the Christian life has two stages: first you accept Jesus as Saviour, and later, if you are especially keen, you accept him as Lord. But the Bible does not separate these. Trusting Jesus and following him belong together. Jesus asked, "Why do you call me 'Lord, Lord,' and not do what I tell you?"

This is not a harsh demand. Jesus is a King who gave his life for his people. His rule is good, and his commands lead us in what is good and right, even when obedience is costly.

8 · Truth 2: Count the Cost

The second part: count the cost. Jesus never hid the cost of following him. In Luke 14, as large crowds followed him, he said something very strong: "If anyone comes to me and does not hate his own father and mother and wife and children and brothers and sisters, yes, and even his own life, he cannot be my disciple."

Does Jesus really want us to hate our families? No. Jesus is using deliberately stark language about supreme loyalty. Matthew records a closely related saying that makes the comparison clear: "Whoever loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me." Jesus is not commanding contempt or cruelty towards family. He is demanding first place. In fact, he strongly criticised people who used religion as an excuse to neglect their parents, as Mark chapter 7 shows. His point is that he must come first, above every other loyalty: family, possessions, ambitions, even our own lives.

Jesus then told two short stories: a builder who sits down to work out whether he can finish a tower, and a king who considers whether he can win a battle. Both count the cost before they begin. Counting the cost does not mean paying for our salvation. Salvation is a gift. It means recognising honestly what loyalty to Jesus may involve, so that we follow him with open eyes.

9 · Truth 3: Obey From Grace

The third part: obey from grace. If salvation is a gift, why obey at all? Jesus puts it simply: "If you love me, you will keep my commandments." Obedience is the response of love, not the price of love. We obey because we have been loved and welcomed, not in order to be loved and welcomed.

Paul puts it this way in Titus 2: "For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people, training us to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions, and to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives in the present age." Notice that grace does two things. It brings salvation, and it trains us. Grace is not only a gift at the beginning; it is a teacher for the whole journey.

Obedience usually grows through small, concrete steps: telling the truth in one difficult conversation, giving up one dishonest habit, taking one step towards forgiving someone, being kind to one difficult neighbour. Nobody obeys perfectly. When we fail, we do not give up or pretend. We turn back to God, confess honestly, and depend on his grace.

And obedience is not a deal with God. Following Jesus does not guarantee health, wealth or an easy life. Jesus himself, the only perfectly obedient person who ever lived, went to the cross.

10 · Truth 4: Show Your Allegiance

The fourth part: show your allegiance. Faith in Jesus is personal, but it is not meant to stay hidden. It becomes visible in two main ways.

The first is baptism. Jesus told his followers to make disciples, "baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit." On the day of Pentecost, Peter told the crowd, "Repent and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ." In baptism, a believer is openly identified with Christ and with the community of his disciples. Christians explain how baptism relates to faith and new birth in different ways, and Book 2 looks at this carefully.

The second is faithful living. Paul praised new believers in Thessalonica because they "turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God." Following Jesus means turning away from old loyalties that compete with him, such as worshipping other gods, trusting in charms, or living for money or status.

At the same time, Christians are called to honour their families and treat their neighbours with respect. The Bible says, "Honor your father and mother," and asks believers to explain their faith "with gentleness and respect." Following Jesus does not mean rejecting our culture or despising people of other faiths. It means that Jesus has first place. Public steps of faith, like baptism, should be taken obediently and wisely, in fellowship with a faithful local church rather than merely because someone is pushing you.

11 · Priya's story

Let me tell you about Priya. Her story is drawn from the experiences of many people. Priya is a young nurse in Chennai, India. She grew up in a Hindu family that she loves, and a year ago she began following Jesus.

When her family planned a large ceremony at home, her father asked her to lead part of the worship. Priya felt torn. "If I say no, I am rejecting my family. If I say yes, I am not putting Jesus first."

Before the day, she asked her pastor for advice. They read Luke 9 and Ephesians 6:2 together. Priya decided that she could help her mother prepare food, welcome the guests and care for her older relatives. But she would not lead the prayers, make an offering or take part in worship directed to another god. She explained this gently to her father beforehand: she now worships Jesus, and she still loves and honours her family. Her father was hurt, and he was quiet with her for several days.

There is no neat ending. Priya keeps honouring her parents in practical ways. She and her pastor are preparing wisely for her baptism while continuing to honour her family.

Priya's decision is not a rule for every Christian at every ceremony. Different situations raise different questions, which is why believers need Scripture and wise local counsel. But the principle is clear: serve and honour your family gladly, and give worship to God alone.

12 · What following Jesus as Lord is not

Because this subject is often misunderstood, here are four common mistakes.

"Lordship is an extra step for very committed Christians." Trusting Jesus and following him belong together from the beginning.

"I must obey well enough to earn God's acceptance." Obedience is the fruit of grace, not its price.

"If I obey God, he will make me healthy and rich." Following Jesus brings real blessing, but it does not guarantee health, wealth or an easy life.

And "Following Jesus means rejecting my family and culture." Jesus comes first, but Christians are called to honour their families and respect their neighbours wherever they can.

13 · This week

Now, because Jesus is Lord, here is what I would like you to do this week. Your assignment is to choose one teaching of Jesus and put it into practice.

First, read Luke 9:18 to 26 again. Second, choose one clear teaching of Jesus that you already know you need to obey. It might be to take one step towards forgiving someone, to let your yes be yes and your no be no, to love an enemy, or to honour your parents in a practical way. Choose just one.

Third, write it down in a single sentence: "Because Jesus is Lord, this week I will…" And finally, do it. Then thank God for his grace, whether it went well or not. Remember: this is a response to grace, not a way to earn it.

This assignment belongs to two of your First Steps: my faith, and my witness, because obedience is one of the clearest ways others see that Jesus is Lord. Below this lesson you will also find the seven-day reading plan, and a short look back at the four parts of the gospel.

14 · Remember

Let us bring it together. It matters how we live, but we live from grace, not for it. Jesus is Lord: he is the risen King, and his loving rule reaches every part of life. Count the cost: Jesus comes first, above every other loyalty; counting the cost does not buy salvation. Obey from grace: we obey because we are loved, step by step, and failure is met with confession and grace. Show your allegiance: faith becomes visible through baptism and faithful living, with public steps taken obediently and wisely.

15 · Prayer

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, you are the Christ of God, the risen King. Thank you that you loved me and gave yourself for me. I want you to have first place in my life: my work, my money, my relationships, my words and my plans. Give me courage to follow you daily, and wisdom and gentleness with my family. When I fail, help me to turn back, confess honestly and depend on your grace. In your name. Amen.
