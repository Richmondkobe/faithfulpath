Jesus Christ and the Good News

People talk about “the gospel” all the time. What actually is it?

About 18 minutesMark 1:14–15First Steps: My faith · My witness

You will learn

  • What the word "gospel" really means, and why it is news rather than advice
  • How Jesus' announcement fulfils God's promise in Isaiah
  • The gospel in four parts: who Jesus is, what he announced, what he did, and how we respond
  • How to explain the gospel in your own words
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · The question

Welcome to Lesson 1 of Begin. I am glad you are here. Whether you have followed Jesus for a week, or you are returning to faith, or you are still exploring who he is, you are welcome.

Let me start with a word you have probably heard many times. The word is gospel. You hear it in church. You hear it in songs, and sometimes on the radio. Some people say "that's the gospel truth" when they mean something is definitely true. Many Christians use the word every week. But if someone stopped you today and asked, "What actually is the gospel?", what would you say?

That is our question for this lesson. By the end, you will be able to explain the gospel in your own words, in four simple parts. You do not need to believe it yet to understand it. You only need to listen carefully. So let us begin where every lesson in this course begins: with the Bible.

2 · Our passage

Our passage is Mark chapter 1, verses 14 and 15. Mark's Gospel is one of four accounts of Jesus' life in the Bible, and it is the shortest. Very near the beginning, Mark sums up Jesus' whole message in two verses. Listen carefully.

"Now after John was arrested, Jesus came into Galilee, proclaiming the gospel of God, and saying, 'The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand; repent and believe in the gospel.'"

Let me read it once more, a little more slowly. "Now after John was arrested, Jesus came into Galilee, proclaiming the gospel of God, and saying, 'The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand; repent and believe in the gospel.'"

3 · Three things to notice

Notice three things in these verses. First, this is Jesus' own message. The gospel did not begin with a church, a preacher or a denomination. Jesus himself came "proclaiming the gospel of God."

Second, it is an announcement. Jesus does not say, "Here is some good advice." He says, "The time is fulfilled." Something has happened.

Third, it calls for a response. Jesus ends with two commands: repent and believe. So the gospel is news to hear, and it is also an invitation to answer. Keep those three things in mind. We will come back to each of them.

4 · Gospel = good news

The New Testament was first written in Greek. The word Mark uses for gospel is euangelion. It simply means good news.

In the wider Greek-speaking world, this word could be used for important public announcements: good news of a victory, or of the rise of a ruler. But Mark's use is also deeply rooted in the Scriptures of Israel, especially passages such as Isaiah 52, which announce the good news that God reigns. We will look at that passage in a moment. Mark opens his whole book with this word: "The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God."

So when Mark speaks about the gospel of Jesus Christ, this is not advice about becoming a better person. It is an announcement of what God has done. Advice tells you what to do. News tells you what has happened. That difference matters, and we will see why later in this lesson.

5 · Kingdom = God's reign

The second word is in verse 15: "the kingdom of God is at hand." The Greek word for kingdom is basileia.

When we hear "kingdom" today, we often think only of a country with borders on a map. Here Jesus is speaking especially about God's reign: his rule, his authority and his kingly power in action. So when Jesus says that the kingdom of God has come near, he is announcing that God's promised rule is breaking into history through him. Wherever Jesus goes, God's reign is breaking in.

These are the only two Greek words I will ask you to remember today: euangelion, good news, and basileia, God's reign. If you remember just those two, Mark chapter 1 will already make more sense to you.

6 · Galilee under Rome, and the promised King

Now let us step into the world where Jesus spoke these words. Galilee was a region in the north of Israel. In Jesus' day, the land was under Roman imperial power. Galilee itself was ruled by Herod Antipas, a client ruler whose authority depended on Rome. Roman imperial power, taxation and political control shaped everyday life.

Notice how our verse begins: "after John was arrested." John the Baptist had been put in prison by that same Herod. So when Jesus walks into Galilee announcing a kingdom, he is not speaking in a quiet, peaceful world. He is announcing God's reign right where earthly rulers seemed to hold all the power.

For centuries, God's people had been waiting for a King God had promised: a King from the family of David who would put things right. In Israel, kings were anointed with oil when God chose them. The Hebrew word māšîaḥ means "anointed one." From it we get the title Messiah. The Greek equivalent is Christos, from which we get "Christ." So Christ is not Jesus' surname. It is a title: Jesus is God's anointed King. Every time we say "Jesus Christ," we are saying, "Jesus is the promised King."

7 · Isaiah 52:7: "Your God reigns"

This announcement did not come out of nowhere. Centuries before Jesus, the prophet Isaiah gave God's people a picture of hope for a time when they were defeated and far from home. Listen to Isaiah chapter 52, verse 7:

"How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him who brings good news, who publishes peace, who brings good news of happiness, who publishes salvation, who says to Zion, 'Your God reigns.'"

Picture it. A messenger runs across the hills towards a waiting city, calling out good news. And what is the news? "Your God reigns." Now listen to Jesus again: "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand." Do you hear the connection? Jesus' announcement echoes that great biblical hope: the promised good news of God's reign is arriving. The moment God had been preparing for has come.

8 · The good news in one sentence

So what is the gospel? Here it is in one sentence. Jesus, the promised King, has come. He died for our sins and rose again, so that through him God now offers forgiveness, reconciliation with God, new life and hope to everyone who turns to him and trusts him.

The rest of this lesson unpacks that sentence in four parts: who Jesus is, what Jesus announced, what Jesus did, and why this is good news and how we respond. You will meet these four parts again and again in this course, so take your time with them.

9 · Truth 1: Who Jesus Is

The first part: who Jesus is. The good news is about a person before it is about anything else.

Jesus was a real man. He lived in Israel about two thousand years ago. He grew up in the town of Nazareth and worked as a carpenter or builder before he began to teach. He ate, he slept, he wept, and he got tired. He was fully human.

But the New Testament presents Jesus as much more than a good teacher or prophet. John's Gospel begins with an extraordinary claim: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God… And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us." John uses "the Word" to speak of Jesus. He is telling us that the one who has always been with God, and who is God, became a human being and lived among us.

So Christians say that Jesus is fully God and fully human. We will explore that more in Book 2. For now, hold on to this: in Jesus, God himself came close. And he is the Christ, the promised King, who now reigns as Lord.

10 · Truth 2: What Jesus Announced

The second part: what Jesus announced. The Bible tells us that God made the world good, but human beings turned away from him. Since then the world has been full of beauty and full of brokenness, side by side: love and cruelty, joy and grief, kindness and injustice. Through the prophets, God promised that one day he would put things right through his chosen King.

Jesus announced that this day had arrived. And as you read on in Mark, you see it happening. Jesus welcomes people others push away. He forgives sins. He heals the sick and sets people free from evil. He calls ordinary men and women to follow him. Wherever Jesus goes, God's kingdom breaks in.

This does not mean everything is fixed straight away. Christians still suffer and struggle, and we still wait for the day when Jesus will make all things new. But the King has come, and his kingdom has begun. It is like the first light of sunrise. The full day has not come yet, but the night is already over.

11 · Truth 3: What Jesus Did

The third part: what Jesus did. If Jesus had only taught and healed, we might remember him as a great man. But the heart of the good news is what happened at the end of his life.

Within the first generation after Jesus' death and resurrection, the apostle Paul wrote to the Christians in the city of Corinth. He reminded them of the message he called "of first importance": "that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures, and that he appeared…"

There are four short claims here. Christ died for our sins. He was executed on a Roman cross, but his death was not an accident or a defeat. He died on our behalf, because of the wrong we have done. He was buried. He really died. He was raised on the third day: not as a ghost or a memory, but bodily and truly alive. And he appeared. Paul says the risen Jesus was seen by many people, once by more than five hundred at the same time, and Paul even notes that most of those witnesses were still alive when he wrote.

The resurrection is the turning point of the whole story. It shows that Jesus is who he said he was, and that death does not have the last word.

12 · Truth 4: Why It Is Good News and How We Respond

The fourth part: why this is good news, and how we respond. The Bible is honest about us. All of us have sinned. We have turned away from God and gone our own way, in what we think, say and do, and in the good we fail to do. Sin is not only breaking rules. It is a broken relationship with the God who made us. And we cannot repair it by trying harder or being more religious.

But here is the good news. Paul writes in Romans chapter 5, verse 8: "But God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us." Notice the order. God acted first. He did not wait for us to fix ourselves.

Because Jesus died and rose again, God now offers four gifts. Forgiveness, so our wrongs are no longer held against us. Reconciliation, so we can know him as our loving Father. New life, as he begins to change us from the inside. And hope that does not end at death. These are gifts, not rewards. No one earns them.

And how do we respond? Jesus said it in two words: repent and believe. To repent means to turn: to turn away from going our own way and turn back to God. To believe means to trust: to put our trust in Jesus and what he has done, rather than in ourselves. We will look closely at those two words in Lesson 2. For now, remember this: the gospel is not only something to understand. It is an invitation to respond.

13 · Ama's story

Let me tell you about Ama. Her story is drawn from the experiences of many people. Ama grew up in a church-going family in Kumasi, Ghana. She sang in the choir for years and knew most of the hymns by heart.

One evening, a friend from work asked her a simple question: "What is the gospel?" Ama answered, "It means going to church, praying and trying to live a good life." But afterwards, Ama began to think carefully about her answer. Everything she had said was about what people do. She had said nothing about who Jesus is or what God had done through him.

The next Sunday, Ama asked an older woman in her church, Auntie Efua, if they could read one of the Gospels together. They chose Mark. They met every Tuesday after work, reading a few verses at a time and asking questions. There was no sudden, dramatic moment. But there was a beginning.

Ama's experience is very common. It is possible to know a lot of church language without yet understanding the gospel clearly. If that describes you, you are in exactly the right place.

14 · What the gospel is not

Because the gospel is so important, it is often confused with other ideas. Here are four common ones.

"The gospel means being a good person." Being good matters, but the gospel is not advice to try harder. It is news about what God has done.

"Jesus was only a good teacher." He was a great teacher, but the gospel announces much more: he is the promised King who died for our sins and rose again.

"I have always gone to church, so I am fine." Belonging to a church is good, but attending church is not the same as trusting Christ.

And "The gospel promises that God will make me healthy and rich." God cares about every part of our lives, but the gospel does not promise wealth or success to everyone who believes. Its centre is Jesus Christ and what God has done through him.

15 · This week

Now, because this is true, here is what I would like you to do this week. Your assignment is to explain the gospel in four parts, in your own words.

First, read Mark 1:14 to 15 and 1 Corinthians 15:3 to 5 again. Second, write one or two sentences for each part: who Jesus is, what he announced, what he did, and why it is good news and how we respond. Third, practise saying it out loud, in about two minutes, as if a friend had just asked you, "What is the gospel?" And finally, look at the fourth part and ask yourself honestly: how am I responding to Jesus? Then tell God your answer in a short prayer.

You do not need to memorise my exact words. The goal is to understand the gospel well enough to explain it simply and faithfully in your own words.

This assignment is part of your First Steps in Following Jesus. It belongs to two of them: my faith, and my witness. Below this lesson you will also find a seven-day reading plan through Mark chapters 1 and 2, with one short question for each day.

16 · Remember

Let us bring it together. The gospel is good news about Jesus. Who Jesus is: fully God and fully human, the promised King. What Jesus announced: God's reign has come near. What Jesus did: he died for our sins, was buried, was raised on the third day, and appeared to many witnesses. And why it is good news: God offers forgiveness, reconciliation with God, new life and hope as a gift, and he calls us to turn and trust.

17 · Prayer

Let us pray. Father, thank you for the good news of Jesus. Thank you that you did not wait for us to fix ourselves, but loved us while we were still sinners. Thank you that Jesus is the promised King, who died for our sins and rose again. Teach me to turn from my own way and trust Jesus more fully. Help me to understand this good news, live in its truth and share it clearly. In Jesus' name. Amen.
