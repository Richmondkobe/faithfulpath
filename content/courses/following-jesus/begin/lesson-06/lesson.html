Beginning With the Bible

I want to read the Bible, but it is long and difficult. Where do I start, and how?

Transcript and Scripture references

1 · A mountain to climb?

Welcome to Lesson 6. In Lesson 3, we saw that new believers are born to grow, and that God grows his children through three gifts: the Bible, prayer and the church. This lesson is about the first of these gifts.

For many people, the Bible can seem like a mountain. The Bible we are using in this course has 66 books, written over many centuries, with unfamiliar names, places and customs. Some people start at Genesis full of good intentions and give up somewhere in Leviticus. Others feel that the Bible is for pastors and educated people, not for them.

So here is our question: where do I start, and how? By the end of this lesson, you will know why the Bible matters, and you will have a simple way to begin, a little at a time.

2 · "Continue in what you have learned"

Our passage is 2 Timothy chapter 3, verses 14 to 17. Paul wrote this letter to Timothy, a younger church leader he had trained. Listen to verses 14 and 15:

"But as for you, continue in what you have learned and have firmly believed, knowing from whom you learned it and how from childhood you have been acquainted with the sacred writings, which are able to make you wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus."

Notice what the Scriptures do. These Scriptures were able to make Timothy "wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus." So Scripture is not merely information to collect. It makes us wise for salvation and leads us to put our faith in Christ.

3 · "Breathed out by God"

And then verses 16 and 17: "All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work."

Paul says that Scripture is useful in four ways. Teaching shows us what is true. Reproof shows us where we have gone wrong. Correction helps us put things right. And training in righteousness shapes us, day by day, in the way we should live.

And notice the goal: that "the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work." Paul says this directly to Timothy, a church leader. But the same Scripture that equips Timothy also teaches, corrects and trains God's people. The goal is not information for its own sake. It is a life shaped by God.

4 · Paul, Timothy and the sacred writings

Let us look at the background. Paul was in prison when he wrote this letter, and he knew that his life was coming to an end. He writes, "the time of my departure has come." So these are some of Paul's last words to Timothy, whom he calls "my beloved child."

And notice Timothy's family. Earlier in the letter Paul mentions Timothy's grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice, and the sincere faith that lived in them. Timothy had known the sacred writings from childhood, and God's word and faith had been present in his family life.

Notice the words "But as for you." Just before this, Paul warns that in difficult times, people will be deceived and lead others astray. Timothy's anchor is the word of God.

When Paul speaks of the sacred writings that Timothy knew from childhood, he means mainly what we now call the Old Testament. The New Testament also shows that apostolic writings came to be recognised alongside the Scriptures. In 2 Peter 3:16, Peter speaks of Paul's letters and then refers to "the other Scriptures." Christians therefore understand Paul's teaching about Scripture as applying to the biblical writings received by the church as Scripture.

And here is one more detail that matters for us. In his first letter, Paul told Timothy, "devote yourself to the public reading of Scripture." In the early church, most believers did not own a copy of the Scriptures. They heard God's word read aloud when the church gathered.

5 · God-breathed = theopneustos

Our original word for today is the word translated "breathed out by God." In Greek it is theopneustos. This word appears only here in the whole New Testament, so we understand it from the sentence it sits in and from the rest of the Bible's teaching. Paul does not say that Scripture is merely inspiring, the way a beautiful song can inspire us. He says that Scripture is breathed out by God. Its source is God himself.

In the Old Testament, God's word and God's breath can go together. Psalm 33 says, "By the word of the LORD the heavens were made, and by the breath of his mouth all their host." When God speaks, things happen. His word carries his power.

And Scripture also shows us that God used real human writers. Peter says that "men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit." The writers used their own languages, styles and personalities. But God was at work through them, so that what they wrote is his word. That is our one word for today: theopneustos, breathed out by God.

6 · The short answer

So, where do I start, and how? Here is the short answer. Start small, start with Jesus, and start today.

In the Bible, God has spoken and continues to address us through his written word, and its great story leads to Jesus. We grow by reading or listening a little every day, asking God to help us understand, and then trusting and obeying what we hear.

The rest of this lesson unpacks that answer in four parts: God has spoken, it points to Jesus, a little every day, and hear and do. And then I will show you a simple way to spend ten minutes with the Bible.

7 · Truth 1: God Has Spoken

The first part: God has spoken. The Bible was written by real people, in their own languages and styles, over many centuries. But Christians believe that God was at work through those writers, so that what they wrote is his word. In Scripture, God has spoken, and he continues to address us through his written word.

This means the Bible is not only human thoughts about God. It is God's trustworthy word, and we can rely on it. The psalmist says, "Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path."

Think of a small lamp on a dark path. It does not light up the whole countryside. It lights the next few steps. The Bible may not answer every question we have, but it gives us enough light to take the next step with God.

8 · Truth 2: It Points to Jesus

The second part: it points to Jesus. The Bible is a library of many kinds of writing: history, law, poetry, songs, wise sayings, prophecy, letters and the Gospels. But it tells one great story. God makes a good world. Humans turn away from him. God makes promises and chooses a people. And he keeps his promises by sending Jesus, who will one day make all things new.

After his resurrection, Jesus walked with two discouraged followers on the road to Emmaus. Luke tells us, "And beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself."

This does not mean that every verse is directly about Jesus, or that we should force him into details a passage is not talking about. It means that the whole story finds its centre and fulfilment in him. That is why the best place for a beginner to start is with a Gospel, one of the four accounts of Jesus' life. In this course, we are reading through the Gospel of Mark.

9 · Truth 3: A Little Every Day

The third part: a little every day. A steady daily rhythm is a simple and helpful way to keep returning to God's word. You do not need to begin with long reading sessions. About ten minutes a day is enough to establish the habit. The psalmist describes the person who delights in God's word and meditates on it day and night. "He is like a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in its season." A tree keeps drawing from the stream, season after season. In the same way, we keep returning to God's word.

And listening counts fully. Remember what we saw: the first believers heard God's word read aloud. The book of Revelation begins, "Blessed is the one who reads aloud the words of this prophecy, and blessed are those who hear, and who keep what is written in it." If reading is hard for you, an audio Bible is not second best.

We also do not read alone. Before we read, we can pray, "Open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of your law." Paul writes in 1 Corinthians that God has given us his Spirit, "that we might understand the things freely given us by God."

Christian traditions differ somewhat in the Old Testament books they include; this course follows the 66-book Protestant Bible. Whatever your tradition, use a reliable translation that you understand clearly, especially in your first language. This course quotes the English Standard Version. Ask a trusted church leader which translations they recommend.

10 · Truth 4: Hear and Do

The fourth part: hear and do. God speaks so that we will trust and obey him. James warns against hearing without doing: "But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves."

James compares such a person to someone who looks at his face in a mirror, then goes away and at once forgets what he looks like. Hearing God's word is good. But it is meant to change us.

So each day, look for one of three things: one truth to trust, one action to take, or one question to carry. Not every passage gives a command, and you do not need to invent one. Some days the passage will show you something about God to trust. Some days it will show you something to do. And some days you will finish with a question. That is fine too. Bring your questions to God, to a mentor or to your group.

11 · Ten minutes with the Bible

Now let me show you a simple way to spend about ten minutes with the Bible each day. It has four steps. Pray. Read or listen. Notice and ask. Pray it back.

Let us try it together with a short passage, Mark chapter 1, verses 16 to 18. First, pray. You might simply say, "Lord, open my eyes to see you in your word."

Second, read or listen, slowly. "Passing alongside the Sea of Galilee, he saw Simon and Andrew the brother of Simon casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen. And Jesus said to them, 'Follow me, and I will make you become fishers of men.' And immediately they left their nets and followed him."

12 · Notice, ask and pray it back

Third, notice and ask. Notice who is there: Jesus, Simon and Andrew. Notice where it happens: by the Sea of Galilee. Notice what comes before and after. Just before, Jesus has been preaching the gospel of God, as we saw in Lesson 1. Just after, he calls James and John too.

Then ask: what does this show me about Jesus? He calls Simon and Andrew in the middle of their ordinary work and commands them to follow him. So here is a truth to trust: Jesus still calls people to follow him in ordinary life. An action to take might be to obey one thing today that I already know Jesus asks of me. And a question to carry might be: what did it cost these men to leave their nets?

And ask one more thing: is this passage describing something, or commanding it? Jesus' call to follow him is for every disciple. But Mark is describing what these fishermen did. He is not telling every believer to leave their job. The Bible honestly describes many things it does not command, so we read each passage in its setting.

Fourth, pray it back. Turn what you noticed into a short prayer: "Jesus, thank you that you call me to follow you in ordinary life. Help me to follow you today." That is all. Pray, read or listen, notice and ask, pray it back. About ten minutes.

13 · Grace's story

Let me tell you about Grace. Her story is drawn from the experiences of many people. Grace sells vegetables at a market in Lusaka. She left school early and reads slowly, so she believed the Bible was for pastors and educated people.

When a leader at her church suggested she listen to an audio Bible on her phone, she tried ten minutes of Mark's Gospel each morning before customers arrived.

One morning she heard Mark 11:25: "And whenever you stand praying, forgive, if you have anything against anyone." She thought of the trader at the next stall. They had argued weeks before and had not spoken since. Grace decided she would no longer hold the quarrel against her. That afternoon, she greeted the woman and offered to watch her stall while she went for lunch. It was one small expression of what she had heard.

Some mornings Grace misses her listening time. She simply starts again the next day. God's word is for ordinary people, and small, steady steps matter more than perfect streaks.

14 · What beginning with the Bible is not

Because beginners often get stuck here, let me mention four common mistakes.

"Reading the Bible earns God's favour." We read because we are loved, not to be loved. Missed days are not a reason for guilt; simply continue.

"The Bible is only for educated people." God's word is for everyone, and listening counts fully.

"Any verse I open to is God's message for me today." Do not open the Bible at random and treat whatever verse you see as a personal message. Read passages in order, in their setting.

And "Everything people do in the Bible is an example to follow." The Bible honestly reports people's sins and failures. Not every description is a command.

15 · This week

Now, here is what I would like you to do this week. Your assignment is to study Mark 1:35 to 39 on your own, using the same four steps you have just practised. In step three, notice and ask, you will write three short lines: observe, understand and apply.

First, plan your Bible time. When, where and how will you spend about ten minutes with the Bible each day? Reading or listening? Keep it simple and realistic.

Second, pray, and then read Mark 1:35 to 39 slowly. Begin a few verses earlier, at verse 32, so that you see what happened the evening before. Third, notice and ask, and write three short lines. Observe: what happens, and who is there? Understand: what does this show me about Jesus? Apply: what is one truth to trust, one action to take, or one question to carry? And ask: is this passage describing something, or commanding it?

And finally, pray it back to God. Then continue through Mark with the seven-day reading plan below this lesson, using the same four steps. This assignment belongs to your First Step called my Bible. By the end of this week, you will have begun a habit that can last a lifetime.

16 · Remember

Let us bring it together. Start small, start with Jesus, and start today. God has spoken: the Bible is God's trustworthy word, a lamp to our feet. It points to Jesus: the whole story finds its centre and fulfilment in him. A little every day: steady reading or listening, beginning with a Gospel, and listening counts fully. Hear and do: each day, look for one truth to trust, one action to take, or one question to carry. And the daily method: pray, read or listen, notice and ask, pray it back.

17 · Prayer

Let us pray. Father, thank you that you have spoken to us. Thank you for your word, which leads me to Jesus. Open my eyes, that I may see wonderful things in your word. Help me to listen a little every day, and when I miss a day, to simply begin again. Make me a doer of your word, and not a hearer only. In Jesus' name. Amen.
