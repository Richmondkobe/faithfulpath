Lesson 3 of 8

Born Again: What God Has Done in You

People say I must be “born again.” What does that actually mean?

About 18 minutesJohn 3:1–16First Steps: My faith · My Bible

In this lesson

You will learn

  • Why even a respected religious teacher needed to be born again
  • What "again / from above" (anōthen) and "wind / Spirit" (pneuma) mean in John 3
  • From Above, By the Spirit, Made New, Born to Grow: what God does in the new birth
  • Why our confidence in God's work does not rest on feelings, and why growth follows
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · The question

Welcome to Lesson 3. In Lesson 2, we saw how we respond to God's grace: we turn, we trust, and we receive. Today we look at the other side: what God does in a person who receives Christ.

You have probably heard the phrase "born again." In some places it is a label for one kind of Christian. Some people use it only for those who had a dramatic conversion. Others picture a particular style of preaching or an emotional church service. But "born again" was not invented by a church or a movement. The words come from Jesus himself.

So here is our question: what does it actually mean to be born again? By the end of this lesson, you will be able to explain it from John chapter 3, in four simple parts.

2 · A visitor at night

Our passage is John chapter 3, verses 1 to 16. It begins with a man named Nicodemus. Listen to verses 1 and 2:

"Now there was a man of the Pharisees named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews. This man came to Jesus by night and said to him, 'Rabbi, we know that you are a teacher come from God, for no one can do these signs that you do unless God is with him.'"

The Pharisees were known for careful obedience to God's law. "A ruler of the Jews" means Nicodemus was probably one of the members of the council that led the Jewish people. Later Jesus calls him "the teacher of Israel." So he was respected, educated and deeply religious. John tells us he came at night. John does not say why, though he mentions it again later in his Gospel. And Nicodemus came respectfully, with a compliment.

3 · "You must be born again"

But Jesus did not respond with a compliment. He went straight to the heart of things. Verse 3: "Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God."

Nicodemus was confused. Verse 4: "How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother's womb and be born?" He was thinking of a second physical birth, which is impossible. Jesus was speaking of something else: a new beginning that only God can give.

And notice what this means. Nicodemus had religious knowledge, status and a serious commitment to God's law. Yet Jesus told even this respected religious teacher that he needed something he could not produce himself: new life from God.

4 · anōthen: again, from above

The Greek word translated "again" in verse 3 is anōthen. It can mean "again," and it can also mean "from above." Nicodemus hears it in the sense of "again" and thinks of a second physical birth.

But John uses the same word later in this chapter when he says, "He who comes from above is above all." There the meaning is clearly "from above."

Jesus is speaking about a birth whose source is God. It truly gives us a new beginning, but it is a new beginning that comes from above. You cannot reach up and produce it. It comes down to you.

5 · Water and Spirit

Jesus then explains more. Verse 5: "Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God."

Jesus expected Nicodemus to understand. A few verses later, he asks him, "Are you the teacher of Israel and yet you do not understand these things?" One important biblical background is Ezekiel 36. There God promises his people, "I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean," and, "I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you," and, "I will put my Spirit within you."

That promise brings cleansing, a new heart and God's Spirit together. It helps us understand Jesus' words about being born of water and the Spirit. Christians understand how this verse relates to baptism in different ways, and we will return to baptism in Book 2. But the main point is clear: new birth is God's cleansing and life-giving work.

6 · pneuma: wind and Spirit

Jesus also gave Nicodemus a picture. Verse 8: "The wind blows where it wishes, and you hear its sound, but you do not know where it comes from or where it goes. So it is with everyone who is born of the Spirit."

Here is something lovely about the Greek. In verse 8, the word translated "wind" and the word translated "Spirit" are the same Greek word: pneuma. Jesus is making a play on words. You cannot see the wind or control it, but you can see what it does: leaves move, washing dries, trees bend. The Spirit's work is like that. It is real even when it is quiet and unseen.

So today's two Greek words are anōthen, again and from above, and pneuma, wind or Spirit.

7 · Lifted up

Then Jesus showed how this new life comes. Verses 14 to 16: "And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life. For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life."

Jesus is pointing back to a story in Numbers chapter 21. After God's people in the wilderness spoke against God and Moses, they were dying from snake bites. God told Moses to make a bronze serpent and lift it up on a pole, and whoever looked to it lived. In the same way, Jesus would be lifted up on the cross, and whoever believes in him receives eternal life.

So the new birth and believing in Jesus belong together. God gives new life, and we receive it by trusting his Son.

8 · The short answer

So what does it mean to be born again? Here is the short answer. To be born again means that God himself gives a person new life.

When we receive Christ, God does something in us that we could never do for ourselves: by his Holy Spirit he gives us new life, makes us new, and begins a lifetime of growth. It is not a feeling we must work up, a religious label, or a self-improvement plan. It is God's work.

The rest of this lesson unpacks it in four parts: from above, by the Spirit, made new, and born to grow.

9 · Truth 1: From Above

The first part: from above. The new birth is God's work, not ours. No one gives birth to themselves. In the same way, we cannot make ourselves new. We turn and trust Christ, but the new life itself is God's gift.

John writes in chapter 1 about those who receive Christ: "But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God, who were born, not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God."

We genuinely receive Christ and believe in him; that is verse 12. But the source of the birth is God; that is verse 13. New life does not come from family descent, "blood," from human desire, "the will of the flesh," or simply from human parentage and initiative, "the will of man." Its ultimate source is God. That is humbling, because we cannot take credit for it. And it is freeing, because it does not depend on how strong or impressive we are.

10 · Truth 2: By the Spirit

The second part: by the Spirit. The new birth is the work of the Holy Spirit, who is God himself, present and at work in people's lives. We will learn much more about the Spirit in Book 2.

Paul describes it in Titus chapter 3: "But when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared, he saved us, not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit." "Regeneration" means rebirth or renewal: the giving of new life. Paul describes salvation here as God's merciful work through the Holy Spirit, not a result of our good works. John speaks of being born from above; Paul speaks here of regeneration and renewal. Both point us to God's life-giving work by the Spirit.

Remember the wind. Some people have a dramatic experience when they come to Christ. Many do not. Some can name the day; others cannot. Our confidence in God's work does not rest on having dramatic feelings, a particular experience, or remembering an exact date. It rests on Christ and God's promises.

And the Bible is clear that every believer has the Spirit. Paul writes, "Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him." Turn that around, and it means that everyone who belongs to Christ has the Spirit.

11 · Truth 3: Made New

The third part: made new. What does the Spirit do? He makes us new. Paul writes in 2 Corinthians 5: "Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come."

Remember Ezekiel's promise: "I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh." A heart of stone is cold and unresponsive to God. A heart of flesh is alive and able to respond to him. God begins to form new desires in us: love for him, a desire to please him and growing care for other people.

And he gives a new relationship. Those born of God become his children, and Paul says, "The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God."

But made new does not mean made perfect at once. A newborn baby is fully alive, but has a great deal of growing to do. New believers still struggle and still have old habits. That does not mean the new birth did not happen. It means growth has begun.

12 · Truth 4: Born to Grow

The fourth part: born to grow. Birth is a beginning, not the finish line. Peter writes to believers: "Like newborn infants, long for the pure spiritual milk, that by it you may grow up into salvation."

Salvation here means the full salvation God will complete one day; growing does not earn it. Babies need milk, care and a family to grow, and believers need nourishment too. Peter has just said that we have been born again "through the living and abiding word of God," and the same word helps us grow.

This growth is not a self-improvement programme. It is the Holy Spirit continuing his work in us, especially through three gifts God has given: the Bible, prayer and the church. That is why the last three lessons of this course are about each of them.

Growth is usually gradual. Some days you will notice change; other days you may feel nothing is happening. Do not be discouraged when growth feels slow. Keep receiving the nourishment God has given you. The one who gave you new life is still at work as you grow.

13 · Mei's story

Let me tell you about Mei. Her story is drawn from the experiences of many people. Mei grew up in Singapore in a family with no religion. In her second year at university, after months of reading the Gospels with a campus group, she quietly told God that she was trusting Jesus.

She had expected to feel something dramatic. She felt calm, but nothing more. That night she messaged her friend: "I think maybe it didn't work."

Her friend suggested they read John 3:8 together the next day. After reading it, her friend said, "Jesus compares the Spirit's work to the wind. You can't control the wind or see where it begins, but it is still real. God's work doesn't depend on whether you feel something dramatic."

Over the following weeks, Mei noticed small changes. She wanted to read the Bible, not only because the group expected it. After an argument with her mother, she apologised, something she had never done before. She still had doubts on some days. These changes did not earn her anything, and they were not a test she had to pass. But they were signs that something new had begun to grow.

Mei now meets a mentor from her church each week. Many believers' experience is like this: God's work often begins quietly, and feelings are not the measure of it.

14 · What the new birth is not

Because this subject is often confused, here are four common misunderstandings.

"Born again means a dramatic experience I can describe." Some people have one; many do not. The Spirit's work is real even when it is quiet.

"If I cannot name the day, I am probably not born again." You do not need to name a date. The new birth is God's work, not something proved by remembering when it happened. We will look at assurance in Lesson 5.

"If I have not spoken in tongues, I do not have the Spirit." Everyone who belongs to Christ has the Holy Spirit. Christians differ about later experiences of the Spirit, but the Bible is clear about this.

And "I can make myself born again by trying hard." The new birth is God's work, from above. Our part is to turn to him and trust Jesus.

15 · This week

Now, because this is true, here is what I would like you to do this week. Your assignment is to study John 3 and explain what God does in the new birth.

First, read John 3:1 to 16 slowly, and mark every phrase that shows God at work: born again, born of water and the Spirit, God so loved, he gave. Second, write a short explanation, in your own words, of what God does in the new birth, using the four parts: from above, by the Spirit, made new and born to grow. Then finish this sentence: "Because new life is God's gift, this week I will…"

Third, choose one way to feed your new life this week: a few minutes in the Bible each day, a simple daily prayer, or joining a church gathering. Start small, and do it. And finally, thank God that new life is his gift, not your achievement.

If you have trusted Christ, you might also notice one small sign of new life, or one area where you want to grow, and keep it between you and God. If you are still exploring, write down one question you have about the new birth. This assignment belongs to two of your First Steps: my faith, and my Bible. Below this lesson you will also find the seven-day reading plan.

16 · Remember

Let us bring it together. To be born again means that God himself gives a person new life. From above: the new birth is God's work; we cannot give birth to ourselves. By the Spirit: the Holy Spirit gives new life; like the wind, his work is real even when it is unseen, and every believer has the Spirit. Made new: God gives a new heart and makes us his children; made new does not mean made perfect at once. Born to grow: birth is a beginning, and the Spirit grows new life through the Bible, prayer and the church.

17 · Prayer

Let us pray. Father, thank you that new life comes from you. Thank you that you so loved the world that you gave your only Son. Thank you for the work of your Holy Spirit, even when it is quiet and unseen. Thank you for the new heart you give. Help me to love you and want to please you more. Help me to grow through your word, through prayer and through your people. Where I still have questions, help me to bring them honestly to Jesus, as Nicodemus did. In Jesus' name. Amen.
