Creation, Human Dignity, Sin and the Fall

If God made everything good, why is the world so broken? And why should I treat every person with respect?

About 18 minutesGenesis 1:26–31 and 3:1–15My Foundations: My beliefs

In this lesson

You will learn

  • What Genesis 1 and 3 say about the world, human beings and sin
  • What it means that every person is made in God's image
  • The four parts: Created Good, Made in His Image, Broken by Sin, Promised Hope
  • Why harm done to us is not our guilt, and how God promised hope from the beginning
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Why is the world so broken?

Welcome to Lesson 3 of Establish. In Begin, you heard that Jesus came to deal with sin, and in Lesson 2 we saw that the one God, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, is the Creator. Now we go one step further, back to the first pages of the Bible, to see what God made, what went wrong, and what God promised.

Look around, and you see two things at once. The world is full of beauty: mountains and oceans, a child's laugh, music, kindness, the skill of a craftsman. And the world is full of brokenness: war, disease, cruelty, loneliness, greed and injustice. People are capable of great love and terrible harm. And in a world where some people are treated as important and others as worthless, what is a human being really worth?

So here is our question: if God made everything good, why is the world so broken? And why should I treat every person with respect?

2 · "In his own image"

Our passages are Genesis chapter 1, verses 26 to 31, and Genesis chapter 3, verses 1 to 15. They are longer than usual, so we will read the key verses together. Genesis 1, verse 26: "Then God said, 'Let us make man in our image, after our likeness. And let them have dominion…'"

Verse 27: "So God created man in his own image, in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them." And verse 28 begins: "And God blessed them." At the end of the chapter, verse 31 says, "And God saw everything that he had made, and behold, it was very good."

3 · Shame, hiding and blaming

Now Genesis 3. Verse 1: the serpent asked the woman, "Did God actually say, 'You shall not eat of any tree in the garden'?" The first humans were invited to doubt God's goodness, and they took the fruit God had forbidden.

Let us use the first question from Lesson 1: what does it say? Notice what happened next. There was shame: "they knew that they were naked." There was hiding: they "hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God." And there was blaming. The man said, "The woman whom you gave to be with me, she gave me fruit of the tree, and I ate," and the woman said, "The serpent deceived me." Relationships that had been open and good were suddenly broken.

4 · "Where are you?"

But the story does not end with hiding. Verse 9: "But the LORD God called to the man and said to him, 'Where are you?'"

And then, speaking to the serpent, God said in verse 15: "I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and her offspring; he shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel." Keep that verse in mind. We will come back to it.

5 · Image = ṣelem

Our Hebrew word today is the word translated "image" in verse 27: ṣelem. It means an image or likeness.

To understand why it matters, we need a little background, and we will look at that next. But first, notice the simple point of the verse: God made human beings, male and female, in his image. We are not gods, but God made us to represent his good rule in the world and to live in relationship with him.

6 · Every person, not only kings

Genesis was first given to Israel, a people living among the nations of the ancient Near East. There, images could represent the presence or authority of a ruler or a god, and kings were sometimes given a special representative status.

Against that wider royal background, Genesis says something remarkable: God's image is not limited to a king or a ruling class. Humanity, male and female, is made in God's image. And notice the passage itself: "in our image" is followed at once by "let them have dominion." The farmer, the servant, the mother, the child: each one is made to represent God's good rule in his world. Psalm 8 marvels at this: "What is man that you are mindful of him…?" and "Yet you have made him a little lower than the heavenly beings and crowned him with glory and honor."

7 · The short answer

So, if God made everything good, why is the world so broken? Here is the short answer. The world is beautiful because God made it good. It is broken because humanity turned away from God. And every person, without exception, is made in God's image.

God made a good world. He made every human being in his image, with equal dignity. Sin has broken what God made, but from the very beginning God promised hope. We will look at this in four parts: created good, made in his image, broken by sin, and promised hope.

8 · Part 1: Created Good

The first part: created good. "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth." God alone is uncreated. Everything else exists because he made it, and he keeps it in being.

Again and again in Genesis 1, God looks at what he has made and sees that it is good. At the end, it is very good. This matters. The physical world is not evil, and it is not unimportant. Bodies, food, work, rivers and animals are good gifts from a good God. "The earth is the LORD's and the fullness thereof."

God also gave human beings a special responsibility. He told them to have dominion, and he put the first man in the garden "to work it and keep it." Dominion does not mean permission to exploit or destroy. It means caring for what belongs to God, as his trusted servants.

9 · Part 2: Made in His Image

The second part: made in his image. Notice who bears God's image: every human being. Not only the rich, the educated, the powerful or the healthy. Not only one nation, one tribe or one race. Paul told the people of Athens that God "made from one man every nation of mankind." Every person, of every nation, gender, age, ability, wealth and status, bears God's image and has equal dignity.

This is why every person deserves respect. James says it is wrong to bless God and then curse "people who are made in the likeness of God." Sin has deeply damaged human life, but it has not erased God's image. Even after the Fall, Scripture still speaks of human beings as made in God's likeness.

So contempt, prejudice, racism, exploitation and treating anyone as less than human are not small matters. They are offences against God, whose image every person bears. And if you have been treated as worthless, this truth speaks to you: your dignity does not depend on anyone's opinion of you. God made you in his image.

10 · Part 3: Broken by Sin

The third part: broken by sin. In Genesis 3, the serpent's question invited the first humans to doubt God's goodness and to decide for themselves what was good and evil. They chose to distrust God and go their own way.

Sin is not only breaking rules. At its heart, it is turning away from God: distrusting him, rejecting his good rule, and choosing our own way. It includes both the wrong we do and the good we fail to do. And it broke every relationship. With God: they hid from him. With each other: they blamed one another. With themselves: they felt shame. And with creation: the ground would now bring thorns and hard labour, and Paul says creation itself now groans.

This brokenness reaches every one of us. "All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God." It is in the world around us, and it is in our own hearts.

11 · Two things to say clearly

Two things need to be said clearly here. First, Genesis 3:16 describes the painful consequences of humanity's fall into sin. It is not permission for men to dominate, harm or silence women. Whatever Christians conclude elsewhere about marriage and church leadership, this verse never makes sinful domination God's ideal. Remember Lesson 1: not everything the Bible describes is approved.

Second, we must not confuse sin we commit with harm done to us. We are each responsible for our own sins. But we are also wounded by the sins of others. Someone who was abused, cheated or mistreated is not guilty of the wrong done to them. Guilt belongs to the one who did wrong. And we should not assume that a person's illness, disability or suffering is punishment for a particular sin. When Jesus' disciples asked whose sin had caused a man's blindness, Jesus answered, "It was not that this man sinned, or his parents."

12 · Part 4: Promised Hope

The fourth part: promised hope. Genesis 3 is not only a story of judgement. Look at what God does. He seeks them: "Where are you?" God did not wait for them to come back. He came looking for them. He clothes them: "the LORD God made for Adam and for his wife garments of skins and clothed them." Even as they faced the consequences of their sin, God cared for them.

And he promises. Genesis 3:15 does not name Jesus. But Christians have long read it as the Bible's first glimpse of the hope that God would one day defeat evil through a descendant of the woman, though that victory would involve suffering. Paul writes, "The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet." And in Romans 5, he explains that just as one man's sin brought ruin to the human family, so one man, Jesus Christ, brings righteousness and life. Lesson 4 will tell that part of the story.

So the Bible's answer to "Why is the world so broken?" is not the end of the story. From the very first pages, God was already promising to put things right.

13 · Christians differ

Christians differ on two questions here, and we will name them briefly. First, how to read the days of Genesis 1. Some Christians understand them as ordinary days; others understand them differently, noting the chapter's highly ordered pattern. Second, how Adam's sin affects us. Scripture teaches that sin came into the world through one man, and that we are each responsible for our own sins. Churches explain how these truths fit together in different ways.

This lesson does not try to settle those questions. Christians agree on what Genesis clearly teaches: God is the Creator; creation belongs to him; human beings bear his image; sin has brought real disorder into God's good world; and every person needs God's grace in Christ. You will find a short note in Go deeper, below this lesson.

14 · Maricel's story

Let me tell you about Maricel. Her story is drawn from the experiences of many people. Maricel is from the Philippines and works as a domestic helper in Hong Kong. Her employer often speaks to her harshly. After six years, she had started to believe she was worth less than the people she served. On Sundays she attends a Bible group with other Filipino workers.

One Sunday the group read Genesis 1:27. The leader asked, "Who is made in God's image?" The woman beside her answered, "All of us. Our employers too, and us too."

Maricel began to understand that her employer's treatment did not define her worth. Her friend told her about a worker-support centre that could explain her rights. The group also helped her bring her anger and pain honestly to God, and to see the difference between the wrong done to her, for which she was not responsible, and how she wanted to carry that pain before God. Maricel is now considering her next safe step. People treated as less than human need to hear that they bear God's image, and that same truth calls all of us to treat others with dignity.

15 · What this lesson is not saying

Here are four common mistakes.

"The physical world is unimportant, or evil." God made it very good, and it belongs to him.

"Some people are worth more than others." Every person bears God's image and has equal dignity.

"If I was harmed, I must share the blame." Guilt belongs to the one who did wrong.

And "Genesis 3 is only a story of judgement." God sought, clothed and promised hope.

16 · This week

This week, your assignment is to honour God's image in others, and to bring your own brokenness to God.

First, read Genesis 1:26 to 31 and 3:1 to 15. Second, write one short line for each of the four parts, in your own words. You can use the book to help. Third, notice one person whom others commonly overlook. If it is appropriate and safe, ask what would show respect, or offer practical help, rather than assuming what they need. If that is not possible, pray for them.

And fourth, follow the seven-day reading plan below this lesson. You will also find a Look back section, where you use the four questions from Lesson 1 on Genesis 1:26 to 31. This assignment belongs to the My Foundations area called my beliefs.

17 · Remember

Let us bring it together. The world is beautiful because God made it good, and broken because humanity turned from him. But God has promised hope.

Created good: God alone is uncreated, and he made everything very good. Made in his image: every person bears God's image and has equal dignity. Broken by sin: turning from God broke every relationship; we sin, and we are also sinned against. And promised hope: God sought, clothed and promised that evil would be defeated.

18 · Prayer

Let us pray. Creator God, thank you for your good world, and for making every person in your image, including me. Forgive me for the ways I have turned from you, and for the times I have treated others as less than they are. Heal what sin has broken in me and around me. Thank you that you came looking for us, and that from the very beginning you promised hope. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Pause and seek help

If you have been told, in words or by how you were treated, that you are worthless, Genesis 1:27 speaks to you: your dignity does not depend on anyone else's opinion, and shame for what others did to you is not yours to carry. If you are being exploited, for example by an employer who takes your documents, withholds wages or threatens you, seek help from a trusted worker-support organisation, embassy, legal advice service or safe person, and see the Support Page "I am in danger or being abused." If you have thoughts of harming yourself, please reach out now to a crisis line or emergency services where it is safe to do so.

Go deeper

The days of Genesis 1. Some Christians understand them as ordinary days, noting the pattern of evening and morning and the connection with the Sabbath in Exodus 20:11. Other Christians understand the days differently, noting features within Genesis 1 itself and the chapter's highly ordered pattern. This course does not attempt to settle that question.

How Adam's sin affects us. Scripture teaches that "sin came into the world through one man" and spread to all people (Romans 5:12), and that we are each responsible for our own sins (Ezekiel 18:20). Churches explain how these truths fit together in different ways. All agree that every person needs God's grace in Christ.
