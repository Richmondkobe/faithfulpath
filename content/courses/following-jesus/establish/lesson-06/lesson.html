The Holy Spirit: Presence, Power, Fruit and Gifts

People talk about the Holy Spirit in very different ways: quietly in some churches, loudly in others. Who is he, and what should I expect from him?

About 18 minutesActs 2:1–21My Foundations: My life in the Spirit

In this lesson

You will learn

  • What happened at Pentecost, from Acts 2:1–21
  • What it means to be a "witness", and where the Spirit's power is pointed
  • The four parts: Present With Us, Power to Witness, Fruit of Character, Gifts for Others
  • How to welcome the Spirit's work and test every claim by Scripture
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Who is he, and what should I expect?

Welcome to Lesson 6 of Establish. In Begin, you learned that the new birth is the work of the Holy Spirit, and in Lesson 2 we saw that the Holy Spirit is fully God, a person and not a force. Now we go one step further and ask what he does.

Visit a few churches and you will notice a difference. In some, the Holy Spirit is mentioned quietly, in a prayer or a creed. In others, people pray loudly, speak in tongues, prophesy and expect healing. Some Christians are excited about the Spirit; others are nervous. Many new believers are simply confused.

So here is our question: who is the Holy Spirit, and what should I expect from him?

2 · The day of Pentecost

Our passage is Acts chapter 2, verses 1 to 21. It is the day of Pentecost, and Jesus' followers are gathered in Jerusalem. Listen to verses 1 to 4: "When the day of Pentecost arrived, they were all together in one place. And suddenly there came from heaven a sound like a mighty rushing wind, and it filled the entire house where they were sitting. And divided tongues as of fire appeared to them and rested on each one of them. And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit gave them utterance."

Visitors from many nations heard these believers "telling in our own tongues the mighty works of God."

3 · On all flesh

Then Peter stood up and explained what was happening: "But this is what was uttered through the prophet Joel: 'And in the last days it shall be, God declares, that I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh, and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy.'" And Peter ends the quotation with this promise: "And it shall come to pass that everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord shall be saved."

Let us use the first question from Lesson 1: what does it say? Notice two things. First, the Spirit's outpouring is not restricted to one small class of people: sons and daughters, young and old, even servants receive God's Spirit. Second, notice the result. Jesus was proclaimed, and about three thousand people came to faith that day.

4 · Witness = martys

Our Greek word today is martys, which means "witness." Just before he ascended, Jesus told his followers, "you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be my witnesses." And in his sermon at Pentecost, Peter says, "This Jesus God raised up, and of that we all are witnesses."

A martys is a witness, someone who bears testimony. In Acts, the apostles bear witness to what they have seen and heard, especially that Jesus has risen. A witness does not invent the story or make it about themselves. They point to what really happened. So the Spirit's power in Acts is not given mainly for show. It is power to tell the truth about Jesus.

5 · Pentecost: the Feast of Weeks

Now the background. Pentecost was a Jewish festival, also called the Feast of Weeks. It came fifty days after Passover, and it was a harvest festival. The name Pentecost simply means "fiftieth."

At this festival, Jerusalem was full of pilgrims. Luke says there were "devout men from every nation under heaven." They were amazed, because the people speaking were ordinary Galileans, yet each visitor heard them in their own language. On the very day Jerusalem was full of nations, God's message began to go out to the nations.

6 · Peter takes us back to Joel

Now use our third question from Lesson 1: where does this connect in the Bible's larger story? Peter himself takes us back to the prophet Joel. Centuries earlier, God had promised, "I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh; your sons and your daughters shall prophesy."

In much of the Old Testament, we read of the Spirit coming on particular people for particular tasks: prophets, leaders and craftsmen. Joel looked forward to a day when God would pour out his Spirit on all his people. At Pentecost, Peter says, "this is what was uttered through the prophet Joel." The promised day had come.

7 · The short answer

So, who is the Holy Spirit, and what should I expect? Here is the short answer. The Holy Spirit is God, present in every believer. He gives power to witness to Jesus, grows the fruit of Christlike character, and gives gifts for the good of others. We welcome his work gladly, and we test every claim by Scripture.

We will look at it in four parts: present with us, power to witness, fruit of character, and gifts for others.

8 · Part 1: Present With Us

The first part: present with us. The night before he died, Jesus promised his disciples, "And I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Helper, to be with you forever, even the Spirit of truth." The Holy Spirit is not a force or an energy. He is God, and he lives in every believer. Paul writes, "Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him." So if you belong to Christ, the Spirit is already with you.

What does he do? Jesus promised that the Spirit would teach his apostles and guide them into truth, and through their witness, preserved in Scripture, he still leads Christ's people in truth. He assures us that we are God's children. He helps us pray when we do not know what to say. And above all, he points to Jesus. "He will glorify me," Jesus said.

9 · Part 2: Power to Witness

The second part: power to witness. Jesus said, "you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be my witnesses." At Pentecost, that is exactly what happened. Frightened disciples, who had hidden behind locked doors, began to speak boldly about Jesus in the streets of Jerusalem. The Spirit's power is power to live for Jesus and speak about him.

Paul also tells believers, "be filled with the Spirit." This is not about getting more of the Spirit, as if he came in pieces. In this course we take it as a call to stay open to him, so that he fills and empowers us for each new day. And Jesus taught his disciples that the Father delights to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him: "how much more will the heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him!" So Christians need not be afraid to ask God for the Spirit's help, filling and empowering. Believers face real spiritual conflict, but they do not need to live in fear, because "he who is in you is greater than he who is in the world."

10 · Part 3: Fruit of Character

The third part: fruit of character. Paul writes, "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control." The Spirit's work is not only powerful moments. It is also slow, patient growth in Christlike character.

Paul wrote to a church that was very excited about spiritual gifts, and he told them, "If I speak in the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I am a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal." Love matters more than any gift. So fruit is a more reliable sign of growing maturity than dramatic experiences. And fruit is not another anxious test of whether you are saved. It is the Spirit's patient work in God's children, as we cooperate with him.

11 · Part 4: Gifts for Others

The fourth part: gifts for others. Paul writes, "To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good." The New Testament mentions many gifts: teaching, serving, encouraging, giving, leading, showing mercy, wisdom, faith, healing, prophecy, speaking in tongues and interpreting tongues. These lists overlap, and none is complete. The Spirit gives them "to each one individually as he wills."

Three things are clear. Gifts are for others, not for status. They are governed by love; that is why Paul's great chapter on love sits right in the middle of his teaching on gifts. And they are used in order: "all things should be done decently and in order." Paul thanked God for the gift of tongues, and in a gathering he wanted tongues to be interpreted, so that everyone is built up. No gift makes one believer better than another.

12 · Welcome and test

Scripture holds two things together. Paul writes, "Do not quench the Spirit. Do not despise prophecies, but test everything; hold fast what is good." We welcome the Spirit's work warmly, and we also test every claim, prophecy and experience. John writes, "do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God."

How do we test? A genuine work of the Spirit points to Jesus and builds people up. A claimed work of the Spirit must never contradict Scripture. And it produces the fruit of the Spirit, not manipulation, fear or self-exaltation. We test with wise counsel in the church, not alone. And remember: spiritual experiences can be real gifts from God, but they are never the proof of salvation. Our assurance rests on Christ and his promises.

13 · Christians differ

Across historic Christian traditions, Christians affirm the Holy Spirit's essential place in the Christian life, while differing over how baptism in the Spirit, filling and particular gifts should be understood. We will name two questions briefly. First, baptism in the Holy Spirit and speaking in tongues. Many churches teach that, after the new birth, believers should seek a distinct experience of being baptised in the Spirit, which empowers them for witness, with speaking in tongues as its initial evidence. Other Christians understand being baptised in the Spirit as part of coming to faith, with later fillings of the Spirit.

Second, whether all the gifts continue today. Many churches believe that all the gifts, including prophecy, tongues and healing, continue today. Some believe that certain gifts were especially connected to the apostles' foundational ministry. This course teaches that everyone who belongs to Christ has the Spirit. It also encourages believers to seek the Spirit's continuing filling and empowering. We welcome all the gifts described in the New Testament today, tested by Scripture and practised in love, while respecting faithful Christians who see it differently. There is a short note in Go deeper, below this lesson.

14 · Chidi's story

Let me tell you about Chidi. His story is drawn from the experiences of many people. Chidi works as a bank clerk in Lagos. At a large meeting, the minister called people forward to receive the Holy Spirit. Ushers gathered around Chidi and kept urging him, "Speak! Just let it out!" After a long time, he made some sounds so that they would stop. He went home feeling empty and fake, wondering whether he was a second-class Christian.

The following week he told his own pastor. She opened Romans 8:9 and 1 Corinthians 12:11. "If you belong to Christ, the Spirit lives in you already," she said. "The Spirit gives gifts as he wills. You can ask God to fill and empower you afresh, but no one should ever pressure you or ask you to fake anything." Then she showed him Galatians 5:22 and 23. "Look for this fruit too. The Spirit grows it patiently."

Chidi still asks God to fill and empower him afresh, and he is open to every gift God may give. He no longer feels like a lesser Christian, and he has noticed that he is becoming more patient with difficult customers. Many believers feel pressured to produce experiences. They need to hear that the Spirit is already present, generous and gentle.

15 · What this lesson is not saying

Here are four common mistakes.

"The Holy Spirit is a force or a feeling." He is God, a person, living in every believer.

"Some Christians have the Spirit and others don't." Everyone who belongs to Christ has the Spirit.

"Believers with certain gifts are better Christians." Gifts are for others, and love matters more than any gift.

And "I must produce an experience to prove I have the Spirit." No one should ever be pressured or asked to fake anything. The Spirit is already present, and he gives as he wills.

16 · This week

This week, your assignment is to welcome the Spirit's work: to speak of Jesus, to grow in his fruit, and to serve others with what he gives.

First, read Acts 2:1 to 21 twice. Second, write one short line for each of the four parts, in your own words. You can use the book to help. Third, each day, ask the Holy Spirit to help you live for Jesus and speak about him. Choose one fruit of the Spirit to grow in this week. 17 · Remember

Let us bring it together. The Holy Spirit is God, present in every believer. We welcome his work, and we test every claim by Scripture.

Present with us: he lives in every believer, teaches, assures us and helps us pray. Power to witness: he empowers ordinary believers to live for Jesus and speak about him, so keep being filled. Fruit of character: he grows love, joy, peace and Christlike character, a more reliable sign of maturity than any display. And gifts for others: he gives as he wills, for the common good, governed by love.

18 · Prayer

Let us pray. Father, thank you for giving your Holy Spirit to all who belong to Jesus. Holy Spirit, thank you that you are with me. Fill me afresh, and give me courage to speak of Jesus. Grow your fruit in me, especially where I need it most, and help me use your gifts in love, for the good of others. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Pause and seek help

Sometimes the Spirit's name is misused to pressure or control people: being pushed to speak in tongues or to fall down; being touched or held without consent during prayer; receiving a "prophecy" about whom to marry, how much money to give or when to leave a job, with pressure to obey; or being told that an illness or mental health struggle is only demonic. A prophecy must never replace a person's consent, responsible judgement, Scripture, medical facts or appropriate professional advice. You may say no, and you may ask for time to test what you have heard. Some experiences, such as hearing voices, intense fear of evil, or sudden changes in mood or behaviour, may have medical or mental health causes; seeking help is not a lack of faith, and prayer can go alongside it. Speak with a trustworthy person outside the situation, and see the Support Page "I am being controlled through prophecy, spiritual language, money or church authority." If you are distressed or unsafe, please seek help now.

Go deeper

Baptism in the Holy Spirit and speaking in tongues. Across historic Christian traditions, Christians affirm the Holy Spirit's essential place in the Christian life, while differing over how baptism in the Spirit, filling and particular gifts should be understood. Many churches teach that, after the new birth, believers should seek a distinct experience of being baptised in the Spirit, which empowers them for witness, with speaking in tongues as its initial evidence (they often point to Acts 2:4; 10:44–46; 19:6). Other Christians understand being baptised in the Spirit as part of coming to faith, with later fillings of the Spirit (they often point to 1 Corinthians 12:13, and to Paul's question "Do all speak with tongues?" in 12:30). This course teaches that everyone who belongs to Christ has the Spirit (Romans 8:9), and encourages believers to seek the Spirit's continuing filling and empowering (Ephesians 5:18).

Whether all the gifts continue today. Many churches believe all the gifts, including prophecy, tongues and healing, continue today (they often point to 1 Corinthians 13:8–12 and 14:39). Some churches believe that certain gifts were especially connected to the apostles' foundational ministry and no longer function in the same way (they often point to Ephesians 2:20 and Hebrews 2:3–4). This course welcomes all the gifts described in the New Testament today, tested by Scripture and practised in love, while respecting faithful Christians who see it differently. Ask a trustworthy church leader how your church approaches these matters, and compare what you hear with Scripture.

Tongues in private and in public. Paul describes speaking in tongues as prayer and praise to God that builds up the speaker (1 Corinthians 14:2, 4, 14–15), and he thanked God for the gift (14:18). In a gathering, tongues should be interpreted so that everyone is built up, and people should speak one at a time (14:5, 27–28). Paul welcomed prayer in tongues, but he did not treat it as a mark of greater spiritual worth. Simple prayer in your own language is not second-class prayer (Begin, Lesson 7).
