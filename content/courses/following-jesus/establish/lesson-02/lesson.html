The One God: Father, Son and Holy Spirit

Christians say there is one God, but they also speak of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Do Christians worship three gods?

Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Do Christians worship three gods?

Welcome to Lesson 2 of Establish. In Begin, you learned to pray "Our Father," and you saw that the new birth is the work of the Holy Spirit. Now we are going one step further. We step back and ask what the Bible teaches about God himself.

It is a fair question, and many people ask it: Muslim and Jewish friends, people of other religions, people with no religion, and many Christians too. Christians pray "Our Father." They worship Jesus. They speak of being filled with the Holy Spirit. Yet they insist that there is only one God. How does that fit together?

So here is our question: do Christians worship three gods? By the end of this lesson, you will be able to answer from Scripture, simply and humbly.

2 · Jesus comes to the Jordan

Our passage is Matthew chapter 3, verses 13 to 17, the account of Jesus' baptism. Verse 13: "Then Jesus came from Galilee to the Jordan to John, to be baptized by him."

John was surprised. Verse 14: "John would have prevented him, saying, 'I need to be baptized by you, and do you come to me?'" Verse 15: "But Jesus answered him, 'Let it be so now, for thus it is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness.' Then he consented."

3 · The Father, the Son and the Spirit

Now listen to verses 16 and 17: "And when Jesus was baptized, immediately he went up from the water, and behold, the heavens were opened to him, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and coming to rest on him; and behold, a voice from heaven said, 'This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased.'"

Let us use the first question from Lesson 1: what does it say? Notice who is present. The Son stands in the water. The Spirit comes down on him. The Father speaks from heaven. Father, Son and Spirit are present and active at the same moment, and the Father's words express his love and pleasure in the Son.

This passage does not explain everything about God. But read alongside the rest of Scripture, it helps us see why Christians speak of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit as truly distinct.

4 · "The LORD is one"

Our one Hebrew word today comes from the most famous sentence in the Old Testament, Deuteronomy 6:4: "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one." The word for one is ʾeḥād.

In its setting, Moses was speaking to Israel as they were about to enter a land full of other gods. The point of the verse is that the LORD alone is God. Israel was not to share its worship with any other god.

We should not try to make this word prove the Trinity, and it does not. Its meaning is plain: there is one God, and only one. Christians hold to that as firmly as anyone.

5 · The prayer Jesus' people prayed

Now the background. This sentence is called the Shema, from its first word, "Hear." The Shema became a central confession of Jewish faith, traditionally recited morning and evening. Jesus himself quoted it when asked which commandment was most important.

His answer began with these very words: "Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God, the Lord is one." And through the prophet Isaiah, God declared, "I am the LORD, and there is no other, besides me there is no God."

The first Christians were Jewish believers who held firmly to this truth. So when they worshipped Jesus and spoke of the Holy Spirit, they were not adding new gods. They were describing what the one God had shown them.

6 · A voice the Scriptures had prepared

John was calling the people of Israel to turn back to God and be baptised in the Jordan River. When Jesus came, the words from heaven would have sounded familiar to anyone who knew the Scriptures.

In Psalm 2, God says to his chosen King, "You are my Son." And in Isaiah 42, God speaks of his servant: "my chosen, in whom my soul delights; I have put my Spirit upon him."

At the Jordan, these two promises meet in Jesus. The Father calls him Son, and the Spirit rests upon him. This is the third question from Lesson 1, where it points: the Old Testament prepared God's people to recognise who Jesus is.

7 · The short answer

So, do Christians worship three gods? Here is the short answer. No. Christians do not worship three gods. The Bible teaches that there is one God, and it also shows us the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, each truly God.

This one God is eternally Father, Son and Holy Spirit: truly distinct, fully divine and perfectly united. And this God draws us into his own life of love.

Christians call this the Trinity, a word meaning tri-unity, or three-in-oneness. The word itself is not in the Bible, but it is a short way of summing up what the Bible shows. We will look at it in four parts: one God; Father, Son and Spirit; equal and united; and drawing us in.

8 · Part 1: One God

The first part: one God. The Bible could not be clearer: there is only one true God. Everything else we say about the Father, the Son and the Spirit stands on this foundation.

Christians are not polytheists. We do not worship three gods. We worship the one living God, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, the God Jesus himself worshipped and obeyed.

9 · Part 2: Father, Son and Spirit

The second part: Father, Son and Spirit. At the same time, the Bible shows us the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit as distinct from one another. They are not simply three names for the same person.

The Father speaks to the Son and about the Son, as we heard at the Jordan. The Son prays to the Father, as in John 17. Jesus says, "I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Helper," the Holy Spirit. And before creation, the Father loved the Son.

When Jesus sent out his disciples, he told them to baptise "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit." Notice: one name, and three named together. Christians often use the word person for the Father, the Son and the Spirit. Here, person does not mean a separate being, or one third of God. It is a way of speaking about the real distinction Scripture shows, while affirming that they are the one God.

10 · Part 3: Equal and United

The third part: equal and united. The Bible speaks of each of the three as fully God. The Father is God: Jesus calls him "the only true God." The Son is God: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." When Thomas met the risen Jesus, he said, "My Lord and my God!" and Jesus accepted his worship. The Holy Spirit is God: Peter asked Ananias, "Why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit?" and then said, "You have not lied to man but to God." Peter's words speak of lying to the Holy Spirit as lying to God.

So the Father, the Son and the Spirit are not three parts of God, each one third divine. Nor are they one person wearing three different masks. None is less divine than the others. In the work of salvation, the Father sends, the Son comes, and the Spirit is given. But different roles do not mean that one is less God than another.

11 · A mystery, not a contradiction

Is this hard to understand? Yes. The Bible does not explain everything about God, and we should not pretend that it does. But a mystery is not a contradiction. Christians do not say one God and three gods. They say there is one God: Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Our minds are small; God is great. Humility is the right response.

This is also why it is best to avoid most illustrations. An egg, with its shell, white and yolk, suggests that God has three parts. Water as ice, liquid and steam suggests that one person keeps changing form. A man who is a father, a son and a husband suggests one person with three roles. None of these fits what the Bible shows. It is better to let Scripture speak, and to admit that no picture fully works.

12 · Part 4: Drawing Us In

The fourth part: drawing us in. The Trinity is not only something to explain. It shapes the whole Christian life.

It shapes how we pray. Paul writes, "For through him we both have access in one Spirit to the Father." Christian prayer is ordinarily offered to the Father, through the Son, by the Spirit. That is the pattern you began in Begin, when you learned to pray "Our Father." The New Testament also shows believers praying to Jesus. As he was dying, Stephen prayed, "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit." Because the Father, the Son and the Spirit are the one God, all three are worthy of worship.

It shapes how we are saved. "God sent forth his Son," and "God has sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts." The Father sends the Son, the Son gives himself for us, and the Spirit brings us into God's family.

And it shows us that God is love. God did not begin to love when he made the world. Before anything was made, the Father loved the Son. Love belongs to who God has always been, and this God invites us into his love.

13 · A note on Oneness teaching

Christians differ on some questions, and this lesson needs to name one difference briefly and fairly. Most churches throughout history have confessed that there is one God who is eternally Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

Some churches, often called Oneness churches, teach that God is one, and that Father, Son and Holy Spirit are different ways in which the one God is revealed and acts. Oneness believers strongly affirm that there is one God and that Jesus is fully divine, but they understand the relationship between the Father, the Son and the Spirit differently.

Because this question concerns who God is, this course treats the Bible's teaching on the Father, the Son and the Spirit as a central truth, not a minor difference. But this never gives anyone permission to mock, mistreat or pressure people from Oneness churches. Welcome them warmly, listen to them fairly, and look at the Scriptures carefully together. Lesson 7 returns to the related question of baptism in Jesus' name.

14 · Daniel's story

Let me tell you about Daniel. His story is drawn from the experiences of many people. Daniel is a university student in Jakarta. His closest friend at university, Arif, is a Muslim. One afternoon Arif asked him, kindly but directly, "Why do Christians worship three gods? We believe God is one."

Daniel tried to explain using an egg: shell, white and yolk. Arif smiled. "So God has three parts?" Daniel realised he did not know how to answer.

He asked his pastor for help. They read Deuteronomy 6:4, Matthew 3:16 and 17, and John 1:1 together. The next week, Daniel told Arif, "You are right that God is one. Christians believe that too. We also believe the Bible shows the one God as Father, Son and Holy Spirit. I can't explain it fully, but I can show you where it comes from."

Arif still disagreed. But he said, "At least now I understand what you actually believe." Daniel and Arif remain good friends, and they still talk about God sometimes.

15 · What the Trinity is not

Here are four common mistakes.

"Christians worship three gods." There is one God. Christians hold to Deuteronomy 6:4 as firmly as anyone.

"God has three parts." The Father, the Son and the Spirit are each fully God, not one third of God.

"God is one person who changes masks or roles." The Father, the Son and the Spirit truly relate to and love one another.

And "I must fully understand the Trinity before I can trust Christ." No one fully understands God. We trust him as he has shown himself in Scripture.

17 · Remember

Let us bring it together. Christians do not worship three gods. The Bible shows one God: Father, Son and Holy Spirit.

One God: the LORD our God, the LORD is one. Father, Son and Spirit: distinct, and truly relating to one another. Equal and united: each fully God, acting in perfect unity; a mystery, not a contradiction. And drawing us in: we pray to the Father, through the Son, by the Spirit, and we are welcomed into God's love.

18 · Prayer

Let us pray. Father, thank you that you are the one true God, and that you have made yourself known to us. Thank you, Lord Jesus, beloved Son, for coming to us and giving yourself for us. Thank you, Holy Spirit, for bringing us into God's family. Help me to worship you humbly, and to speak of you with respect. And may "the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all." Amen.

Pause and seek help

For some people, "Father" is a painful word because their own father was absent, harsh or abusive. God is not like a father who harmed you; he shows what fatherhood was always meant to be: faithful, safe and loving (Psalm 27:10; Isaiah 66:13). It is fine to take time with this. If memories of abuse are troubling you, speak with a trustworthy person outside the harmful situation, and see the Support Page "I am in danger or being abused."
