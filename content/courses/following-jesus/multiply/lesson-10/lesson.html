Leading and Multiplying a Healthy Discipleship Group

I've been asked to lead a small group, or I'd like to start one. How do I lead without dominating, keep people safe, and help others lead too?

Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Asked to lead

Welcome to Lesson 10, the last lesson of Multiply. In Establish, you saw that leaders are shepherded with care, serving under Christ. In Lesson 7, you learned to be gentle like a mother, and in Lesson 9, to know your limits and refer. This last lesson shows how to lead a healthy discipleship group, and help others lead too, so that healthy groups can multiply.

Small groups are one of the main places where disciples grow: reading Scripture, praying, encouraging one another and following Jesus together. But leading a group can feel daunting. Some leaders dominate. Some groups become closed circles, loyal to one person. And some leaders try to do everything alone, until they burn out.

So here is our question: I've been asked to lead a small group, or I'd like to start one. How do I lead without dominating, keep people safe, and help others lead too?

2 · To be with him

Our first passage is Mark chapter 3: "And he went up on the mountain and called to him those whom he desired, and they came to him. And he appointed twelve (whom he also named apostles) so that they might be with him and he might send them out to preach and have authority to cast out demons."

Let us use the first question: what does it say? Jesus appointed the Twelve first "to be with him", and then to be sent. Their ministry flowed from their relationship with him.

3 · Entrust to faithful people

Our second passage is 2 Timothy chapter 2: "You then, my child, be strengthened by the grace that is in Christ Jesus, and what you have heard from me in the presence of many witnesses entrust to faithful men, who will be able to teach others also."

Paul begins with grace. Leadership flows from grace, not willpower. Then he describes a chain of faithful handing on: from Paul, to Timothy, to faithful people who are able to teach, and then to others.

4 · Entrust = paratithēmi

Our Greek word today is the one translated "entrust" in 2 Timothy 2:2: paratithēmi. It means to entrust, to hand something over into someone's care.

Luke uses the same word on the cross, when Jesus says, "Father, into your hands I commit my spirit!" And in Acts, after Paul and Barnabas appointed elders in the young churches, "they committed them to the Lord."

So in 2 Timothy 2, Paul tells Timothy what to do with what he has received. He is to entrust it to faithful people who will be able to teach others also. The message is precious, and it is to be handed on faithfully. Disciple-making is not simply passing on information. It includes entrusting what we have received to people who can faithfully pass it on to others.

5 · Called, taught, sent and rested

Now the background. The Twelve had a unique place in Jesus' mission. This passage does not make every group leader an apostle, or give leaders authority over other people. We draw one principle from it: Christian service grows out of relationship with Christ.

Now our second question: what did it mean to the people who first heard it? Mark shows that the Twelve often misunderstood Jesus. Yet he kept teaching, correcting and sending them. Later, he sent them out two by two. And when they came back, he said, "Come away by yourselves to a desolate place and rest a while."

Leaders and apprentices grow over time, by grace.

6 · A letter from prison

Paul wrote this letter from prison, near the end of his life. He wrote to Timothy, a younger leader he had mentored for many years.

The chain is simple: Paul, Timothy, faithful people, others. The word translated "men" can refer to people generally, and churches apply it in different ways. The emphasis is on faithfulness and the ability to teach, not on status.

7 · The short answer

So, how do we lead a healthy group? Here is the short answer.

Stay close to Jesus first. Lead as a servant, not a boss. Share leadership, keep people safe and welcome accountability. And entrust the work to others, step by step, so that healthy groups can multiply.

That gives us four parts: Called to Be With Him, Lead as Servants, Shared, Safe and Accountable, and Entrust to Others.

8 · Part 1: Called to Be With Him

The first part: Called to Be With Him.

Christian service grows out of continuing relationship with Christ. A group leader's first task is to keep following Jesus personally, with a sustainable rhythm of Scripture, prayer, rest and church life, as you saw in Grow.

A healthy discipleship group centres on Christ. Like the first believers, it gives itself to "the apostles' teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers." Members let the word of Christ dwell in them richly, teaching and encouraging one another.

The group exists to help people follow Jesus, not to build the leader's reputation, or to become an end in itself.

9 · Part 2: Lead as Servants

The second part: Lead as Servants.

When his disciples argued about status, Jesus said, "It shall not be so among you. But whoever would be great among you must be your servant… even as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve." Peter told elders not to be "domineering over those in your charge, but being examples to the flock."

A good group leader is a facilitator. They help everyone take part. They ask questions rather than lecturing, as you learned in Lesson 8. They welcome quieter voices, gently manage those who dominate, and keep the group focused on Christ.

Leading is not about control, status or having every answer. It is about serving the group's growth. And "I don't know; let's find out" is always allowed.

10 · Part 3: Shared, Safe and Accountable

The third part: Shared, Safe and Accountable.

Jethro advised Moses to share leadership with trustworthy people, and Paul and Barnabas appointed elders "in every church". In Scripture, leadership is usually shared.

Healthy groups have more than one leader, where possible. They have clear agreements about the group's purpose, and about confidentiality and its limits. They pay attention to safeguarding, including knowing the church's safeguarding contact, as you learned in Lesson 9. And a formal church group has clear, trustworthy and proportionate oversight.

Accountability means that leaders can receive support, correction and safeguarding guidance. It does not mean monitoring members' private lives, controlling their friendships, or reporting every conversation.

Paul warned the Ephesian elders to "pay careful attention to yourselves and to all the flock." Leaders watch for harm and false teaching, refer needs beyond their role, and welcome review and correction themselves. No group should become a closed circle, loyal to one person.

11 · Part 4: Entrust to Others

The fourth part: Entrust to Others.

Jesus trained his disciples and sent them out two by two. When they returned and told him all they had done, he invited them to come away and rest a while.

Healthy groups multiply by preparing apprentices: people who watch, then help, then lead parts of the meeting with support, then lead together with others. Before someone leads, consider their character, teachability, reliability, healthy boundaries, ability to work with others, and any safeguarding requirements. Apprenticeship is preparation, not a promise of appointment. Churches also differ over who may lead small groups, as you saw in Establish; ask a trustworthy church leader how your church approaches this.

When a group grows and multiplies, discuss it openly, rather than announcing it suddenly. Members are people, not numbers to be divided between leaders. No one should be pressured to move, lead or host a group.

Multiplication is not about numbers or speed, but about faithful people helping others follow Jesus.

12 · Christians differ: how quickly groups should multiply

Christians differ on one question here: how quickly should groups multiply?

Some Christians emphasise rapid multiplication of groups and new leaders, inspired by the swift spread of the gospel in Acts. Others emphasise slower, deeper formation of leaders before multiplication, pointing to Paul's warnings not to appoint a leader too quickly.

The passages in Acts describe growth, rather than commanding rapid multiplication. Second Timothy 2:2 is the clearer passage on preparing others.

Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that faithfulness, character and safety matter more than speed or numbers.

13 · Valentina's story

Let me tell you about Valentina. Her story is a composite, drawn from the experiences of many people.

Valentina is a social worker in Bogotá, Colombia. After completing the first three courses of Following Jesus in a small group, she was asked to help lead a new group for young adults. She felt nervous. She was not a pastor, and she worried about saying the wrong thing, or being unable to answer questions.

Her pastor, Andrés, arranged for her to co-lead with an experienced leader, Lucía, for the first term. Lucía showed her how to ask questions, draw quieter members in, and gently stop one member who kept dominating the discussion. From the third week, Valentina led the Bible discussion, with Lucía alongside her. At the first meeting, the group agreed its guidelines: confidentiality with clear limits, respect, no pressure to pray aloud, and the church's safeguarding contact.

When a member told Valentina about a possible safeguarding concern involving danger, she remembered Lesson 9. She explained what she needed to do, sought the member's agreement where possible, did not investigate, and contacted the safeguarding lead promptly. Lucía and Valentina met monthly with Andrés to review how the group was going.

After a year, Lucía stepped back to rest, and Valentina began co-leading with Camilo, a member she had been helping to prepare. Valentina still asks for advice regularly, and she is learning to say "I don't know" when needed. She has learned that healthy leadership grows through being with Jesus, serving others, sharing responsibility, and entrusting the work to others step by step.

14 · What healthy leadership is not

Here are six common mistakes about leading a group.

"Group leadership is a reward for maturity, or a position of status." No. It is service under Christ.

"A good leader has every answer, and does most of the talking." No. A good leader helps everyone take part.

"A healthy group is loyal to its leader." No. No group should become a closed circle loyal to one person.

"Accountability means watching members' private lives." No. It means leaders receive support and correction.

"Faithfulness is measured by how fast groups multiply." No. Character and safety matter more than speed.

And "Completing this course qualifies me to lead." No. Churches discern, prepare and appoint leaders.

16 · Remember

Let us bring it together. We stay close to Jesus, lead as servants, share leadership safely and accountably, and entrust the work to others.

Called to be with him: service flows from continuing relationship with Christ, and the group centres on Christ. Lead as servants: facilitate, include and serve; never dominate or control. Shared, safe and accountable: more than one leader, clear agreements, safeguarding and oversight; never a closed circle. And entrust to others: prepare apprentices patiently; people, not numbers; faithfulness over speed.

Over these ten lessons, you have learned that every disciple is sent, and how to live, speak, serve, walk with others, and care for people safely. None of it depends on your strength. It flows from being with Jesus.

Help me to entrust what I have received to faithful people, step by step, so that others may follow you too. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Pause and seek help

Groups can become unhealthy. Warning signs include a leader who cannot be questioned, demands loyalty or secrecy, discourages members from other friendships or church involvement, controls members' decisions, uses prophecy or spiritual language to pressure people, or handles money without accountability; if you see these signs, seek advice from someone outside the group's leadership. Groups must follow safeguarding procedures: leaders know the church's safeguarding contact, keep appropriate boundaries, avoid isolated one-to-one meetings that break procedure, and never investigate allegations themselves (Lesson 9); groups involving children, young people or vulnerable adults need particular safeguards, written procedures and appropriately checked leaders. A leader who is exhausted or struggling should step back for a season; this is wisdom, not failure. Completing this course does not qualify anyone to lead (Support Page: "I am being controlled through prophecy, spiritual language, money or church authority").

Optional

Go deeper

Who may lead small groups. Some churches invite any committed, trustworthy believer to lead a small group after suitable preparation (they often point to Colossians 3:16 and 2 Timothy 2:2). Others ask that groups be led by recognised elders, deacons or trained leaders, and some limit certain roles to men (they often point to Titus 1:5–9 and 1 Timothy 3:1–13; see Establish, Lesson 9). Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that group leaders should be of good character, prepared, accountable and safe.

How quickly groups should multiply. Some Christians emphasise rapid multiplication of groups and new leaders, inspired by the swift spread of the gospel in Acts (Acts 2:47; 16:5). Those passages describe growth rather than commanding rapid multiplication; 2 Timothy 2:2 is the clearer passage on preparing others. Other Christians emphasise slower, deeper formation of leaders before multiplication (they often point to 1 Timothy 3:6, 10 and 5:22). Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that faithfulness, character and safety matter more than speed or numbers.

The word "entrust". Paratithēmi means to entrust, to hand something over into someone's care. Its use in Luke 23:46 and Acts 14:23 illustrates the word; the lesson does not build a larger claim on those verses.

Completing Multiply. Completing Multiply means you have completed the Multiply stage of the Following Jesus pathway; completing all four courses means you have completed the pathway. It records participation and completion. It does not mean that someone is a mature Christian, or qualified or approved to lead, teach, mentor or counsel others.

If you are still exploring. You may be part of a group led by Christians. This lesson describes what healthy leadership should look like: humble, shared, safe and open to correction. No one will ask you to lead or to say publicly whether you have become a Christian.
