Every Disciple Is Sent

Isn't sharing my faith the job of pastors and evangelists? I'm not confident, and I don't want to pressure anyone.

About 18 minutesJohn 20:21–22 and Acts 1:8My Disciple-Making Plan: Three people I will pray for

In this lesson

You will learn

  • What Jesus gave his frightened disciples before he sent them (John 20:19–22)
  • Why the Spirit's power is given for witness, and why the results belong to God (Acts 1:8)
  • The four parts: Sent by Jesus, Empowered by the Spirit, Witnesses Where We Live, Love Without Pressure
  • How to love the people around you freely, with gentleness and respect, and without pressure
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · The job of pastors?

Welcome to Multiply, the fourth and final course in Following Jesus. In Begin, you took your first steps. In Establish, you learned what Christians believe. In Grow, you saw how Jesus reshapes character and everyday life. Now we ask a new question: how can I join Christ's mission and help others follow Jesus, without manipulating or controlling anyone?

In Establish, you saw that the Holy Spirit gives power to witness, and you learned to live now as citizens of God's kingdom. Now we are going one step further.

Many believers love Jesus but feel uneasy about sharing their faith. Some think it is a job for specialists. Some are shy. Some have seen pushy methods and do not want to treat people as targets. And some live where speaking about Jesus is risky.

So here is our question: Isn't sharing my faith the job of pastors and evangelists? I'm not confident, and I don't want to pressure anyone.

2 · As the Father has sent me

Our first passage is John chapter 20, verses 21 and 22: "Jesus said to them again, 'Peace be with you. As the Father has sent me, even so I am sending you.' And when he had said this, he breathed on them and said to them, 'Receive the Holy Spirit.'"

Let us use the first question from Establish: what does it say? Notice the order. Jesus gives peace, then he sends, and with the sending he gives the Holy Spirit. He does not send his disciples out alone.

3 · You will be my witnesses

Our second passage is Acts chapter 1, verse 8: "But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth."

Notice how the promise and the purpose belong together. The power of the Spirit is given so that his people can be witnesses to Jesus, starting where they are and reaching outward.

4 · Witnesses = martys

Our Greek word today is the one translated "witnesses" in Acts 1:8: martys. A martys is a person who tells what they have seen and know. The word was at home in the law courts, though it was used more widely too. Luke uses the same word when the risen Jesus tells his disciples, "You are witnesses of these things."

Think of a witness in a court. A witness is not the judge, and a witness does not have to win the case. A witness tells the truth about what they have seen. That is a picture, not a definition, but it helps.

So Jesus is not asking you to become a salesperson or a debater. He is asking you to tell the truth about him.

5 · Behind locked doors

Now the background. These words come on the evening of the very first Easter. John tells us the disciples were behind locked doors "for fear". They had run away when Jesus was arrested. They were not bold, confident people.

Now our second question: what did it mean to the people who first heard it? Jesus came and stood among them. He said, "Peace be with you." He showed them his hands and his side, and "the disciples were glad when they saw the Lord." Only then did he send them. The mission began with his peace and his presence, not with their courage.

One more thing. Jesus' own mission is unique. He is the Son sent by the Father to do the saving work that only he could do. We cannot repeat that. But those he sends are to reflect the one who sends them, with truth, love, humility and a willingness to suffer.

6 · From timetables to mission

Our second passage comes forty days later. Just before Acts 1:8, the disciples asked, "Lord, will you at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?" They wanted a timetable. Jesus said, "It is not for you to know times or seasons that the Father has fixed by his own authority." Then he turned them to mission. As you saw in Establish, our part is not to calculate dates but to live faithfully now.

Acts 1:8 is also the outline of the book of Acts. The good news spreads from Jerusalem, to Judea and Samaria, and out towards the end of the earth. And it often spread through ordinary believers. When persecution scattered the church in Acts 8, the apostles stayed in Jerusalem, but "those who were scattered went about preaching the word." These were ordinary followers of Jesus.

Jesus first spoke these words to the disciples in that room. That his sending reaches every believer is something we learn from the whole New Testament, such as Acts 8 and 1 Peter 2, not from one verse alone.

7 · The short answer

So, is sharing faith only the job of pastors and evangelists? Here is the short answer.

As the Father sent Jesus, Jesus sends his followers. The Spirit empowers ordinary believers to bear witness. We begin where we already live. And we love people freely, with gentleness and respect, leaving the results to God.

That gives us four parts: Sent by Jesus, Empowered by the Spirit, Witnesses Where We Live, and Love Without Pressure.

8 · Part 1: Sent by Jesus

The first part: Sent by Jesus.

After his resurrection, Jesus told the eleven, "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations." Paul says, "we are ambassadors for Christ, God making his appeal through us." And Peter tells all God's people that they are called to "proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light."

So belonging to Christ's mission is not a special calling for a few. The mission belongs to every follower of Jesus. But we take part in different ways. Not everyone has the same gift, confidence, opportunity or responsibility.

A quiet believer who prays faithfully, loves a colleague well, and speaks about Christ when it is appropriate is as truly part of Christ's mission as a gifted public evangelist.

9 · Part 2: Empowered by the Spirit

The second part: Empowered by the Spirit.

Jesus breathed on his disciples and said, "Receive the Holy Spirit." And in Acts he promised, "You will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you." Christians understand the relationship between these two moments in different ways, but both connect Christian mission with the presence and power of the Spirit.

So witness does not depend on confidence, eloquence or a strong personality. The disciples were frightened men behind locked doors. Later, after they prayed, "they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and continued to speak the word of God with boldness." The same Spirit helps ordinary believers today.

And the results belong to God. Paul wrote, "I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth." We can speak, serve and pray faithfully, but only God can bring someone to new life. Our part is faithful witness, not producing results. No one's eternal destiny depends on how well you perform.

10 · Part 3: Witnesses Where We Live

The third part: Witnesses Where We Live.

Acts 1:8 moves outward from Jerusalem. For most of us, witness begins in the places where we already are: our family, our work, our school, our neighbourhood, and online.

When a Samaritan woman met Jesus at a well, she went straight back to her town and told people about him. John says, "Many Samaritans from that town believed in him because of the woman's testimony." She had no training, only an honest account of meeting Jesus.

Jesus calls his followers the salt of the earth and the light of the world: "let your light shine before others, so that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father who is in heaven." Honest work, kindness, patience and integrity are part of our witness. People often notice how we live alongside what we say we believe.

And the early believers' courage under persecution does not mean we should seek danger or put other Christians at risk. Where speaking about Jesus is dangerous, a quiet, faithful life is also witness.

11 · Part 4: Love Without Pressure

The fourth part: Love Without Pressure.

Peter writes, "in your hearts honor Christ the Lord as holy, always being prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that is in you; yet do it with gentleness and respect." And Paul says, "Let your speech always be gracious."

Jesus' own ministry joined proclamation with compassionate action. In Nazareth he said, "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor." We cannot repeat his unique saving mission, but our witness should likewise never separate truthful words from genuine love.

So Christian witness never uses manipulation, deception, fear, bribes, threats, or help that depends on interest in faith. And where someone depends on you for work, care or essential help, don't use that position to start a faith conversation.

Each person is free to accept or reject the message. And they remain worthy of love and respect, whatever they decide.

12 · Christians differ

Christians differ on one question here: is every believer called to evangelise?

Some Christians emphasise that every believer is called to share the gospel actively with others. They often point to the Great Commission in Matthew 28, to the scattered believers in Acts 8, and to 1 Peter 3:15.

Others emphasise that Christ gives some people the particular gift of evangelism, while every believer is called to be a faithful witness in life and to be ready to explain their hope. They often point to Ephesians 4:11, where Christ gives "the evangelists", and to "Philip the evangelist" in Acts 21.

Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that every believer bears witness to Christ, that some are especially gifted for evangelism, and that all should support the spread of the gospel. Christians also differ over how evangelism and practical care relate; you will find that under Go deeper on the lesson page.

13 · Oyuna's story

Let me tell you about Oyuna. Her story is a composite, drawn from the experiences of many people.

Oyuna is a dental nurse in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia. She came to faith three years ago and loves her small church, but she is quiet, and she assumed evangelism was for pastors. When her church launched a campaign asking every member to bring five new people to an outreach event, she felt anxious and guilty. She invited no one, and she began avoiding the announcements.

Her small group leader, Tsetseg, read John 20 and Acts 1 with her. "Jesus sends you with his peace and his Spirit, not with a quota," she said. "Start where you already are. Who do you see every day?"

Oyuna thought of her colleague Gerel, whose young son was often ill. She began praying for Gerel privately. When Gerel's son was in hospital, Oyuna covered a shift for her and brought soup to her home.

A few weeks later Gerel asked, "Why are you so kind to me?" Oyuna answered simply that Jesus had changed her life, and that she prayed for Gerel's family. She asked whether Gerel would like her to pray, and accepted it calmly when Gerel said, "Maybe another time." Oyuna would keep caring for Gerel even if Gerel never asked another question about faith.

Gerel has not become a Christian. But she sometimes asks Oyuna questions, and their friendship has grown. Many believers discover that witness begins with prayer, practical love and honest answers, not with programmes or targets.

14 · What being sent is not

Here are six common mistakes about being sent.

"Only pastors and evangelists are sent." No. Every follower of Jesus belongs to his mission, in different ways.

"I must be confident and outgoing to bear witness." No. Witness depends on the Spirit, not on personality.

"People's eternal destiny depends on my performance." No. God gives the growth. Our part is faithful witness.

"Faithfulness is measured by the number of conversions or contacts." No. Christ sends us with peace, not quotas.

"Kindness is a technique to get people interested in Jesus." No. Care is genuine, and it never depends on interest in faith.

And "I should share my faith whatever the risk to others." No. Where witness is dangerous, seek wise counsel from experienced local believers.

15 · This week

This week, your assignment is simple: love the people around you, and pray for them.

First, read John 20:19 to 23 and Acts 1:1 to 11. Second, write one short line for each of the four parts, in your own words. You can use the book to help.

Third, think of the ordinary places where you spend most of your time, and one or two people there whom you could pray for and care for genuinely. If you write anything down, use initials only. This is not a list of targets. These are people to love.

Fourth, pray for them each day this week, asking the Holy Spirit for love and wisdom.

Fifth, if it is appropriate and safe, continue or begin one ordinary act of care for one of them. Let the care be genuine, whether or not faith is ever discussed. If they ask about your faith, answer honestly and respectfully. If this is not possible or safe, simply pray for them.

And sixth, follow the seven-day reading plan below this lesson. If you miss a day, simply begin again the next day. This week's praying will help you with the first part of My Disciple-Making Plan at the end of the course: three people I will pray for.

16 · Remember

Let us bring it together. Jesus sends all his followers, the Spirit empowers us, we bear witness where we live, and we love without pressure.

Sent by Jesus: as the Father sent him, Jesus sends his followers, and every believer belongs to his mission, in different ways. Empowered by the Spirit: witness depends on the Spirit, not on confidence, and God gives the growth. Witnesses where we live: in our family, work, school, neighbourhood and online, our good works point to God. And love without pressure: with gentleness and respect, with no manipulation and no conditions, and people remain free.

17 · Prayer

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you for your peace. Thank you that you send me, and that you do not send me alone. Holy Spirit, give me love, wisdom and courage. Help me to see the people around me at home, at work and in my neighbourhood, and to love them freely, with gentleness and respect. I leave the results with you, because you give the growth. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Pause and seek help

Where sharing faith is dangerous, seek wise counsel from experienced local believers; a quiet, faithful life and private conversations when asked are also witness. Where someone depends on you for work, grades, housing, healthcare, money or essential help, do not start a conversation about faith within that relationship; if they ask, answer respectfully and briefly, and make clear it will not affect the help they receive. Never ask a child to keep faith conversations, prayer or baptism secret from parents or guardians; if involving the family might put a child at risk, seek safeguarding advice at once. If a church pressures you with targets for conversions or invitations, or shames you for not "winning souls", seek advice outside that situation (Support Page: "I am being controlled through prophecy, spiritual language, money or church authority").

Optional

Go deeper

How evangelism and practical care relate. Some Christians emphasise that proclaiming the gospel is the church's first task, with practical care flowing from it (they often point to Romans 10:14–17 and 1 Corinthians 15:1–4). Others emphasise that word and deed belong together as equal expressions of God's kingdom (they often point to Luke 4:18–19 and Matthew 25:35–40). Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that practical care must never become bait for conversion, and that spoken witness must never become an excuse to neglect suffering.

Is every believer called to evangelise? The full Christians differ box from the book, with the passages each view uses (summarised on Slide 12).

Where witness is risky. In some countries, families or workplaces, sharing faith openly can lead to serious danger, legal penalties or loss of livelihood. Your safety and the safety of others matter to God. Never put others at risk by sharing their names or stories without consent.

Where you hold power over others. Take particular care if you are an employer, manager, teacher, doctor, aid worker, landlord or church leader. People in these situations may feel unable to say no. Follow the rules of your profession or organisation. Speaking about faith with children or vulnerable adults must follow safeguarding procedures (Lesson 9) and, for children, respect the role of parents or guardians.

If you are still exploring. You may be reading this as someone Christians have tried to persuade, sometimes well and sometimes badly. This lesson says that real Christian witness should never pressure you, and that you remain worthy of respect whatever you decide. No one will ask you to share faith with others or to say publicly whether you have become a Christian.
