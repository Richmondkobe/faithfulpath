Using Your Gifts to Serve Others

People say every Christian has gifts, but I don't know what mine are. And when I've served before, I either felt useless or got overloaded. How do I find my place?

In this lesson

You will learn

  • How Romans 12 grounds gifts in grace, humility and one body
  • What it means to be a "steward" of God's varied grace (1 Peter 4:10)

Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Where do I fit?

Welcome to Lesson 6. In Establish, you learned that the Spirit gives gifts for others, and that the church is built up together as every member plays a part. In Grow, you saw what it means to offer our whole lives to God. This lesson helps you recognise the gifts God has given you, use them in love, and find a healthy place to serve.

Many believers want to serve but feel unsure where they fit. Some have tried roles that did not suit them, and felt useless. Others said yes to everything, and became exhausted. And some have seen gifts used to claim status or to control others, and want nothing to do with that.

So here is our question: People say every Christian has gifts, but I don't know what mine are. And when I've served before, I either felt useless or got overloaded. How do I find my place?

2 · One body, many gifts

Our first passage is Romans chapter 12, verses 4 to 6: "For as in one body we have many members, and the members do not all have the same function, so we, though many, are one body in Christ, and individually members one of another. Having gifts that differ according to the grace given to us, let us use them."

Let us use the first question: what does it say? We are many, and we are different. But we belong to one another, in one body. The gifts differ, and they come from grace. And Paul's instruction is simple: let us use them.

3 · Stewards of God's grace

Our second passage is 1 Peter chapter 4, verses 10 and 11: "As each has received a gift, use it to serve one another, as good stewards of God's varied grace: whoever speaks, as one who speaks oracles of God; whoever serves, as one who serves by the strength that God supplies, in order that in everything God may be glorified through Jesus Christ."

Notice that each believer receives from God's grace, and these gifts are for serving one another. Peter divides gifts broadly into speaking and serving, and calls believers to use both in dependence on God. And the goal of it all is that God may be glorified through Jesus Christ.

4 · Stewards = oikonomos

Our Greek word today is the one translated "stewards" in 1 Peter 4:10: oikonomos. It means a household manager or steward. An oikonomos was someone entrusted with what belonged to the master, and responsible for managing it for the household. Jesus uses the same word for the "faithful and wise manager" who gives the household their food at the proper time.

So a steward does not own what he manages. He holds it on someone else's behalf, and he uses it for others.

That is how Peter describes our gifts. They are not our possessions, our achievements or our status. We hold them on God's behalf, for the good of others.

5 · After the offering

Now the background. Romans 12 does not begin with gifts. It begins with Paul's call to offer our whole lives to God as a living sacrifice, which you met in Grow, Lesson 2. Gifts are part of that offering.

Now our second question: what did it mean to the people who first heard it? Paul begins with humility. No one should "think of himself more highly than he ought to think." Then he describes the body, and then he lists gifts together with how to use them: generously, with zeal, with cheerfulness.

This is one of several lists of gifts in the New Testament, and the lists are not identical. Together they show the variety of God's grace in the church. And the very next verse says, "Let love be genuine."

6 · Written to suffering believers

Peter wrote to believers who were facing suffering and hostility. He reminded them that "the end of all things is at hand." So he told them to pray, to keep loving one another earnestly, to show hospitality, and to use their gifts to serve one another.

The Bible also shows God giving people practical abilities for particular work. When Israel built the tabernacle, God said of Bezalel the craftsman, "I have filled him with the Spirit of God, with ability and intelligence, with knowledge and all craftsmanship."

And in Acts, when Tabitha died, the widows of Joppa wept and showed Peter "tunics and other garments that Dorcas made while she was with them." Her quiet, practical care was remembered.

7 · The short answer

So, how do we find our place? Here is the short answer.

Every believer receives gifts from God's grace. We are different parts of one body. Our gifts are for serving others in love. And we discover and grow our gifts through faithful, healthy service.

That gives us four parts: Grace-Given Gifts, Different Parts, One Body, Serve in Love, and Grow Through Faithful Service.

8 · Part 1: Grace-Given Gifts

The first part: Grace-Given Gifts.

Paul speaks of "gifts that differ according to the grace given to us." Gifts are not rewards for spiritual maturity, and they are not signs of importance. They are grace. "Grace was given to each one of us according to the measure of Christ's gift."

Scripture gives several overlapping examples: teaching, encouraging, giving, leading, showing mercy, serving, hospitality, prophecy, healing and others. The lists overlap but are not identical.

And like Bezalel, practical skills can be offered to God in service. Cooking, building, organising, caring, fixing and welcoming can all be used for God's glory.

Christians differ over how such abilities relate to spiritual gifts, and over how helpful gift questionnaires are. Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that every ability comes from God, that gifts are recognised over time in community, and that no test can replace love, character and faithful service.

9 · Part 2: Different Parts, One Body

The second part: Different Parts, One Body.

"So we, though many, are one body in Christ." Paul warns against two opposite mistakes. The first is thinking too highly of ourselves. The second is thinking we do not belong because we are different: "If the foot should say, 'Because I am not a hand, I do not belong to the body,' that would not make it any less a part of the body."

Paul adds, "the parts of the body that seem to be weaker are indispensable." Notice the word "seem". Paul is speaking about human judgements of importance. He is not labelling people with disability or illness as spiritually weak.

A person's place in Christ's body does not depend on how much they can do. People with disability or illness, people with little free time, and people with quiet personalities are full members whose contributions matter. Those who cannot take on a role at present, because of illness, age, caring responsibilities or exhaustion, still belong fully, and should receive the church's care. No gift makes anyone more important than others.

10 · Part 3: Serve in Love

The third part: Serve in Love.

Gifts without love are empty. Paul says, "if I have prophetic powers, and understand all mysteries and all knowledge… but have not love, I am nothing."

Jesus taught his disciples that greatness in his kingdom takes the form of service. He himself "came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many." His ransom is unique, but his self-giving service becomes the pattern for his followers. He washed his disciples' feet and said, "you also ought to wash one another's feet."

Service is for others' good and God's glory. It is never for status, recognition or control. A claimed gift never places anyone above correction, or gives anyone authority over other people's lives.

Some service is visible, like teaching or leading. Much is hidden. And hidden service is seen by God.

11 · Part 4: Grow Through Faithful Service

The fourth part: Grow Through Faithful Service.

Gifts are often discovered by serving, not before serving. Here is a healthy way to begin. Start small: try a small, time-limited role with appropriate supervision. Agree the details: what the role involves, what preparation is needed, how long the trial will last, and how either side can end it. Ask and notice: ask trusted people what they see, and notice where God seems to work through you. And grow. Paul told Timothy to "fan into flame the gift of God." Gifts, and the skills to use them well, develop through practice, training, feedback and dependence on God.

Some roles, such as those involving children, vulnerable adults, money or authority, rightly require preparation and checks before anyone begins. That protects everyone; it is not a sign of distrust.

Sometimes a church may conclude, together with you, that a role is not a good fit. That is not failure. "Let us not grow weary of doing good", and let us also not serve ourselves into exhaustion. It is right to serve within your capacity and season of life, and to step back when needed.

12 · Kalani's story

Let me tell you about Kalani. Her story is a composite, drawn from the experiences of many people.

Kalani is a retired nurse in Hilo, Hawaii. After joining a small church, she agreed to help wherever she was asked: the children's ministry, the welcome team and the kitchen. Within a year she was exhausted. She felt frustrated in the children's ministry, where she struggled with noisy groups, and she wondered whether she had any gifts at all.

Her pastor, Leilani, read Romans 12 and 1 Peter 4 with her. "Let's notice where God has already worked through you," she said. Kalani remembered how people often called her after hospital stays, and how she enjoyed visiting the sick. Two friends from her small group said they saw mercy and encouragement in her.

With Leilani's help, Kalani stepped back, kindly and with notice, from the children's ministry and the kitchen. She joined the church's visiting team, which had clear guidelines, training and a coordinator. She now visits people who are ill or housebound, always with their consent. She keeps confidences. She does not give medical advice just because she is a retired nurse. And she passes concerns to the coordinator, rather than trying to solve everything herself.

Kalani still finds some visits hard, and she meets the coordinator monthly to talk through difficult situations. She says she now serves from joy rather than guilt. Kalani discovered more about her gifts by trying, listening to trusted people and adjusting, rather than waiting until she was certain.

13 · What gifts and service are not

Here are six common mistakes about gifts and service.

"Some gifts make people more important." No. Gifts are grace, and no gift makes anyone more important.

"If I can't do much, I don't really belong." No. Your place in Christ's body does not depend on how much you can do.

"A gift test will tell me exactly what my gifts are." No. Gifts are usually recognised over time, in community.

"Faithful Christians say yes to every request." No. It is right to serve within your capacity, and to step back when needed.

"Having a gift means I am ready for any role." No. Some roles rightly require preparation, checks and oversight.

And "A gift gives someone authority over others." No. A claimed gift never places anyone above correction.

15 · Remember

Let us bring it together. God gives every believer gifts by grace; we are different parts of one body; we serve in love; and we grow through faithful, healthy service.

Grace-given gifts: gifts are grace, not rewards, and God also gives practical skills. Different parts, one body: every member belongs, whatever they can do, and no gift makes anyone more important. Serve in love: gifts without love are empty, so serve for others' good and God's glory. And grow through faithful service: start small, agree the details, ask and notice, and rest when needed.

16 · Prayer

Let us pray. Father, thank you for every gift of your grace, in me and in the people around me. Thank you that I belong to the body of Christ, whatever I can or cannot do. Help me to serve in love, as Jesus served. Show me a healthy place to serve, and give me the wisdom to rest. May you be glorified in everything, through Jesus Christ. Amen.

Pause and seek help

Serving should not destroy your health, family or work. Warning signs of overload include constant exhaustion, resentment, neglecting rest or family, and being unable to say no; it is right to step back or change roles, and to seek counsel and rest (Grow, Lesson 12). If a church shames you for not serving more, or treats saying no as disloyalty, seek advice from someone outside that situation. Roles involving children, young people, vulnerable adults, money, counselling or leadership rightly require preparation, references, training and, where the law allows, background checks; no single check proves someone is safe, so churches also use written boundaries, supervision and regular review. Feeling compassionate does not qualify anyone to give professional counselling (Lesson 9). A claimed gift never places anyone above correction; prophecy, claims of healing, teaching and leadership must all be tested and accountable (Support Page: "I am being controlled through prophecy, spiritual language, money or church authority").

Optional

Go deeper

Lists and tests of spiritual gifts. Some Christians find gift questionnaires helpful for starting reflection and identifying possible gifts (they often point to Romans 12:6–8 and 1 Corinthians 12:4–11, treating the lists as categories). Others are cautious, noting that the New Testament lists overlap and are not complete, and prefer to discover gifts through serving and the feedback of the church (they often point to 1 Peter 4:10 and Acts 6:3). Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that gifts are recognised over time in community, and that no test can replace love, character and faithful service. (Establish, Lesson 6 explained differences over whether all gifts continue today.)

How gifts relate to natural abilities. Some Christians distinguish clearly between spiritual gifts given by the Spirit and natural talents (they often point to 1 Corinthians 12:7–11). Others see God at work in both, with the Spirit taking up natural abilities and adding new capacities for service (they often point to Exodus 31:1–6 and Romans 12:6–8). Across historic Christian traditions, Christians hold that every ability comes from God and should be used in love, for his glory and others' good.

Testing and preparation. For recognised responsibilities such as deacons, Paul required that they "be tested first" (1 Timothy 3:10), and Acts 6:3 shows careful selection for entrusted service. Churches can rightly apply this wider principle to sensitive responsibilities, with training and oversight. Ask a trustworthy church leader how your church approaches these matters, and compare what you hear with Scripture.

If you cannot serve much at present. Illness, disability, age, caring responsibilities or exhaustion do not reduce your belonging. Receiving care is part of life in the body too (1 Corinthians 12:26).

If you are still exploring. You may already have gifts and skills that help others. This lesson explains how Christians understand gifts as grace from God, given for serving. No one will ask you to take on a church role or to say publicly whether you have become a Christian.
