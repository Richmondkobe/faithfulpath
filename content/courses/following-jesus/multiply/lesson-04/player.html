The Short Answer

Start by listening to where the person is. Tell the good news of who Jesus is and what he has done. Invite a response simply and without pressure. And leave the person free, trusting the Holy Spirit to work.

Part 1

Start Where They Are

“I found also an altar with this inscription: ‘To the unknown god.’”Acts 17:23

“What do you already believe about Jesus?” · “What do you think Christianity is about?”

Use everyday words, and explain any biblical word you use

Respect the person · listen to what they actually believe

Part 2

Tell the Good News

“The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand; repent and believe in the gospel.” (Mark 1:15)

“Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures… he was buried… he was raised on the third day.”1 Corinthians 15:3–4

“By grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God.”Ephesians 2:8

A few clear sentences, in answer to a real question, are often enough

Part 3

Invite a Response

repent and believe · baptism · joining God's people

“Would you like to follow Jesus?”
“Would you like to read one of the Gospels with me?”
“What questions or concerns do you still have about trusting Jesus?”

Salvation rests on Christ, not on saying a prayer perfectly

Part 4

Leave Them Free

“…he will convict the world concerning sin and righteousness and judgment.”John 16:8

Christians Differ

Prayers of commitment and public invitations

Some invitea prayer of commitment, or a public response
Romans 10:9–13 · Acts 2:37–41
Others emphasiserepentance, faith, baptism and joining the church
Acts 2:38 · Acts 8:36–38

A prayer can express genuine faith, but the prayer itself does not save

no false assurance from repeated words · no assurance withheld for a forgotten prayer

Across historic Christian traditions: salvation is by grace through faith in Christ, and no response should be pressured or treated as a formula
