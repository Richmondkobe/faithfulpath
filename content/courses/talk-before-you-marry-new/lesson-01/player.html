1 · Why silence feels like peace

If you cannot talk about money while you are engaged, marriage will not make that conversation easier. It will make it more expensive.

2 · Six patterns

Six patterns that keep couples from the conversations they need

1
2

4
5
6

Not diagnoses. Not fixed types. Not labels for each other.

Notice them, so that you can choose differently.

1Avoidance
  • “It’s not the right time.”
  • “We don’t need to talk about that yet.”
  • “I don’t want to upset them.”
  • “We’ll deal with it later.”

Postponement never feels like a decision.

2Assumption

“We’re going to build our life together.”

Not “I assume you…” but “Tell me what you think about…”

3People-pleasing

“Whatever you want is fine.” It is not fine.

I love you, but I see this differently. Can we talk about it?

4Defensiveness

“So you think I’m a bad partner?”

“Help me understand why this matters to you.”

5Conflict avoidance

“I just wanted the argument to stop.”

The person who raises their voice remains responsible for their behaviour.

6Unspoken expectations

“I expect something from you, and I have never told you what it is.”

Two pictures of a marriage — and neither ever shown.

3 · The map you brought from home

talk it out · wait it out · hold your ground · somebody leaves · someone pays · nothing changes

Keep what was good. Question what was harmful. Tell each other your map.

4 · The question underneath

What does this disagreement mean about us?

“Pastor, is it normal for us to disagree this much?”

Are we going to make it?

Disagreement is not a prediction of failure.

What matters is what the differences reveal, and how you handle them.

Better questions

  • Can we disagree without attacking each other’s character?
  • Can one of us tell the truth without being punished for it?
  • Can we listen when we do not like what we hear?
  • Can we repair properly after one of us has hurt the other?
  • Can we stay committed while a difference is unresolved?

“Better is open rebuke than hidden love. Faithful are the wounds of a friend …”Proverbs 27:5, 6a

Proverbs: friendship and wise counsel

The goal is not to become a couple who agree about everything.

The goal is to become a couple who can tell each other the truth — and still be safe.

Prepare on your own · The Three Subjects

Write three subjects you have been steering around.

What have I decided we’ll deal with after the wedding? · What have I started to bring up, then changed my mind? · What do I hope they already know? · What did I say I was fine with, when I wasn’t? · What would I be relieved never to discuss?

For each: how long I’ve avoided it · what I fear will happen if I raise it.

A blank list is allowed. Do not invent a problem.

If either of you is afraid of how the other will respond, do not do the together part.

Stop, and speak with someone you trust on your own first.

Talk together

Share only the subjects you choose. Take turns; listen without interrupting.

  • Did anything you heard surprise you?
  • Did anything you shared feel risky to say?
  • Which of these subjects feels hardest to raise?

Choose one manageable subject for Lesson 4.
