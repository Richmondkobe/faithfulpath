Lesson 4 · Book chapter 4 · Part 2: Learn the Skills

Learn to Say the Hard Thing

How do I raise something difficult so that it can be heard?

Ephesians 4:14–16

“so that we may no longer be children, tossed to and fro by the waves and carried about by every wind of doctrine, by human cunning, by craftiness in deceitful schemes.

Rather, speaking the truth in love, we are to grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ,

from whom the whole body, joined and held together by every joint with which it is equipped, when each part is working properly, makes the body grow so that it builds itself up in love.”Ephesians 4:14–16 (ESV)

Growing up together

  • Paul is describing how a church grows up into Christ.
  • Truth spoken in love is how the body is built up.
  • Here we apply it to two people preparing to marry.

The word: speaking the truth

ἀληθεύω

alētheuō · “to speak the truth, to be truthful”

In Ephesians 4:15: ἀληθεύοντες (alētheuontes)

Truth without love becomes a weapon. Love without truth becomes a hiding place.

A soft answer

“A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.”Proverbs 15:1 (ESV)

Not a softer truth. A truth that can be heard.

A gentle answer can reduce unnecessary conflict; it cannot guarantee how another person will respond.

“What you are doing is not good. You and the people with you will certainly wear yourselves out, for the thing is too heavy for you. You are not able to do it alone.”Exodus 18:17–18 (ESV)

The shape of a good hard sentence

He saw it · He named it · He said why · He offered a way forward

Jethro was advising Moses, not commanding him. This is not a marriage passage: we draw one practical lesson from how he raised a difficult concern.

1. The first sentence

An opening that works describes a concern. An opening that fails delivers a verdict.

Not softer, but possible to hear

Start with “I”, or with “Can we talk about…” But an accusation can wear an “I” as a costume.

You are not trying to make the hard truth softer. You are trying to make it possible for the other person to actually hear it.

2. Say what actually happened

What happened. What I felt. What I needed.

Events, not identities

  • “You are unreliable” often invites a defence of character instead.
  • You may find out you were wrong.
  • A pattern can be named too: specifically, as a pattern.

3. Say it once, clearly

“I’d like us to have Saturday evenings together. Can we make that ours?”

Do not make your partner pass a test you never explained.

Simply “Yes” or “No”

“Let what you say be simply ‘Yes’ or ‘No’.”Matthew 5:37 (ESV)

“This is what I want. How do you feel about it?”

  • A clear no is information. A sexual no is respected immediately.
  • “Once” means do not wear someone down. You may agree when to return.

4. What to raise, what to let go

  • Is this important?
  • Is this becoming a pattern?
  • If I don’t address it, will I eventually resent it?

Not everything, and not buried

Do not turn every irritation into an indictment.

Letting go is not the same as burying.

Let go, discuss, seek support

Let go of the insignificant

A different preference about dinner tonight

Discuss the recurring

Your concerns being brushed aside

Seek support for the harmful

dishonesty · hidden debt · sexual pressure · intimidation · fear · violence

If you fear retaliation, a better opening sentence is not the solution. Seek confidential, independent support.

Bringing it together

  1. Open with a concern, not a verdict
  2. Say what happened, what you felt, what you needed
  3. Say it once, clearly, and leave room for the answer
  4. Let go, discuss, or seek support

Prepare on your own · The First Real Conversation

Before you begin: your Lesson 1 subject · your rules · who begins · when · stop at forty minutes

The event · The effect · The ask

Four tests: no exaggerated “always” or “never” · no description of character · something they can answer · the whole of what I mean

Talk together

You can prepare now, and have the conversation after Lesson 5. You do not need to do both today.

If either of you is afraid of how the other will respond, do not do the together part.

Stop, and speak with someone you trust on your own first.

Talk together

  • Read your opening, then stop
  • The other asks a question first
  • Keep the rules · stop at forty minutes
  • Afterwards: review the conversation, not the subject
  • Write it down, with a date to come back to it

If you felt unsafe, stop joint practice and seek independent support.

Remember

“speaking the truth in love”Ephesians 4:15

“A soft answer turns away wrath.”Proverbs 15:1

Prayer

Give us courage to say what is true, and love to say it well.

Guard our first words from becoming verdicts, and our silence from becoming a test.

Teach us what to raise, what to let go, and when we need help.

Grow us up together into you. In Jesus’ name. Amen.
