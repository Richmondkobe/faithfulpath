For further study (optional)

Transcript and Scripture references

Slide 1 · The fifth door

Welcome back. This is Lesson 10, and we open the fifth room of the house: faith and church. Two people can agree on the word Christian without ever defining what they expect it to mean in the daily running of a marriage.

So the question for this room is this: what will our faith actually look like at home?

Slide 2 · The word of Christ, dwelling richly

Scripture: Colossians 3:16–17

Our passage is Paul’s letter to the Colossians, chapter 3, verses 16 and 17.

“Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly, teaching and admonishing one another in all wisdom, singing psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, with thankfulness in your hearts to God. And whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him.”

Slide 3 · Written to a whole church

Scripture: Colossians 3:16–17

Paul was writing to a whole church. The word of Christ was to dwell richly among believers as they taught and encouraged “one another”. That is the original setting. Here, we are applying the same principle to the shared life of a Christian couple, while remembering that marriage is not a replacement for belonging to the wider church. And notice how far it reaches: “whatever you do, in word or deed”. Faith is not only what happens on Sunday. It reaches into work, meals, money and the ordinary week. That is why this room matters.

Slide 4 · In secret, and together

Scripture: Matthew 6:6; Hebrews 10:24–25

Jesus said, “But when you pray, go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret.” And Hebrews says, “And let us consider how to stir up one another to love and good works, not neglecting to meet together.” Both matter: a private faith that no one sees, and a shared faith with God’s people. A marriage will need to make room for both.

Slide 5 · Priscilla and Aquila

Scripture: Acts 18:1–3

Here is a couple from the New Testament. Paul arrived in Corinth and found a Jew named Aquila, with his wife, Priscilla, recently come from Italy. Luke tells us, “and because he was of the same trade he stayed with them and worked, for they were tentmakers by trade.” Paul stayed with them and worked alongside them because they shared the same trade. Their ordinary working life became part of their shared service.

Slide 6 · A home opened together

Scripture: Acts 18:26; Romans 16:3–5

Later, when a gifted preacher named Apollos needed fuller understanding, Priscilla and Aquila “took him aside and explained to him the way of God more accurately.” And Paul greets “the church in their house”. A couple who worked, served and opened their home together.

This is an encouragement, not a template. You do not need a church in your living room, or a shared trade. But their story shows that faith can be woven into a couple’s ordinary life together.

Slide 7 · Point 1: Same word, two lives

Our first point: same word, two lives. One imagines, “We’ll be deeply involved: serving, evangelising, at church several times a week, our children raised right in the middle of it.” The other, just as sincerely: “We’ll attend, we’ll pray, we’ll live as Christians, but our family life won’t revolve around church programmes.” Both pictures can belong to sincere Christian couples. Neither level of church activity, by itself, tells us the depth of a person’s faith. But the difference matters if neither partner has explained what they expect.

Then within a year, one feels their spouse is spiritually passive, while the other feels the marriage is being consumed by ministry commitments they never agreed to. So the conversation has to move from “are we Christians?” to “what does our faith actually require of the marriage we are building?”

Slide 8 · Out of the abstract

So force faith out of the abstract and into ordinary weeks. What role does prayer have in our marriage? Do I expect us to pray together daily? What does going to church mean: the same service, together, every week? How involved do I want to be in ministry, and how much time can I give before it starts costing the marriage? How do we make major decisions when we disagree?

And what does “spiritual leadership” mean? Two people can both affirm the phrase while picturing entirely different arrangements: one imagines someone who decides, another someone who serves, another someone who leads family devotions on a Sunday evening and otherwise decides jointly. The phrase does almost no work until you say what you mean by it. Lesson 11 looks at this more fully. But whatever your understanding of spiritual leadership, it must never become permission to control, intimidate or silence your spouse.

Slide 9 · Point 2: If no one at church could see it

Our second point is one question worth sitting with: what would my faith look like if nobody at church could see it? That question helps us distinguish outward church activity from the faith we practise when nobody is watching. They belong together, but they are not identical. A person can be very busy at church while neglecting prayer, truthfulness, kindness or repentance in ordinary life. So ask yourself: what does my faith look like on an ordinary Tuesday?

Slide 10 · Point 3: Different places on the journey

Our third point is the most sensitive part of this room: different places on the journey. Sometimes both people are genuinely Christians and simply at different places. One prays, reads Scripture and serves with discipline. The other believes in Christ sincerely, and rarely does those things with much discipline. That is not automatic grounds for calling off a wedding. But it does matter, and it will not stay quiet. The real question is: can the two of you respect each other’s actual current reality, and build a marriage on that?

Two dangerous assumptions often turn up, usually one in each person. The one who wants more involvement thinks, “Once we are married, I will bring them along.” The one who wants less thinks, “I don’t want this, but I will agree rather than risk losing the relationship.” Both are marrying a future version of the other person. That is people-pleasing from Lesson 1, and hoping marriage will change it from Lesson 3, appearing together.

Slide 11 · Two questions, each your own

So there are two questions, and each of you considers your own. To the one who wants more shared spiritual activity, a question about choosing now: if your partner’s present practice stayed the same, would you still freely choose this marriage? To the other, a question about the long term: if the present difference stays as it is, could you live peacefully with it for thirty years? When it is safe, consider your own question honestly, first on your own. You may share your answer, or return to it later. These are among the most clarifying questions in this course.

Slide 12 · Not a project

And here is the hardest part of this room. Do not let “more spiritually mature” become a synonym for “more spiritually important”. Length of service is not maturity. Maturity shows itself in character: in humility, faithfulness, self-control, repentance, love, truthfulness, and a willingness to be corrected. Neither of you should use this room to make the other person a copy of yourself. Ask a harder question: what kind of Christian spouse am I actually becoming?

And one instruction above all others here: do not marry someone’s spiritual potential. Marry the person in front of you. We hope and pray that both of you will grow. But a marriage should not rest on a private project to fix or upgrade your spouse. That makes one a perpetual disappointment and the other a perpetual supervisor. The better question is: can we build a marriage in which our faith draws us towards God, towards truth, and towards each other, even when our journeys are not identical?

Slide 13 · Point 4: Church, prayer, Scripture, giving, children

Our fourth point is the practical week. Which church, and how often? Do we serve, in what, and for how many hours? Do we pray together: when, and who leads? Do we read Scripture together, separately, or not much? What do we give, and how do we decide? As we saw in Lesson 6. And how are our children raised in the faith, week by week? As we saw in Lesson 9.

Some denominational differences can be managed with mutual understanding and practical agreement. Others involve convictions that neither person can responsibly set aside. Before marriage, discuss which church you will attend, what you believe about baptism and the Lord’s Supper, how you understand spiritual authority, and how you intend to raise children in the faith. Talk with your pastor. Do not assume these questions will resolve themselves after the wedding.

Slide 14 · The conversation

The conversation comes in five parts. Share what you freely choose, and you may pass on a question or return to it later. Part one is your own faith. Where am I actually, spiritually, not where I would like to be? What does my faith look like on an ordinary Tuesday, and if no one from church could see it? Where do I struggle? What do I doubt? Most Christians have doubts they have never told anyone. Part two is the practical week. Part three is authority and decisions: what does spiritual leadership mean to me, concretely? How do we decide when we disagree about a matter of faith? Who do we go to for counsel? What if one of us wants to leave our church? And part four is the children: how are they raised in the faith, and what if one of them, later, does not want to go?

Slide 15 · Part five: The honest one

Part five is the honest one. Each of you returns to your own question from earlier. Then there is one more: “Am I hoping you will become something different from what you are now?” When it is safe, consider it honestly, first on your own. You may share your answer, or return to it later.

And if either of you is in a period of real doubt, it is far better said, when you are ready, than carried silently into a marriage where the other believes they are marrying certainty. If your partner shares a doubt, listen as you learned in Lesson 5. A doubt honestly spoken is not a betrayal.

Slide 16 · If you find a real difference

Some differences in this room can be worked through with patience. But faith or Scripture used to control, shame or frighten is not an ordinary difference. As rule four in Lesson 2 says, Scripture is not a weapon. If that is happening, stop the joint exercises and seek confidential support on your own.

Now the differences that can be worked through. Different levels of church activity: agree specific commitments, with enough time for rest and for your relationship. More activity does not automatically mean deeper faith. Different denominations: discuss worship, membership and your children’s faith before marrying; do not assume it will settle itself. Different expectations of faith in daily life: if either of you is agreeing to a life you do not want, the wedding will not resolve that. A conviction one of you cannot compromise: infant baptism, for instance, becomes concrete the moment there is a baby. Identify these now. And one of you is doubting: say so, when you are ready, and let it be heard.

Slide 17 · Bringing it together

Let us bring it together. Same word, two lives: say what you mean by Christian. Ask what your faith would look like if no one at church could see it. Where you are at different places, marry the person in front of you. And agree the practical week specifically. The Christian question is not simply, “Do we both believe in Jesus?” It is: can we build a marriage in which our faith draws us towards God, towards truth, and towards each other? That is how two chairs turn, a little more towards each other.

Slide 18 · Prepare on your own

Now the exercise. First, on your own, prepare for the room. Begin with one small step: write down what your faith would look like if nobody at church could see it. Keep your inventory from Lesson 3 beside you. Then write your answers to the five parts: your own faith, the practical week, authority and decisions, the children, and the honest one. Nothing you type on the lesson page is saved.

Slide 19 · Talk together

When you are ready, come together. But first, if either of you is afraid of how the other will respond, do not do the together part. Stop, and speak with someone you trust on your own first.

For everyone else: review your rules from Lesson 2, and allow 45 to 60 minutes. Work through the five parts, sharing what you freely choose. You may pass on a question, stop earlier, or agree when to continue. Before you leave the room, describe a realistic week of worship, service, rest and time together. And record any unresolved conviction rather than smoothing it over.

Slide 20 · Remember

Scripture: Colossians 3:16–17

Remember these words of Scripture. “Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly.” And, “whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus.” Faith at home is made of ordinary days.

Slide 21 · Prayer

Let us pray. If you are watching together, you may like to pray this aloud together.

Lord Jesus, let your word dwell in our home richly. Keep us honest about where we are, and patient with each other on the way. Guard us from making each other a project, and teach us to love the person in front of us. Make our ordinary days thankful, and our home a place of welcome. In your name we pray. Amen.
