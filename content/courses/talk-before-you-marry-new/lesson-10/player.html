Room 5 · Faith and Church

What will our faith actually look like at home?

Colossians 3:16–17

“Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly, teaching and admonishing one another in all wisdom, singing psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, with thankfulness in your hearts to God.”Colossians 3:16 (ESV)

“And whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him.”Colossians 3:17 (ESV)

Written to a whole church

  • Paul writes to a whole church: “one another”
  • Applied here to the shared life of a Christian couple, who still belong to the wider church
  • “Whatever you do”: faith reaches into ordinary weeks

In secret, and together

“But when you pray, go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret.”Matthew 6:6

“And let us consider how to stir up one another to love and good works, not neglecting to meet together…”Hebrews 10:24–25

A private faith, and a shared one

Priscilla and Aquila (Acts 18; Romans 16)

“…and because he was of the same trade he stayed with them and worked, for they were tentmakers by trade.”Acts 18:3

Their ordinary working life became part of their shared service.

A home opened together

They “took him aside and explained to him the way of God more accurately.”Acts 18:26

“Greet also the church in their house.”Romans 16:5

An encouragement, not a template

1. Same word, two lives

  • One: deeply involved, serving, at church several times a week
  • The other: we’ll attend and pray, but family life won’t revolve around church programmes
  • Both can belong to sincere Christian couples. The difference matters if neither has explained what they expect.

Out of the abstract

  • Do we pray together daily?
  • Church: the same service, together, every week?
  • How much ministry before it costs the marriage?
  • How do we decide when we disagree?

“Spiritual leadership”: say what you mean by it

Lesson 11 looks at this more fully.

It must never become permission to control, intimidate or silence your spouse.

2. If no one at church could see it

What would my faith look like if nobody at church could see it?

They belong together, but they are not identical.

What does my faith look like on an ordinary Tuesday?

3. Different places on the journey

Can you respect each other’s actual current reality, and build a marriage on that?

  • “Once we are married, I will bring them along.”
  • “I will agree rather than risk losing the relationship.”
  • Both are marrying a future version of the other person.

Two questions, each your own

  • Choosing now: if your partner’s present practice stayed the same, would you still freely choose this marriage?
  • Living with it: if the present difference stays as it is, could you live peacefully with it for thirty years?

When it is safe, consider your own question first on your own. Share it if you choose.

Not a project

Length of service is not maturity.

humility faithfulness self-control repentance love truthfulness teachability

Do not use this room to make the other person a copy of yourself.

Do not marry someone’s spiritual potential. Marry the person in front of you.

Not a project: not a perpetual disappointment, nor a perpetual supervisor.

Can our faith draw us towards God, towards truth, and towards each other?

4. The practical week

  • Which church, and how often? Do we serve, and how many hours?
  • Do we pray together: when, and who leads?
  • Scripture: together, separately, or not much?
  • What do we give, and how do we decide? (Lesson 6) · Children (Lesson 9)

Different denominations

  • Some differences can be managed; others involve convictions neither can set aside.
  • Before marrying: which church; baptism and the Lord’s Supper; spiritual authority; children’s faith.
  • Talk with your pastor.

The conversation · share what you freely choose

  • Your own faith: where am I actually? On an ordinary Tuesday? Where do I struggle, or doubt?
  • The practical week
  • Authority and decisions: what does spiritual leadership mean to me, concretely? Who do we go to for counsel?
  • The children: how are they raised in the faith, week by week?

Part five · The honest one

“Am I hoping you will become something different from what you are now?”

When it is safe, consider it first on your own. Share it if you choose.

If you are in a period of real doubt, it is better said, when you are ready. A doubt honestly spoken is not a betrayal.

If you find a real difference

Faith or Scripture used to control, shame or frighten is not an ordinary difference. Stop the joint exercises; seek confidential support.
Church activity

Agree specific commitments, with time for rest and each other. More activity does not automatically mean deeper faith.

Denominations

Discuss worship, membership and your children’s faith before marrying.

Faith in daily life

If either is agreeing to a life they do not want, the wedding will not resolve it.

A conviction you cannot compromise

Identify it now (for example, infant baptism).

One of you doubting

Say so when you are ready, and let it be heard.

Bringing it together

  1. Same word, two lives: say what you mean
  2. Faith if no one at church could see it
  3. Different places: marry the person in front of you
  4. The practical week, agreed specifically

Can we build a marriage in which our faith draws us towards God, towards truth, and towards each other?
