What do I actually expect — and where did it come from?

What we intend can lie deeper than what we say.

The well and the bucket

Proverbs 20:5 speaks of understanding another person.

Here we apply it first to ourselves, before God.

1. How you find out what you expected

“When have I thought, about anyone, ‘of course they should have known better’?”

A strong reaction is worth understanding: what happened, what did I expect, and was a reasonable boundary crossed?

Find it, trace it, decide

Not every expectation is baggage. Not every reaction is an overreaction.

Find it. Trace where it came from. Then decide: communicate it, adjust it, or let it go.

2. The rules you never knew were rules

Have they broken an agreement we made, or a rule I never told them existed?

Three questions from the side

  1. What did my family always do that I assumed every married couple does?
  2. What would genuinely surprise me if my spouse didn’t do it?
  3. What would make me think something is wrong with our marriage, even if my spouse saw nothing wrong at all?

Custom or conviction?

“Each one should be fully convinced in his own mind.”Romans 14:5 (ESV)

Not every custom is a command.

When family customs are treated as God’s requirements, the other person is no longer free to discuss them honestly.

3. Preference, conviction or deal-breaker

  • Preference: something you would like and can live without
  • Conviction: something you hold because of your faith, your values, or your understanding of marriage
  • Deal-breaker: something you cannot accept in a marriage at all
  • Intimidation or violence is not a preference to negotiate.

Sorting honestly

  • Is this something I need, something I believe, or something I simply like?
  • If my partner never changed on this, could we still have a healthy marriage?
  • A conviction may also be a deal-breaker.
  • Do not call something a deal-breaker because you are angry about it today.

The log and the speck

“You hypocrite, first take the log out of your own eye, and then you will see clearly to take the speck out of your brother’s eye.”Matthew 7:5 (ESV)

A genuine biblical conviction should be examined through Scripture, not defended by attaching an isolated verse to something we simply prefer.

4. Say what you actually want

Money · Sex · Children · Faith and church · Family

  • This is what I want.
  • This is why I want it.
  • This is what I would be hoping for from you.
  • What do you want?

“I don’t know” is a real answer. Write it down.

Bringing it together

  1. Disappointment can reveal what you expected
  2. Some of your rules were never spoken
  3. Sort them: preference, conviction or deal-breaker
  4. Say what you actually want, and ask what they want
