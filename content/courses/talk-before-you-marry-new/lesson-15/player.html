Keep Talking

What do we do with what we have found — and how do we keep telling the truth for the next thirty years?

Colossians 3:12–15

“Put on then, as God’s chosen ones, holy and beloved, compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience, bearing with one another and, if one has a complaint against another, forgiving each other; as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive.”Colossians 3:12–13 (ESV)

“And above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony. And let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, to which indeed you were called in one body. And be thankful.”Colossians 3:14–15 (ESV)

The peace of Christ in a shared life

  • Paul writes to a church: the peace of Christ governing its common life, “in one body”
  • Applied here, as an application, to a couple: compassion, patience and forgiveness
  • Never a method for deciding who wins a disagreement, or whether a wedding should go ahead

The word: rule

βραβεύω

brabeuō · “to rule”

In Colossians 3:15: βραβευέτω · “let… rule”

Let the peace of Christ rule among you, shaping how you live together as members of one body.

An explanation of how it applies, not a replacement for the ESV’s “rule in your hearts”

1. When you find a real disagreement

  • First: don’t panic. A serious disagreement may be a reason to pause; it does not automatically mean ending.
  • Second: don’t minimise it. Not “We’ll work it out.”
  • Finding no major disagreement is not a failure, if your answers were honest and freely given.

Sort it (Lesson 3)

Preferencecan be negotiated
Convictionunderstanding and respect, without agreement
Incompatibilityneither can honestly live with
  • “I want to live in Bangkok; you want Chiang Mai.” “I definitely want children; you definitely do not.”
  • Understand what is at stake before you sort it.

Understand it first

Do not solve it in one conversation. Understand it first.

  • What does each of you actually want? Why? What fear, value, expectation or inherited assumption sits underneath?
  • A genuine compromise is freely agreed and respects what matters to both. It is not one person surrendering.

2. The ways forward

Agree Keep talking Postpone Decide not to marry

Deciding not to marry does not mean this course has failed. Do not let the deposit on the venue make the decision.

“A delayed wedding is far easier to manage than a deeply unresolved marriage.”

3. When to bring someone in

  • The same issue keeps returning · conversations become explosive or shut down
  • Serious distrust or concealment · a significant disagreement you cannot move

“Without counsel plans fail, but with many advisers they succeed.”Proverbs 15:22

“We have taken this as far as we can on our own.”

Where more communication is not the answer

Abuse, coercion or fear of retaliation: stop the joint exercises, and seek confidential specialist support independently. One of you being afraid to speak honestly belongs here too.
Serious deception or an ongoing addiction

Needs appropriate assessment. Match the help to the actual concern.

Choosing who to ask

  • Listens carefully; respects your faith and your autonomy
  • Explains their qualifications and the limits of confidentiality
  • Recognises abuse, and will not manufacture equal blame

“The counsellor’s job is not to help you win. It is to help you see clearly.”

An ordinary disagreement

A qualified premarital counsellor or trusted pastoral adviser, together

Abuse, intimidation or fear

Confidential specialist support, independently, not a joint session

Your own decision

You may speak with someone on your own

Ask before it becomes a crisis if you can. If you are already in crisis, it is still worth asking.

4. The habit that outlasts the engagement

The skills do not survive on their own. They need a habit to live in.

Something ordinary and scheduled: once a week, or once a fortnight, whatever you will actually keep.

The weekly check-in

  1. How are we doing?
  2. Is there anything you’ve found difficult to tell me?
  3. Is there anything I’ve done recently that hurt you?
  4. Is there anything you’re carrying that I haven’t noticed?
  5. What’s one thing we’re doing well?

“But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called ‘today’”Hebrews 3:13

Why the rhythm matters

If the only time you talk deeply is when something has gone wrong, every serious conversation will feel like a crisis.

Where honesty is part of the ordinary rhythm, difficult things have somewhere to land: smaller, earlier, among many ordinary conversations in which you listened well.

What this course was for

Not one difficult conversation, but a couple who can keep having difficult conversations for decades.

Not simply how rarely you disagree.

Years from now, can we still speak honestly without fear?
