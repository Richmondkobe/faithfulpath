Transcript and Scripture references

Slide 1 · Keep talking

Welcome back. This is Lesson 15, the last lesson of the course. You have walked through all nine rooms of the house. This lesson asks two questions. What do we do with what we have found? And how do we keep telling the truth for the next thirty years?

Slide 2 · Put on love

Scripture: Colossians 3:12–15

Our passage is Paul’s letter to the Colossians, chapter 3, verses 12 to 15.

“Put on then, as God’s chosen ones, holy and beloved, compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience, bearing with one another and, if one has a complaint against another, forgiving each other; as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive.

“And above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony. And let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, to which indeed you were called in one body. And be thankful.”

Slide 3 · The peace of Christ in a shared life

Scripture: Colossians 3:12–15

Paul was writing to a church. He is describing the peace of Christ governing the common life of believers, called together “in one body”. That is the setting, and we should hear it first. Then, as an application: a couple who want Christ’s peace to shape their shared life will practise compassion, patience and forgiveness with each other. This verse is not a method for deciding which of you wins a disagreement, or whether a wedding should go ahead. It describes the kind of people who can face those questions well.

Slide 4 · The word: rule

Scripture: Colossians 3:15

One word in that passage gives this lesson its shape. The Greek word translated “rule” is brabeuo. In the verse, Paul says, “let the peace of Christ rule”. The word was used of an umpire or judge at the games, and that picture is one recognised way of explaining it. But the point is not a referee between two players. Paul says, “rule in your hearts”, to people “called in one body”. So one way to explain how it applies is this: let the peace of Christ rule among you, shaping how you live together as members of one body.

Slide 5 · Point 1: When you find a real disagreement

Our first point: when you find a real disagreement. First, don’t panic. A serious disagreement does not automatically mean you should end the engagement, but it may be a reason to pause. Second, don’t minimise it. With a wedding date approaching, the temptation is to file the difficult thing under, “We’ll work it out.” That is Lesson 1 arriving at the finish line. And if you have found no major disagreement, that is not a failure either. The question is whether your answers were honest and freely given.

Slide 6 · Sort it

Then sort it, using the categories from Lesson 3. Is this a preference that can be negotiated? A conviction that needs mutual understanding and respect, without agreement? Or a fundamental incompatibility neither of you can honestly live with? Consider the difference between, “I want to live in Bangkok; you want Chiang Mai,” and, “I definitely want children; you definitely do not.” A location difference may be negotiable, but it can also involve essential work or caregiving. The disagreement about children may reveal a fundamental incompatibility. Understand what is at stake before you sort either one.

Slide 7 · Understand it first

Do not try to solve it in one conversation. Understand it first. What does each of you actually want? Why? And what sits underneath it: what fear, what value, what expectation, what inherited assumption? Only then ask, what would a genuine compromise look like, and what would simply be one person surrendering? A workable compromise is freely agreed and respects what matters to both people. An agreement made under pressure may leave a serious issue unresolved.

Slide 8 · Point 2: The ways forward

Our second point: the ways forward. Three common ways forward are to agree, keep talking with a date to return, or postpone the wedding. There is also a fourth possibility: after careful consideration, one or both of you may decide not to marry. That decision does not mean this course has failed. If something cannot be resolved before the wedding, do not hide it because the date is approaching. Do not let the deposit on the venue make the decision. A delayed wedding is far easier to manage than a deeply unresolved marriage. That sentence is worth hearing twice, if it applies to you.

Slide 9 · Point 3: When to bring someone in

Scripture: Proverbs 15:22

Our third point: when to bring someone in. Asking for help is not evidence that your relationship has failed. Bring someone in when the same issue keeps returning with no progress. When conversations reliably become explosive, or reliably shut down. When there is serious distrust, or something has been concealed. When you have found a significant disagreement that you cannot move. Proverbs says, “Without counsel plans fail, but with many advisers they succeed.” Sometimes the wisest sentence available to a couple is, “We have taken this as far as we can on our own.”

Slide 10 · Where more communication is not the answer

But there are situations where better communication is not the answer. If there is abuse, coercion or fear of retaliation, stop the joint exercises, and seek confidential specialist support independently. One of you being afraid to speak honestly belongs here too. Serious deception or an ongoing addiction also needs appropriate assessment, but neither should automatically be treated as the same situation. Match the help to the actual concern.

Slide 11 · Choosing who to ask

Choosing who to ask matters. Look for someone who listens carefully, respects your faith and your autonomy, explains their qualifications and the limits of confidentiality, and recognises abuse. Fairness does not require treating harmful conduct as a difference of opinion. A good helper will not manufacture equal blame, or try to preserve an engagement at any cost. The counsellor’s job is not to help you win. It is to help you see clearly. For an ordinary unresolved disagreement, a qualified premarital counsellor or trusted pastoral adviser may help you talk together. For abuse, intimidation or fear of retaliation, seek confidential specialist support independently, rather than arranging a joint counselling session. And if you need advice about your own decision, you may speak with someone on your own. Ask for help before the issue becomes a crisis if you can. If you are already in crisis, it is still worth asking. You have not missed your chance.

Slide 12 · Point 4: The habit that outlasts the engagement

Our fourth point: the habit that outlasts the engagement. Here is one way this work can lose its effect. A couple learn the skills and do the exercises. They marry, and then they put it all away like a textbook after an examination. Eighteen months later they are conducting their marriage exactly as they would have without any of it. The skills do not survive on their own. They need a habit to live in. So build a regular conversation: not only when something is wrong, but something ordinary and scheduled, once a week, or once a fortnight, whatever you will actually keep.

Slide 13 · The weekly check-in

Scripture: Hebrews 3:13

Here are five questions for that conversation. How are we doing? Is there anything you’ve found difficult to tell me? Is there anything I’ve done recently that hurt you? Is there anything you’re carrying that I haven’t noticed? And what’s one thing we’re doing well? That last question is not decoration. A marriage where the only structured conversation is about problems becomes a marriage where the sight of your spouse sitting down to talk produces dread. Hebrews, written to a church, says, “But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called ‘today’.” Encouragement is meant to be regular, not rare.

Slide 14 · Why the rhythm matters

Why does the rhythm matter more than the technique? If the only time you talk deeply is when something has gone wrong, every serious conversation will feel like a crisis. Couples can accidentally train themselves to associate honesty with danger, so the honesty gets postponed, and by the time it arrives it has grown. But where honesty is already part of the ordinary rhythm of a marriage, difficult things have somewhere to land. They arrive smaller, earlier, and among many ordinary conversations in which you listened well.

Slide 15 · The Readiness Review

Now the exercise for this lesson: the Readiness Review. Each of you, alone first, reviews all nine rooms. For each meaningful matter you identified, decide whether it is resolved, needs another conversation, or is serious enough that postponing the wedding may be wise. You do not need to invent an issue for every room. There is no score, no total, and no “ready” verdict. This page will not tell you whether to marry. It helps you see clearly what you have found.

Slide 16 · If something is in the third column

Some things do not belong in any column. If there is abuse, coercion or fear of retaliation, stop the joint exercises and seek confidential specialist support independently.

Now the third column. If a matter is there, consider what support or further information is needed; the lesson page links to Talk to a Pastor. You may seek advice independently, and neither of you needs the other’s permission to reconsider the wedding. If one of you wants to postpone, take that seriously. No one should be pressured into a wedding. Pressure from dates, deposits or family is not a reason to marry. And postponing, or deciding not to marry, can be faithful decisions. They are not failures.

Slide 17 · What this course was for

Let us bring it together. The goal of this course was never to prepare you for one difficult conversation. It was to help you become the kind of couple who can keep having difficult conversations for decades. Your engagement will end. The wedding will end, faster than the planning suggests. This course will end. The habit has to continue. And if you keep only one thing, keep this. The health of your marriage will not be measured simply by how rarely you disagree. One important measure is whether, years from now, both of you can still speak honestly without fear of punishment or retaliation.

Slide 18 · Prepare on your own

Now the exercise. First, on your own, complete the Readiness Review. Go back through the nine rooms, and place each meaningful matter in one of the three columns. Keep your inventory from Lesson 3 beside you, as your own private reference. Remember, there is no score and no verdict. Nothing you type on the lesson page is saved.

Slide 19 · Talk together

When you are ready, come together. But first, if either of you is afraid of how the other will respond, do not do the together part. Stop, and seek confidential support independently, from a suitable specialist or trusted support service.

For everyone else: review your rules from Lesson 2, and allow 45 to 60 minutes. Share what you freely choose from your Readiness Review. You may pass on a question, stop earlier, or agree when to continue. Compare your three columns, and agree what to do with anything in the second or third. Then set the date of your first weekly check-in, and write it down.

Slide 20 · The last reading: Colossians 3:9–17

Scripture: Colossians 3:9–17

We close the course with one last reading, joining the passages from Lessons 10 and 12. Colossians, chapter 3, verses 9 to 17.

“Do not lie to one another, seeing that you have put off the old self with its practices and have put on the new self, which is being renewed in knowledge after the image of its creator. Here there is not Greek and Jew, circumcised and uncircumcised, barbarian, Scythian, slave, free; but Christ is all, and in all.

“Put on then, as God’s chosen ones, holy and beloved, compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience, bearing with one another and, if one has a complaint against another, forgiving each other; as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive. And above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony. And let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, to which indeed you were called in one body. And be thankful.

“Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly, teaching and admonishing one another in all wisdom, singing psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, with thankfulness in your hearts to God. And whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him.”
