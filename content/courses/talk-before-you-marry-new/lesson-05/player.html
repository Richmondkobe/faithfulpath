Lesson 5 · Book chapter 5 · Part 2: Learn the Skills

Learn to Hear the Hard Thing

How do I listen when the hard thing is about me?

James 1:19–20

“Know this, my beloved brothers: let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger; for the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of God.”James 1:19–20 (ESV)

Hearers and doers

  • James teaches believers to receive God’s word humbly and put it into practice.
  • “quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger” also challenges how we respond to others.
  • Not a passage specifically about marriage, but a principle for difficult conversations.
  • “But be doers of the word, and not hearers only.” James 1:22

The word: quick

ταχύς

tachys · “quick, swift”

In James 1:19: ταχὺς εἰς τὸ ἀκοῦσαι · “quick to hear”

For our conversations: eager to hear, unhurried to answer.

The listening ear

“If one gives an answer before he hears, it is his folly and shame.”Proverbs 18:13 (ESV)

“he who listens to reproof gains intelligence”Proverbs 15:32

A challenge to the listener, never a verse to use against your partner.

1. What defensiveness teaches

“I feel like you don’t listen to me when we’re making decisions.”

“That’s not true. I listen to you all the time. You’re always complaining about something.”

When honesty becomes expensive

“When I tell the truth about how I feel, I pay for it.”

If you punish honesty, do not be surprised when you eventually receive silence.

  • “Tell me more.”
  • “What did I do that gave you that experience?”
  • “I may see this differently, but I want to understand you first.”

2. Listening is not waiting

One question, and then silence: “What makes you feel that way?”

Understanding is not agreement

Understanding is not agreement.

“I understand why you felt neglected, and I didn’t realise the hours were landing on you that way. I’d also like you to understand why I’ve been working them.”

The repeat-back rule

  • Summarise what you heard. Ask: “Have I got that right?”
  • Both people get to speak.
  • Understand before you defend. You do not have to stay in a harmful conversation.

“I understand why that hurt you.”

3. Find the information

There may be a genuine concern beneath the exaggeration. That does not make every accusation true.

“The way you said that hurt me. But is there something you’re trying to tell me that I need to understand?”

The truth inside what I hear

“Let me find the truth inside what I’m hearing.”

Not “everything my partner says is true”. Not “everything my partner says is an attack”.

No one has to accept a false accusation to show humility.

4. What the truth requires of you

Do not only acknowledge the truth. Decide what the truth requires of you.

Four responsibilities

Acknowledge it · Examine yourself · Take responsibility · Make it concrete

“From now on, anything over this amount we decide together.”

When you disagree, or need time

  • You may hear it fully, understand it properly, and still disagree.
  • “I hear you. I need to think about this properly.” Then name when you will come back, and come back.
  • A change · an apology · a boundary · another conversation · “I understand, and I don’t agree.”

What must not happen is that you dismiss the concern without considering it.

“So Moses listened to the voice of his father-in-law and did all that he had said.”Exodus 18:24 (ESV)

He heard, and something changed.

Bringing it together

  1. Notice what defensiveness teaches
  2. Listen before you answer; repeat it back
  3. Find the truth inside what you hear
  4. Decide what the truth requires of you

Prepare on your own · The Other Side

Done the Lesson 4 conversation? Now swap roles.

Waited? Do the first conversation first, and return to this later.

Never two difficult conversations in one sitting.

The one raising it: the event · the effect · the ask, and the four tests

The one receiving it:

What do I usually do when I feel criticised?

What is my early warning sign?

What is the sentence I most want to say, that I am going to hold?

If either of you is afraid of how the other will respond, do not do the together part.

Stop, and speak with someone you trust on your own first.

Talk together

  1. Ask a question
  2. Summarise, and check
  3. Only now, respond

Say what it requires

  • I’m going to change…
  • I understand, and I disagree…
  • I need time; I’ll come back on…
  • I can do this, and not this…

“Did you feel heard, or did you feel handled?”

In one month: did anything actually change? If not, seek help rather than repeating the technique.

Remember

“let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger”James 1:19

“But be doers of the word, and not hearers only.”James 1:22

Prayer

Father, you hear us when we speak, and you are patient with us.

Make us quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger.

When the hard thing is about us, give us humility to find what is true, and courage to change.

Guard us from punishing honesty, and from accepting what is false.

Make us doers, and not hearers only. In Jesus’ name. Amen.
