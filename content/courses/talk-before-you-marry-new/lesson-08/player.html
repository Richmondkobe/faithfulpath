Room 3 · Family and Boundaries

How do we honour our parents and still build a new home?

Genesis 2:24

“Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and they shall become one flesh.”Genesis 2:24 (ESV)

The first marriage

  • Genesis 2:24 follows the creation of the woman and the first marriage.
  • Jesus quotes it when he teaches about marriage (Matthew 19:4–6).
  • The verse speaks of the man; the new household belongs to both.

Honour, for life

  • “Honor your father and your mother” Exodus 20:12
  • Repeated for believers in Ephesians 6:2 · “make some return to their parents” 1 Timothy 5:4
  • Family responsibility, not an unlimited obligation to every request
  • Leaving changes the order of loyalties. It does not end the duty of love.

The words: leave, and hold fast

עָזַבʿāzaḇ · “leave, depart from”in the verse: יַעֲזָב
דָּבַקdāḇaq · “cling, hold fast”in the verse: וְדָבַק

A new marriage bond: not necessarily distance, but a new relationship that decides together.

Two homes, and a new one

The paths stay open. The new home has its own door.

A boundary is a gate, not a wall.

1. What usually goes wrong

Feelings, not arrangements: “I love my parents.” “My family is very important to me.”

  • Who decides about our home? How often will parents visit?
  • Can a parent stay indefinitely? How much money will we provide?
  • What happens when a parent criticises my spouse?
  • When your spouse and your parents disagree, who comes first?

No united position

“That’s my mother. You can’t tell her she needs permission to come to her son’s house.”

What she hears: “My mother’s comfort matters more than yours.”

Are we a married couple who decides things together?

The same happens just as often the other way round.

Loyalty conflict

  • Setting any boundary with a parent feels like betrayal.
  • So the spouse is expected to absorb the consequences quietly.
  • Resentment builds towards the spouse, not the parent: exactly backwards.

2. Leaving and holding fast

  • Marriage creates a new primary unit that can make its own decisions.
  • Honour is not obedience to every parental demand.
  • “I love you, Mum, but my wife and I have decided differently.”
  • “Dad, I appreciate the advice, but this is something the two of us need to decide.”

A boundary is not rejection

  • Support your parents without giving them a vote in your household.
  • Visit often without letting them govern your marriage.

“I love you enough to have an honest relationship with you, and I will not let this relationship run our marriage.”

3. Where Christian couples get this wrong

Scripture used as leverage, on both sides

“The Bible says I have to honour her.” · “You’re supposed to choose me.”

Rule four (Lesson 2): Scripture is not a weapon.

  1. Honour your parents.
  2. Leave your parents.
  3. Hold fast to your spouse.
  4. Love extended family without letting it govern your marriage.

4. Part one · The arrangements

Be specific where you can; record what is still open.

  • How often will we see each set of parents?
  • Can family visit without checking with both of us?
  • Will a parent have a key?
  • Can parents stay with us, and for how long?
  • Financial responsibility to parents: the actual figure
  • If a parent needs money, who decides?
  • Christmas, Easter and significant days: the same every year?

Part two · What we tell our parents

  • When we disagree, what can I tell my parents?
  • You forgive each other. Your parents may go on carrying the offence.
  • Keeping the marriage private must never mean keeping harm secret. No one needs permission to seek confidential help.
  • A sibling, a friend, a pastor: where is the line?

Parts three and four

  • When a parent criticises you · when parents want what we have decided against
  • Who speaks to their own parents? Each of us, where it is safe and practical.
  • Neither of you is left alone to absorb hostility.

When it is safe, each of you may consider:

“When we are married, who do I expect to disappoint sometimes: my parents or my spouse?”

Discuss it if you are both comfortable. You may pause, or return later.

When you must choose, you choose together.

If you find a real difference

Pressure to marry, intimidation, isolation or control are not ordinary differences. No joint exercise: seek confidential support.
Closeness

Agree visits, notice and private time. Culture is never an excuse for intrusion or control.

Money to parents

Name the actual amount and expectation (Lesson 6).

A parent who does not accept the marriage

Decide together; not managed by the one they disapprove of.

A parent who already governs decisions

Be honest now; talk with someone outside both families.

A parent who will need care

Say so now: where, who provides, what it costs.

Bringing it together

  1. Arrangements, not only feelings
  2. Leave and hold fast; keep honouring
  3. Scripture is not leverage
  4. Visits, holidays, privacy and support, agreed specifically

What boundaries will protect our marriage while we continue to love our families well?
