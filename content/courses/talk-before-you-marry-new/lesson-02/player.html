Lesson 2 · Book chapter 2 · Part 1: Make Honesty Safe

Build a Relationship That Can Hold Disagreement

How do we make it safe to tell the truth before we ask for it?

Contract or covenant?

  • A contract normally defines particular obligations and conditions.
  • Scripture describes marriage as a covenant: a solemn commitment of faithfulness before God.
  • For now, you are preparing for that covenant, and remain free to reconsider.

What covenant never means

Covenant never means tolerating abuse.

Engagement is a time of discernment. You remain free to postpone or end it.

2. The rules you agree on first

We are going to make it safe to tell the truth before we ask each other to tell the truth.

How we speak · How we handle tension · What we do afterwards

How we speak

  1. Tell the truth without trying to wound
  2. Keep to one issue
  3. Speak from your own experience
  4. Do not use Scripture as a weapon

How we handle tension

  1. Listen before you defend
  2. Either of you can ask for a break, and you agree when to check in
  3. Do not use silence as punishment
  4. Do not use threats of separation to win an argument

What we do afterwards

  1. Do not confuse explanation with excuse
  2. Seek support without recruiting people to shame each other
  3. Repair the harm you cause

Most of them are about making it safe to be told something you do not want to hear.

3. Choosing the moment

Choose the moment when you are both most capable of hearing it.

If you postpone it, name when you are coming back to it, and come back.

4. Repair

Separate the problem from the damage done while trying to solve it.

Four steps of repair

Acknowledge · Own your part · Reconnect, if welcome · Return to the issue

“We have not resolved the problem. But we can take responsibility for the harm we caused while discussing it.”

Forgiveness, reconciliation and restored trust are related, but not the same.

Go first

“First be reconciled to your brother, and then come and offer your gift.”Matthew 5:23–24 (ESV)

Jesus puts reconciliation before worship.

Where there is fear or coercion, safety and appropriate help come first.

Prepare on your own · Your Rules of Engagement

Which of the eleven rules will be hardest for me to keep?

Which rule do I most need my partner to keep, and why?

Is there something not on the list that I need in order to feel safe telling the truth?

If either of you is afraid of how the other will respond, do not do the together part.

Stop, and speak with someone you trust on your own first.

Talk together

  • Share what you choose from your answers
  • Go through the eleven rules and reword them
  • Put your own additions at the top
  • Agree how you will pause, and your signal

Write down your agreed rules. Signing is optional; no one should be pressured. Review them in six weeks.
