1. Different histories, no shame

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”1 John 1:9 (ESV)

What was done to you was not your fault.

Both arrive at the same door, and both receive the same welcome.

What usually goes wrong

Silence

Everything discussed, except this.

Shame about the questions

Real questions, and a voice saying you should not ask them.

Attraction is not preparation

Chemistry tells you almost nothing about expectations.

Imported expectations

From films, friends, novels or pornography.

“Once we’re married, it will all happen naturally.”

The wedding changes your commitments. It does not make comfort or shared desire automatic.

  • What did I come to believe was shameful?
  • Which messages do I still believe? Which do I want to leave behind?

What do I believe it is for in marriage?

closeness bonding covenant children

Different frameworks produce different disappointments.

3. Mutuality, not entitlement

  • Consent is freely given, and can be withdrawn at any time.
  • Silence, a previous yes or the marriage itself is not continuing permission.
  • No one should have to bargain for the right to refuse.

No must be safe. If it is not, that is not a difference of expectation. It needs help from outside the two of you.

Language for learning each other

  • “Can we try that differently?”
  • “I like this.”
  • “I’m not comfortable with that.”
  • “I need more time.”
  • “I’m not in the mood tonight, but I want to be close to you.” (optional)

You do not owe an explanation to make a no valid.

The wedding night, and health

The wedding night is the beginning of learning each other. It is not an examination.

  • Pain is a reason to stop, not a test of commitment. Seek a qualified healthcare professional.
  • Before marriage: ask about health, testing and contraception. Preparation, not an accusation.

4. The conversation, in five parts

Part one · What you were taught

Part two · What you believe now

  • What is it for, in a marriage?
  • What does affection mean to me outside of sex?
  • What do I hope it will be like between us?

Part three · The fears

  • What am I nervous about?
  • What am I afraid you will expect of me?
  • What am I afraid I won’t be able to give you?

The quiet here is usually not because there is nothing to say.

Part four · The agreements

  • How will we tell each other what we want, in the moment and afterwards?
  • What will we do when one of us wants to and the other doesn’t?
  • How do we make sure a no is never punished?
  • Contraception: have we both actually thought about it?
  • What is off-limits for each of us, without having to justify it?

Part five · The hard one

When it is safe, each of you may consider:

“Is there anything about my history, my struggles or my expectations that you need to know before we marry?”

No graphic details. You may pause, seek confidential advice, or return later.

Listen without pressing. Thank them. Ask what support they want. Allow time.

If you find a real difference

Pressure, coercion or a refused no are not ordinary differences. Stop the joint exercises; seek confidential support.
Frequency

No level is “correct”; no quota. Consent applies each time.

Comfort levels

The more cautious person’s boundary holds.

Pornography

Take a current struggle seriously and seek support. Marriage will not fix it.

Painful experiences

Not your fault. Support at your own pace.

Bringing it together

  1. Different histories, no shame
  2. What we were taught, and what we believe now
  3. Mutuality, not entitlement
  4. The conversation, taken slowly

The goal is not to enter marriage knowing everything. It is to enter marriage knowing you can approach difficult subjects with honesty, respect and freedom.
