κεφαλή · kephalē · “head” The question is how Paul uses “head” here: as Christ is the head of the church.

Two readings, fairly

Complementarian reading

Verse 21 introduces the passage; 22–33 show submission in particular relationships.

The husband has a real, loving responsibility to lead, always sacrificially and with his wife’s counsel.

Egalitarian reading

Verse 21 governs the whole passage; Paul applies mutual submission to the households of his day.

Significant decisions are made together; neither spouse holds a final say.

Real differences, with real consequences, held by Christians who love Christ.

Even within each reading, Christians differ in practice. Ask for examples.

Not negotiable in this course
  1. Christ’s love is never an excuse for domination.
  2. No interpretation of submission permits coercion or violence.
  3. Neither of you may use Scripture to silence a legitimate concern.
  4. Before you marry, work out what your beliefs mean in actual household decisions.

When you hold different positions

  1. Explain your conviction, without attacking the other’s. Question first · repeat it back · then respond.
  2. Which passages matter most to me, and how did I come to hold them?
  3. What would this belief actually require of my spouse in everyday life?

I will not tell either of you which position to take.

Any account of marriage that leaves one person unable to raise a concern, unable to disagree, or unable to be heard is not a biblical arrangement.

Complementarian and egalitarian marriages can both be healthy. Neither can be healthy where honesty is unsafe.

If each of us described an ordinary Tuesday five years from now, what would our roles look like?

Two people who agree in principle can describe two different Tuesdays.

  • What counts as a major decision?
  • How do we decide when we agree, and when we do not?
  • Can either of us say no, and on what?
  • How is a decision that turned out badly raised?
  • No automatic veto over everything.
  • No one forced, threatened or punished into agreement.
  • Body, safety and wellbeing are never overridden by appeal to headship or submission.

Bringing it together

  1. The same words can hide two different marriages
  2. Submitting to one another; wives to husbands as to the Lord; husbands loving as Christ loved
  3. Two readings, much shared ground, four principles
  4. It has to work on an ordinary Tuesday, with both of you free to speak

Prepare on your own

My ordinary Tuesday, five years from now: morning to night.

What I mean by headship · submission · partnership, with a practical example for each.

The example will help you discover whether you both mean the same thing.

What would my belief require of my spouse in everyday life?

If either of you is afraid of how the other will respond, do not do the together part.

Stop, and speak with someone you trust on your own first.

Talk together

  • Share the parts of your Tuesdays you choose. Listen first, then ask.
  • The three words, each with its example
  • The household tasks, including planning and remembering
  • The decisions and the test cases

If one of you cannot say no in your arrangement: stop, and speak to someone outside the relationship.
