Building a Volunteer Team

Who should I ask — and how do I ask them well?

Why a general announcement may not be enough

“We’re desperate for help. If you feel led, see me afterwards.”

A clear invitation helps people know whether a role suits them.

Exodus 18:13–22

Exodus 18 · verses 13–14, 17–18 and 21–22

“The next day Moses sat to judge the people, and the people stood around Moses from morning till evening. When Moses’ father-in-law saw all that he was doing for the people, he said, ‘What is this that you are doing for the people? Why do you sit alone, and all the people stand around you from morning till evening?’”Exodus 18:13–14 (ESV)

“Moses’ father-in-law said to him, ‘What you are doing is not good. You and the people with you will certainly wear yourselves out, for the thing is too heavy for you. You are not able to do it alone.’”Exodus 18:17–18 (ESV)

“‘Moreover, look for able men from all the people, men who fear God, who are trustworthy and hate a bribe, and place such men over the people as chiefs of thousands, of hundreds, of fifties, and of tens. And let them judge the people at all times. Every great matter they shall bring to you, but any small matter they shall decide themselves. So it will be easier for you, and they will bear the burden with you.’”Exodus 18:21–22 (ESV)

A father-in-law’s visit

  • Exodus 18:1–12 — Jethro, priest of Midian, visits after the exodus
  • Exodus 18:13–16 — Moses judging from morning till evening
  • The setting: the appointment of judges in Israel. The principle: shared responsibility.

The original meaning

ʾanšê ḥayil“able men” · Exodus 18:21

ḥayil: strength · capability · worth

Proverbs 31:10“An excellent wife” — the same word, ḥayil
Exodus 18:21ability, strength, competence: “able” means more than available
ablefear Godtrustworthyhate a bribe

Capability and character together.

Three things to notice

  1. Character, not just availability
  2. Chosen deliberately — with a fair route for anyone to offer
  3. Given real decisions: “any small matter they shall decide themselves”

Jesus prayed all night, then chose

“All night he continued in prayer to God. And when day came, he called his disciples and chose from them twelve.”Luke 6:12–13 (ESV)

“…so that they might be with him and he might send them out to preach.”Mark 3:14 (ESV)

Being with him, as well as being sent.

1. Who to ask

  1. Character — “of good repute, full of the Spirit and of wisdom” Acts 6:3
  2. Faithfulness in something small — “entrust to faithful men, who will be able to teach others also” 2 Timothy 2:2
  3. Some evidence of the gift — “God arranged the members in the body” 1 Corinthians 12:18
  4. Teachability, alongside the competence the role requires

What the role itself requires

Every role has its own suitability and preparation requirements.

Children and young people: references, safeguarding checks, training and supervision before anyone begins.

The same criteria and checks for everyone.

2. How to ask

  1. In person, one at a time
  2. For a specific role, not for “help”
  3. Say why you asked them

How to ask, part two

  1. Give the whole picture: tasks, hours, term, training, who they report to, any checks
  2. Set a term and a review date — for example, six months
  3. “It’s completely fine to say no.”
  4. “Take a week, pray about it, and let me know.”

Nehemiah’s lesson about ownership

“…each one opposite his own house.”Nehemiah 3:28 (ESV)

Consider where people already have a meaningful connection to the work.

When they say no

Take it graciously. A no is information, not rejection.

Sometimes it is about timing. Sometimes the role is not a fit.

Accept the first clear no.

Bringing it together

  1. Share the load with capable, trustworthy people, and give them real decisions
  2. Choose prayerfully, and build relationship before tasks
  3. Know what the role requires, and use the same checks for everyone
  4. Ask clearly, give the whole picture, and let people say no

“So it will be easier for you, and they will bear the burden with you.”

What made it work

  1. One person · in person · a specific role
  2. Why him · the whole picture · a review date
  3. Subject to approval and checks · freedom to say no · time to answer

Even a no can leave someone knowing they are seen and valued.

This week’s action

1. First confirm the role is needed, and that you have permission to invite someone.

2. Ask one person, in person if you can, for one specific role.

3. Say why you chose them. Give the hours, the term and review date, the training, and any checks.

4. Give them permission to say no.

One conversation. Do it properly.

Remember

“You are not able to do it alone.”Exodus 18:18

“So it will be easier for you, and they will bear the burden with you.”Exodus 18:22

Where the work is more than one person should carry, invite others to carry it with you.
