Lesson 6 · Book chapter 6 · Part 2: Learn the Craft

Running a Meeting People Want to Attend

How do I lead a meeting that respects people’s time and moves the work forward?

Volunteers resent wasted evenings

A volunteer will often give you a Saturday morning cheerfully.

What can gradually wear them down is ninety minutes that achieves nothing.

Every meeting needs a clear purpose.

A question about the gospel

  • Acts 15:1–2 — “no small dissension and debate”
  • The question: must Gentile believers be circumcised and keep the law of Moses?
  • A unique decision, with apostolic authority. We draw only the shape of the process.

The original meaning

ἔδοξεν

edoxen“it seemed good” · Acts 15:22 · 25 · 28

a common word for reaching a considered judgement

“For it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us…”Acts 15:28 (ESV)

Unique to the council — not a pattern for claiming divine authority over our own preferences.

Seek God · listen carefully · reach a clear judgement · tell people plainly

Five things the council did

  1. One clear question
  2. Evidence from people who had been there
  3. Genuine debate
  4. A stated decision
  5. A written message to those affected

Five features of this account, not five rules for every meeting.

Acts 6, again — for the process

A problem stated plainly · a proposal · clear criteria · the group chooses

“And what they said pleased the whole gathering.”Acts 6:5 (ESV)

Clear responsibility and follow-through, not speed at any cost.

“Then I arose in the night… and inspected the wall.”Nehemiah 2:12, 15 (ESV)

“Let us rise up and build.”Nehemiah 2:18 (ESV)

His preparation helped him involve others well.

1. Do the thinking before, not during

“Without counsel plans fail, but with many advisers they succeed.”Proverbs 15:22 (ESV)

Arrive with a proposal, not a blank page.

“Here’s what I’m thinking, and why. Tell me what I’ve got wrong.”

2. Order is a spiritual concern

“For God is not a God of confusion but of peace.”1 Corinthians 14:33 (ESV)

“But all things should be done decently and in order.”1 Corinthians 14:40 (ESV)

Starting and finishing on time is a way of caring for people.

3. One page, sent at least two days ahead

Checking in between meetings keeps the next one from becoming a review of what didn’t happen.

4. Make room for every voice

“I’d like to hear from someone who hasn’t spoken yet.”

“Would you like to add anything, now or after the meeting?”

Make room for different ways to contribute.

How often?

Meet as often as the work and the people need.

Habit alone is a poor reason to meet.

Bringing it together
