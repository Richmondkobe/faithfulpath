Growing the Next Leader

Who will carry this after me — and am I making room for them?

The measure nobody uses

Most of us measure leadership by what is happening now.

What will still be standing five years after you leave, and who will be leading it?

It is often the last thing new leaders think about. It should be one of the first.

The fear that stops us

Time, resources and people can be real obstacles. Sometimes the obstacle lies within us.

The fear rarely announces itself. It often looks like high standards.

“He must increase, but I must decrease.”John 3:30 (ESV) · John, speaking of Christ

Christians differ

“…and greater works than these will he do, because I am going to the Father.”John 14:12 (ESV)

Christians understand “greater works” in different ways. None of these readings makes a disciple greater than Jesus.

Whatever we build, the work was always his.

How to do it

  • ✦ 1. Spot them by faithfulness, alongside character, ability, willingness and the needs of the role
  • ✦ 2. Bring them into the thinking, not just the doing
  • ✦ 3. Give them something real, early

Roles with children, young people or vulnerable adults: your church’s checks come first.

How to do it

  • ✦ 4. Room to learn, with clear limits
  • ✦ 5. In low-risk matters, let them do it differently
  • ✦ 6. Talk about their future, not your vacancy

Hand over in the open, then leave well

Ordinary delegation is not an appointment.

When someone is appointed to lead, hand over in the open, with the agreement of those who appoint leaders. Then visibly stop deciding it.

Leave it better documented, better staffed and better understood.

Prayer

Give me grace to be faithful, and eyes to see the faithful people around me.

Make me a Barnabas: quick to vouch, willing to go and fetch, and glad to make room for others.

Free me from the fear of being replaceable.

Help me to pass on what you entrusted to me, whole and unspoiled.

In Jesus’ name. Amen.
