Knowing Your Role

What is mine to decide — and how do I serve under authority with a clear conscience?

When roles are unclear

Many new-leader problems begin with unclear roles.

Often unclear boundaries, not poor character. And boundaries can be fixed.

1 Peter 5:1–5

“So I exhort the elders among you, as a fellow elder and a witness of the sufferings of Christ, as well as a partaker in the glory that is going to be revealed:1 Peter 5:1 (ESV)

shepherd the flock of God that is among you, exercising oversight, not under compulsion, but willingly, as God would have you; not for shameful gain, but eagerly; not domineering over those in your charge, but being examples to the flock.1 Peter 5:2–3 (ESV)

And when the chief Shepherd appears, you will receive the unfading crown of glory.1 Peter 5:4 (ESV)

Likewise, you who are younger, be subject to the elders. Clothe yourselves, all of you, with humility toward one another, for ‘God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble.’”1 Peter 5:5 (ESV)

A fellow elder, writing to scattered churches

1 Peter 1:1 — “To those who are elect exiles of the Dispersion”

Peter writes to elders. We apply the pattern, carefully, to every leader in the church.

Leaders serve without domination.

Accountability runs both ways

Leaders answer to the chief Shepherd — and, in practice, through the church’s oversight and processes.

The younger are called to be subject; all are called to humility.

Accountability belongs on both sides.

Point 1

Delegated authority

“For I too am a man under authority, with soldiers under me.”Matthew 8:9 (ESV)

“With no one in Israel have I found such faith.”Matthew 8:10 (ESV)

Under authority, within limits

A church is not an army. The centurion’s words illustrate delegated authority; they are not a model of church government.

Your responsibilities are entrusted to you. They have limits, and remain accountable to Scripture and proper oversight.

Point 2

Accountability

You answer to someone — and so do they.

“…as those who will have to give an account.”Hebrews 13:17 (ESV)

Point 3 · Obedience and cooperation

“Obey your leaders and submit to them, for they are keeping watch over your souls, as those who will have to give an account. Let them do this with joy and not with groaning, for that would be of no advantage to you.”Hebrews 13:17 (ESV)

Cooperation, read in its setting

Leaders keep watch over souls, and must give an account.

Make their work a joy, not a groaning.

Cooperation is not silence, and it is not agreement with wrongdoing.

Honest disagreement, in the right room

  • Galatians 2:11–14 — Paul opposes Peter “to his face” over the truth of the gospel
  • Acts 15 — a serious dispute, openly discussed, decided together
  • Acts 15:36–40 — Paul and Barnabas part company; God used both

Before a decision: say it honestly. After: support it, or step back from that part.

For ordinary, lawful decisions. Review, appeal and reporting remain open.

Point 4

Reporting wrongdoing

Numbers 16: a grave challenge to Moses and Aaron’s appointed roles.

Not a verse to quote at anyone who asks a hard question.

Asking for help is not rebellion

Do not spread accusations or stir up division to gain influence.

Seeking advice, gathering evidence, using a complaints process or reporting harm is not rebellion.

Submission never requires covering wrongdoing.

Getting the boundary drawn

  1. 1. What decisions can I make alone?
  2. 2a. Which decisions need approval before I act?
  3. 2b. Which situations must I report promptly, whether or not I have already acted?
  4. 3. What do you want to hear about, even when I’ve handled it?
  5. 4. How do you want me to raise a disagreement, and when?

Bringing it together

  1. Delegated authority: entrusted, limited and accountable
  2. Accountability: you answer to someone, and so do they
  3. Cooperation: honest before, faithful after, in ordinary decisions
  4. Reporting wrongdoing through appropriate channels is not rebellion

This week’s action

1. Ask your pastor or ministry head for twenty minutes.

2. Take the four questions. Write the answers down while you’re there.

3. Email them back the same day: “Here is what I understand we agreed.”

If an urgent concern needs attention, use the right route now. Don’t wait for this meeting.

Remember

“Shepherd the flock of God that is among you… not domineering over those in your charge, but being examples to the flock.”1 Peter 5:2–3

“Clothe yourselves, all of you, with humility toward one another.”1 Peter 5:5

Your authority is a trust. Hold it humbly, use it to serve, and know where it begins and ends.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, chief Shepherd of your people, thank you for the trust you have given me.

Help me to serve willingly and eagerly, never domineering, but as an example.

Give me humility to work under those who lead me, and courage to speak honestly in the right place.

Keep me from gossip and division, and never let me stay silent about what is wrong.

Make my part of your church a place where people are safe and served.

In your name. Amen.
