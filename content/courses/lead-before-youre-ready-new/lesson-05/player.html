Lesson 5 · Book chapter 5 · Part 2: Learn the Craft

Helping Volunteers Stay and Grow

Why do good volunteers quietly fade — and what helps them stay and grow?

Recruiting is the easy part

People say yes, serve for a few months, drift to the back of the rota, and quietly stop.

People leave for many good reasons. Three problems can be prevented.

Ephesians 4:11–16

Ephesians 4 · verses 11–16

“And he gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, the shepherds and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ,Ephesians 4:11–12 (ESV)

until we all attain to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to mature manhood, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ, so that we may no longer be children, tossed to and fro by the waves and carried about by every wind of doctrine, by human cunning, by craftiness in deceitful schemes.Ephesians 4:13–14 (ESV)

Rather, speaking the truth in love, we are to grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ, from whom the whole body, joined and held together by every joint with which it is equipped, when each part is working properly, makes the body grow so that it builds itself up in love.”Ephesians 4:15–16 (ESV)

The gifts of the risen Christ

  • Ephesians 4:1–6 — one body, one Spirit, one hope
  • Ephesians 4:7–11 — “he gave”: gifts from the ascended Christ
  • Verse 12 — the purpose: to equip the saints for the work of ministry

The original meaning

καταρτισμόν

katartismos“to equip” · Ephesians 4:12 · once in the New Testament

making ready for use: preparing · putting in order · fitting out

Mark 1:19“in their boat mending the nets”
Galatians 6:1“restore him in a spirit of gentleness”

These pictures help us hear the word family — Ephesians 4:12 is about making people ready for ministry.

“…when each part is working properly” Ephesians 4:16

Three things to notice

  1. The work of ministry belongs to the whole body
  2. Equipping means patient preparation
  3. The aim is growth in love, not only tasks done

Jesus sent them, then listened

“He called the twelve and began to send them out two by two.”Mark 6:7 (ESV)

“The apostles returned to Jesus and told him all that they had done and taught.”Mark 6:30 (ESV)

“Do not rejoice in this… but rejoice that your names are written in heaven.”Luke 10:20 (ESV)

Prepare people · send them out together · listen when they return

1. Prepare, send, and listen

  1. Prepare: say exactly what is expected
  2. Send: not alone, where you can
  3. Listen: ask how it went, and how they are

2. Four steps that pass on a skill

  1. I do it, you watch
  2. We do it together
  3. You do it, I watch
  4. You do it, I’m available

Safeguarding, finances or confidential information: follow your church’s supervision requirements throughout.

3. Delegate the outcome, not only the task

“Put out forty chairs in six rows.”

“Make sure the room is ready and feels welcoming by half past nine.”

Agree the outcome, the limits and the standard, within the person’s role.

4. Thank people specifically

“…esteem them very highly in love because of their work.”1 Thessalonians 5:13 (ESV)

Name the person · name the action · name why it mattered

Watch for weariness

“And let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up.”Galatians 6:9 (ESV)

“grow weary” · losing heart, not only being tired

“…Aaron and Hur held up his hands, one on one side, and the other on the other side. So his hands were steady until the going down of the sun.”Exodus 17:12 (ESV)

Ask what else they are carrying.

An honest way to continue, change or stop

What do you most enjoy? · What drains you? · Continue, change or stop?

Make “stop” a genuinely acceptable answer.

When someone isn’t working out

Consider whether a different role would fit better.

Do not let a problem run for months without a direct conversation.

“And there arose a sharp disagreement, so that they separated from each other. Barnabas took Mark with him…”Acts 15:39 (ESV)

“Get Mark and bring him with you, for he is very useful to me for ministry.”2 Timothy 4:11 (ESV)

Bringing it together

  1. Prepare, send, and listen — to the work and to the person
  2. Pass on skills in four steps, without skipping the middle
  3. Delegate the outcome, with clear limits and standards
  4. Thank specifically, watch for weariness, and give an honest way to continue, change or stop

“…when each part is working properly.”

Watch it happen: the first two Sundays

He said yes. Approval and checks were completed. Now his training begins.

Step 1 · I do it, you watch
“Today, just watch, and I’ll tell you what I’m thinking.”

Afterwards: “What did you notice?”

Step 2 · We do it together
“You greet. I’ll stay right beside you.”

Watch it happen: stepping back

Step 3 · You do it, I watch

With children, money or confidential information: follow the church’s supervision requirements throughout.

“Two things went really well. Here’s one thing to try next time.”

Step 4 · You do it, I’m available
“The aim is that anyone who comes in on their own is greeted, helped to a seat, and introduced to one person.”

“I’ve seen you remember people’s names and help them feel welcome. Thank you.”

This week’s action

1. Think of the volunteer most at risk of quietly fading.

2. Take them for coffee, with no agenda about tasks.

3. Ask what they enjoy, what drains them, and what else they are carrying.

4. Thank them for one specific thing you have watched them do.

Invite without pressure. They are free to decline, and need not share personal circumstances.

One conversation. Listen more than you speak.

Remember

“To equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ.”Ephesians 4:12

“When each part is working properly, makes the body grow so that it builds itself up in love.”Ephesians 4:16

You are not only getting the work done. You are helping people grow.
