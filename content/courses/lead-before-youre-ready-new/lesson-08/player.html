Lesson 8 · Book chapter 8 · Part 3: Face What Tests You

Criticism, Control and Ongoing Pastoral Needs

How do I respond to the situations one good conversation cannot fix?

Three different situations

1 · Criticism to assess“The member who criticises everything you do.”
2 · Over-centralised responsibility to address“The volunteer who treats one area as personal property.”
3 · Ongoing care needs to support“The person in genuine pain absorbing every hour you have.”

They need different responses, and sometimes they overlap.

Describe what is happening. Do not put a fixed label on the person.

Nehemiah 6 · selected excerpts

Outside opponents of the work

  • Nehemiah rebuilds Jerusalem’s wall, with the king’s permission
  • Sanballat, Tobiah and Geshem: regional figures who opposed the rebuilding from outside
  • The people who criticise you in your church are not Sanballat and Tobiah.

Four tactics

“So we built the wall… for the people had a mind to work.”Nehemiah 4:6 (ESV)

Against hostile distraction: pray · answer briefly · keep building

Different situations call for different responses.

Section 1 · Criticism to assess

  • ✦ The faithful critic is a gift — protect and thank them.
  • ✦ Frequency alone does not tell you whether a concern is valid.
  • ✦ Assess anonymous or relayed concerns on their content.
  • ✦ Refer safeguarding concerns straight away — never delay for more examples.
What is accurate?address the substance first
What needs checking?ask for specific examples

Example: an email that stings

“The welcome is cold, and you never listen to anyone.”

She waits until the next morning.

AccurateThe door was unstaffed twice this month.
Needs checking“You never listen to anyone.”
“Thank you for telling me. You’re right that the door wasn’t covered twice this month, and I’m sorting that out.
Could you give me an example of when you felt you weren’t listened to? I’ll come back to you by the end of next week.”

Address the substance first.

Section 2 · Over-centralised responsibility to address

“…Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first, does not acknowledge our authority.”3 John 9 (ESV)

An extreme case. What you meet is usually a different and much more ordinary problem.

Not every person who holds too much responsibility is seeking control.

Improve the structure, with the right authority

  1. Improve the structure, not fight over territory — and explain changes respectfully
  2. Be clear about authority (Lesson 3)
  3. Involve the appropriate leader early

“As for a person who stirs up division, after warning him once and then twice, have nothing more to do with him.”Titus 3:10 (ESV)

About divisiveness, never mere disagreement — and a decision for the church’s leadership.

Example: the sound desk

With his pastor’s approval: a written guide · a second person trained · keys signed out from the church office

“You know this desk better than anyone. Would you help me train Sam?”

Section 3 · Ongoing care needs to support

“Do you want to be healed?” … “Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool…”John 5:6–7 (ESV)

Never evidence that someone in long-term pain does not want help.

“What support would be useful now, and what has made it hard to access?”

The original meaning

βάρηbarē (plural) · “burdens” · Galatians 6:2
φορτίονphortion (singular) · “load” · Galatians 6:5

restore gently · watch yourself · test your own work (Galatians 6:1–5)

Mutual help, without pretending one person can carry another’s whole life.

A shape, more than one helper, and honest limits

  • ✦ A shape that fits the person’s needs
  • ✦ Do not be their only support
  • ✦ Professional help alongside spiritual support
  • ✦ Your church’s safeguarding arrangements, and the limits of confidentiality

Example: a realistic shape

“Could we meet on the first Tuesday of each month, for an hour?”

“Would it help if one of the pastoral team joined us, or met with you too?”

“I think it would be good to talk to your doctor as well.”

Monthly was right for her. For someone else, it might be more frequent, or a prompt referral.

Do not come down off the wall

“I am doing a great work and I cannot come down.”Nehemiah 6:3 (ESV)

Not permission to ignore complaints or people in distress.

When you face hostile distraction: answer briefly, pray honestly, then get back on the wall.

Reflect, on your own, after the lesson

Five questions are waiting on the lesson page.
Take them at your own pace.

To start you thinking: Describe the situation currently taking most of your attention. Is it criticism to assess, over-centralised responsibility to address, a care need to support, or a combination?

Keep your notes general. No names or identifiable pastoral details.

This week’s action

Choose one appropriate step for the situation you described.

Criticism: name the specific concern, what needs checking, and who should respond. Give a realistic follow-up date.

Over-centralised responsibility: with the right authority, agree a plan for shared oversight of one responsibility or system.

Ongoing care: agree a realistic next step with the person and, where appropriate, your pastoral leader.

Do not delay urgent reporting while waiting to feel calm.

Remember

“I am doing a great work and I cannot come down.”Nehemiah 6:3

“Bear one another’s burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ.”Galatians 6:2

Stay on the wall. Carry burdens together, not alone.

Give me courage to change what needs changing, with the right authority.

Help me to care for those who are hurting, with others beside me, and with honest limits.

Keep me faithful to the work you have given me. In Jesus’ name. Amen.
