The procession of triumph

θριαμβεύσας

thriambeusas · “triumphing over them”COLOSSIANS 2:15

The verb occurs twice in the New Testament.

What looked like Jesus’ public shame was the public defeat of the powers.

Point 1

Not God’s equal

Adversary Accuser Slanderer Deceiver Liar

Each name tells us how he works.

He opposes God’s purposes · accuses God’s people · twists the truth · seeks to destroy

Not God’s opposite

Not two equal forces

God: eternal · all-powerful · everywhere present · all-knowing

The devil: a creature, with a beginning, limits and an appointed end

“Only against him do not stretch out your hand.”Job 1:12

Greater is he who is in you

“…he who is in you is greater than he who is in the world.”1 John 4:4

Point 2

Defeated at the cross

“…he shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel.”Genesis 3:15

A promise kept at the cross

The record set aside

Our guilt was real. For those who belong to Christ, it no longer provides grounds for condemnation.

Confess and make amends — without believing you are beyond his mercy.

Free from fear

“…that through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil,Hebrews 2:14

and deliver all those who through fear of death were subject to lifelong slavery.”Hebrews 2:15

Christ came to free his people from fear.

Already, and not yet

“…prowls around like a roaring lion…”1 Peter 5:8

“The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet.”Romans 16:20

Decisive victory, continuing opposition. The illustration does not explain every detail of Paul’s imagery.

We do not fight towards an uncertain victory. We stand from a victory already secured.

Help me trust you when old fears return.

Keep me watchful but not afraid.
