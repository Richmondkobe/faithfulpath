Lesson 9 · Chapters 14–15 · Part 3: Standing Firm in Everyday Life

Submit, Resist and Worship

What do I do when the battle does not end quickly?

Imagine…

“If I resist the devil, he will flee.” So why am I still struggling?

James 4:6–10

But he gives more grace. Therefore it says, “God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble.”James 4:6 (ESV)

Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you.James 4:7–8 (ESV)

Cleanse your hands, you sinners, and purify your hearts, you double-minded. Be wretched and mourn and weep. Let your laughter be turned to mourning and your joy to gloom.James 4:8–9 (ESV)

Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you.James 4:10 (ESV)

Point 1

Submission comes first, then resistance

“What causes quarrels and what causes fights among you? Is it not this, that your passions are at war within you?”James 4:1 (ESV)

“God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble.”James 4:6 (ESV)

Submitting to God means humbling ourselves before him: turning from pride, admitting our need and coming back to him.

James calls us to mourn: to take our sin seriously and turn back to God. He gives more grace.

Resist

antistēteGreek · James 4:7

“stand against”, refuse to give way

“…that you may be able to withstand in the evil day…”Ephesians 6:13 (ESV)

Answering the enemy’s deception with what God has said.

“Be angry and do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger, and give no opportunity to the devil.”Ephesians 4:26–27 (ESV)

Not one dramatic moment, but standing again and again in the strength God provides.

What resistance is not

The authority belongs to Christ, not to our technique.

“The Lord rebuke you.”Jude 9 (ESV)

Our confidence rests in the Lord.

“He will flee”

“Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.”James 4:7 (ESV)

The promise does not mean every symptom, temptation or hardship will disappear at once, or never return.

“…he departed from him until an opportune time.”Luke 4:13 (ESV)

When it comes back, our earlier resistance did not fail. We resist again, and keep drawing near to God.

Point 2

When the battle does not end quickly

“…a thorn… in the flesh, a messenger of Satan to harass me…”2 Corinthians 12:7 (ESV)

“My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness.”2 Corinthians 12:9 (ESV)

God answered, but not with removal. He answered with grace.

Persistent suffering need not mean that prayer or faith has failed.

We keep praying, for God does heal. Our confidence rests in his grace, whether he removes the difficulty now or gives strength to carry it.

Point 3

Worship as trust

“Then Jehoshaphat was afraid and set his face to seek the LORD, and proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah.”2 Chronicles 20:3 (ESV)

“We do not know what to do, but our eyes are on you.”2 Chronicles 20:12 (ESV)

“The battle is not yours”

“Do not be afraid and do not be dismayed at this great horde, for the battle is not yours but God’s.”2 Chronicles 20:15 (ESV)

“Stand firm, hold your position, and see the salvation of the LORD on your behalf, O Judah and Jerusalem.”2 Chronicles 20:17 (ESV)

Before they saw the outcome, they fell down before the Lord, worshipping him.

Stand firm: the call of this whole course.

The singers went first

“Give thanks to the LORD, for his steadfast love endures forever.”2 Chronicles 20:21 (ESV)

“And when they began to sing and praise, the LORD set an ambush…”2 Chronicles 20:22 (ESV)

The armies that had come against Judah turned on one another (vv. 22–23).

Reading carefully

“You will not need to fight in this battle.”2 Chronicles 20:17 (ESV)

A specific promise for a specific situation. God kept his word.

Lasting principles for God’s people in trouble

  • They turned to God when they were afraid
  • They humbled themselves together
  • They prayed honestly
  • They listened for God’s word
  • They worshipped before they saw the outcome

Worship is not pretending

Lifting our eyes from the size of the problem to the greatness and faithfulness of God.

“Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why are you in turmoil within me? Hope in God; for I shall again praise him, my salvation and my God.”Psalm 42:11 (ESV)

Paul and Silas prayed and sang hymns in prison, before their release (Acts 16:25).

Worship can include lament and tears. It tells the truth about God, even while sorrow remains.

Fasting

“And when you fast…”Matthew 6:16 (ESV)

Not a way of earning God’s favour or forcing his hand.

Always voluntary. Seek advice where your health makes food fasting unwise.

Anyone can set aside media or another distraction and give that time to God.

What matters is the heart: turning from ordinary comforts to seek God more fully.

Two misunderstandings

1 · Technique

“If we are intense enough, loud enough or long enough, God must act.”

God is not moved by technique. He responds with grace, according to his wisdom.

2 · Passivity

“Submitting means accepting whatever happens, including sin and evil.”

Submit to God and resist the devil.

We humble ourselves before God, and we stand firmly against everything that opposes him.

Bringing it together

  • 1 · Submit to God first, then resist the devil
  • 2 · Keep praying, and receive grace while we wait
  • 3 · Worship before we see the outcome

We stand by submitting to God, resisting the devil and worshipping, even when the battle is long.
