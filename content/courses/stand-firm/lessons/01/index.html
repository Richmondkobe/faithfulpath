What Is Spiritual Warfare?

Is the devil behind everything that goes wrong in my life?

About 15 minutesEphesians 6:10–12Part 1 · Understanding the Battle

By the end of this lesson you will be able to: apply the four reflection questions to a difficulty without claiming certainty about its cause.

1Watch and Learn

About 15 minutes: Scripture, its meaning and a practical example.

In this lesson

  • Why Paul’s call to stand begins with the Lord’s strength, not with the enemy (Ephesians 6:10–12)
  • Why the people who hurt us are not our ultimate enemy
  • How the world, the flesh, the devil and ordinary needs differ, and why the devil is real but not behind every problem
  • Why no Christian is meant to stand alone
Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Stand Firm

Welcome to Stand Firm. I’m Pastor Richmond Kobe. For more than twenty years I have served as a pastor, and in every place I have met believers who hear the words “spiritual warfare” often, but have never had them explained clearly. My prayer is that this course will help you recognise spiritual opposition, stand firm in Christ, and practise daily faith without living in fear. We begin with a question many Christians carry quietly.

2 · Imagine a man named Simon

Imagine a man named Simon. He has followed Jesus for eleven years, and for most of that time his faith has felt steady. But this year everything seems to be going wrong at once. His car has broken down twice. His manager has become cold towards him. He and his wife keep arguing about small things. And when he tries to pray, his mind wanders, and a troubling thought comes: God is tired of you. On Sunday a friend at church listens to all of this and says, “Brother, this is spiritual warfare. The enemy is attacking you.” Simon goes home unsure what to think. Part of him feels relieved, because at least there is a name for it. Another part feels frightened. Is the devil behind his car, his manager and his marriage? And what is he supposed to do about it?

3 · Ephesians 6:10–12

Let us open the Bible together. Our Scripture readings in this course are from the English Standard Version. Ephesians chapter 6, verses 10 to 12. “Finally, be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might. Put on the whole armor of God, that you may be able to stand against the schemes of the devil. For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the cosmic powers over this present darkness, against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly places.”

4 · “Finally, be strong in the Lord”

Now notice what Paul does first. Before he names a single enemy, he points us to the Lord. “Be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might.” The battle begins with where our strength comes from, not with the enemy. That order matters for everything else in this course. We look to Christ first, and we look at the enemy only in the light of what Christ has done.

5 · Not written from a comfortable chair

To hear this passage rightly, we need to know where Paul was when he wrote it. He was a prisoner. Three times in this letter he refers to his imprisonment. He was not imagining spiritual battle from a safe distance. He had lived it. His first readers lived in Ephesus, a great city full of magic, idols and fear of unseen powers. The book of Acts tells us that when people there came to faith, many of them brought their books of magic arts and burned them in public. So Paul is not inventing a new fear for these believers. He is telling them the truth about a world they already knew was not empty.

6 · palē: a close struggle

Paul says, “we do not wrestle.” The Greek word is palē. It was used for wrestling as a contest, and it appears only here in the New Testament. It suggests a struggle that is close and personal, not a battle fought far away. That fits what many of us know. Spiritual warfare is often close to home: in our thoughts, our choices, our relationships and our faithfulness.

7 · What spiritual warfare is

So what is spiritual warfare? Many Christians stand where Simon is standing. Some of us have been taught to see a demon behind every problem. Others have quietly learned that the devil is an old-fashioned idea. Neither view comes from the Bible. Here is a simple definition that we will use throughout this course. Spiritual warfare is the Christian’s struggle to remain faithful to Christ amid the pressures of the world, the desires of the flesh and the opposition of the devil. And we stand through God’s strength and the resources he gives us.

8 · Point 1: People are not the enemy

Here is our first main point. People are not the enemy. Paul tells us who our struggle is not against: “flesh and blood.” This is one of the most important lines in the whole passage. The people who hurt us, oppose us or frustrate us are not our ultimate enemy. Simon’s manager is not a demon. His wife is not his enemy. Paul’s words protect us from turning people into targets.

9 · Slower to hate, quicker to pray

That does not mean people are never responsible for what they do. Wrongs should be named, and sometimes they must be confronted or reported. Seeking safety and help can be a faithful response. But a Christian who understands spiritual warfare will be slower to hate, quicker to pray, and more careful to fight the right battle.

10 · Point 2: Three fronts

Our second main point: the enemy is real, but he is not behind every problem. Ephesians six is not the only place where Paul describes what we face. Earlier in the same letter, in chapter 2, he describes our life before Christ. “And you were dead in the trespasses and sins in which you once walked, following the course of this world, following the prince of the power of the air, the spirit that is now at work in the sons of disobedience—among whom we all once lived in the passions of our flesh…” Look closely and you will see three influences in one sentence. Christians through the centuries have summarised them as the world, the flesh and the devil. And these three can overlap.

11 · The world, the flesh and the devil

The world is the pattern of life around us that leaves God out. It shapes us through culture, pressure, values and voices that tell us what matters. The flesh here means our sinful orientation: the fallen desires within us that pull us away from God. It does not mean the body itself is evil; God made it good. James is honest: “each person is tempted when he is lured and enticed by his own desire.” Not every temptation needs an outside explanation. Sometimes the problem is my own heart. And the devil is a real, personal enemy who opposes God and his people. Paul speaks of his “schemes,” his plans and methods. A healthy understanding keeps all three in view. If we only talk about the devil, we may stop taking responsibility for our own sin. If we never talk about him, we may be unprepared for his schemes.

12 · Real, but not everywhere

The Bible does not allow us to dismiss the devil. Jesus himself spoke of him, confronted him in the wilderness and set people free. Peter warns, “Your adversary the devil prowls around like a roaring lion, seeking someone to devour.” But the Bible never encourages us to become fascinated by him, afraid of him, or to blame him for everything. Simon’s worn-out car, his stressed manager and those tired arguments may be the ordinary troubles of a fallen world. They still call for prayer. They may also call for a mechanic, an honest conversation at work and a humble apology at home.

13 · Four questions for reflection

So when something difficult happens, here are four questions that can help you pray honestly. They are prompts for reflection, not a test that identifies the cause of a difficulty. First, my own heart: is there sin in me that I need to confess or turn from? Second, the world around me: am I being shaped by pressures, values or voices that pull me away from God? Third, truth and lies: is there a thought, an accusation, a fear or a temptation pressing on me that contradicts what God says? And fourth, ordinary needs: is there a practical, physical, relational or medical need that should be cared for as well? More than one answer may be true at the same time.

14 · The four questions with Simon

Let us walk through the four questions with Simon. For him, they might lead to an apology after an argument with his wife; to giving less attention to voices that feed his fear; to remembering that he belongs to Christ; and to arranging the car repair. He does not need one explanation for everything. Together, the questions suggest several possible faithful steps.

15 · “God is tired of you”

Look again at the thought that came to Simon when he prayed: God is tired of you. It needs to be tested against the gospel, and the gospel says otherwise. “There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.” Notice this carefully. Simon does not need to establish the precise source of that thought before he responds. Whether it comes from discouragement, from beliefs he learned long ago, or from spiritual opposition, he can bring it to God, answer it with what God has said in Christ, and seek support. You can do the same. You do not need to settle whether something is a spiritual attack before you take a faithful step.

16 · Point 3: You are not meant to stand alone

Our third main point: you are not meant to stand alone. Ephesians six was written to a church, not to one person. The commands are plural. You all put on the armour. You all stand. Standing firm includes asking trusted believers to pray with us, to help us examine our assumptions, and to offer practical help. A mature Christian friend may help Simon see what he cannot see on his own, and may simply help him get his car to the mechanic. No Christian is meant to fight alone.

17 · Stand

Notice the word Paul repeats again and again: stand. “That you may be able to stand.” “Having done all, to stand firm.” “Stand therefore.” Paul does not promise that the evil day will never come. He promises that in Christ we can stand when it does. Some battles end quickly. Others last a long time. Paul himself prayed three times for a painful thorn to be removed, and the Lord answered, “My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness.” So if your difficulty has not gone away, it does not mean you have failed. Standing firm may look like still trusting God on the hundredth difficult morning. That is not defeat. That is faith.

18 · A victorious King

And here is why we can stand. The devil is real, but he is not God’s equal. His defeat has already been secured. “The reason the Son of God appeared was to destroy the works of the devil.” In Lesson 2 we will see how Christ triumphed over the powers at the cross. For now, hold on to this: we do not fight for victory, as though the outcome were uncertain. We stand in the victory Christ has already won.

19 · Bringing it together

Let us bring it together. Spiritual warfare is real, but it is not behind every problem. People are not the enemy. The world, the flesh and the devil are all real, and so are our ordinary needs, and more than one may be at work at once. And we stand together. Christ calls us to stand, not to fear.

20 · This week’s practice

Here is your practice for this week. Choose one difficulty in your life right now. Then work slowly through the four questions in prayer. You do not need to settle whether this is a spiritual attack before taking a faithful step. Let the questions help you pray honestly, and notice what step each one might ask of you: a confession, a change, a truth to hold on to, or a practical need to meet. And if you would like a short daily guide for this week, you will find the seven-day Battle Plan on the lesson page.

21 · Remember

Remember this word of Scripture: “Be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might.” And here is this week’s declaration. It is not a formula. It is a statement of trust. My strength is in the Lord. Christ has already won, and by his grace I will stand.

22 · Prayer

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, thank you that you came to destroy the works of the devil, and that you have triumphed over every power of darkness. Thank you that I do not have to live in fear. Give me wisdom to see my life clearly: to confess my own sin, to resist the pull of the world, to recognise the lies of the enemy, and to care for the ordinary needs in front of me. Where I have treated people as my enemies, forgive me and teach me to love. When the battle is long, help me to keep standing. Make me strong in you and in the strength of your might. Amen. This week, choose one difficulty and pray through the four questions. Then stop here for today, or continue to Lesson 2.
