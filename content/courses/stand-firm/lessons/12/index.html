Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Until he comes

Welcome back to Stand Firm. In Lesson 11 we learned to guard our hearts and to pass faith on. This is our final lesson. Over twelve lessons we have looked at the enemy, the armour of God and what it means to stand in everyday life. Now we look ahead, with one last question. What does standing firm look like for the rest of my life?

2 · Remember Simon

Do you remember Simon, from our first lesson? Everything seemed to be going wrong at once: his car, his job, his marriage and his prayer life. A friend told him it was spiritual warfare, and Simon went home unsure whether to feel relieved or afraid. When he tried to pray, a troubling thought came: God is tired of you. A year has passed, and the battles of life have not stopped. So what does standing firm look like for Simon now, and for the rest of his life? Let us open the Bible.

3 · Matthew 28:18–20

Matthew chapter 28, verses 18 to 20. And Jesus came and said to them, “All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age.”

4 · Point 1: Authority that sends us out

Our first main point: authority that sends us out. After his resurrection, the eleven disciples went to the mountain in Galilee where Jesus had told them to go. Matthew writes, “And when they saw him they worshiped him, but some doubted.” Even then, Jesus came to them. He did not wait for every doubt to be settled. He declared, “All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me.” In Lesson 2 we saw that Jesus is our victorious Captain, who disarmed the powers of darkness through his cross and resurrection. Here the risen Lord stands before his followers with all authority.

5 · “Go therefore”

Notice what follows: “Go therefore.” Christ’s victory is not something to keep to ourselves. Because he has all authority, his people are sent to make disciples. Making disciples includes sharing the gospel, baptising those who come to faith, and teaching them to live by what Jesus commanded. When the risen Jesus appeared to Paul, he described Paul’s mission like this: “to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light and from the power of Satan to God.” So spiritual warfare is not only defensive. Every time the gospel is shared, every time someone turns to Christ, and every time a believer grows in obedience, God’s kingdom advances. And Matthew closes with Jesus’ promise: “I am with you always, to the end of the age.” We do not go alone.

6 · Every believer has a part

Not everyone is called to be a pastor or to serve overseas. But every believer has a part in making disciples. That might mean sharing your faith with a neighbour, teaching children, mentoring a younger believer, welcoming newcomers, praying for those who share the gospel, or showing Christ’s love through practical service. Peter says to be ready to give “a reason for the hope that is in you; yet do it with gentleness and respect.” Age does not disqualify us. The psalmist says of the righteous, “They still bear fruit in old age.” And our usefulness to God is not measured by how much we can do. For those living with illness or limited energy, praying, encouraging others and staying part of the church’s life are real ways of sharing in Christ’s work.

7 · Empowered by the Spirit

And Jesus did not send his disciples out in their own strength. He told them to wait “until you are clothed with power from on high.” At Pentecost the Spirit was poured out, and fearful disciples became bold witnesses. The same Spirit empowers believers today. He gives courage, he gives gifts to every believer “for the common good,” and Paul urges us to keep being “filled with the Spirit.” Mission is not a burden we carry alone. It is the Spirit’s work through ordinary people who depend on him.

8 · Point 2: “They have conquered him”

Our second main point: the certain end of the enemy. The book of Revelation is full of symbolic visions, and Christians understand many of its details in different ways. But some of its central messages are clear. Revelation 12 describes the defeat of “the accuser of our brothers,” and then says: “And they have conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony, for they loved not their lives even unto death.” The word for conquered is enikēsan, from the same root as nikē, victory. God’s people have conquered the enemy, not by their own power, but in Christ.

9 · How they conquered

Notice how they conquer. “By the blood of the Lamb“: the victory rests first on Christ’s sacrifice. As we saw in Lesson 5, the accuser has no final case against those Christ has redeemed. “By the word of their testimony“: their faithful witness to Jesus. It is not a spoken formula, but loyalty to Christ that speaks. And “they loved not their lives even unto death.” Around the world today, many Christians face opposition for their faith. Their faithfulness reminds us that victory in Christ does not always look like escape from suffering. Sometimes it looks like steadfast loyalty to him through it.

10 · The certain end

And the story does not end in a stalemate. Revelation tells us that the devil, who deceived the nations, will be thrown into the lake of fire. Christians differ about the timing and order of the events around Christ’s return. But the outcome is not in doubt. The enemy’s final judgement is certain, and God will make all things new. “He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more, neither shall there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain anymore, for the former things have passed away.” We stand firm now, often in weakness, because we know how the story ends.

11 · Hope without fear

Some believers become anxious about the end times, watching every headline. But Jesus said, “See that you are not alarmed.” And when his disciples asked about the timing of his kingdom, he said, “It is not for you to know times or seasons that the Father has fixed by his own authority. But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be my witnesses.” Jesus turns us from speculating about dates to living as his witnesses. His return is our “blessed hope,” not a source of dread. Because Christ is risen and death will be defeated, even quiet acts of faithfulness matter to him. As Paul writes at the close of his great chapter on the resurrection, “Therefore, my beloved brothers, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that in the Lord your labor is not in vain.”

12 · Point 3: Having done all, stand

Our third main point: having done all, stand. Paul’s words in Ephesians 6, which we heard in our very first lesson, are worth hearing once more: “Therefore take up the whole armor of God, that you may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand firm.” Paul does not promise that the evil day will never come. He assumes that it will. And the goal is not a dramatic victory parade. The goal is to remain faithful to Christ through the evil day, however long the pressure lasts.

13 · What standing firm looks like

Sometimes standing firm looks like resisting a temptation, answering a lie with the truth, or praying through a difficult night. Sometimes it looks like confessing sin, asking for help, or taking a wise practical step. And sometimes it simply means continuing to trust God when relief has not yet come. Paul knew that kind of standing well. He wrote, “We are afflicted in every way, but not crushed; perplexed, but not driven to despair; persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed…” If your struggle continues, that does not, by itself, mean you have failed. You are being invited to stand, not in your own strength, but in the strength of the Lord.

14 · You are not alone

And you are not meant to stand alone. Christ stands with you. He has all authority, and he has promised, “I am with you always.” The Holy Spirit lives in you, helping you in your weakness. And God has given you his people, so that you can stand side by side, carry one another’s burdens and encourage one another until the end. The armour of God was described to a church, and it is best worn together.

15 · The journey we have taken

Look back at the journey we have taken. In Part 1, we saw that spiritual warfare is real, but we do not need to fear it: the enemy is real, but already defeated by Christ. In Part 2, we put on the armour of God: truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation and the word of God, worn through prayer. In Part 3, we brought that armour into everyday life: testing spiritual claims, submitting and resisting, worshipping, and walking in freedom together. And in Part 4, we looked to the long road ahead: guarding our hearts, passing on our faith, and joining Christ’s mission until he comes.

16 · Simon, a year later

Imagine Simon a year later. Not every problem has been solved. His manager is still difficult, and some weeks are still hard. But Simon has learned to respond wisely to ordinary troubles, without needing to identify the source of every difficulty. He and his wife have learned to pray together, and to stop treating each other as the enemy. When the old thought returns, God is tired of you, he now knows how to answer it with the gospel: “There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.” And one Sunday, when a younger man at church tells him that everything is going wrong, Simon listens carefully, and offers to pray with him. He belongs to Christ, and he is not standing alone.

17 · Bringing it together

Let us bring it together. The risen Jesus has all authority, and he sends every believer to share in his mission, with the Spirit’s power. The enemy’s end is certain, so we live in hope, not fear. And until Christ comes, having done all, we stand, even when relief has not yet come. Christ’s victory is certain, so we stand, serve and hope, even before relief comes.

19 · Remember

Remember this word of Scripture: “Therefore take up the whole armor of God, that you may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand firm.” And here is this week’s declaration, a statement of trust: I am strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might. 20 · Prayer and blessing

Let us pray. Lord Jesus, all authority in heaven and on earth belongs to you. Thank you that you have already won the victory, and that you are with us always, to the end of the age. I take up the whole armour of God today, not in my own strength, but in yours. Where I am weary, renew me. Where I am afraid, give me peace. Where the battle has not yet ended, help me to keep standing. Fill me with your Holy Spirit, and make me your witness where I live. Surround me with your people, and keep me faithful until the day you return. In your name, Amen. Receive this blessing from the letter of Jude. “Now to him who is able to keep you from stumbling and to present you blameless before the presence of his glory with great joy, to the only God, our Savior, through Jesus Christ our Lord, be glory, majesty, dominion, and authority, before all time and now and forever. Amen.” Thank you for walking this course with us. If you’d like to make a plan for the weeks ahead, you’ll find My Stand Firm Plan on the lesson page. And having done all, stand firm.

Rafael’s story

Imagine a man named Rafael. He is sixty-four and recently retired after thirty years as a schoolteacher. He has followed Christ since his twenties and served faithfully in his church, but since retiring he has felt unsettled. The news seems full of wars and disasters, and he has started watching online videos claiming that the final days have arrived; the more he watches, the more anxious he becomes. He also feels his useful years are over: “I’m not a pastor or a missionary. I’m just a retired teacher. What difference can I make now?” When he shares his worries with his small group, one member gently suggests that he take a break from the end-times videos and read through Revelation slowly with a good guide instead. Rafael agrees, and finds that the book, read carefully, leaves him with more hope than fear. Around the same time, his church starts a homework club for children from families who have recently arrived in the area. Rafael volunteers, and his years of teaching become a gift once again. Over time, some of the parents begin asking him about his faith, and he shares simply what Christ has done for him. Rafael still prays, “Come, Lord Jesus.” But now he prays it with hope.

Talk about it (for a small group)
  1. How does Jesus redirect the disciples from their question about timing towards their calling as witnesses (Acts 1:6–8)?
  2. What part might God be inviting you to play in his mission in this season of your life?
  3. Who could you share this journey with, so that you are not standing alone?
