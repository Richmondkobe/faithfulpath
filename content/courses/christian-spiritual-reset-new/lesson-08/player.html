Understand the original meaning

Quieted · Hebrew

dāmam

דָּמַם

to become still, or silent

Not “My soul became quiet”, but “I have quieted my soul”

The verb is active: he quieted his own soul, before God.

Not achieved by effort alone

But we can take part.

Explore the background · Psalm 131

A weaned child

A Song of Ascents (Psalms 120–134)

Usually understood as songs for the journey up to Jerusalem

A nursing infant comes urgently, seeking milk.

The weaned child: a more settled kind of nearness

Resting before God, without demanding an immediate answer

Not emptiness, but settled nearness

Explore the background · Matthew 5:21–24

The gift left at the altar

The Sermon on the Mount: anger and contempt matter to God, not only murder (Matthew 5:21–22).

Leave the gift where it is, and go.

“Your brother has something against you”

When we have genuinely wronged someone, worship is no excuse to avoid responsibility.

acknowledge the wrongapologise, without excusesmake it right where we canleave them free to respond

Not a call to chase everyone who has wronged you, or to contact someone who is unsafe

Point 1

Slow Down Before You Arrive

The body sits; the mind keeps running.

Lists appear. Held-off anxieties move into the empty space.

Coming down from speed is not wasted (Lesson 6).

Slowing before you arrive gives the retreat more room.

A seven-psalm week

Ten minutes a day, for the five to seven days before

Day 1 · Psalm 23God’s care
Day 2 · Psalm 62Rest and waiting
Day 3 · Psalm 27One central desire
Day 4 · Psalm 42Spiritual thirst
Day 5 · Psalm 63Longing for God
Day 6 · Psalm 139Honest self-examination
Day 7 · Psalm 131Quiet trust

Fewer days? Choose the psalms that will help you prepare.

No catching up. You need not complete every reading.

The plan is below this lesson, with a box to tick each day.

Each day

  1. Read the psalm twicethe second time more slowly
  2. Sit quietly, up to three minutesreturn gently to one phrase · one minute is fine · eyes open is fine · or walk
  3. Pray one sentence“Lord, I am coming to be with you. Prepare me, and prepare the days.”

The purpose is attention, not endurance.

Calmer or not, you will have practised returning to quiet.

Point 2

Notice Your Resistance

sudden urgency about tasks“this is self-indulgent”“someone else needs me more”a vague dread

Resistance is information, not a command.

What might it be telling me?

ordinary reluctancefear of what may surfacea practical problema concern about safety

Responding wisely

a responsibility→handle it before you go
safety, health or readiness→the four paths (Lesson 5)
something the quiet may surface→write it down; bring the page

Do not try to resolve it beforehand. Simply name it.

Not every distraction or anxious feeling is spiritual opposition.

Notice. Pray honestly. Respond wisely.

Whatever condition you arrive in, that is the God you are coming to.

Point 3

Unfinished Relationships and Known Sin

Is there anyone I have hurt, or am in open conflict with, whom I could reasonably approach before I go?

Not every strained relationship. Not old wounds the retreat itself needs to look at.

a sharp worda debt not acknowledgeda misunderstanding

If it is safe and wise, address it before you leave.

Apologise without demanding forgiveness.

Centre the harm done to them. No pressure on their timetable.

Two cautions

1 A wrong you have genuinely done

You are not required to contact someone who has harmed you, or who is unsafe.

Forgiveness and reconciliation are not the same thing (Session 6).

2 Do not use this to talk yourself out of going.

Too complicated for a week? Bring it to the retreat.

Confession before you go

Sometimes an unconfessed wrong weighs on our conscience.

“I acknowledged my sin to you, and I did not cover my iniquity”Psalm 32:5 · ESV

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”1 John 1:9 · ESV

Confess what you know. Receive the forgiveness promised in Christ.

Session 5: the deeper kind that surfaces slowly in quiet

Forgiveness removes condemnation; it does not erase every consequence.

Harm, abuse, crime or safety: seek qualified guidance. A private retreat is no way around it.

Do not turn this into a search.

Confess what you know. Leave the rest to God.

Confessing again and again? Speak with a pastor or a mental-health professional.

Point 4

Pray, Ask Others to Pray,
and Hand Over Well

Jehoshaphat “was afraid and set his face to seek the Lord.”2 Chronicles 20:3

Not an instruction to take a retreat: an example of deliberately seeking God

Turning, in advance, towards the days you have set aside

The practical thingsarrangements, few interruptions, rest
The deeper thingshonesty, not running, meeting God

Ask someone trustworthy to pray on each retreat day.

“I am taking some days to be with God, and I would value your prayers.”

Your solitude, connected to your community

Handing over well

Handover note

What needs doing, and who is doing it

When I will return; how real emergencies are handled

One person for urgent contact, with clear authority

“What you are doing is not good… You are not able to do it alone.”Exodus 18:17–18 · ESV

Poorly handed over: a low hum of worry

Well handed over: absent in fact, not only in location
