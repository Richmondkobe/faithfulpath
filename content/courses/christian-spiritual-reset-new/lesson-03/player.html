From permission to pattern: a pattern, not a formula

How did Jesus rest, and what does that teach me?

Mark 14:32–36English Standard Version

“And they went to a place called Gethsemane. And he said to his disciples, ‘Sit here while I pray.’ And he took with him Peter and James and John, and began to be greatly distressed and troubled. Gethsemane belongs to Christ’s unique path to the cross.

No retreat of ours compares to it.

It shows us how honestly Jesus prayed.

Understand the original meaning

Greek · a word from Lesson 1

erēmos

ἔρημος

desolate, deserted, solitary: a place away from the crowds

Mark 1:35

“he departed and went out to a desolate place, and there he prayed.”

Matthew 14:13

“he withdrew from there in a boat to a desolate place by himself.”

The word itself does not mean renewal: fewer people, fewer demands.

What mattered was what Jesus did there: he prayed to his Father.

Explore the background · Mark 1:21–35

A Sabbath in Capernaum

The Sabbath ended at sunset; it seems many waited until then to carry their sick.

He went before anyone else could reach him.

Explore the background · Matthew 14 · Luke 6

In a season of loss

John had prepared the way for Jesus and baptised him. Their mothers were relatives (Luke 1:36).

Seeking solitude after loss is not weakness.

Before a decision

Only Luke records the night of prayer before the choosing of the Twelve.

The direction came after the withdrawal, not instead of it.

Point 1

A Pattern, Not a Program

Some were deliberate withdrawals for prayer. The wilderness and Gethsemane were unique moments in his mission.

Not a whole night without sleep: protecting your sleep may be part of receiving God’s care.

No single mood qualified him for solitude.

After demandMark 1:35
In lossMatthew 14:13
Before a decisionLuke 6:12
In testingLuke 4:1–2
Before sufferingMark 14:32

You do not need to wait to feel a particular way.

Read the Scripture · Luke 4:1–2

“And Jesus, full of the Holy Spirit, returned from the Jordan and was led by the Spirit in the wilderness for forty days, being tempted by the devil.”Luke 4:1–2 · ESV

A unique period of fasting, testing and preparation at the start of Jesus’ ministry.

You are not being asked to reproduce it.

Solitude does not always feel peaceful.

Difficulty during a retreat does not necessarily mean you have made a mistake.

Sometimes the quiet shows us what the noise helped us avoid.

Point 2

Time With the Father

Prayer was central to his life and ministry.

thanksgivingintercessionhonest requestssubmission to the Father’s will

“Remove this cup from me… Yet not what I will, but what you will.” (Mark 14:36)

Silence, reflection and rest belong in a Christian retreat, but they are not its centre.

We do not withdraw merely to become calmer. We withdraw to become present to God.

a hillside
a garden
a boat
a quiet shore

No retreat centre needed. Lesson 6 will help you choose your place.

Point 3

When the Quiet Is Interrupted

“When he went ashore he saw a great crowd, and he had compassion on them and healed their sick.” (Matthew 14:14)

“And after he had dismissed the crowds, he went up on the mountain by himself to pray.” (Matthew 14:23)

He did get his time alone. It simply came later.

A genuine need, or a distraction?

1. Protect your retreat. Lesson 9 will show you how.

2. An interruption does not automatically ruin it.

respond to genuine needsreturn to the quietreschedule what you cannot finish

The time you gave to God was not wasted.

Compassion does not require an immediate answer to every notification, request or preference.

A genuine need, or an avoidable distraction? The retreat will teach you the difference.

Point 4

Not Always Alone

Peter, James and John: imperfect companions, yet witnesses to moments they would never forget.

Solitude with God is essential.

Unhealthy isolation from people is not the goal.

Where possible, let at least one trustworthy person know about your retreat.

No one yet? Consider whom you could begin approaching.

Testing what becomes clear: Lesson 12

More than an example

Jesus is not merely the example behind this retreat.

He is the one who invites you: “Come to me.”

The pattern tells you what the days may look like. The person tells you who is waiting in them.

The shape of your retreat follows the pattern we have seen.

Some things may continue as before; others may need to change.

Not back to busyness, but to life with greater attention to God.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you rose while it was still dark and went out to pray.

You withdrew in grief, you prayed through the night, and in the garden you told your Father the truth.

Teach me to come to the Father as you did, honestly and without pretending.

When I am interrupted, give me compassion and wisdom, and bring me back to the quiet.

Thank you that you are not only my example; you are the one who invites me.
