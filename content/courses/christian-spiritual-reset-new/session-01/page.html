Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Day 1, morning

Welcome to the first session of your retreat. This is Day 1, morning.

If you have just arrived, you do not need to begin quickly. Unpack, find where you will sit, put your phone where you agreed to put it, and make a drink. If you are tired, rest first. There is no prize for beginning quickly.

When you are ready, have your Bible and your journal beside you, with the pages you wrote before you came: your check-in from Lesson 4 and your intention from Lesson 7.

We begin with a question many of us carry into a retreat without saying it: do I have to arrive properly?

2 · The son in the far country

Our passage is Luke chapter 15, verses 11 to 24. Jesus said:

"There was a man who had two sons. And the younger of them said to his father, 'Father, give me the share of property that is coming to me.' And he divided his property between them. Not many days later, the younger son gathered all he had and took a journey into a far country, and there he squandered his property in reckless living. And when he had spent everything, a severe famine arose in that country, and he began to be in need. So he went and hired himself out to one of the citizens of that country, who sent him into his fields to feed pigs. And he was longing to be fed with the pods that the pigs ate, and no one gave him anything. But when he came to himself, he said, 'How many of my father's hired servants have more than enough bread, but I perish here with hunger! I will arise and go to my father, and I will say to him, "Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Treat me as one of your hired servants."'"

3 · While he was still a long way off

"And he arose and came to his father. But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him. And the son said to him, 'Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son.' But the father said to his servants, 'Bring quickly the best robe, and put it on him, and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet. And bring the fattened calf and kill it, and let us eat and celebrate. For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found.' And they began to celebrate."

Notice verse 20. Before the son has said a word, the father has seen him, felt compassion, and run.

4 · Why Jesus told the story

Jesus told this parable in response to people who complained that he welcomed sinners and ate with them. Luke chapter 15 begins:

"Now the tax collectors and sinners were all drawing near to hear him. And the Pharisees and the scribes grumbled, saying, 'This man receives sinners and eats with them.'"

So Jesus told three stories about something lost and found: a sheep, a coin, and a son. The third ends with an elder brother who resents the welcome. We will not deal with him today. But it is worth knowing that the running father is not only private comfort. He is Jesus' picture of God's joy in receiving the lost, told to people who thought such joy was a scandal.

5 · Felt compassion = splanchnizomai

Our Greek word is the one translated "felt compassion" in verse 20: splanchnizomai. It means to be moved with compassion.

Luke uses the same word in two other stories. When Jesus met a widow at Nain, on the way to bury her only son, "he had compassion on her." And when the Samaritan saw the wounded man by the road, "he had compassion."

These are different kinds of story: something Jesus did, and two of the stories he told. What they share is compassion that moves someone to act. Jesus touches the bier, and the bearers stand still. The Samaritan goes to the wounded man. And the father runs.

6 · The speech prepared

Most people arrive at a retreat the way the younger son came home: with a speech prepared.

In the far country he formed it: "Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Treat me as one of your hired servants." Parts of the speech are deeply honest. He has sinned, and he knows it. But it also contains a plan for managing his return. He will come back not as a son who must be received, but as a worker who can earn a place.

Now look at what happens. When the son speaks in the ESV's main text, he confesses his sin and his unworthiness, but the line about becoming a hired servant is absent. Some manuscripts include that line. Either way, the father's welcome begins before the son speaks, and the father's response is restoration rather than negotiation: the robe, the ring, the shoes and the feast. The father does not receive him because he can be useful. He welcomes him as his son.

7 · Come as you are

I begin the retreat here because many people come to God the way the son came home: with a plan for how to be acceptable. They intend to be very disciplined, or very repentant, or very focused, so that by the end of the retreat they will have earned the right to be received.

The passage says something simpler. The father's compassion begins before the son can explain himself. The son has already turned towards home, and he comes honestly, acknowledging his sin. He does not purchase his father's welcome through the quality of his speech. The initiative belongs to grace.

That is what "come as you are" means. You do not come as you are because your condition is unimportant. You come as you are because change begins with being received truthfully, rather than presenting a repaired version of yourself. Jesus' invitation in Matthew 11 is to "all who labor and are heavy laden." That is a description, not a qualification. The tired, distracted, doubtful or numb version of you is the one who is invited.

And if you already know of sin that needs confessing, you do not need to postpone it. Name it honestly. But the quality of your remorse does not purchase the Father's welcome. Session 5 will give unhurried space for deeper confession.

8 · One question

So here is one question to carry into the practice. What speech have you prepared? In other words, what have you been trying to prove to God? What have you been planning to achieve or perform during these days, so that the retreat will count?

You do not need to answer it now. You will find it on the page.

9 · Put the screen aside

This is where the teaching stops. Pause the player now, and put the screen aside if you can.

On the page you will find the session's practice: a short check-in, a guided prayer, your intention read to God, ten minutes of silence, a few questions for your journal, and a simple exercise of setting things down. Take your time.

You are not trying to feel anything. You are letting yourself be seen. "While he was still a long way off, his father saw him."

The practice

Now put the screen aside

Allow approximately one hour, or longer if needed. This is not a timed exercise. Take breaks and move at your own pace.

Write in your journal or on the Session 1 workbook pages. Nothing you write is collected.

1Check-in

Answer briefly. This is a check-in, not a test.

  1. How is your body right now? Tired, restless, tense, heavy, numb, calm? Name it without fixing it.
  2. How is your mind? Racing, foggy, anxious, blank, quietly sad?
  3. What speech have you prepared? In other words, what have you been planning to prove, achieve, or perform during these days so that the retreat will count?
  4. Look back at your Lesson 4 check-in. Does it still describe you today? What has changed since you wrote it, if anything?

2Guided prayer

Guided prayer: Come As You Are · about 3 minutes
Words of the recording

Find a position in which you can remain for a few minutes. You may close your eyes, or keep them open and rest them on something still.

Take one slow breath. If noticing your breathing feels uncomfortable, notice your feet on the floor or the chair supporting you instead.

Let us pray. Father, I am here. If the word “Father” is difficult because of your history, you may simply pray, “Lord, I am here.”

That is the most honest thing I can say. I am here.

I have come tired. I have come with explanations. I have come with fears and with plans for proving myself to you. I set those plans down now.

I cannot earn the welcome you give by grace. I never could. You saw me before I began this journey. While I was still a long way off, you saw me.

Help me receive your mercy before I try to explain or improve myself. Let me be honest with you during these days. Not impressive. Honest.

I place the outcome of this retreat in your hands.

If the picture of an embrace feels difficult, you do not have to use it. Pray instead: “Help me know that I am welcomed in your presence.”

Now take the intention you wrote before coming. Read it to God exactly as you wrote it, including what you had not said and the line about releasing the outcome. If you need more time than the silence that follows, pause this recording. Continue when you are ready.

Your intention does not need to be improved. It only needs to be honest. Father, I am here. Or simply: Lord, I am here. Amen.

Remain where you are and move gently into the silence. Your anchor phrase is: “While he was still a long way off, his father saw him.” Or, if you prefer something shorter: “I am here.”

3Your intention

The recording leads you through this step. If you read the prayer instead, do it now: take the intention you wrote in Lesson 7 and read it to God, exactly as you wrote it, including the part you had not said and the line about releasing the outcome. It does not need to be improved.

4Silence

Sit, stand, or walk slowly. When your attention drifts, return to the phrase. You are not trying to feel anything. You are letting yourself be seen.

5Journal

  1. What did you notice during the silence? Thoughts, feelings, physical sensations, nothing in particular. Write it down without interpreting it.
  2. The father ran. What is your honest reaction to the idea that God is not waiting for you to arrive properly, but is already moving toward you in compassion? Relief, suspicion, disbelief, indifference? Write the true answer.
  3. The son planned to be a servant rather than a son. Where in your life, or your faith, have you been trying to be useful instead of being received?
  4. Finish this sentence, and do not think about it too long: “The thing I most need to tell God honestly at the start of this retreat is…”

6Setting things down

  • Bring into the retreat. Some of what you carried here belongs here: grief, anger, spiritual questions, shame, relationships that need prayer, and your intention. Do not seal these away. They are what the next sessions are for.
  • Set aside until the end. Write the ordinary demands on index cards, one item per line: routine work tasks, non-urgent emails, shopping and errands, decisions that do not need to be made this week, problems already delegated.
  • Read the list once. Put the cards in an envelope, seal it, and write on the front the words for your plan. Put it out of sight. You will reopen it in Session 10.
  • You are not throwing anything away. You are giving yourself permission to put the ordinary demands down for these days.

7Closing declaration

Read only what you can say honestly. If a line feels beyond you, change “I” to “I want to believe,” “I am willing to consider,” or “Help me receive.”

I have come as I am.

I am seen from a long way off, with compassion.

I do not have to earn these days.

During this retreat, I will practise laying down performance, and I will begin learning a different way to live when I return.

On a shorter plan

One-day retreat. Watch the teaching; do the check-in and guided prayer; silence of ten minutes or less; journal questions 1 and 4; the setting-down exercise in full, with the envelope marked “Not until the end of today”. About 45 minutes.

Three-hour reset. Read Luke 15:11–24 and pray the guided prayer, then do the setting-down exercise with an envelope marked “Not for these three hours”. Skip the teaching and the journal. About 25 minutes.

The father saw his son while he was still far away and moved towards him with compassion. You do not need to prepare a perfect speech before coming honestly to God.

For pastors and leaders

When you write the ordinary demands on the cards, include routine sermon preparation, meetings, administrative responsibilities and pastoral matters that can safely wait. Urgent pastoral needs or safeguarding concerns must be handled appropriately rather than placed in the envelope.

A short note

The envelope is for ordinary demands only. A safety concern, a medical need, a crisis, thoughts of harming yourself, or an urgent responsibility are not placed in the envelope to wait; handle them now. Finding Help Where You Live

If a session brings up more than you can hold, stop the session. The sessions are offered, not assigned.

After this session

Take a substantial break, ideally an hour or more. Follow your planned meal and medication schedule, rest if needed, and move gently if that is accessible and safe. Session 2 is best begun after real time away from the page.
