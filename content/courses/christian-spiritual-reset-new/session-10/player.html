Point 1

Go, Return on Your Way

A retreat intends to return (Lesson 2).

Re-entry is where insight meets ordinary life.

Not resolutions: rhythm

“Come away by yourselves to a desolate place and rest a while.”Mark 6:31 · ESV

Engagement and withdrawal: a pattern of life, not a timetable

A small, repeatable pattern for ordinary weeks

Point 3

Expect a Dip

Flat · irritable · discouraged

Not necessarily a sign that the retreat was unhelpful: it may take time to adjust.

Do not judge the retreat by your mood in the first week.

Severe distress, worsening depression, inability to function, or safety concerns: seek help.

Share selectively and safely

Some of it still needs testing (Session 7).

Support personwhat would help them support you
A safe spouseany practical rhythm that affects shared life
Qualified counselsignificant impressions or decisions; not secrecy as spirituality

Not public announcement: embodied obedience and shared work

Point 4

Rejoice That Your Names Are Written

Not the quality of a retreat experience

God’s saving grace, and the belonging Christ gives

Service grows from grace; it does not purchase it.

Dry days do not diminish that belonging.

Rich days are not what to build on.

A careful word

Cards that need help now

Immediate safety · medical care · suicidal thoughts · abuse · safeguarding · a genuine crisis

Not the 30-day plan: seek help promptly.

Private cards

Sealed or securely stored.

If your belongings may be searched or monitored, carry nothing that could put you at risk.

One question to carry into the practice

What am I afraid will happen when I get home?

And what one thing do I most want to be true in a month’s time?
