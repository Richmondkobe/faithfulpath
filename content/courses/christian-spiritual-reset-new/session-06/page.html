Transcript and Scripture references

1 · An optional session

This session is optional, and it is not in the standard three-day plan. It belongs in the at-home retreat, where it has its own day; in the 30-day plan after you return, in Lesson 16; or in a supported setting, with a pastor, counsellor, or trauma-informed professional. So before anything else, decide honestly whether now is the time.

Consider going on if you are rested and settled, the harm is no longer active, and you want to explore what forgiveness might mean without forcing a final outcome. Defer for any reason that makes today feel unsuitable. You do not need to prove that your reason is valid.

If the harm you carry is abuse, Lesson 5’s guidance applies throughout. You are not required to forgive on a schedule, and you are never required to contact or return to someone who harmed you. Our question is this: what does forgiveness ask of me, and what does it not ask?

2 · "As many as seven times?"

Our central passage is Matthew 18, verses 21 to 35. In the book you are asked to read it twice, and the second time to notice both the mercy and the warning. Listen for both now.

"Then Peter came up and said to him, “Lord, how often will my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? As many as seven times?” Jesus said to him, “I do not say to you seven times, but seventy-seven times. Therefore the kingdom of heaven may be compared to a king who wished to settle accounts with his servants. When he began to settle, one was brought to him who owed him ten thousand talents. And since he could not pay, his master ordered him to be sold, with his wife and children and all that he had, and payment to be made. So the servant fell on his knees, imploring him, ‘Have patience with me, and I will pay you everything.’ And out of pity for him, the master of that servant released him and forgave him the debt."

3 · "From your heart"

"But when that same servant went out, he found one of his fellow servants who owed him a hundred denarii, and seizing him, he began to choke him, saying, ‘Pay what you owe.’ So his fellow servant fell down and pleaded with him, ‘Have patience with me, and I will pay you.’ He refused and went and put him in prison until he should pay the debt. When his fellow servants saw what had taken place, they were greatly distressed, and they went and reported to their master all that had taken place. Then his master summoned him and said to him, ‘You wicked servant! I forgave you all that debt because you pleaded with me. And should not you have had mercy on your fellow servant, as I had mercy on you?’ And in anger his master delivered him to the jailers, until he should pay all his debt. So also my heavenly Father will do to every one of you, if you do not forgive your brother from your heart."

4 · An impossible debt

Peter suggested seven, which he apparently regarded as a generous number. Jesus’ answer refuses to turn forgiveness into a tally. A talent was commonly estimated at many years of a labourer’s income, so ten thousand of them is an intentionally impossible debt. A hundred denarii, roughly a hundred days’ wages, was still a substantial sum, but tiny beside the first.

Just before the parable, Matthew 18, verses 15 to 17, describes a process for dealing with a brother who has sinned against you: go to him, then take witnesses, then involve the community. Forgiveness in Matthew 18 sits inside truth-telling and accountability; it is not pretending nothing happened. But these steps are not a procedure for abuse, criminal conduct, coercive control, or unequal power. There, do not begin with private confrontation. Seek specialist, safeguarding, legal, or law-enforcement guidance.

5 · Five Movements, Not One

Much of the confusion around forgiveness comes from asking one word to do five different jobs. So this session separates them. Truth: naming the wrong without minimising it. Safety: ending exposure to continuing harm. Justice: reporting, restitution, consequences, and protection. Release: relinquishing personal vengeance, and the compulsion to collect. And reconciliation, which is possible only with repentance, safety, and rebuilt trust.

You can begin telling the truth, protecting yourself, seeking justice, and relinquishing personal vengeance without the other person’s cooperation. Reconciliation requires more than one person.

6 · Mercy and Warning

The parable contains both extravagant mercy and a severe warning, and both need to be held. The warning should never be used to coerce traumatised people into declaring forgiveness on demand. Yet Jesus does confront the contradiction of receiving mercy while cultivating a settled refusal to extend mercy to others.

We do not purchase God’s grace by performing forgiveness correctly. At the same time, Jesus treats a merciless heart as spiritually serious. Colossians 3 puts it this way:

"As the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive."

God’s mercy is not a debt you repay by performing forgiveness. Yet the mercy we receive calls us to become merciful people. This does not mean forcing reconciliation, abandoning justice, or pretending that a wounded heart has healed.

7 · Christians differ

Christians have differed over how repentance relates to forgiveness between people. You can, on your own, relinquish personal vengeance, pray for freedom from hatred, set boundaries, and entrust judgement to God. Some Christians call that forgiveness; others keep the word forgiveness for a relational response to repentance. That is why this course uses the word release for the first, and reconciliation for the second.

But on the common ground there is no disagreement. Scripture consistently forbids personal vengeance, and calls believers toward mercy.

8 · What Release Is Not (1)

Now let me say plainly what release is not, because many of the reasons people cannot forgive are really reasons they cannot do something else, wrongly called forgiveness.

It is not saying the harm did not matter. Truth comes first. You cannot release a debt that was never owed, and you are invited to trust that God sees the wrong truthfully, and does not require you to deny its seriousness.

It is not forgetting. Joseph eventually rejected vengeance, reassured his brothers, and provided for them, and he remembered every detail:

"You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good."

Remembering is not the opposite of releasing. And it is not reconciliation. Genesis 42 to 44 shows Joseph testing his brothers at length, with truth coming to light and evidence of change, before the relationship was openly restored. The story resists the idea that mercy requires immediate trust. Joseph’s story illustrates that restored trust was not immediate; it is not an instruction to test an abusive person yourself.

9 · What Release Is Not (2)

It is not the removal of safety, boundaries, or justice. You can release personal vengeance and still not let the person near your children, still report what they did, still leave, still refuse to work with them again. Romans 12 forbids personal vengeance:

"Never avenge yourselves, but leave it to the wrath of God."

It does not remove public justice. In the next chapter, Paul describes governing authority as responsible for responding to wrongdoing. Leaving vengeance to God does not stop lawful authorities, churches, employers, or safeguarding bodies from doing their work.

It is not reducible to a feeling, and it is not always completed by one decision. Feelings do not have to change on command, and a memory can hurt after a genuine decision against vengeance. That does not prove that release failed. And it is not required to be spoken to the person. In many cases it should not be. Release is between you and God.

10 · Receiving God’s Verdict

A word about forgiving yourself. Scripture does not quite use the phrase, and the difference matters. You are not the judge in your own case. What you can do is receive the verdict God has given:

"There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus."

Even after receiving forgiveness, a person may still feel guilt or shame. That does not mean they are rejecting God. It helps to separate four things. Healthy conviction identifies wrongdoing and moves toward confession and repair. Shame says the whole person is beyond grace. Consequences address what must still be repaired or protected. Self-punishment tries to pay for guilt through suffering. So forgiving yourself can mean ceasing deliberate self-punishment, and undertaking repair without making suffering into payment.

And for people who have died, or are gone, or will never acknowledge what they did: release does not require them. It happens before God, whether or not the other person is in the room.

11 · A careful word

A careful word before the practice. If the person who harmed you may still put others at risk, forgiveness must never replace safeguarding and reporting. And if this session brings the person too close, into fear, panic, or reliving rather than remembering, stop, open your eyes, and use the orientation exercise from Lesson 5. That is not failure. It means this session needs support, and it can wait.

12 · One question

So here is one question to carry into the practice. What safety measure, boundary, report, or consequence will remain in place, whatever you release? If it is safe and helpful, write it down, or simply name it before God. Naming it may make release possible.

13 · Put the screen aside

This is where the teaching stops. Pause the player now, and put the screen aside if you can.

On the page you will find the session’s practice: a gentle check-in, a guided prayer, ten minutes of silence, which is deliberately short, a few questions for your journal, and one simple prayer: "God, I place this wound in your care and ask for help with the next faithful step." That is not a lesser prayer. It is a complete one. A further step is there, only for those who are ready and safe.

God is not waiting for you to forgive before he cares for the wound. "Into your hands I place this wound."

The practice

Now put the screen aside

Allow approximately an hour to an hour and a half. This is not a timed exercise. Take breaks, move at your own pace, and stop whenever you need to.

Write in your journal or on the Session 6 workbook pages, or pray without writing. Nothing you write is collected, and no name or detail is ever asked for.

1Check-in

Gently, in your journal.

  1. Is there a person whose actions still carry unresolved grief, anger, fear, or a desire for justice in you? Name them, or use an initial, or write nothing identifying.
  2. Which of those responses needs grief, which needs protection, which needs accountability, and which may be moving toward release? Use the five movements.
  3. Which parts of what you want depend on the other person? Which faithful steps remain available to you even if they never respond as you hope?
  4. Is there something you have been punishing yourself for that God has already forgiven, or that you have never brought to him?

If any of this brings up more than you can hold, stop, and defer the session. That is a legitimate decision.

4Journal

  1. What happened in the silence? Write it without interpreting it.
  2. Which of the “release is not” statements did you most need to hear? Which have you been confusing with forgiveness, and how has that confusion kept you from it?
  3. What safety measure, boundary, report, or consequence will remain in place whatever you release? Write it down if it is safe and helpful, or simply reflect on it. Naming it may make release possible.
  4. If you have been punishing yourself, which of the four (conviction, shame, consequence, self-punishment) is actually at work? What would it mean to accept Romans 8:1 as the verdict?

5The default prayer

Write only this, or pray it without writing, and let it be the whole exercise:

“God, I place this wound in your care and ask for help with the next faithful step.”

That is not a lesser prayer. It is a complete one, and for many readers it is the only honest one available. The next faithful step may be a counsellor, a safety plan, a report, a boundary, or simply rest. God is not waiting for you to forgive before he cares for the wound.

Show the optional further stage, for those who are ready and safe

Writing, sealing and keeping the card are all optional. You may simply pray these words and finish there. Completing this exercise does not depend on performing any symbolic action.

Take an index card and write, in your own words or in this shape:

Today, as far as I am able, I release personal vengeance and place justice in God’s hands.

This does not surrender safety, truth, restitution, reporting, or lawful accountability.

I do not promise what I cannot yet give.

If the pain returns, I will not assume this prayer failed; I will ask whether I need lament, support, protection, treatment, or renewed trust.

I ask for help with the next faithful step.

If you write it, use an initial or no details. Read it to God. Do not send it, do not read it to the person, and do not write anything that would be unsafe for someone else to find. If you choose to keep the card, put it in an envelope, seal it, and write on the front Placed in God’s care and today’s date. Nothing is destroyed.

If Session 5 left you with something to release in yourself, a second card:

God has forgiven ______.

I have been responding through ______.

Help me distinguish faithful accountability from self-punishment.

What repair is mine, I will pursue.

What suffering I am using as payment, help me release.

If you keep it, seal it in the same envelope.

6Closing declaration

Read only what you can say honestly. Adjust the words if you need to.

I am not required to deny the wrong or abandon justice.

I release, or ask for help releasing, personal vengeance.

I will preserve the boundaries, truth, reporting, and accountability that remain necessary.

I place what I cannot control into God’s care.

God’s mercy does not require me to remain in danger.

On your plan

Three-day retreat. Session 6 is not part of your plan. If forgiveness is the thing you most need to address, it has a place in week 4 of the 30-day plan (Lesson 16), only if the conditions in the card above are met, or in a supported setting.

One-day retreat and three-hour reset. Not part of your plan: forgiveness should not be compressed into a single day. It remains available in the 30-day plan (Lesson 16), and your retreat is complete without it.

At-home retreat. Not in the core version (eight days). In the extended version (twelve days), Session 6 is Day 8, after a second rest day on Day 7, and only if the conditions in the card above are met. Use the default short prayer unless you are ready for more. If the day is not right, skip it and continue; it remains available later.

Retreat for couples. Not part of your plan. If either of you does it later, do it on your own. The release is private: no one is asked about it, and neither of you reads the other’s cards.

Pastors and leaders. Not part of your plan; it remains available in the 30-day plan. If what you carry involves misconduct by you or by someone in ministry, forgiveness and fellowship are not the same as return to office: seek qualified guidance about accountability and reporting, and do not use the retreat to manage it privately.

After this session

Whatever you did or did not release today, take a break before anything else, and be kind to yourself in it. If you deferred the session, record the next form of support you want, not a deadline for forgiveness: a conversation with a counsellor, a strengthened boundary, a report, further grieving, or simply revisiting the question when you feel safer. If it would help and is safe, tell your support person that you deferred. You do not need to identify the person or disclose the details.

If this session brought the person who harmed you too close, or reopened something that feels dangerous, contact your support person and, if the harm is ongoing, a domestic-abuse service or the appropriate professional. If the person has access to children, vulnerable adults, ministry members, patients, students, employees, or others who may be at risk, forgiveness must never replace safeguarding and reporting; seek the appropriate specialist or authority. Forgiveness is never a reason to stay in harm’s way, and no part of this retreat asks you to.
