1 · What does the first month home look like?

Welcome back. This is the last lesson of the course. It is for Day 30, or for whenever you reach the end of your first month home. You do not need to watch it early to know what to do. The plan for the month has been on the page Your First 30 Days Home since your first morning back, and you have been following it week by week, as far as the weeks allowed.

The four lessons before this one each covered a piece of the return: testing, action, rhythm, and help. This lesson brings them together on one calendar, so that you can see the shape of the month you have just lived, and then it guides your Day 30 review. So it asks: what does the first month home look like?

2 · One thing that does not wait

Before the calendar, one thing that does not wait for it. If any warning sign from Lesson 4 or Lesson 5 applies to you, if you are in danger, being harmed, unable to function, in severe distress, or dependent on a substance, go to Lesson 15 now, on whatever day it is, and act on its guidance. If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services now.

Safety and professional help come before every week of this plan.

3 · He who began a good work

Our passage is short. The Scripture readings in this course are from the English Standard Version. Philippians chapter 1, verse 6:

"And I am sure of this, that he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ."

And two lines you heard in Session 10, from the end of Elijah’s story. God said to him: "Go, return on your way." And of Elisha, the young man he called: "Then he arose and went after Elijah and assisted him."

4 · Not Day 30

Now the background. Paul wrote this letter while imprisoned, to the church in Philippi, a church he loved. He thanks God for their partnership in the gospel, and he is sure of one thing: the one who began a good work in them will complete it. Notice who does the work. God began it, and God will bring it to completion.

And notice when. "The day of Jesus Christ" is the day of Christ’s return. Paul, who had more reason than most to expect rapid transformation, did not set a thirty-day limit on it. The day of completion is not Day 30. Day 30 is a review.

5 · Elijah, sent on with a companion

Our portrait is Elijah, the prophet you met under the broom tree at the very start of this course. The book closes by tracing what God did for him. God fed him before he sent him. He let him sleep. He asked him what he was doing there, and listened to the answer, twice. He reoriented him, gave him work that was his to do, and gave him a companion and a successor.

And he did not tell him to try harder. That has been the pattern of this whole course, and it is still the pattern for the month you have just lived.

6 · The short answer

So, what does the first month home look like? Here is the short answer. A month of imperfect repetition: a small daily minimum, four light weeks, and a review on Day 30 that is not a deadline.

That gives us four points: what the month is for; the daily minimum; landing, doing, shaping, settling; and Day 30 is a review, not a deadline.

7 · Point 1: What the Month Is For

The first point: what the month is for. Three things. First, to let the rhythm from Session 10 become more familiar through a month of imperfect repetition. Thirty days will not necessarily make it automatic. They will show whether it is small and realistic enough to continue.

Second, to work through your cards at a pace that does not recreate the burnout. Third, to give the optional sessions and the testing the time they need, so that what was begun on retreat is finished in ordinary life rather than abandoned there. It is not for becoming a different person in thirty days.

8 · Point 2: The Daily Minimum

The second point: the daily minimum. Every day of the month, the rhythm from your Session 10 card. That is all that is required daily. If your rhythm is weekly rather than daily, a protected period of ceasing or a weekly hour, then the daily minimum is smaller still: a psalm and a few minutes of quiet, the practice from Lesson 8, on the days you can. When you miss, resume the next day, without compensation or guilt.

And a psalm for each week, if you want one to return to: Psalm 23 in the first week, Psalm 62 in the second, Psalm 27 in the third, and Psalm 131 in the fourth. They are the psalms from Lesson 8. Reading them again at home will show you something about the distance travelled.

9 · Point 3: Landing and Doing

The third point: landing, doing, shaping, settling. These are the four weeks you have been following on Your First 30 Days Home. Here they are again, so that you can look back over them. Week 1 is landing: the soft week. Expect the dip, and do little. Keep the rhythm, imperfectly. Tell your support person you are home: one conversation, not a report. If capacity allows, watch Lesson 13 on Day 2 or 3, and complete one Act card or one Seek-counsel step. Do not pronounce a final verdict on the retreat, and do not redesign your life this week.

Week 2 is doing: the week the cards leave the pile. Hold one Share conversation. Have the Eli conversation, if you are testing anything, and bring the worksheet. If you left Session 8 for this month, this is its week. And at the two-week point, ask whether the rhythm is livable. If not, shrink it. Do not abandon it.

10 · Shaping and Settling

Week 3 is shaping: the week the rule is written. Write your rule of life from Lesson 14, and share it, or the parts you want accountability for, with your Eli. If capacity allows, take one structural request to whoever can change something. If you left Session 9, this is its week. And look at Lesson 15 again, whether or not you first thought it applied. Sometimes a need becomes clearer after several weeks at home.

Week 4 is settling. Session 6, on forgiveness, only if the conditions at the top of it are met: you are rested and settled, the harm is not active, and you want to explore what release might mean without forcing an outcome. If you are in doubt, use the short prayer and leave the rest. And if you are testing a major direction, return to the worksheet from Lesson 12. If the testing supports a small, responsible, reversible step, consider taking it. If it still needs more time, advice, or information, keep testing beyond Day 30.

11 · Point 4: Day 30 Is a Review, Not a Deadline

The fourth point: Day 30 is a review, not a deadline. No decision has to be made by Day 30, and nothing becomes overdue if you reach it late. Sit down for an hour, with your journal, your cards, your intention from Lesson 7, and your answers from Lesson 1. Not to grade the month, but to see it.

And begin with grace, before anything is evaluated. Where did you notice help, kindness, provision, truth, rest, or God’s presence during the month? What happened that you did not produce through effort?

12 · Looking back

Then read your intention again, all four parts. Session 10 asked what had shifted after three days. Ask again after thirty: what has become clearer, and what still needs attention? Read your answers from Lesson 1 beside where you are now: when you last felt close to God, what you kept doing while feeling nothing, your own "It is enough". Do not force a conclusion. Notice.

Look at your rhythm. How often did you keep it? What helped it happen, and what repeatedly prevented it? Use the answers to keep, shrink, move, or replace it. And look at your cards. Which became actions, conversations, help, or release? Of those remaining, which belong in the testing pile, which need a different kind of help, and which can simply be let go?

13 · What remains, and what comes next

Then the unresolved. What remains open without needing to be forced? Name it, and leave it named. If Lesson 15 applied to you, did you act? If not, what is the one step this week? And book your next pause, which is your step in a moment.

At the end, write one paragraph, dated: what the month has been. Then close the journal. The month is finished. Below this lesson, My Day 30 Review is a shorter version, in six areas. All eight questions, and the dated paragraph, are on the printable Day 30 Review worksheet. Answer what you can. A partial review is still a review.

14 · If you missed most of it

Some of you will reach Day 30 having done a fraction of this. Illness, a family crisis, a work emergency, or simply the dip lasting longer than expected will have taken the month. If that is you, two things. First, the plan does not expire, and it does not need restarting from the beginning. Look at the four weeks and find the next unfinished step that still matters. Begin there. If you need the gentleness of the landing week again, take it. The cards keep. The sessions keep. The intention keeps.

Second, ask whether the part you did was the important part. Many who think they missed the plan kept the rhythm on some days, made one appointment, had one conversation. Those are not a fraction of the plan. They are the plan, in the form your month allowed. Doing the word is a habit built by resuming, not by never failing. Resume.

15 · Your step

Here is your step. Choose one realistic next pause, and put it in your calendar now. The book suggests a three-hour reset next month, a day in three months, or a retreat within the year. These are suggestions, not obligations; one pause you will keep is better than three you will not. A rhythm needs a next time, and the calendar is where rhythms survive. If you wrote a purpose sentence in Session 9, read it too. Does it still fit? Rewrite it if the season has changed.

Then, if you wish, complete My Day 30 Review, below this lesson, on Day 30 or whenever you are ready. You can save part of it and come back. It is private, it is not scored, it does not measure your spiritual health, and it never becomes overdue.

16 · A closing word

You began this course tired in a place that sleep does not reach. You took days you probably felt you could not afford, and you spent them being honest with God about what you were carrying, and letting him carry some of it. You came home, perhaps to many of the same demands, and you have begun learning to carry what is yours differently.

So, as Elijah was sent: go on your way, and go with others. Work when it is yours to work. Stop when it is time to stop. The world is held by God, not by you.

17 · Remember

Let us bring it together. The month is for imperfect repetition, not for becoming a different person in thirty days. The daily minimum is your rhythm, and when you miss it, you resume without guilt. The four weeks are light: landing, doing, shaping, settling. And Day 30 is a review, not a deadline: see the month, and book the next pause. He who began a good work in you will bring it to completion.

18 · Prayer and blessing

Let us pray. Faithful God, you began this good work in me, and you will bring it to completion. Thank you for the grace I did not earn this month. Teach me to work when it is mine to work, to stop when it is time to stop, and to go on my way with others. Amen.

The Lord bless you and keep you.
