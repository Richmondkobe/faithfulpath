Explore the background · Mark 10

A man by the road

On the way to Jerusalem, after the third prediction of what would happen there (Mark 10:32–34)

“Son of David”: a title of hope for the promised King

The crowd told him to be quiet. He shouted louder.

“What do you want me to do for you?”

His need was visible; Jesus still invited his request.

A clear and personal request

Explore the background · Mark 10:35–37

The same question, twice

James and John“seats of honour… in your glory”
Bartimaeus“Rabbi, let me recover my sight.”

Their answer revealed something they might not have admitted otherwise.

In both cases, the answer revealed the heart of the person asking.

Saying it plainly, in his hearing, tells us the truth about ourselves.

From a vague hope to an honest request, with the outcome entrusted to God

Explore the background · 1 Samuel 1:9–18

Hannah at Shiloh

Only her lips moved; no sound came out.

Eli assumed she was drunk.

“I have been pouring out my soul before the Lord.”1 Samuel 1:15

She did not tidy up her anguish. She asked plainly.

Her face was no longer sad, before she knew how God would answer.

Not a promise that everyone will feel lighter straight away

We can bring our deepest longing to God before we know how he will answer.

Point 1

“What Do You Want Me to Do for You?”

Your intention is your answer to his question.

Not the answer you think a mature Christian should give.

The real one.

Provisional, not controlling: a place to begin

affirmredirectdelayreshape

Not instructions for God, but the truth about what you are bringing

The concern that matters most now

Your intention is allowed to change.

Expectation or intention?

ExpectationIntention

“God must tell me whether to leave my job.”

“I want to bring my fear and uncertainty about work before God, without forcing an immediate answer.”

“I must forgive everything by Sunday.”

“I want to become honest about the wound, and open to the next safe step towards freedom.”

“I need to feel God again.”

“I want to remain present with God, and truthful about the distance I feel.”

“This retreat must cure my burnout.”

“I want to recognise what has emptied me, and become willing to change what I can.”

Point 3

Honest, Not Polished

“I want to draw closer to God and be renewed in my faith.”

or, if this is the truer sentence:

“I am so angry about what happened that I cannot pray, and I have not told anyone.”

Scripture does not reward polished prayers.

Hannah poured out her soul.

Enter the retreat as the person you actually are.

  1. Is this what I truly want to bring to God?
  2. Am I using general spiritual language to avoid something more specific?
  3. Does this sound like me?
  4. Can I say it without pretending?

You need not say more than you can safely hold.

The hope underneath

What else am I hoping for?

Sometimes there is a hope underneath:

confirmation of a decision already madethat God will change someone who is not thereproof that I am still spiritualescape from a conversationa dramatic experience

None of these disqualifies you.

Left unspoken, they can distort what you hear.

Is there anything else I am quietly hoping for, or something I have found difficult to admit?

Your motives do not have to be perfectly pure before you begin.

Bring as much of yourself into the light as you can at present.

“Search me, O God, and know my heart! Try me and know my thoughts! And see if there be any grievous way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting!”Psalm 139:23–24 · ESV

Point 4

Open Hands

An intention is a request, not a demand.

You bring it to God, and leave the outcome with him.

clarity→perhaps peace about not knowing yet
his felt presence→perhaps quiet faithfulness
forgiveness→perhaps grief first

To God’s people in exile:

“You will seek me and find me, when you seek me with all your heart.”Jeremiah 29:13 · ESV

Not a promise of a dramatic retreat

Not comparing our decisions with the cross, but a pattern:

“Remove this cup from me. Yet not what I will, but what you will.”Mark 14:36 · ESV

“…but I am open to what you have for me instead.”

What open hands do not mean

Open hands do not mean passivity

towards abuse, injustice, danger, or responsibilities that require action.

Surrender: refusing to manipulate the retreat into giving the answer you want

wisdomboundariessafety

These you keep.

If you cannot find an intention

Too tired or too numb to know what you want?

“I do not know what I need. I only know I need to stop and be with you.”

That is a complete and honest intention.

Elijah: “It is enough.” God cared first for his body.

“I have reached my limit and need to stop.” Write that honestly, and go.

If “It is enough” means you do not want to remain alive, do not begin the retreat alone. Seek support straight away. See the support box below this lesson.

Practise · Your step

Your main intention may be one sentence. Four short prompts help you find it.

  1. What I am carryinga few sentences · your Lesson 4 check-in
  2. What I am askingone sentence, to God, in plain words
  3. What I have not saidanything I am quietly hoping for, or find hard to admit
  4. What I am releasingone sentence, letting go of a particular outcome

“God, I will not force an answer during these days…”

Read it to God in Session 1, and again in the final session.

Sharing it is your choice. You are never required to share what is private.
