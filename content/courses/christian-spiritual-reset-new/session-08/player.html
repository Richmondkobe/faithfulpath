Point 1

Beloved Before the Work

The declaration comes before the public work.

Luke 2: years of faithful, hidden life first

Not earned through public performance

His obedience expresses his identity; it does not manufacture it.

When the doing falters

Worth attached to usefulness, achievement, or being needed

Burnout can involve working conditions, heavy responsibilities and organisational pressures (Lesson 4).

Exhaustion can reveal a dependence that was hard to notice.

Point 3

Children, Not Slaves

The declaration belongs uniquely to Jesus, the eternal Son.

Through Christ and the Spirit of adoption, truly welcomed into God’s family

“See what kind of love the Father has given to us, that we should be called children of God; and so we are.”1 John 3:1 · ESV

Not “so we will be.” So we are.

“Because you are sons, God has sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying, ‘Abba! Father!’ So you are no longer a slave, but a son, and if a son, then an heir through God.”Galatians 4:6–7 · ESV

Women and men alike, as heirs

The younger son hoped to be a hired servant (Session 1).

Received through Christ, not earned through usefulness

Point 4

Roles Matter, Worth Is Received

parentpastornurseprovider

Real callings with real weight

Losing one is a real loss (Session 4).

A foundation when a role changes or ends

Grief and disorientation still come.

The role was never the whole of who you were.

A careful word

Not positive thinking

Grounded in what Scripture says about belonging to Christ, true whether or not you feel it.

Struggling to feel its comfort is not spiritual failure.

If worthlessness is persistent, or comes with hopelessness or thoughts of death, please seek professional care. Lesson 5 applies.

One question to carry into the practice

Without achievements, productivity, income, influence or usefulness, what would I want someone to understand about me?

Two sentences.
