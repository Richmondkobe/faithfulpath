Point 1

God Commanded Rest

Written into the Ten Commandments, beside the commands against murder, theft and adultery

A Sabbath and a retreat are not the same. The Sabbath command was given to Israel within the covenant at Sinai.

No commandment tells you to take this retreat. It is a response to an invitation.

A retreat: one practical way to step into that rhythm

Lesson 14: building a weekly rhythm of rest

Point 2

Withdraw in Order to Return

“But he would withdraw to desolate places and pray.” (Luke 5:16) His habit, not a single emergency.

Running away often means avoiding something we need to face.
A retreat is time with God, with the intention of returning.

Its value does not depend on a dramatic change.

Caring for your soul can help you return with greater patience, attention and honesty.

Point 3

Jonah or Elijah?

“But Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the Lord.” (Jonah 1:3)

Elijah “was afraid, and he arose and ran for his life.” (1 Kings 19:3)

God dealt with both men in their journeys.

Will we turn towards God honestly, and respond to what he shows us?

Retreat or avoidance?

What do I intend to do with the silence?

Facing God honestly, or avoiding a person, a responsibility, a decision or a conflict?

A genuine retreat creates space to consider what faithfulness requires next.

returning with renewed courageor making a necessary change

Point 4

Three Lies and a Permission

Lie 1: “If I stop, everything will fall apart.”

Preparation is different from postponement.

delegatesimplifyreschedulerelease for a time

“Unless the Lord builds the house, those who build it labor in vain. Unless the Lord watches over the city, the watchman stays awake in vain. It is in vain that you rise up early and go late to rest, eating the bread of anxious toil; for he gives to his beloved sleep.”Psalm 127:1–2 · ESV

The psalm’s title links it with Solomon, a king with great responsibility.

Diligent work is good. But our work cannot replace dependence on God.

Three lies and a permission

Lie 2: “Other people have it harder, so I have no right to be tired.”

Comparison does not restore anyone.

“Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden”, not “all who labor more than average”Matthew 11:28

Lie 3: “I will rest once this season is over.”

one deadline, then anotherchildren grow, parents ageministry never runs out of need

Exhaustion may begin to make decisions for you.

Elijah reached deep distress and exhaustion. He asked God to take his life, and then he slept. (1 Kings 19:4–5)

We do not have to wait until we reach that point before making room for rest.

Permission

You are allowed to stop.

Rest matters to God, and Jesus himself made room to withdraw and pray. You do not have to earn permission to recognise your limits.

prepare responsiblycommunicate clearlyprotect the time

A shorter retreat is still a real retreat. Lesson 6 will help you choose.
