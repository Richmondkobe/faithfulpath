When is it more than tiredness, and who can help?

Before anything else

If you are in danger now

If you may act on thoughts of suicide, have a plan or the means, cannot keep yourself safe, or are in immediate danger from another person:

Contact emergency services, or go to the nearest emergency department, now.

Suicidal thoughts without a plan or intention still need prompt professional help: a doctor, crisis line or urgent mental-health service, today.

Do not remain alone. Tell a trusted person. If it is safe, put distance between yourself and anything you could use to harm yourself.

Signs the Retreat Surfaced More

The dip has not lifted

Flat most days for two weeks or more, or deepening rather than easing

Do not wait

Severe, rapidly worsening, stopping ordinary life, or thoughts of suicide or self-harm: seek help now.

You do not have to wait two weeks, or reach any threshold, before asking.

The grief remains overwhelming

Disrupting sleep, eating, or your ability to cope

A counsellor can help well before any clinical threshold.

You do not need to prove it is serious enough.

Point 2

Who Does What

Pastors are essential, but not the right first call for everything. The full table is below this lesson.

Pastor or spiritual directorDryness, faith questions, guidance, church hurt: prayer, Scripture, counsel. Not diagnosis or treatment.
Counsellor or therapistGrief, relationships, transitions, moderate anxiety or low mood. Many respect faith; ask.
Doctor or mental-health professionalPersistent low mood, hopelessness, sleep or appetite changes, panic, intrusive thoughts, trauma: assessment and treatment

When it is urgent or specialised

Thoughts of death or suicideA crisis line or urgent service, even without a plan. Emergency services if you may act or cannot stay safe.
Abuse or coercive controlA specialist service, from a safe device. Joint counselling may be unsafe.
Substance dependenceA doctor and an addiction service. Do not stop abruptly on your own.
ScrupulosityA clinician experienced in OCD. Reassurance can feed the compulsion.
Misconduct or safeguardingThe appropriate authority

Two things the table implies

More than one kind of helpA pastor is often one part, not the whole.
The pastor’s role is realSomeone in treatment for depression can also benefit from prayer, Scripture and a caring community, as they are able to receive it.

The mistake is not going to a pastor. It is going only to a pastor, for something a pastor is not trained to treat.

Point 3

Faith and Help Together

“Seeking therapy means my faith is not enough.”

Faith does not require you to refuse appropriate care.

Prayer, pastoral support, medicine and therapy can work together.

“Medication would be a spiritual defeat.”

A medical treatment, not a verdict on your faith

Decide with a qualified prescriber. Do not start, stop or change medication because of the retreat.

“A Christian should be able to pray this away.”

Keep praying, and let prayer lead you towards truthful help.

Help may be one of the ways God provides care.

Two more objections, and choosing well

“My pastor should be enough.”

Responsible pastors recognise when other expertise is needed.

“People will find out.”

Generally confidential, within the law. Ask about its limits before you begin.

You may start with questions, not your whole story.

Point 4

For Pastors and Leaders

Find help outside your own church

Care from someone you lead is rarely free to be honest, and unfair to both.

your denomination’s provisiona supervisora counsellor experienced with clergya pastor in another town

Who in leadership needs to know you are receiving support?

Keep clinical details private. If disclosure could put you at risk, seek confidential advice first.

Ministers may not be unwell? You may begin to change that, without exposing yourself to harm.

For those in a crisis of faith

Not sure what you believe, or whether you believe?

Not a mental-health condition, and not necessarily a sign that anything is wrong with you

Often a faith carried by activity stops moving and has to stand on its own.

Not alone, and not with someone who will panic

a pastor outside your churcha spiritual directora believer who has walked through doubt

Keep a gentle connection with God. Do not make prayer a test of belief.

Be patient. Honest questions can lead to a more mature faith.
