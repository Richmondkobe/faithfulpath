Point 1

Choose the Length You Can Protect

Day 1slow down and begin to release
Day 2deeper honesty
Day 3listening, identity and returning

Three days is recommended for this course.

Not a spiritual requirement or a biblical measure of devotion

Nothing has to be resolved on a schedule. Walks, meals and quiet come between sessions.

Like reading quickly a letter that deserved to be read slowly

Not only Friday to Sunday: three weekdays may be easier.

Three consecutive days, without ordinary work in between

Other lengths

One day8–10 hours, with meals, walks and rest
Three hoursan immediate pause, not the full retreat, not crisis care
At home7 chosen sessions over 8 days, with a rest day

A selection of sessions, not all ten squeezed into eight days. Good for parents and caregivers.

A one-day retreat that happens is worth more than a three-day retreat that stays on your list for a year.

Not automatically better

Longer is not automatically better.

More than three days, long silence, fasting or intense exercises? Seek guidance from someone experienced, and remember Lesson 5.

Begin modestly, and learn how you respond.

Point 2

Choose Your Place

Home alonefree and familiar; one room, other doors closed
Home with othersthe hardest; best in the at-home format
A borrowed homeoften the best option. Ask.

Set up one room as your retreat space, and follow the digital boundary in Lesson 9.

The other adults agree to protect the time. You will be interrupted (Lesson 3).

People are usually glad to help.

More places

A church buildingquiet for a day; confirm access; someone knows when you arrive and leave
A retreat centrebuilt for silence; a very good choice for a first three-day retreat
A hotelif you are disciplined about the television and the phone
Outdoorsexcellent for parts of a retreat, rarely for all of it

A spiritual director? Ask about training, approach, confidentiality and safeguarding first.

Tell your support person where you are staying.

Solitude does not require placing yourself beyond the reach of help.

Point 3

Seven Questions for Any Place

  1. Can I have protected time here?
  2. Does this place help me feel safe and settled?
  3. Can I sleep well here? Elijah slept before God spoke.
  4. Can I eat simply here? (Lesson 10)
  1. Can I control my connectivity without losing access to help?
  2. Can this place support my physical needs?
  3. Can I afford it without stress?

A “no” needs a solution, or a different place.

The full checklist is below this lesson.

A word about cost

Some never go, because they cannot afford a retreat centre. This course was written for them.

More expensive does not mean more spiritual.

A retreat that puts you in debt, or causes tension at home, has already undermined its purpose.
