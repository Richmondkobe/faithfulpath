Point 1

Hearers and Doers

“and perseveres, being no hearer who forgets but a doer who acts”James 1:25 · ESV

Not one heroic act. Continuing.

Doing the word is not earning grace. It is what grace grows into.

Grace does not leave us unchanged; it teaches us to live differently.

Not dramatic. Actual.

Point 2

Insight, Then a Plan

Insight, then time

A plan with specifics

A quiet look at the facts before the announcement

Work broken into pieces people can actually do

Not announcing everything on the first evening. Not doing nothing.

James gives the command; Nehemiah, an example of doing it prayerfully and wisely.

The Share cards

They will not share themselves. First: what kind of sharing?

Three sentences

This is the burden.

This is how I have been carrying it alone.

This is the specific help I am asking for.

“Would you take over the monthly bills?”

Specific requests are easier to answer than general appeals.

A request, not a demand that they rescue you.

Some will say no. Some will be glad to be asked.

The Seek-counsel cards

Make the appointment. Not the decision; the appointment.

The first step is complete when it is booked.

Keep the card until you have the guidance you need.

two providersthe waiting lista referrala helplinethe cost

Urgent safety or medical concern? Do not wait for an ordinary appointment (Lesson 5).

A delayed medical or mental-health appointment: the card that matters most.

Entrust cards, and repairs

Entrust cards: no action. That is the point.

Read the card, pray the words, put it back.

Not neglecting a responsibility, tolerating harm, or refusing help.

New facts? Reclassify the card.

Repairs from Session 5: centre the harm done to them, not your own relief.

An apology obliges no one to forgive, reply, or restore trust.

Before any contact

Not if it would be unsafe, unwanted, legally restricted, or a further burden to them.

Seek qualified guidance first.

Point 4

The Jethro Conversation

Rest does not fix a structure (Lesson 4).

Three days away, then straight back into the pattern that emptied you.

Jethro to Moses

You are doing too much.

Some of this can be done by others.

Here is how to divide it.

Illness, bereavement, circumstances that cannot be reorganised: not a failure to delegate.

Where the workload can change: with yourself, and with whoever can change it.

a manageran elder boarda spousefamilya ministry teama care coordinatorcommunity services · respite care

Five questions

  1. What am I doing that only I can do?
  2. What could someone else do, if asked and trusted?
  3. What does not need to be done at all?
  4. What am I doing because I am afraid of what will happen if I stop?
  5. What would this role look like if designed for a human being with legitimate limits?

Be strict. The list is usually shorter than it feels.

One structural change

Ask for one structural change. One.

1a protected day off
2a task removed
3a responsibility shared
4a meeting that stops

A no? Ask why, and whether another form of relief is possible.

Expect some resistance: a system used to your overfunctioning has to adjust.

Neither makes you wrong to have asked.

When it does not work

a request declineda change refusedan appointment weeks awaya rhythm missed

None of that undoes the retreat.

“When I miss it, I will start again the next day without guilt.”

A refused request is information. A missed rhythm is restarted. A booked appointment is more than it was.

When you miss a step, return to what is right, and continue.

Practise

Your step: the minimum two-week plan

  1. One Act step, and the day
  2. One Share conversation: with whom, and three sentences
  3. One Seek-counsel appointment or access step
  4. The five Jethro questions, in your journal

Alongside: your Session 10 rhythm, imperfectly. An ongoing practice, not another task.

If capacity allows: a second Act step, a second Share conversation, one structural change.

Less capacity? Choose what most protects your health, safety and faithfulness.
