Point 1

A Psalm Full of Noise

One of the most quoted lines in the Bible, almost always quoted out of its setting

In the psalmInto the turmoil: God’s command to cease, and recognise that he is God
For usStop acting as though everything depends on our control. Trust the God who remains sovereign.

Not the stillness of a calm world

The stillness of a person who knows something true in the middle of a world that is not calm

Point 2

Present Help

“A very present help in trouble.”

Not distant · not delayed · not waiting for the trouble to end

Not a promised feeling. Trouble does not place you beyond his knowledge, his sovereignty, or his care.

In two places, and fully in neither

tomorrow’s meetinglast month’s conversationthe people at homethe thing we should have said

“Therefore do not be anxious about tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble.”Matthew 6:34 · ESV

Not shaming anxiety, or forbidding wise preparation: not carrying tomorrow’s burden before it arrives

Professional care and spiritual practice can go together. No shame in that.

Point 3

The Storm and the Sleeper

The text does not describe his inner state in detail.

The calm came from who he was and what he commanded, not from a technique.

Their fear was understandable: the danger was real.

Experienced fishermen would have understood how dangerous a filling boat could be.

Seeing the storm in the light of the one who was with them

Point 4

Coming Back Is the Practice

Not to make the storms stop · not to pretend they are not there

this roomthis hourthis body

and God is here too

Notice where you went, and come back.

The coming back is the practice.

Distraction does not have to become failure.

Presence helps with lament, confession, release and listening ahead.

Divided attention does not disqualify your prayer.

God receives you even while you are learning to return.

Where is my attention right now?
