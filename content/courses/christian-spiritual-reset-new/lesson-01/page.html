1 · Tired in a place sleep does not reach

Welcome to The Christian Spiritual Reset. I'm Pastor Richmond Kobe. Over this course I want to walk with you the way I would walk with someone who came into my office and admitted they were running on empty.

The course has three stages. First, we get ready: we understand what is happening in us, and we prepare to meet with God. Then we take the retreat itself, for three hours, one day, three days or longer, whatever you can truly protect. And finally, we come home well, with a rhythm that can last.

We begin with a question many tired believers carry quietly: why am I still tired after I have rested?

2 · Still showing up

Perhaps this describes you. You are still showing up. You still go to work, still answer messages, still sit in church on Sunday. If someone asked how you were doing, you would say "fine", and it would not exactly be a lie. Nothing has collapsed.

But somewhere underneath the routine, something has gone quiet. Prayer feels like talking into a room where nobody is home. The Bible has become one more thing you scroll past. You are not angry with God. You are just tired in a place that sleep does not reach. And feeling distant from God does not mean that God is absent.

If that is you, hear this before we go any further. You are not a failure, and you are not a fraud. You are a person whose body has kept moving after the soul asked for a rest it never received.

3 · A prophet under a broom tree

Our first passage tells the story of a prophet who reached that place. The Scripture readings in this course are from the English Standard Version. First Kings chapter 19, verses 3 to 8:

"Then he was afraid, and he arose and ran for his life and came to Beersheba, which belongs to Judah, and left his servant there. But he himself went a day's journey into the wilderness and came and sat down under a broom tree. And he asked that he might die, saying, 'It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life, for I am no better than my fathers.' And he lay down and slept under a broom tree. And behold, an angel touched him and said to him, 'Arise and eat.' And he looked, and behold, there was at his head a cake baked on hot stones and a jar of water. And he ate and drank and lay down again. And the angel of the LORD came again a second time and touched him and said, 'Arise and eat, for the journey is too great for you.' And he arose and ate and drank, and went in the strength of that food forty days and forty nights to Horeb, the mount of God."

4 · An invitation to the tired

And our second passage is the invitation of Jesus in Matthew chapter 11, verses 28 to 30:

"Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."

A tired prophet, and an inviting Saviour. Keep both of them in mind as we go on.

5 · Desolate = erēmos

Our Greek word in this lesson comes from Mark chapter 6, where Jesus invites his disciples to rest: "Come away by yourselves to a desolate place and rest a while." The word translated "desolate" is erēmos. It can describe a deserted, solitary, uninhabited or wilderness place, away from the crowds and the ordinary demands of life.

Mark uses the same word in chapter 1 for the place where Jesus himself went to pray: "And rising very early in the morning, while it was still dark, he departed and went out to a desolate place, and there he prayed."

So erēmos describes a deserted, solitary or wilderness place. The word itself does not mean spiritual renewal. What matters is Jesus' invitation, and the opportunity it gives to withdraw from constant demands and be with him.

6 · From Carmel to the broom tree

Now the background. Just before our passage, in 1 Kings 18, Elijah stood on Mount Carmel against 450 prophets of Baal. He prayed, fire fell, and the people fell on their faces and said, "The LORD, he is God." It was one of the most dramatic public victories of Elijah's life. Then came the execution of Baal's prophets, the end of a long drought, and a run ahead of King Ahab's chariot all the way to Jezreel.

Then Queen Jezebel sent him a message: by this time tomorrow, she would take his life. The threat was real. And this same man ran south to Beersheba, at the far end of the land, left his servant there, and went on alone, a day's journey into the wilderness.

Notice the timing. Elijah's collapse did not come after a defeat. It followed a remarkable victory and a genuine threat to his life. He travelled into the wilderness alone, where Scripture describes his despair and God's provision for him. Scripture does not give Elijah a modern label, and neither should we. It simply tells us honestly what happened to a faithful man.

7 · No time even to eat

Our Greek word came from a second scene. In Mark chapter 6, Jesus had sent the twelve out two by two. They preached, cast out demons and healed the sick. Then they came back and told him all that they had done and taught. The crowds were pressing so hard that, Mark says, "they had no leisure even to eat."

And Jesus did not say, "Well done, now keep going." He said, "Come away by yourselves to a desolate place and rest a while." And they went away in the boat.

Notice that their tiredness came after faithful, fruitful work. It was not a sign that something had gone wrong. And Jesus himself called them to rest.

8 · The short answer

So, why am I still tired after I have rested? Here is the short answer.

Some tiredness lives deeper than the body. Before Elijah received further direction, God cared for him with food, water and sleep. Jesus invites tired people to come away with him and find rest for their souls. And a reset is not a performance; it is making yourself available to God.

That gives us four points for this lesson: a tiredness sleep cannot fix; rest first, direction second; come away and rest; and what a reset is, and is not.

9 · Point 1: A Tiredness Sleep Cannot Fix

The first point: a tiredness sleep cannot fix. Physical tiredness may improve after a good night's sleep or a quiet weekend. Soul-tiredness often remains even after the body has rested. You can lie on the sofa for two days and still get up on Monday feeling empty.

It usually shows up in small ways first. Things that used to feed you no longer do. Worship music plays and you feel nothing. You are short with people you love. You pray for others out of habit, but cannot remember the last time you prayed honestly about yourself. Decisions that used to be simple now feel heavy. You may even feel a low-grade guilt about all of this, which only makes you more tired.

Most Christians respond to this in the same way: by trying harder. A new reading plan. An earlier alarm. More devotionals. Sometimes that helps for a week. Often it becomes one more thing we feel we are failing at.

These signs can have many causes, physical and emotional as well as spiritual, and a retreat is not the answer to all of them. Lesson 4 will help us tell them apart. But for many tired believers, trying harder is not the answer. What may be missing is not another spiritual assignment, but unhurried, undistracted time in the presence of God, with nothing to prove and nothing to perform.

10 · Point 2: Rest First, Direction Second

The second point: rest first, direction second. Look at what God did for Elijah under the broom tree. He did not rebuke him. He did not give him a sermon on faith. An angel touched him and said, "Arise and eat." There was a cake baked on hot stones and a jar of water. Elijah ate, drank and lay down again.

Then the angel came a second time: "Arise and eat, for the journey is too great for you."

It happened twice. God's first recorded response to Elijah's exhaustion was not a rebuke or a sermon. It was food, water and sleep, with a simple word about the journey ahead. And notice the gentle realism of those words: "the journey is too great for you." God did not pretend Elijah was stronger than he was.

This is good news for anyone who has quietly assumed that God must be disappointed with their tiredness. In this story, the first thing God gives is not a lecture, but care for a tired body.

11 · God's order

Only after Elijah had rested and eaten did the journey begin. He "went in the strength of that food forty days and forty nights to Horeb, the mount of God." There God would speak with him, and we will return to that mountain in Session 7.

Before Elijah received further direction at Horeb, God provided food, water and sleep for the journey ahead. This is a pattern we see in Elijah's story, not a rule that God must always follow. But it reminds us that caring for our bodies and making room for rest can be part of preparing to hear and obey God. Many of us want direction while skipping the rest that may help us receive it.

12 · Point 3: Come Away and Rest

The third point: come away and rest. When the apostles came back tired, Jesus invited them to come away with him. And this was not an exception in his life. Luke tells us that as the crowds grew larger, Jesus "would withdraw to desolate places and pray." It was his habit, and he invited his closest followers into the same rhythm.

Notice when he withdrew. Luke says that "great crowds gathered to hear him and to be healed of their infirmities." Even as the crowds grew and the demands on his ministry increased, Jesus continued to withdraw to solitary places and pray. He returned to teach and heal, but his service was sustained by a rhythm that included withdrawing to pray.

So if Jesus deliberately made room for withdrawal and rest, you are not being weak when you need it too. Needing rest is part of being human, and Jesus does not shame you for it. He invites you.

13 · Rest for your souls

Then there is the invitation almost every tired Christian has heard, but few have actually accepted: "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." And a moment later: "you will find rest for your souls."

Hear that phrase again. Not only rest for your body. Rest for your souls. Jesus names the very tiredness we have been describing, and he says that he is the one who gives rest. And he tells us what he is like: "gentle and lowly in heart." The one who invites you is not harsh.

And notice what else he says: "Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me." Jesus does not invite us to abandon following him. He invites us to learn from him and walk under his gentle leadership. His rest is found in relationship with him, not simply in escaping our responsibilities. We will look at his yoke more closely in Session 3.

Notice also who the invitation is for: "all who labor and are heavy laden." That is a description, not a qualification. There is no exam to pass to prove you are tired enough. If you are tired, you are invited.

But an invitation only becomes rest when you come. This course, and the retreat it leads to, is simply a structured way of coming.

14 · Point 4: What a Reset Is and Is Not

The fourth point: what a reset is, and what it is not. A spiritual reset is not an attempt to manufacture an emotional encounter with God. It is not a way to force God to speak. And it is not a test of how spiritual you are.

It is a deliberate pause from noise, responsibility and performance, so that you can become honest before God again. On the retreat you may experience tears, clarity, peace or conviction, or nothing dramatic at all. The goal is not to produce a feeling. The goal is to make yourself available to God. Some people finish a retreat with a strong sense of having met God. Others finish having been faithful and honest, and still feeling dry. Both have done the retreat.

Some sessions will be gentle, and some will go to harder places. So in the lessons before the retreat, we will take time to understand what is happening in us, and to prepare well.

15 · Your step

Here is your step for this lesson. Take two minutes. Not to plan or to fix anything; just to answer honestly. You will find the three questions below this lesson.

First: can you remember a time when you felt close to God, not in a service or a meeting, but on your own? If so, what was different then? If not, what are you hoping to experience or understand as you begin this retreat?

Second: what is one thing you have kept doing for God while feeling nothing? Do not judge it. Just name it.

Third: Elijah said, "It is enough." If you were completely honest with God right now, what would your sentence be?

Write your answers somewhere private, and keep them. You will read them again at the end of the retreat, in Session 10, and on Day 30, and you may notice what has shifted.

16 · Remember

Let us bring it together. Some tiredness lives deeper than the body, and trying harder is not always the answer. God provided food, water and sleep for Elijah before he continued his journey towards Horeb. Jesus invites tired people to come away with him and find rest for their souls. And a reset is not a performance; it is making yourself available to God.

17 · Prayer

Let us pray. Lord God, you saw Elijah under the broom tree, and you did not turn away from him. You see me too, tired in places I cannot always explain. Thank you that you care for the tired before you ask anything of them. Lord Jesus, you said, "Come to me." I am coming, as honestly as I can. Teach me to rest in you, and prepare my heart for the days ahead. In Jesus' name. Amen.
