Explore the background

A doctor among Paul’s companions

Luke 5:31 is a spiritual picture of Jesus’ mission to sinners.

It assumes an ordinary truth: when you are ill, you seek a doctor.

Paul calls Luke “the beloved physician” (Colossians 4:14).

Traditionally: Paul’s travelling companion, and the author of Luke and Acts

Paul gave Timothy practical advice for his health (1 Timothy 5:23).

Dependence on God and care through ordinary means

God can work through both.

Point 1

What a Retreat Can Do

interrupt a patternunhurried honestyname what you carryrestore perspectivea place to meet Godbegin things

God is not summoned by a schedule. A retreat is not a transaction.

Beginnings are not completions, but they are how completions start.

Point 2

What It Cannot Do

  • replace medical or psychological care
  • fix in three days what took years to form
  • guarantee that you will feel God’s presence
  • substitute for community
  • make a difficult decision for you

Psalm 88 ends in darkness, and God kept it in his book.

Hold impressions lightly

The listening sessions may help.

Any of us can mistake fear, desire, memory or strong emotion for God’s direction.

Hold everything lightly. Write down what you sense.

Not God’s direction: anything that contradicts Scripture · promotes harm · inflates your importance · removes accountability · demands reckless urgency

Unless you need to act to protect yourself or someone else from harm, avoid major irreversible decisions during the retreat.

Record your impressions · seek wise counsel · test them afterwards

Lesson 12 will help.

Point 3

Help Is Not a Lack of Faith

Seeking counselling or medication is not a failure of faith.

“in an abundance of counselors there is safety” (Proverbs 11:14)

Not exactly modern counselling, but a rejection of isolated decision-making

A doctor · a counsellor · a pastor

Different knowledge, care and support, often working together

Already in care? Tell them about your retreat and ask what to adjust.

Need care? Take one step towards it before you begin.

Do not let the retreat become the reason you postpone help.

When support comes first

Some situations need support before or during a retreat.

Below this lesson: what to do first, in the book’s own words

A retreat will still be here when you are safe and supported.

Postponing is not failing. It may be the wisest way to prepare.

When to pause

You may pause, change your plan, or stop.

More than you can hold? Stop the session.

Not the retreat, necessarily: the session.

stand upgo outsidewalk · water · eat“Lord, help”contact your support person

Severe, or danger to anyone? A professional, a crisis service or emergency services.

You may return later, change to a supported format, or stop altogether.

A retreat that is paused is not a retreat that failed.

Pausing is often exactly how God cares for us.

Point 4

Choose Your Path

Proceed independentlystable, safe alone, able to pause
Proceed with supportregular contact with a trusted person or professional
Use an adapted formatshorter, guided or group (Lesson 6)
Postpone for nowseek support first

All four paths are faithful choices.

Postponing is not failing. It may be the wisest way to prepare.

Practise · Your step

  1. Is there any support you need to arrange before you begin? When will you arrange it?
  2. Who will you call if a session becomes too much? Have you told them?
  3. What are you hoping the retreat will do? Mark anything that belongs in “cannot”. Separate what you ask of God from what you expect your retreat to produce.
  4. Which of the four paths are you choosing?
