Point 1

Mercy Before Confession

He begins with God’s character, not with his own defence.

That order is central to the theology of confession.

Resting on God’s revealed character and the mercy given in Christ, not on our ability to feel certain

Point 2

Harm Is Not Erased

“Against you, you only, have I sinned”Psalm 51:4

Not a claim that Bathsheba and Uriah were unharmed

Confession to God
Accountability for harm

belong together

Not every hardship after confession is punishment.

Forgiveness does not automatically reverse relational, legal, professional, or practical consequences.

Point 3

Not Every Limitation Is Sin

A pattern may have become clearer during the retreat.

Silence is not an infallible detector of sin. Test what surfaces.

Wrongdoing is not the same as limitation.

What did I choose?Who was affected?What response is needed?

Point 4

Restore to Me the Joy

Not to be tolerated: to be made new, and to have joy back

Joy may return gradually. No immediate relief does not mean the confession was invalid.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”1 John 1:9 · ESV

Resting on Christ’s saving work, not the intensity or wording of our confession

Her love is the response to mercy, not the price of it.

“Your faith has saved you; go in peace.” (Luke 7:50)

Two careful words

Scrupulosity

Repeated confession and checking may be compulsions. No new rule about how often or how perfectly to confess.

You may skip the written exercise. Follow your clinician’s plan.

Serious harm

Abuse · exploitation · criminal conduct · misconduct · someone’s present safety

Confess it to God, then seek qualified guidance about accountability, reporting, restitution and disclosure.
