Transcript and Scripture references

1 · Day 2 evening, or Day 3 morning

Welcome back. If you are beginning this session on the evening of Day 2, make sure you have genuinely rested since the lament. If you are beginning on the morning of Day 3, good morning. Either way, begin only after genuine rest, when you have enough emotional capacity. The default is the morning, after sleep.

This session is about confession, and I want to say at the outset what it is not. It is not an interrogation. It is not a search for hidden sins to explain your dryness; Lessons 4 and 8 both said that dryness is not always caused by sin, and that remains true. It is not a session for people to punish themselves. This is a session about grace. Confession is not how you persuade God to become merciful; it is how you step honestly into the mercy God has already revealed.

If you have diagnosed or suspected scrupulosity, or a pattern of repeated confession and reassurance-seeking, please read the note on the page before deciding whether to do the exercise at all. Our question is this: how do I confess without punishing myself?

2 · "Have mercy on me, O God"

Our central passage is Psalm 51, verses 1 to 12. In the book you are asked to read it twice, and to notice the second time how much of it is about God’s character, and how little is about the details of what David did. Listen for that now.

"Have mercy on me, O God, according to your steadfast love; according to your abundant mercy blot out my transgressions. Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin! For I know my transgressions, and my sin is ever before me. Against you, you only, have I sinned and done what is evil in your sight, so that you may be justified in your words and blameless in your judgment. Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me. Behold, you delight in truth in the inward being, and you teach me wisdom in the secret heart."

3 · "Create in me a clean heart"

"Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow. Let me hear joy and gladness; let the bones that you have broken rejoice. Hide your face from my sins, and blot out all my iniquities. Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me. Cast me not away from your presence, and take not your Holy Spirit from me. Restore to me the joy of your salvation, and uphold me with a willing spirit."

4 · "You are the man"

The traditional heading connects Psalm 51 with the story told in 2 Samuel 11 and 12. David abused his royal power, summoned Bathsheba, slept with her, and then arranged the death of her husband, Uriah, to conceal what had happened. He continued as king until the prophet Nathan confronted him with a story about a rich man who stole a poor man’s lamb. David condemned the rich man, and Nathan said, "You are the man!"

It is worth reading the psalm with that story in mind, because Psalm 51 is not the prayer of a person with a vague sense of unworthiness. It is the prayer of a person who has done real and serious harm, and knows it.

5 · Steadfast love = ḥeseḏ

Our Hebrew word is the one translated "steadfast love" in the first verse: ḥeseḏ. It is God’s loyal, merciful love: his faithfulness to his people, even when they have been unfaithful.

When God revealed his name to Moses, he described himself this way:

"The Lord, the Lord, a God merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness."

That is what David appeals to. Not his own record, and not the quality of his remorse, but God’s steadfast love. It is the ground of the whole prayer.

6 · Mercy Before Confession

David’s first words appeal to God’s steadfast love and abundant mercy. He does not begin by minimising the wrong, or defending himself; he begins by asking the merciful God to deal with it. That order is central to the theology of confession. Christian confession rests not on our ability to feel certain, but on God’s revealed character, and the mercy given in Christ.

7 · Harm Is Not Erased

Two things in the psalm need careful handling. First, David says, "Against you, you only, have I sinned." That is not a claim that Bathsheba and Uriah were unharmed. The verse uses intensely God-centred language; it does not teach that sins against people are only private matters between the offender and God. Confession to God and accountability for human harm belong together.

David’s own consequences, in the story, were severe and lasting. That does not mean every hardship after confession is divine punishment. It means that forgiveness does not automatically reverse relational, legal, professional, or practical consequences.

8 · Not Every Limitation Is Sin

Lesson 8 asked you to confess known sin before you came. This session lets you consider whether a recurring attitude, choice, or pattern has become clearer during the retreat. But silence is not an infallible detector of sin. It can also produce memories, fears, intrusive thoughts, mistaken interpretations, and self-criticism, so anything that surfaces must be tested, rather than automatically confessed.

Not every limitation is sin. Exhaustion, numbness, intrusive thoughts, fear, illness, trauma responses, and the need for boundaries are not automatically moral failures. If you are burned out, you may be tempted to confess emptiness as though it were cruelty. So ask whether you knowingly chose deception, cruelty, manipulation, neglect, exploitation, or another violation of love, not merely whether you felt afraid, empty, or unavailable. The page gives you five simple questions to help.

9 · Restore to Me the Joy

Second, the psalm does not end in the dirt. It moves toward restoration: "Create in me a clean heart, O God … Restore to me the joy of your salvation." David does not ask to be tolerated. He asks to be made new, and to have joy back. That joy may return gradually, and may need relational repair, treatment, and time. A lack of immediate relief does not mean the confession was invalid.

The New Testament gives the promise plainly:

"If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness."

God is faithful and just because forgiveness rests in Christ’s saving work, not in the emotional intensity or perfect wording of our confession. And in Luke 7, a woman known in the town as a sinner weeps at Jesus’ feet, and Jesus says, "her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much." The parable he has just told explains the order: the one forgiven much loves much. Her love is the response to mercy, not the price of it. And he says to her, "Your faith has saved you; go in peace."

10 · Two careful words

Two careful words before the practice. If you have diagnosed or suspected scrupulosity, repeated confession, moral checking, and attempts to achieve perfect certainty may be compulsions rather than spiritually useful practices. Do not use this session to create a new rule about how many times or how perfectly you must confess. Follow the plan developed with a clinician who understands OCD and respects your faith. You may skip the written exercise entirely.

And if what surfaces is a sin that involved harm to another person, abuse, exploitation, criminal conduct, professional misconduct, or someone’s present safety, confess it to God, and then seek qualified guidance about accountability, reporting, restitution, and appropriate disclosure. Do not use this session to avoid a responsibility that needs to be faced in the open.

11 · One question

So here is one question to carry into the practice. What does the first verse of Psalm 51 reveal about the mercy to which you are bringing this? On the page, you will write that first, before anything else.

And if, when you look honestly, you do not see a pattern, that is an acceptable answer.

12 · Put the screen aside

This is where the teaching stops. Pause the player now, and put the screen aside if you can.

On the page you will find the session’s practice: a gentle check-in, a guided prayer, fifteen minutes of silence or the length you can manage, a few questions for your journal, and a simple exercise of confession and assurance, with an envelope marked "Forgiven". Take your time.

You are not hunting for sins. You are stepping honestly into mercy. "Have mercy on me, O God, according to your steadfast love."

The practice

Now put the screen aside

Allow approximately an hour to an hour and a half. This is not a timed exercise. Take breaks, move at your own pace, and stop whenever you need to.

Write in your journal or on the Session 5 workbook pages. Nothing you write is collected, and no confession is ever asked for.

1Check-in

Gently. This is a review of a season, not a hunt.

  1. What does Psalm 51:1 reveal about the mercy to which you are bringing this? Write it before you write anything else.
  2. Has a repeated choice or relational pattern become clearer during the retreat? If so, name it. If nothing comes, do not manufacture it; “I do not see a pattern” is an acceptable answer. Move to the prayer.
  3. Did that pattern involve actual wrongdoing, a human limitation, a health or trauma response, or some combination? Use the five questions below.
  4. Who was affected, and what impact can you responsibly acknowledge?
The five questions
  1. What did I choose?
  2. Who was affected?
  3. What truth or responsibility did I knowingly avoid?
  4. Was this wrongdoing, a human limitation, a symptom, or some combination?
  5. Do I need confession, care, boundaries, treatment, repair, or more than one of these?

3Silence

Suggested length: fifteen minutes, or the length you can manage.

“According to your steadfast love.” · or: “Have mercy on me.”

An unwanted thought, image, impulse, or memory is not automatically a sin, a confession, an intention, or a revelation of your character. Do not confess a thought merely because it appeared.

In this silence, do not scan for sins. Rest in the first verse of the psalm. If you remember a specific choice or pattern that may require attention, write it down for later discernment. Do not interpret every thought or feeling as evidence of sin. If nothing comes, rest in the mercy. Both are the session.

15:00

A short spoken opening, then silence, then a short spoken closing.

When the timer ends, take one unhurried breath before moving. If you want to remain longer, you may; if you are ready to stop, stop. This timer needs the screen to stay on.

4Journal

  1. What, if anything, surfaced during the silence? Write it plainly, without excuse and without exaggeration, and then apply the five questions to it.
  2. David’s consequences did not disappear when he was forgiven. Is there any repair, apology, or accountability that your confession points toward once you are home? Write what it is and who it involves. If it is serious in the way described in the boundaries card, write only the name of the qualified person you will consult.
  3. Has concealment, defensiveness, or refusal to take responsibility affected your ability to love someone well? If so, how?
  4. If I began living from the assurance of forgiveness while still accepting appropriate consequences, one possible change would be…

5Confession and assurance of forgiveness

If the scrupulosity note applies to you, you may skip this exercise and use a brief, familiar prayer instead.

Take one index card, or two if needed. Write only the specific matter or pattern that has already become clear, in the plainest words you can, without softening and without self-punishment. You do not need to search for additional things to confess.

Keep it safe. Do not record names, identifying details, illegal conduct, abuse details, or information that could endanger another person if discovered. Use general language and keep the envelope secure. For serious matters, record only the name of the qualified professional you will contact.

Writing and sealing are optional. You may simply read the two promises below aloud and finish there, without writing or sealing anything. This is especially wise if symbolic acts tend to become repeated reassurance.

If you have written a card, read it to God, aloud if you have privacy. Then read aloud these two passages:

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.” (1 John 1:9)

“As far as the east is from the west, so far does he remove our transgressions from us.” (Psalm 103:12)

Receive these passages as promises given to God’s people and applicable to those who come to him in repentance and faith. This is not a ritual with power of its own. It is you hearing, in God’s own words, what he has said about what you have just brought him.

If you wish, put the cards in an envelope and seal it. Write on the front the word Forgiven and today’s date. You do not need to open it again. Some people keep it as a reminder of the date; some, weeks later, dispose of it at home as an act of closing. Either is fine. The sealed envelope symbolises that confession has been placed under God’s promise of forgiveness. It does not close questions of accountability, repair, reporting, or continuing care.

If any card also carries an action, an apology, a repair, a consultation, copy that action onto one of the Act or Seek-counsel cards from Session 3 before you seal the envelope. The confession is answered; the action is still yours.

James 5:16 places confession and prayer within Christian community: “confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed.” Some sins should be acknowledged to people directly affected; others may benefit from carefully chosen pastoral accountability. Not every private sin must be disclosed broadly, and not every trusted friend is equipped to receive serious information. Seek guidance before disclosing abuse, criminal conduct, sexual misconduct, professional violations, or information that affects another person’s safety. Before disclosing a serious matter, ask what confidentiality the person can and cannot offer; pastors, counsellors, teachers, and other professionals may have reporting obligations. God’s forgiveness does not depend on their hearing it. Session 10 will return to this.

6Closing declaration

Read only what you can say honestly. Adjust the words if you need to.

God’s steadfast love is revealed before my confession begins.

I have named what I can see, without exaggeration or excuse.

In Christ, forgiveness rests on God’s faithfulness, not the intensity of my feelings.

I will accept both mercy and the faithful actions that accountability requires.

(Optional:) Forgiven much, I am free to love much.

On your plan

Three-day retreat. Day 2 evening only if you feel physically rested, emotionally settled, and genuinely willing, not merely because you want to clear Day 3. If in any doubt, leave it for Day 3 morning. If Session 4 brought up more than you could hold, do not attempt Session 5 tonight.

One-day retreat and three-hour reset. Session 5 is not part of your plan. If confession needs more time, plan a three-day or at-home retreat later. You do not need to wait for a retreat to confess known sin.

At-home retreat. Your Day 6, only if settled. If Day 4 is still open in you, make Day 6 another rest day, and move Session 5 to Day 7, Session 7 to Day 8, and Session 10 to Day 9.

Retreat for couples. Each of you does the session on your own. No one is asked what they confessed. Confession to a spouse, where it is needed, is a separate and carefully prepared conversation, not part of the retreat’s shared moments.

Pastors and leaders. Two cautions. First, the temptation to confess pastorally, to produce a confession that would sound right from a pulpit, is strong in ministers: use Hannah’s honesty (Lesson 7). Second, if what surfaces involves misconduct, the safeguarding guidance in the card above applies to you with particular force.

After this session

Session 6, on forgiving others, is the last of the heavier sessions. If you did Session 5 on the evening of Day 2, sleep before Session 6; do not attempt it tonight. If you did Session 5 on the morning of Day 3, take a proper break, a meal and a walk at least, before anything further. Session 6 may be deferred to the at-home format or to the weeks after the retreat; any session left unfinished can be completed at home without being treated as failure. You do not need to force forgiveness into a schedule.
