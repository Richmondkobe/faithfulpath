Explore the background · Matthew 23:4

“All who labor and are heavy laden”: not limited to one kind of burden

“They tie up heavy burdens, hard to bear, and lay them on people’s shoulders, but they themselves are not willing to move them with their finger.”Matthew 23:4 · ESV

A yoke: labour and submission

In Jewish teaching, an image for accepting a teacher’s authority and way of life

“Take my yoke”: come under his teaching.

Understand the original meaning

Easy · Greek

chrēstos

good · kind · pleasant, rather than harsh

The same word, used of God:

“kind to the ungrateful and the evil”Luke 6:35
“the Lord is good”1 Peter 2:3

Not a life without commitment

Still a yoke. The one who calls us is gentle and lowly in heart.

Point 1

Come to Me

Familiarity is the danger.

Rest begins in relationship with Christ, not in a technique.

Religious exhaustion is among the burdens you may bring.

Laid on you in God’s name? It is still a burden, and Jesus still says “Come.”

Point 2

Take My Yoke

Not “I will take your yoke away”

To take his yoke is to become his disciple.

Rest and discipleship belong together.

The difference: the character of the teacher

not fearnot humiliationnot endless proving

That is not the way of the gentle teacher who calls you.

Point 3

Learn From Me

Not only relief: a different way of living, learned under a gentle Lord

Not in a morning. But you can begin.

Release does not mean “drop everything”.

Children still need feeding. Work still needs doing.

Not responsible for producing another person’s choice, or guaranteeing an outcome

Test it through Scripture, wise counsel, and your real commitments.

“Humble yourselves, therefore, under the mighty hand of God so that at the proper time he may exalt you, casting all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you.”1 Peter 5:6–7 · ESV

Responsibilities do not disappear. Your strength does not control every outcome.

Treatment, medication, practical support and trust can belong together.

One caution

Release is not silence about harm

Not pretending the harm did not happen, or relieving the person who caused it of responsibility.

Not withdrawing a report · abandoning a safety plan · cancelling a boundary · concealing wrongdoing · relieving an offender of accountability

Sessions 4 and 6 give these their own space.

You do not need to wait for another session to seek protection, report harm, or get support.
