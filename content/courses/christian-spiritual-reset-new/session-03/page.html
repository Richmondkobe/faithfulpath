Transcript and Scripture references

Our question is this: what should I put down, and what is mine to carry?

2 · "Come to me"

Our central passage is Matthew chapter 11, verses 28 to 30. Jesus said:

"Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."

Now hear the first words again, slowly, as if they were spoken to you by name. "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

3 · Heavy burdens, and a yoke

Two pieces of background help us hear this passage. First, Matthew does not limit "all who labor and are heavy laden" to one kind of burden. Yet later in his Gospel he records Jesus condemning religious leaders who did this:

"They tie up heavy burdens, hard to bear, and lay them on people’s shoulders, but they themselves are not willing to move them with their finger."

Second, a yoke was associated with labour and submission, and it became a natural image in Jewish teaching for accepting a teacher’s authority and way of life. So when Jesus says "Take my yoke," he is inviting people to come under his teaching.

4 · Easy = chrēstos

Our Greek word is chrēstos, translated "easy" in verse 30. It can describe what is good, kind or pleasant, rather than harsh.

Elsewhere the same word is used of God himself. Luke records Jesus saying that God "is kind to the ungrateful and the evil." And Peter writes of those who "have tasted that the Lord is good."

Jesus is not saying that following him involves no commitment. His yoke is still a yoke, but the one who calls us to follow him is gentle and lowly in heart.

5 · Come to Me

You may know this passage well. Familiarity is the danger. It is possible to have heard "Come to me" so many times that it no longer lands. So let us hear it again, in the movements the passage itself makes.

Come to me. Rest begins in relationship with Christ, not in a technique. Because of what Jesus said about heavy burdens laid on people’s shoulders, it is right to include religious exhaustion among the burdens you may bring, while not reducing the invitation to that alone. Some of what you are carrying may have been laid on you in God’s name. It is still a burden, and Jesus still says "Come."

6 · Take My Yoke

Take my yoke. Notice that he does not say, "I will take your yoke away." To take Jesus’ yoke is to become his disciple: to place yourself under his teaching and learn his manner of obedience. He does not merely offer a temporary break. He calls people to himself, places them under his teaching, and invites them to learn his way of life. The rest and the discipleship belong together.

So the difference lies not in the absence of a yoke, but in the character of the teacher. His yoke is kind. Its master is "gentle and lowly in heart," not cruel or oppressive. He does not drive his disciples through fear, humiliation, or endless attempts to prove their worth. If you have been driving yourself through fear, humiliation or endless attempts to prove your worth, that is not the way of the gentle teacher who calls you to follow him.

7 · Learn From Me

Learn from me. The rest he offers is not only relief; it is a different way of living, learned under a gentle Lord. You will not learn it in a morning. But you can begin.

This matters for the practical work of this session, because "release" can be misunderstood. Some people hear it as "drop everything," and then feel guilty because their children still need feeding and their work still needs doing. That is not what Jesus offers, and it is not what this session asks.

8 · Act, Share, Entrust, Seek Counsel

In this session, release means four things: taking faithful action on what genuinely belongs to you, sharing what should not be carried alone, entrusting what you cannot control, and seeking counsel where responsibility is unclear.

To entrust is to release the attempt to control what lies outside your authority. You may still have a faithful response to make, but you are not responsible for producing another person’s choice, or guaranteeing an outcome. Some expectations placed on you are unhealthy, manipulative, or inconsistent with Scripture. Others may be real responsibilities you do not want to face. Do not make that distinction from feeling alone; test it through Scripture, wise counsel, and the commitments you have actually made.

And Peter writes:

"Humble yourselves, therefore, under the mighty hand of God so that at the proper time he may exalt you, casting all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you."

Peter does not promise that responsibilities disappear. You still take faithful action, but you do not act as though your strength controls every outcome. And an anxiety disorder is not proof that someone has refused to cast their cares on God. Professional treatment, medication, practical support and spiritual trust can belong together.

9 · One caution

One caution before the practice. Some burdens are the direct result of harm done to you, and release is not a way of pretending the harm did not happen, or of relieving the person who caused it of responsibility.

Entrusting the outcome to God does not mean withdrawing a report, abandoning a safety plan, cancelling a boundary, concealing wrongdoing, or relieving an offender of accountability. Release is not silence, reconciliation, or surrender to further harm. If what you carry includes abuse, injustice, or serious wrong done against you, this session is not asking you to let that go. Sessions 4 and 6 will give those things their own space. And you do not need to wait for another retreat session to seek protection, report harm, or get appropriate support.

10 · One question

So here is one question to carry into the practice. What are you carrying right now? Responsibilities, worries, fears, hopes you are holding for other people, expectations, guilt, unresolved situations.

You do not need to answer it now. On the page, you will write it all down first, without sorting it.

11 · Put the screen aside

This is where the teaching stops. Pause the player now, and put the screen aside if you can.

On the page you will find the session’s practice: a list of what you are carrying, a guided prayer, fifteen minutes of silence or the length you can manage, a few questions for your journal, and a simple exercise of handing over, with index cards. Take your time, and stop if you need to.

You are not trying to solve everything this morning. You are coming to the one who said, "Come to me, and I will give you rest."

The practice

Now put the screen aside

Allow approximately an hour to an hour and a half. This is not a timed exercise. Take breaks and move at your own pace.

Write in your journal or on the Session 3 workbook pages. Nothing you write is collected.

1What you are carrying

In your journal, make a list. Take ten minutes or more. Write down everything you are carrying right now: responsibilities, worries, fears, hopes you are holding for other people, expectations, guilt, unresolved situations. Do not sort them yet. Include the things that feel too small to mention. Include what you can name safely; if something feels too large, write only a general word such as “grief,” “harm,” or “something for professional support.” You do not need to record details.

When the list is done, read it once, and then answer:

  1. How long is the list? Did it surprise you?
  2. Which item on it has been there longest?
  3. Which item most affects your daily capacity? What kind of support, change, or faithful response might make it more manageable?

2Guided prayer

If noticing your body is uncomfortable, rest your eyes on something still instead, or simply listen to the words.

Guided prayer: Release What You Are Carrying · a few minutes
Words of the recording

Before we pray, notice where you are holding tension. Perhaps in your jaw, shoulders, hands, or somewhere else. You do not have to change it. Simply notice.

Jesus, you said, “Come to me.” And I have come.

I am carrying more than I can hold. I have been carrying some of it as though no one else would.

Some of it is mine to act upon. Some of it I cannot control. Some of it should be shared with another person. Some of it needs counsel or qualified help. And I cannot always tell the difference.

Teach me. Show me what is mine to do. Show me what to share. Show me where I need counsel or help. Show me what to entrust to you.

Say your own name now, quietly. Then receive the invitation of Jesus personally: “Come to me.”

Amen.

Remain where you are and move into the silence. Your anchor phrase is: “Come to me, and I will give you rest.” Or, more simply: “Gentle and lowly in heart.”

3Silence

Suggested length: fifteen minutes, or the length you can manage.

“Come to me, and I will give you rest.” · or: “Gentle and lowly in heart.”

Sit with your list in front of you if that is helpful, or close the journal if it is not. Do not work on the list during the silence. Simply be in the presence of the one who said “Come,” with everything you have written still in the room. Notice which item your attention returns to, but do not assume repetition makes it the one you must address. You will choose where to begin based on safety, readiness, and wisdom.

4Journal: sorting the list

Go back through your list and mark each item with one or more of these letters. An item can carry more than one; “A/E” might mean take the action that belongs to me and entrust the result.

MarkMeaningQuestion to ask
A · ActSomething within my responsibility or controlWhat is the next faithful action?What is the next faithful action?
S · ShareA burden needing practical, pastoral, relational, or professional supportWho should help carry this?Who should help carry this?
E · EntrustAn outcome or choice I cannot controlWhat am I trying to guarantee that belongs to God or to another person?What am I trying to guarantee that belongs to God or to another person?
? · Seek counselResponsibility is unclear or potentially seriousWhose qualified wisdom do I need?Whose qualified wisdom do I need?

Then:

  1. Look at the items marked E. What have you been trying to guarantee? Where did the sense that it was yours to guarantee come from?
  2. Look at the items marked S. For each, name the kind of help it needs: a friend, a spouse, a pastor, a doctor, a counsellor, a colleague, a professional service.
  3. Look at the items marked ?. For each, name the right kind of guidance. A pastor or counsellor is not the correct authority for every burden; some need a doctor or mental-health professional, a domestic-abuse specialist, a safeguarding leader, a lawyer or financial adviser, an employer or HR representative, or a trusted family member.
  4. Was anything on the list laid on you in God’s name that Jesus might describe as a heavy burden, hard to bear? Write about it honestly. If you are not sure, say so.

5Handing over

Take the index cards. Write one card per item, using the category it received. The wording below is a starting point; use your own words if they are truer.

  • Entrust cards: “I cannot control ______. I release my demand to guarantee the outcome. Show me any faithful action that still belongs to me.”
  • Share cards: “I need help carrying ______. The person or service I will approach is ______.”
  • Act cards: “The next faithful step within my responsibility is ______. I do not have to solve everything today.”
  • Seek-counsel cards: “I will not make this decision alone. I will seek appropriate guidance from ______.”

Read each card to God, aloud if you have privacy. Then place the Entrust cards face down somewhere set apart: under your Bible, in front of the cross if you brought one, or in an envelope marked with today’s date. The location has no spiritual power. It is simply a physical way of marking the decision you have made in prayer. You are not destroying these; you may want to look at them again on Day 3 or after the retreat.

Keep the Share, Act, and Seek-counsel cards together. They go back into your ordinary life with you, and Session 10 will help you turn them into a plan.

If any card, at any point, brings up more than you can hold, stop, set it aside, and use the pause guidance from Lesson 5. That card can wait for Session 4, Session 6, or a conversation with the right person after the retreat.

6Closing declaration

Read only what you can say honestly. Adjust the words if you need to.

I have come to Christ, who gives rest.

I release my demand to control what is beyond me.

I will seek help with what should be shared.

I will take the next faithful step in what belongs to me.

Christ is gentle and humble in heart, and I am learning his way.

On a shorter plan

One-day retreat. Watch the teaching; make the list; the guided prayer; silence of ten minutes or less; in the journal, the sorting and question 1 only (the items marked E); then the handing-over exercise. About an hour, possibly more: this is the day’s heaviest session; do not rush it, and do not shorten the practical exercise simply to keep the clock.

Three-hour reset. Read Matthew 11:28–30, make the list of what you are carrying, and mark each item A, S, E or ?. Then write one Entrust card and one Share card. Skip the teaching and the full exercise. About 40 minutes.

Jesus says, “Come to me.” His yoke is still a yoke, but he is gentle. Act on what is yours, share what is too heavy, and entrust to him what you cannot control.

At-home retreat. This is your Day 3. Put the Entrust cards in a place the household will not disturb.

Pastors and leaders. Pay particular attention to the Share and Seek-counsel marks. Ministers habitually mark everything Act. The structural answer God gave Moses in Exodus 18 (Lesson 4) was shared load, and this exercise is where you begin to admit which loads should have been shared years ago.

You are following the whole session, as above. Nothing is shortened.

A short note

Release is not silence about harm

Entrusting an outcome to God does not mean withdrawing a report, abandoning a safety plan, cancelling a boundary, concealing wrongdoing, or relieving an offender of accountability. If you are not safe now, or someone else is at risk, act on that today: contact the appropriate service. You do not need to wait for another session.

If a card or memory brings panic, flashbacks, a feeling of unreality, or severe distress: stop, set the card aside, and ground yourself, as in Lesson 5. Immediate danger, chest pain, fainting or severe breathlessness: call your local emergency number. Thoughts of harming yourself, or not being able to stay safe: call your local emergency number or a crisis line now. Finding Help Where You Live

If a session brings up more than you can hold, stop the session. The sessions are offered, not assigned.

After this session

Before Session 4: this is important. Session 4 is about lament and grief, and it should not follow directly on this one. Take a substantial break, preferably an hour or more: rest, gentle movement, or another accessible change of position, then lunch, slowly. Follow your planned food, medication, rest, and movement needs. Begin Session 4 only when you feel sufficiently settled. If you cannot take a substantial break, defer Session 4 rather than compressing the schedule.

One-day retreat. Take the long break, about an hour and a quarter: movement, rest, a psalm. Then Session 7. If this session leaves you needing more rest than the schedule allows, leave out Session 7 and go from the long break to Session 10. A one-day retreat of four sessions is still a retreat.

Three-hour reset. Take a ten-minute break, then continue with the Session 10 elements.

At-home retreat. This is the end of today’s retreat time. Session 4 is your next retreat day; choose a time when you will not need to be composed for others immediately afterwards.
