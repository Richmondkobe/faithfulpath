Point 1

Why Silence Unsettles Us

When the noise drops, we notice what was already there.

Sound can also help us concentrate, feel safe, or manage a health condition.

boredommy own thoughtswhat I will feel if I stop performingsensing nothingsomething surfacing

After trauma, stillness itself can bring the body to alarm.

None of these fears is foolish. They deserve care, not shame.

Is silence helpful and safe for you now? Begin gently, pray another way, or seek support first.

One clarification

If you hear a voice that others cannot hear

especially one giving commands, threatening you, or making it hard to tell inside from outside:

Do not assume it is God.

End the retreat, and seek prompt professional assessment.

Point 2

Silence Is Attention, Not Emptiness

Not emptying your mind

Becoming present to God, who is already present to you

a line of Scripturea phrase of prayera questionyour intention (Lesson 7)

Not soundlessness

Birds, traffic, household noise, a gentle background sound

With tinnitus, a fan or low neutral sound may help.

When thoughts come

Notice them, and return.

A thought that keeps returning? Write it down if that helps.

Repetition is not proof that God is emphasising it.

Unwanted thoughts are not confessions, intentions, prophecies, or evidence of your character.

Persistent religious fears? A professional who understands scrupulosity can help.

Not a battle against your mind: a gentle, repeated returning

Three forms of silence

Stillsitting with Scripture or a prayer phrase
Movingslow walking, gentle movement
Supportedeyes open, neutral sound, someone nearby

None is more spiritual than the others.

Adaptation is not avoidance.

Point 3

Start Small, Use an Anchor

1–5 min
a little more
stay as long as you need

You do not have to begin with thirty minutes.

Ten attentive minutes can be a complete practice.

God is not measuring your devotion with a stopwatch.

Use a timer. A plan, not a contract.

Psalm 46:10 first addressed nations in conflict.

You do not hold the world together.

Breathing, only if it helps. Or:

feet on the floorthe chair holding youa textured objectname what you see

Eyes open is fine.

No spiritually superior posture. Falling asleep may be the rest you need.

Discomfort or distress?

Discomfort

restlessness · boredom · wandering attention · an urge to check something · mild sadness

Notice, and return gently to your anchor.

Never required to stay with anything unsafe, unfamiliar or quickly getting stronger

Distress

panic · a racing heart that does not settle · back inside a memory · unreality · fear out of all proportion

Stop. Open your eyes, stand, move. Name where you are, the date, and what you see, hear and touch.

  • Immediate danger, chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness: emergency medical help
  • Thoughts of harming yourself: emergency services or a crisis line, now
  • Distress without immediate danger: a professional or crisis support

Silence is offered, not assigned.

Point 4

If You Sense Nothing

The quietest fear: nothing will happen. It may not.

A strong sense of God’s presence
Faithfulness without feeling

Both are real retreats.

You have not failed, and you have not been abandoned.

The waiting itself is prayer.

Sometimes significance is recognised only later.

A retreat is not judged by what you felt on day two.
