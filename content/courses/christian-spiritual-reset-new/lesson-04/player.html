Point 1

Burnout: Demands Outrun Recovery

Long demands that keep exceeding your resources, support and opportunity to recover

Here used in a pastoral sense: not a clinical diagnosis

dreading what used to be meaningfulnothing left to givecynicism or numbnessirritability or withdrawalgoing through the motions

New or lasting physical symptoms deserve a doctor’s attention.

You may need a shared load, not only rest.

Three days of rest cannot change a pattern by themselves. Lessons 13 and 14 will help.

Point 2

Dryness: When God Seems Absent

prayer feels one-wayScripture reads like a documentworship leaves you unmoved

Belief has become a fact rather than a relationship.

Not always the result of sin.

Sometimes there is sin to confess (Psalm 32:3–4).

Often there is nothing to find: “Oh, that I knew where I might find him” (Job 23:3).

The retreat cannot force God to feel close again.

It can give you unhurried space to be honest, and to keep turning towards him.

Point 3

Overload: Too Much to Carry

postponed griefunspoken disappointmentburied angerbackground fear

Overloaded people often look fine.

“…he had compassion for them, because they were harassed and helpless…” (Matthew 9:36)

“Cast your burden on the Lord, and he will sustain you…” (Psalm 55:22)

“casting all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you.” (1 Peter 5:7)

You no longer have to carry it as though you were alone.

The middle sessions give gentle room; nothing has to be resolved in three days.

Point 4

The Whole Person

A medical condition can make prayer difficult. Grief can disturb sleep. Overwork can make God feel distant.

Not every troubling symptom is evidence of sin.

Wise care asks about the whole person: not purely medical, not purely spiritual.

Recognising all three does not mean you are beyond help.

Your retreat needs to be gentle and unhurried. Lesson 6 will help you choose.

When to ask for more help

You do not need to wait until things become severe before you ask for help.

A retreat is not a substitute for that care.

Practise · Your step

A personal check-in: mark the signs from the past month.

Private. Not a test, not a diagnosis.

  1. Which experiences seem most relevant to you right now? You may recognise more than one.
  2. How long has this been going on?
  3. Can you connect it to a specific event, or did it build gradually?
  4. Did the note about asking for more help describe you? If so, who will you contact?

Keep your answers for Session 1.

Naming it before God

Naming is not solving. It is the beginning of honesty.

You will not have to explain to God what you have not yet admitted to yourself.

He already knows.

Where you seem far away, keep me turning towards you.

Give me courage to ask for help when I need it.
