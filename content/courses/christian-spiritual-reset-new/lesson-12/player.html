Read the Scripture · Acts 17:11

From Thessalonica to Berea

“Now these Jews were more noble than those in Thessalonica; they received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so.”Acts 17:11 · ESV

Understand the original meaning

Test · Greek

dokimazō

δοκιμάζω

to test, to examine, and so to recognise what is genuine

Also used of testing metals: gold “tested by fire”

1 Peter 1:7 · the fire shows what is real

“Test everything” (5:21): a plural command, to the church as a community

Not our private judgement alone: Scripture, prayer, and the wisdom of mature believers

Explore the background

A young church, only months old, told two things at once:

Do not despise propheciesDo not dismiss what may be from God.
Test everythingDo not accept it untested.

He holds both together, and so should we.

Christians differ on how the Holy Spirit guides believers today, including prophecy and personal impressions.

Scripture calls us to examine spiritual claims carefully, hold fast to what is good, and reject what is contrary to God’s Word.

The Bereans

“More noble”

Not sceptical. Not credulous.

Eagerreceived with all eagerness
Carefulexamined the Scriptures daily

Both at once.

Neither passage is about private impressions on a retreat.

Receive spiritual claims openly. Examine them carefully.

If the Bereans examined Paul, you may examine a card written on the third morning, when you were tired.

Understand the teaching

The short answer

You test it, slowly, and not alone.

Point 1

Testing Is Faith, Not Doubt

If God spoke, why examine it?

Because God speaks, and because we are fallible receivers.

Testing honours God: it refuses to attach his name to

our own fearour own desireour own exhaustion

You were tired. Test it.

The safety exception

Not for waiting

Immediate danger · abuse · urgent medical care · legal reporting duties · deadlines that cannot safely be postponed

Seek qualified help, and take the safest necessary action now.

Do not remain in danger to complete a discernment process.

Point 2

Sort What You Have

1 · Nothing significantNothing to test. Keep it in your journal; go on to Lesson 13.
2 · Low-risk and reversiblea counselling appointment · a conversation · two hours of rest · an apology written, not sent

Lesson 11: this is a complete outcome.

Test them lightly, and then do them.

Category three

Major, high-impact, costly or difficult to reverse

leaving a job, a church or a countrystarting a ministrya large financial commitmenta confrontation or disclosure

Full testing, over weeks, with other people. No action until it is done.

Where abuse or danger is involved, leaving or seeking protection is a safety matter, not a decision that must wait for weeks of testing.

Explore a step before you execute it.

Be glad you wrote it down instead of acting on it.

Point 3

The Nine Tests

Over the coming days, in your journal, one status for each test:

supportsraises concernnot yet examinedrequires qualified advice

1 Does it agree with Scripture, read in context?

Not passed by finding a verse that matches.

If it contradicts clear biblical teaching, it fails here.

2 Does it reflect the character and fruit of Christ?

“But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control; against such things there is no law.”Galatians 5:22–23 · ESV

Harder, more secretive, more self-important, less accountable: concern.

3 Is it truthful about facts, not merely feelings?

Write the facts down. What has actually been said and done?

Then check them with people who know.

The nine tests · 4 to 6

4 Does it withstand time and careful questioning?

For major decisions, allowing several weeks or longer for careful examination can be wise, where circumstances permit.

What would I say to a friend? What are the three strongest reasons not to do it?

5 What do mature and appropriately qualified advisers say?

Health, law, money, employment or abuse: professionally qualified, not only spiritually mature.

6 Does it respect safety, consent, justice and accountability?

Those affected should ordinarily have a voice.

An unsafe or controlling person does not receive a veto over your safety.

The nine tests · 7 to 9

7 Does it require secrecy, superiority, recklessness or immunity from challenge?

“they would not understand”“God has shown me”“between me and God”“before anyone can talk me out of it”

Genuine direction from God can bear examination.

8 Is it proportionate and reversible?

Reduce hours before resigning
Have the conversation before leaving

9 Are you rested, regulated, informed and supported enough to decide?

In the dip, or exhausted? A major decision can usually wait.

Urgent safety decisions cannot wait. Get help to make them.

When the tests disagree

Not yetThe evidence is incomplete. Keep testing; revisit in a month.
Let it goIt clearly contradicts Scripture or requires wrongdoing.
A small stepIt has passed the tests, with wise people, and still holds.

Directions genuinely from God usually survive a long wait.

That is not a loss. It is the testing doing what it was for.

Not infallible certainty: a responsible, proportionate next step (Lesson 13).

Point 4

Consult Your Eli

Samuel ran to Eli (Session 7).

Mature · trustworthy · knows Scripture · respects boundaries · no interest in controlling your decision

Contact them this week, and ask for an hour.

Bring the card, the tests done so far, and the facts gathered.

You do not need to finish all nine tests first.

“Help me test it,” not “confirm it.”

Three questions

What in this do you recognise as wise?

What concerns you?

What have I not considered?

Then listen without defending.

More than one voice

a pastor: the spiritual reasoninga doctor · a lawyer · a counsellor · a financial adviser: the facts

Not an Eli who will simply agree with you.

Two wise people, the same concern: weigh it heavily.

A spouse’s serious reservations about a family decision are part of the test.

In an abusive relationship, consult a domestic-abuse specialist privately.

A note on fleeces and signs · Judges 6:36–40

Gideon’s fleece is recorded, not commended.

He already had God’s word, and was asking for reassurance.

Asking God for wisdom is right (James 1:5). Do not make a sign the deciding test.
