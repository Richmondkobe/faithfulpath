1 · How do I know whether it was God?

Welcome home. This is the first lesson of Part 4, meant for your first morning back. The house is loud, or the inbox is, and the quiet of the retreat may already feel far away. That is normal. Session 10 warned you about it.

This lesson is about one card in particular: the card from Session 7 marked "What I sense", and any cards marked "To test" from Session 9. Session 7 asked you to write down what you sensed, and to test it before acting. This lesson is how.

So it asks: how do I know whether it was God?

2 · Your first morning

Before we begin, here is the shape of this morning. Do not rush into major decisions today. The full testing may take days or weeks, and you do not need to work through the nine tests this morning. Your whole assignment for this first morning is five things.

Sort each card into one of three categories. Take no major action today. Do any immediately necessary safety action. Schedule a conversation with the appropriate adviser. And put dates on the worksheet for beginning and reviewing the test. Then let the rest of the day be ordinary.

3 · Test everything

Our first passage comes from the end of Paul’s first letter to the church in Thessalonica. The Scripture readings in this course are from the English Standard Version. 1 Thessalonians chapter 5, verses 19 to 22:

"Do not quench the Spirit. Do not despise prophecies, but test everything; hold fast what is good. Abstain from every form of evil."

4 · Examining the Scriptures daily

And our second passage comes from the same journey. After Paul preached in Thessalonica, he moved on to the town of Berea. Acts chapter 17, verse 11:

"Now these Jews were more noble than those in Thessalonica; they received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so."

5 · Test = dokimazō

Our Greek word in this lesson comes from verse 21: "test everything". The word translated "test" is dokimazō. It means to test, to examine, and so to recognise what is genuine. It could also be used of testing metals: Peter uses the same verb when he speaks of gold that is tested by fire. The fire shows what is real.

And notice that in verse 21 it is a plural command. Paul gives it to the church as a community. That reminds us that spiritual discernment should not depend entirely on our private judgement. We benefit from Scripture, prayer, and the wisdom of mature believers. Hold on to that. It will matter when we come to your Eli.

6 · Do not despise, do not swallow whole

Now the background. The church in Thessalonica was young, only months old. Paul tells it two things at once. Do not despise prophecies: do not dismiss what may be from God. And test everything: do not accept it untested. He holds both together, and so should we.

Christians differ on how the Holy Spirit guides believers today, including the place of prophecy and personal impressions. This course does not settle that question. But Scripture calls all of us to examine spiritual claims carefully, to hold fast to what is good, and to reject what is contrary to God’s Word.

7 · The Bereans

Luke calls the Bereans more noble. Not because they were sceptical, or credulous. They received Paul’s teaching with all eagerness, and they examined the Scriptures daily to see whether what he said was so. Eager, and careful. Both at once.

Neither passage is specifically about private impressions on a retreat. But together they give us a clear Christian posture: receive spiritual claims openly, and examine them carefully. If the Bereans examined Paul, you may certainly examine a sentence you wrote on an index card on the third morning of a retreat, when you were tired.

8 · The short answer

So, how do I know whether it was God? Here is the short answer. You test it, slowly, and not alone.

That gives us four points: testing is faith, not doubt; sort what you have; the nine tests; and consult your Eli.

9 · Point 1: Testing Is Faith, Not Doubt

The first point: testing is faith, not doubt. If God spoke, why examine it? The answer is that Scripture commands examination precisely because God speaks, and because we are fallible receivers.

Testing does not insult God. It honours him, by refusing to attach his name to something that may be our own fear, our own desire, or our own exhaustion. Session 7 said that any of us can mistake strong emotion for divine direction, especially when tired. You were tired. Test it.

10 · The safety exception

One exception belongs here, before anything else. Everything in this lesson about waiting, consulting, and taking time applies to ordinary discernment. It does not apply to immediate danger, abuse, urgent medical care, legal reporting duties, or deadlines that cannot safely be postponed. In those situations, seek qualified help, and take the safest necessary action now. Do not remain in danger merely to complete a discernment process. Lesson 5’s guidance on abuse and crisis comes before anything else in this lesson.

11 · Point 2: Sort What You Have

The second point: sort what you have. Take out the "What I sense" card, and any "To test" cards, and sort them into three kinds.

First, nothing significant. If the card says "nothing", or records only a general sense of peace, or a familiar verse without any particular direction attached, there is nothing to test. Keep it in your journal, and move on to Lesson 13. Many of you will be here, and Lesson 11 already said that this is a complete outcome.

Second, a low-risk, reversible step. Arranging a first appointment with a counsellor. Asking a trusted person for a conversation. Protecting two hours for rest this week. Writing an apology without sending it yet. These are supported by Scripture and ordinary wisdom, easily undone if mistaken, and harmful to no one. Test them lightly, and then do them.

12 · Category three

And third, a major, high-impact, costly, or difficult-to-reverse direction. Leaving a job, a church, or a country. Starting a ministry. A large financial commitment. A confrontation or disclosure that will change relationships. Anything that affects other people substantially, or cannot easily be undone.

These get the full testing, over weeks, with other people, and they are not acted on until the testing is done, subject always to the safety exception. Where abuse or danger is involved, leaving or seeking protection is a safety matter, not a decision that must wait for weeks of spiritual testing. Notice that the low-risk steps explore a step rather than execute it. That distinction is worth keeping.

Most of what people bring home is in the second category. If yours is in the third, be glad you wrote it down instead of acting on it.

13 · Point 3: The Nine Tests (1 to 3)

The third point: the nine tests. Session 7 listed them. Here is how to actually use them. Over the coming days, work through them in your journal, giving each a status: supports, raises concern, not yet examined, or requires qualified advice.

Test one: does it agree with Scripture, read in context? This is the first and weightiest test, and it is not passed by finding a verse that matches. Ask what the whole of Scripture teaches about the kind of thing you are considering, and if you are not sure, find out, with help. An impression that contradicts clear biblical teaching fails here, and nothing else needs to be checked.

Test two: does it reflect the character and fruit of Christ? Would acting on it reflect the Jesus of the Gospels? Hold it against Paul’s list:

"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control; against such things there is no law."

Christlike love is not self-erasure or submission to abuse. But directions that make you harder, more secretive, more self-important, or less accountable raise concern, however spiritual they sound.

Test three: is it truthful about facts, not merely feelings? Write down the facts the direction depends on. If it is "leave this church because they do not value me", the facts are what has actually been said and done, not how it felt. Then check them with people who know.

14 · The Nine Tests (4 to 6)

Test four: does it withstand time and careful questioning? Scripture does not set a fixed period. My pastoral advice, where circumstances permit, is to allow about a month for a major decision, and often longer. Notice whether it strengthens, weakens, or changes. And question it yourself. What would I say to a friend who brought me this? What are the three strongest reasons not to do it? If you cannot think of any, you are not yet questioning it carefully.

Test five: what do mature and appropriately qualified advisers say? This test cannot be done alone. For matters involving health, law, money, employment, or abuse, "qualified" means professionally qualified, not simply spiritually mature.

Test six: does it respect safety, consent, justice, and accountability? Who is affected? Does it require anyone to be harmed, deceived, pressured, or kept in the dark? Does it leave you accountable, or place you beyond correction? When a decision rightly belongs to more than one person, those affected should ordinarily have a voice before it is made. But an unsafe or controlling person does not receive a veto over your safety, and no one must be warned before a confidential safeguarding or legal report.

15 · The Nine Tests (7 to 9)

Test seven: does it require secrecy, superiority, recklessness, or immunity from challenge? If you find yourself thinking "they would not understand", or "God has shown me and that is enough", or "this is between me and God", those are warning signs, not confirmations. So is urgency that exists mainly to prevent examination: "I have to act before anyone can talk me out of it." Genuine direction from God can bear examination. It does not need protecting from wise people.

Test eight: is the proposed action proportionate and reversible? If there is a smaller, reversible version of the same step, take that first. Reduce your hours before resigning. Have the conversation before leaving.

And test nine: are you sufficiently rested, regulated, informed, and supported to make this decision? Strong emotion does not invalidate a decision, but it may mean you need more time or support first. If you are in the dip after the retreat, or exhausted, a major decision can usually wait. Urgent safety decisions cannot wait, and should not be postponed because you are distressed. Get help to make them.

16 · When the tests disagree

Sometimes the tests point in different directions: Scripture permits it, but your adviser has concerns; or the facts check out, but you are not rested enough to decide. When the evidence is incomplete like this, the direction is not ready, and that is the answer for now. Not "no", but "not yet". Keep testing. Revisit in a month. Directions that are genuinely from God usually survive a long wait.

But "not yet" is only for incomplete evidence. If a direction clearly contradicts Scripture, or requires wrongdoing, it is not waiting for more time. Let it go. That is not a loss. It is the testing doing what it was for.

And if, after weeks of testing, a direction has passed every test, has been weighed by wise people who know you, and still holds, it may be time to act, with the proportionate step first. Passing the tests does not give infallible certainty or guarantee an outcome. It means the direction has become responsible enough to take a proportionate next step. Lesson 13 is about that.

17 · Point 4: Consult Your Eli

The fourth point: consult your Eli. In Session 7, Samuel ran to Eli, and Eli helped him recognise what was happening. Session 7 asked you to name your own Eli: a mature, trustworthy Christian who knows Scripture, respects boundaries, and has no interest in controlling your decision. Contact them this week, and ask for an hour.

Bring your card, any tests you have completed so far, and the facts you have gathered. You do not need to finish all nine tests before speaking with your Eli; your adviser can help you work through them. Tell them what you sensed, and tell them you are not asking them to confirm it; you are asking them to help you test it. Then ask three questions. What in this do you recognise as wise? What concerns you? What have I not considered? Then listen without defending. Their concerns are the most valuable thing they will give you.

18 · More than one voice

Your Eli does not have to supply every kind of wisdom. A pastor may test the spiritual reasoning, while a doctor, lawyer, counsellor, or financial adviser addresses the facts.

Do not choose an Eli who will simply agree with you, and do not go from adviser to adviser until you find one who does. If two wise people independently raise the same concern, weigh it heavily. And if your spouse, where you have one, has serious reservations about a direction that affects the family, that is not an obstacle to overcome. It is part of the test. That assumes a relationship in which concerns can be expressed without coercion or fear. In an abusive relationship, consult a domestic-abuse specialist privately; joint discernment may not be safe.

19 · A note on fleeces and signs

One more thing. When a matter feels unsettled, it is tempting to ask God for a sign to settle it: a fleece, after Gideon in Judges 6. Be cautious. Gideon’s fleece is recorded, not commended. He already had God’s clear word, and he was asking for reassurance, and the narrative presents him as hesitant rather than exemplary.

That does not make every request for confirmation wrong; Scripture invites us to ask God for wisdom. The caution is about making a sign the deciding test. Signs are easy to manufacture and easy to misread. If you want confirmation, seek it through the ordinary means Scripture commends: Scripture itself, truthful examination, wise counsel, time, prayer, and the fruit produced, rather than trying to decode coincidences.

20 · Your step

Here is your step, which is also below this lesson and on the worksheet. Sort your cards into the three categories. Take no major action today, unless the safety exception applies, and then act with help. For each card you are testing, start the worksheet: write what you sensed, its category, and a date for beginning and a date for review. You do not need to complete the nine tests this morning; they come over the following days and weeks. And schedule the conversation with your Eli.

One more question for your journal: is there anything you are reluctant to test? Write down why. Reluctance to test is itself something to test.

Then let the rest of the day be ordinary. Lesson 13 comes later in your first week home. If you want to see the shape of the month, the page Your First 30 Days Home is linked below.

21 · Remember

Let us bring it together. Testing is faith, not doubt. Sort what you have: most of it needs only light testing. Apply the nine tests slowly to anything major; when the evidence is incomplete, "not yet" is an answer. And ask your Eli not to confirm what you sensed, but to help you test it.

22 · Prayer

Let us pray. Lord, I came home with what I sensed. Thank you that I do not have to carry it alone, or act on it today. Give me eagerness and care, like the Bereans. Show me what is good, and help me hold it fast. What is not from you, help me let go. Amen.

When you are ready, sort your cards, put a date on the worksheet, and send one message to your Eli. That is enough for this morning.
