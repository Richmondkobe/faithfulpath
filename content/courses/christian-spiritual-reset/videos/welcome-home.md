You are home.

The house is loud, or the inbox is, or the children are. The quiet of the retreat may already feel like something that happened to someone else.

That is normal. I told you on Day 3 that returning might feel this way.

I want to say three things to you this morning.

First, make no major or irreversible decisions today—not about your job, your church, your marriage, your finances, or anything else that significantly affects you or other people.

Whatever you sensed during the retreat was written down for a reason. Today, sort your cards. Read Lesson 24. Give what you heard time to be tested.

There is one exception: safety.

If the retreat helped you recognise that you are in danger, that someone else may be in danger, or that you need urgent medical, professional, legal, or safeguarding help, act on that now. Safety does not wait for an ordinary discernment process.

Second, allow for a dip.

You may feel flat, tired, unsettled, or strangely sad during your first days home. That does not automatically mean the retreat failed. You have moved from protected quiet back into ordinary demands, and the transition may take time.

Keep the small rhythm you chose on Day 3, imperfectly. Tell your support person that you are home. Have one honest conversation—not a complete report.

If the dip becomes severe, continues to deepen, prevents you from functioning, or affects your safety, do not simply wait for it to pass. Lesson 27 will help you identify the right support.

Third, remember that the retreat was a beginning, not a verdict.

If you experienced a strong sense of God's presence, do not spend this week trying to reproduce the feeling.

If you felt very little, do not conclude that nothing happened or that God was absent. Let the retreat remain what it was without forcing an interpretation.

The next thirty days have a gentle shape. Lesson 28 will guide you through a week of landing, a week of doing, a week of shaping a rule of life, and a week of settling. You will not have to decide every morning what to do next.

Elijah came down from the mountain with work to do and a companion with whom to do it. He did not come down with everything resolved.

Neither will you.

That is not failure. That is the pattern.

So, this morning, sort your cards. If you have something significant to test, schedule a conversation with your mature, trustworthy adviser—the person we have called your Eli.

Then allow the rest of the day to be ordinary.

You do not have to carry the whole retreat home in one morning.

Welcome home.
