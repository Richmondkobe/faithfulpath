This is the week when your rule of life takes shape.

Do not write the rule you wish you had the capacity to keep. Write the one you can sustain during a difficult, ordinary week.

Begin with three core commitments: a regular period of ceasing and rest; a realistic practice of prayer and Scripture; and one boundary that protects your health or calling.

Add no more than two optional commitments.

Keep the whole rule on one page. Add the dates for your two-week check and three-month review. Then share it with your Eli or support person.

A rule that nobody else knows about is easy to abandon quietly.

If your commitments affect a spouse, family, or household, involve them where that is safe and appropriate. A rhythm imposed on other people without conversation will eventually collide with their lives.

If the retreat revealed a structural change that someone else has the authority to make, this is the week to make one clear request.

One request—not a complete redesign.

If the answer is no, ask why and whether another form of relief is possible. A refusal is important information about whether the present arrangement is sustainable.

Return to Lesson 27 this week, even if you did not initially think it applied. Sometimes a need becomes clearer only after you have been home for a while.

Psalm 27 is your psalm for this week:

"One thing have I asked of the LORD."

Write the rule you can actually keep.
