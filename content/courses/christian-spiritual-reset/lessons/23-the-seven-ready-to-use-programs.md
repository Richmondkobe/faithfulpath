# The Seven Ready-to-Use Programs

## How to use these programs

Module 3 gave you the ten sessions. This page arranges them into schedules you can follow without planning. Each program lists which sessions to do, in what order, with rest, meals, and movement built in, and each is written so that you can copy it into your journal or print the page and take it with you.

A few things apply to every program.

The times are suggestions. They show a realistic shape for the day, not a timetable to keep to the minute. Shift everything to suit when you wake, when you eat, and when your medication is due. What matters is the order of the sessions and the rest between them.

The one-day and three-hour formats shorten selected sessions deliberately; do not compress them further. If a program says "rest" or "movement" between two sessions, that is not filler. The breaks reduce emotional and physical strain, create space for recovery, and help you notice whether continuing is appropriate. They do not replace the safeguards in Lesson 5 or professional support where needed. Do not close the gaps to fit in more.

Every session remains adjustable. The silence lengths, the journaling, and the exercises are all subject to what Lesson 12 said: shorten, adapt, or stop as you need. A program is a container, not a contract.

The safety guidance holds throughout. Lesson 5's four paths, the pause rule, the emergency arrangements from Lesson 9, and the food and medication plan from Lesson 11 apply whichever program you choose.

Sessions Six, Eight, and Nine are not required parts of the core three-day program. Session Six is reserved for a supported or extended format. Sessions Eight and Nine are optional additions only when capacity and time genuinely permit. The standard route contains seven sessions. If Session Four or Five needs to be deferred for safety or capacity, the retreat remains valid and complete as a three-day period of prayer and rest. Deferred sessions are optional material for later support, not missing requirements.

## Program 1: The Three-Day Christian Renewal Retreat

The recommended format. Up to seven core sessions across three days.

| Day | Core sessions | Optional |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Sessions 1 and 2 | None |
| Day 2 | Sessions 3 and 4 | Session 5, only if fully settled |
| Day 3, Route A (Session 5 completed on Day 2) | Sessions 7 and 10 | Session 8 or 9, not both |
| Day 3, Route B (Session 5 still needed) | Sessions 5, 7, and 10 | No flex sessions |
| After the retreat | 30-day plan (Module 5) | Sessions 6, 8, or 9 as appropriate |

Best for: anyone who can take three consecutive days away from ordinary work. Friday to Sunday is the most common shape; three weekdays work equally well, and some readers prefer them.

Before you arrive: complete Module 2. In particular, have your intention (Lesson 7), your phone arrangement tested (Lesson 9), your bag packed (Lesson 10), and your food and medication plan settled (Lesson 11). Do the Lesson 8 practice for the week before if you can.

Arrival: if you can, arrive the evening before Day 1. Unpack, eat, set up the room, and sleep. Do no sessions. This is optional and may require an additional night's accommodation; if that is not affordable or practical, arrive on the morning of Day 1 and use the first hour, or longer, to settle before Session One.

If you sleep through the afternoon, do Session Two in the early evening instead and keep the evening short. Session Two provides helpful preparation for Day 2. If you cannot complete it, use its shortened silence and brief noticing exercise, defer Day 2, or continue only if you feel sufficiently settled. Safety and capacity take priority over sequence.

|---|---|---|

If Session Four brought up more than you could hold, stop there for the day. Do not attempt Session Five. Contact your support person if you need to, follow Lesson 5's guidance, and sleep. Day 3 will still hold, with Session Five in the morning or deferred to the 30-day plan.

If you chose to skip Session Four because your loss is very recent or overwhelming, as Session Four permits, use the afternoon for rest and Scripture. Skipping Session Four does not automatically mean you should replace it with Session Five. Decide independently whether confession is appropriate. Rest may remain the wisest use of the afternoon and evening.

### Day 3: Renew and Return

Choose your route by whether Session Five was completed on Day 2.

### Route B: Session Five is completed on Day 3

| Time | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wake | Wake, breakfast, quiet | A gentler morning. |
| Morning | Session Five: Confession and Grace | 60–90 minutes. |
| Late morning | Long break | Rest or gentle movement. An hour at least. |
| Midday | Session Seven: Listening for God's Direction | 60–90 minutes. Session Five followed by Session Seven already makes a substantial morning. Treat that as sufficient. |

Timing Session Ten: choose its start by working backwards from checkout and safe travel. If there is not enough time to complete it without rushing, begin earlier or stay until the following morning. If possible, complete Session Ten before the final night and depart after sleep.

Departure: depart according to your booking and travel plan, but do not drive or travel alone if you are too tired, distressed, or disoriented to do so safely. Tell your support person your plan and confirm when you arrive.

Session Six is not scheduled. If forgiveness is the thing you most need to address, the at-home format gives it a day of its own, and Module 5's 30-day plan gives it a place after you return. It is not omitted because it is unimportant; it is omitted because it should not be squeezed between other heavy work.

### The whole retreat on one page

Copy this into the front of your journal.

- Day 1: Session One → lunch → rest → Session Two → free evening
- Day 2: Session Three → long break → Session Four → long break → stop (Session Five this evening only if fully settled) → sleep
- Day 3, if Session Five is still needed: Session Five → long break → Session Seven → long rest → Session Ten
- Day 3, if Session Five is complete: Session Seven → lunch and rest → optional Session Eight or Nine → long break → Session Ten
- After the retreat: Session Six and any unused flex sessions remain available in the 30-day plan.

Three days. A clear path. Enough space to rest.

## Program 2: The One-Day Retreat With God

Five sessions across an unhurried day, approximately eight to nine hours including meals, rest, and movement.

Sessions used: 1, 2, 3, 7, 10, each in a shortened form.

Not used: 4, 5, 6, 8, 9. Lament, confession, and forgiveness are not included because they should not be compressed into a single day alongside four other sessions. They remain available in the 30-day plan, and the one-day retreat is complete without them.

Best for: someone who cannot get away overnight but can protect one full day. Lesson 6 said this is the best alternative to three days, and it is. It should not feel like a nine-hour seminar; roughly a third of the day is meals and rest.

Before you begin: complete Module 2 as far as you can. The intention (Lesson 7), the phone arrangement (Lesson 9), and the food plan (Lesson 11) matter most. If you are doing this at home, follow the home-alone or home-with-household guidance in Lesson 6.

Shortening the sessions: in each session, read the central passage and the teaching, do the self-assessment and guided prayer, keep the silence at ten minutes or less, answer the two journaling questions named below, and do the practical exercise in full. Most shortened sessions will take 45 to 60 minutes;

Session Three may take longer. Do not shorten a practical exercise simply to keep the clock.

- Session One: journaling questions 1 and 4
- Session Two: journaling questions 1 and 3
- Session Three: journaling questions 1 and 2 (the sorting), then the exercise
- Session Seven: journaling questions 1 and 3
- Session Ten: self-assessment question 1 and journaling question 2

The clock times below assume an 8:30 start. Shift them all to suit your morning.

| Time | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 8:30 | Breakfast, quiet, settle | Follow your medication and meal schedule. Set up the room, phone away, intention page and cards out. Do not start early to fit more in. |
| 9:15 | Session One: Come As You Are (shortened) | About 45 minutes. Seal the envelope. |
| 10:00 | Break | Twenty minutes. A drink, a stretch, outside if possible. |
| 10:20 | Session Two: Be Still and Become Present (shortened) | Keep the full noticing exercise; it is the heart of the session. About an hour. |
| 11:20 | Lunch and rest | An hour and a quarter. Eat slowly. Lie down if you want to. |
| 12:35 | Session Three: Release What You Are Carrying (shortened) | About an hour, possibly more. Produces the Act, Share, Entrust, and Seek-counsel cards. This is the day's heaviest session; do not rush it. |
| 13:45 | Long break | An hour and a quarter. Movement, rest, a psalm. |
| 15:00 | Session Seven: Listening for God's Direction (shortened) | About 45 minutes. Silence at ten minutes. Produces the "What I sense" card. |
| 15:45 | Break | Twenty minutes. Eat something. |
| 16:05 | Session Ten: Returning With a New Rhythm (shortened) | About an hour. Open the envelope. Choose the four cards. Read the blessing. |
| 17:05 | Simple meal, rest | The retreat is over. Add nothing. |

If Session Three leaves you needing more rest than the schedule allows, drop Session Seven and go from the long break to Session Ten. A one-day retreat of four sessions is still a retreat. Session Seven will be waiting.

One-day retreat on one page: Session One → break → Session Two → lunch and rest → Session Three → long break → Session Seven → break → Session Ten → blessing.

## Program 3: The Three-Hour Spiritual Reset

A compressed pause for someone who needs to stop now and cannot wait for a day to open up.

Sessions used: brief elements of 1, 2, 3, and 10.

What this is: an immediate spiritual pause, not the full retreat. Lesson 6 was clear that it is not crisis care and not a substitute for professional help. If you are in distress that needs more than a pause, Lesson 5 applies before this program does.

Best for: a Saturday morning, a weekday afternoon of leave, a quiet three hours when the household is out. It can be repeated monthly as part of the rhythm you build in Module 5, and many readers will use it that way after a full retreat.

Before you begin: phone away or on the Lesson 9 arrangement, a Bible, a journal, a timer, a drink. Tell the people who might otherwise interrupt you that you will be unavailable for the full three hours, and explain how a genuine emergency should reach you. Allow half an hour of transition afterwards, so the practical commitment is three and a half hours. Write a one-line intention before you start, even if you have not done Lesson 7 in full.

The card marks used below, from Session Three: A (Act: a next faithful action within my responsibility), S (Share: a burden needing support), E (Entrust: an outcome I cannot control), ? (Seek counsel: responsibility unclear or serious).

| Time | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Settle | Sit. Drink. Read Psalm 62 slowly. Write your one-line intention. |
| 0:15–0:40 | From Session One: read Luke 15:11–24, the guided prayer, and the "setting things down" exercise | Write the ordinary demands on cards and place them in a temporary envelope marked "Not for these three hours." Skip the teaching and journaling. |
| 0:40–0:50 | Break | Stand, stretch, refill your drink. |
| 0:50–1:30 | From Session Two: read Psalm 46, ten minutes of silence with "Be still," then the noticing exercise | Twenty minutes for the exercise, outdoors if possible and safe, or at a window. |
| 1:30–1:45 | Break | Eat something small. |
| 1:45–2:25 | From Session Three: read Matthew 11:28–30, make the list of what you are carrying, mark each item A, S, E, or ? | Write one Entrust card and one Share card. Skip the full exercise. |
| 2:25–2:35 | Break |  |
| 2:35–3:00 | From Session Ten: open the envelope, sort the cards, choose one thing to entrust, one action, one person, and one rhythm; read the blessing | Twenty-five minutes. Do not rush the blessing. |

What to leave out: everything from Sessions Four, Five, Six, Seven, Eight, and Nine. Three hours is not enough for lament, confession, or listening, and attempting them in a compressed form does them a disservice. If the reset shows you that you need them, that is useful information: plan a day or three days, or use the 30-day plan.

Afterwards: do not go straight into a demanding task. Give yourself the half-hour, tell your support person you took the reset, and put the four cards where you will see them.

## Program 4: The At-Home Retreat

Seven core sessions across eight days, including one full rest day, for readers who cannot step away from daily responsibilities. An extended twelve-day version includes all ten sessions and two rest days.

Sessions used, core version: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10 across eight days.

Sessions used, extended version: all ten, with two rest days, across twelve days.

Best for: parents of young children, caregivers, shift workers, people in ministry roles that cannot be handed over, and anyone for whom three consecutive days are impossible. Lesson 6 said this format requires more discipline, because ordinary life keeps interrupting, and that is true. It is also the format the later sessions recommended for anyone engaging lament, confession, and forgiveness deeply, because it gives each of those its own day and its own night's sleep.

Before you begin: the at-home guidance in Lesson 6, the at-home digital boundary in Lesson 9, and the household agreement below. Choose your daily session time now: early morning before the household wakes, or evening after it settles, are the usual options. Protect the same time each day if you can.

The household agreement. If you share your home, sit down with the other adults before Day 1 and agree three things: the daily session time and that it will be protected; who handles ordinary needs during that hour; and how genuine emergencies will reach you. Write it down. Ask them to pray for you if that is appropriate. If the other adults cannot or will not protect the time, choose a time when they are absent, or use the one-day format on a day when you can be elsewhere.

The daily shape. Each session takes 45 to 75 minutes. Do it in one sitting if you can. Sessions One, Two, Three, Seven, Eight, Nine, and Ten may be divided, doing the passage, teaching, prayer, and silence in the morning and the journaling and exercise in the evening. If possible, complete Sessions Four, Five, and Six in one sitting; if you must divide one, pause only at a clearly marked point and complete the closing prayer or grounding exercise before returning to ordinary responsibilities. Between sessions, live normally, with the light-touch boundary Lesson 9 described. Keep a psalm from the Lesson 8 list for the days between sessions. Do not do two sessions in one day to catch up.

### Core version: eight days

| Day | Session | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Session One: Come As You Are | Seal the envelope. Tell yourself the retreat has begun; the house will not tell you. |
| Day 2 | Session Two: Be Still and Become Present | The noticing exercise can be a walk to school, the shop, or the garden, done differently. |
| Day 3 | Session Three: Release What You Are Carrying | The cards go in a place the household will not disturb. |
| Day 4 | Session Four: Lament, Grief, and Honest Prayer | Choose a time when you will not need to be composed for others immediately afterwards. Evening may be better than morning. |
| Day 5 | Rest day | No session. A psalm, a walk, an early night. This day exists so that lament and confession are separated by sleep and a full ordinary day. Do not fill it. |
| Day 6 | Session Five: Confession and Grace | Only if settled. If Day 4 is still open in you, make Day 6 another rest day, move Session Five to Day 7, Session Seven to Day 8, and Session Ten to Day 9. |
| Day 7 | Session Seven: Listening for God's Direction | The longest silence; choose the quietest hour the house offers. |
| Day 8 | Session Ten: Returning With a New Rhythm | The return deserves its own morning. Open the envelope, choose the four cards, read the blessing. |

If the retreat is interrupted by illness, a family crisis, or a week that simply overwhelms it, do not abandon it and do not try to catch up. Pause, and resume with the next session when you can, even if it is a week later. Session Three's cards and Session One's envelope will keep. An at-home retreat that takes three weeks instead of one has still been a retreat.

## Program 5: The Retreat for Couples

The three-day retreat taken side by side, with most sessions done separately and a few shared moments.

Sessions used: the standard three-day program (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10), with the additions below.

What this is, and is not. This is two people taking the retreat at the same time, in the same place, supporting each other. It is not a marriage-repair program, and it is not a couples' counselling weekend. Each of you does the personal work of the sessions alone. The shared moments are brief and structured, and their purpose is to let you witness each other's rest rather than to process the relationship. If your marriage needs repair, a retreat may help you both come to that work with more capacity, but the work itself belongs to counselling, and this program will not substitute for it.

Who should not use this program. If there is abuse, coercion, or fear in the relationship, do not take a retreat together. Lesson 5's abuse guidance applies; the person being harmed needs a safety plan and specialist support, not three days alone with the person harming them, and if there is immediate danger, contact a domestic-abuse service or emergency services. If one partner is being pressured into the retreat by the other, do the personal retreat separately or not at all. And if the relationship is in acute crisis, a shared retreat may intensify it; see a qualified professional first, and consider separate retreats.

Before you begin. Each of you completes Module 2 individually: your own intention, your own check-in, your own phone arrangement. Then, together, agree three things: that the personal sessions are private, and neither will ask to read the other's journal or cards; how you will signal that you need space, without explanation; and what you will do if one of you needs to stop a session or the retreat. Write it down. Agree also on the practicalities: who cooks, when you will eat together, and where each of you will do the sessions. Use separate rooms if possible. Otherwise, use different parts of the property or take the sessions at different times so that neither person feels watched.

The daily shape. Follow Program 1's schedule, doing each session separately at the same time, in separate spaces. Eat together. Walk together if you wish, in silence or in ordinary conversation, but not to discuss the sessions. Follow your usual and agreed sleeping arrangements; couples preparing for marriage should arrange separate sleeping spaces consistent with their convictions and boundaries. Add the three shared moments below.

Shared moment 1: the end of Day 1. After Session Two, sit together for twenty minutes. Each of you says one thing, and only one, that you noticed today. Not what you wrote, not what you concluded; something you noticed. The other listens without responding, then says their own. Then pray together briefly, each praying one sentence for the other. That is all.

Shared moment 2: the end of Day 2. After the long break following Session Four, and before any Session Five, sit together for twenty minutes. Each says one thing, and only one, that today was like. "Heavy." "Better than I expected." "I cried, and I do not want to say why yet." The other listens. If either of you needs to say "I cannot talk tonight," that is a complete answer. Pray one sentence each. Do not ask about the lament, and do not offer to fix anything.

Shared moment 3: after Session Ten. Sit together for up to an hour. Each person shares the rhythm they hope to establish and any practical action that will affect the marriage, because a rhythm your spouse does not know about will not survive. The other cards, the thing entrusted and the person or source of support, may be shared, but sharing them is optional. Discuss the practical implications of each other's rhythm, and agree how you will protect each other's. Then, together, read the blessing.

What to keep private. Everything else. The intention, the confession, the lament, the release, the identity and purpose cards, the "What I sense" card. If either of you wants to share more than the program asks, wait until you are home and rested, and share as much as feels safe. A retreat is not a reason to disclose more than the relationship can hold this week. Privacy does not, however, mean secrecy about immediate danger, abuse, serious misconduct, or anything that must be reported for someone's protection.

For couples preparing for marriage, the shared moments work the same way, with one addition: you will each learn something about how the other rests, grieves, and hears God. Notice it. It will matter more than you expect.

## Program 6: The Small-Group and Church Retreat

The retreat run for a group of adults, with a leader, over a weekend or a series of weekly meetings.

Sessions used: the standard program (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10) for a residential weekend; or Sessions 1, 2, 3, 7, and 10 for a one-day church retreat; or one session per week for a small group meeting over seven to ten weeks.

This program is written for adults. Retreats involving minors require age-appropriate material, parental arrangements, trained safeguarding personnel, and the church's youth-protection procedures. Do not adapt this program for young people without that framework.

What the leader is, and is not. The leader is a facilitator: someone who holds the schedule, reads the passages, times the silences, and keeps the group safe. The leader is not a counsellor, not a therapist, and not the person to whom everyone's confessions and griefs are addressed. If you are leading, say this at the start, and mean it. The most important thing you will do all weekend is protect people from being pushed further than they can safely go, including by you.

Group size. Six to twelve is workable for a small group. For a church retreat with more than twelve, break into groups of six to eight for any sharing, with a facilitator in each. Do not run sharing in a group larger than that; people will either stay silent or say too much.

**Before the retreat: the leader's preparation.**

- Complete the whole course, including Lesson 5, before you lead anything. Do the retreat yourself first if at all possible.
- Know your church's safeguarding policy and your reporting obligations, and tell the group what they are before any sharing begins. A leader cannot promise absolute confidentiality, and should not.
- Appoint a safeguarding lead who has accepted the role, and arrange an external professional or crisis contact before the retreat. Do not assume that a participant who happens to be professionally qualified will act as the emergency responder. Know the local crisis and emergency numbers, and have a quiet room available where someone can go with a companion if a session overwhelms them.
- Ask participants to complete Module 2 in advance and ask them to do Lessons 7 and 9 before they come. A group of people who arrive without an intention or a phone plan will spend Day 1 finding one.
- Ask participants to read each session's teaching privately before the group gathers for it. Reading every teaching aloud turns the retreat into a seminar and lengthens the schedule considerably.
- Decide in advance what will be shared and what will not, and tell the group. The default below is that almost nothing from the sessions is shared aloud.

- At the first gathering, agree how participants want to be checked on if they leave a session: a quiet message, a designated companion, or being given space unless there is a safety concern.

The rules for sharing. State these at the first gathering and repeat them before each sharing time.

1. Sharing is always optional. "Pass" is a complete contribution, and no one will be asked why.
2. Share what you noticed, not what you concluded, and never what someone else in the room wrote or said.
3. No advice, no fixing, no interpreting another person's experience. The response to a share is "thank you," or silence.
4. Everyone agrees not to repeat another person's story outside the group. The leader will explain that this is a serious commitment, subject to the safeguarding limits already stated, though no group can guarantee absolute confidentiality.
5. Anyone may leave any session, any silence, or any sharing, without explanation, and will be checked on in the way agreed at the start.

The shape of each session in a group. Participants read the teaching privately beforehand. The group gathers for the passage, a brief introduction from the leader, the guided prayer, and the silence. Participants then go to separate places for the self-assessment, journaling, and exercise. The group regathers only for optional sharing where the schedule includes it. In Session Five, the leader introduces the passage and then gives participants complete privacy for the prayer, journaling, and exercise; no personal confession is spoken to the group or the facilitator.

The one-day church retreat. Follow Program 2, with the same pattern: passages and silences together, journaling and exercises apart, brief optional sharing after Sessions Two and Three, and a closing with the blessing.

The weekly small group. One session per meeting, over seven to ten weeks, with the leader reading the passage, a shortened silence together, and the teaching, journaling, and exercise done at home before the next meeting. Open each meeting with brief optional sharing about the previous session's exercise. Take a week off between Sessions Four and Five, and between Five and Six if you include Six. Do not include Six unless the group is small, well-established, and the leader has considered Session Six's conditions carefully. The 30-day plan can be done together as the final weeks.

When someone is in trouble. If a participant becomes distressed, the safeguarding lead or a designated leader goes with them to the quiet room, sits with them, uses Lesson 5's orientation exercise if needed, and does not try to counsel them. If the distress is severe, involves safety, or does not settle, the leader contacts the arranged professional or emergency services, and does not leave the person alone. Afterwards, the leader follows up within a day and helps them find ongoing support, seeking the person's agreement wherever possible; if there is immediate danger or a safeguarding obligation, follow the agreed emergency and reporting procedure even when consent cannot first be obtained. None of this is announced to the group.

A word to pastors leading this. You may be more tired than anyone in the room. Do not lead this retreat as a substitute for taking it. Program 7 is for you.

## Program 7: The Retreat for Pastors and Christian Leaders

The three-day retreat with additions for people whose work is ministry.

Sessions used: the standard three-day program (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10), with the additions and cautions below. Session Nine, on purpose, is particularly relevant and is recommended as the one flex session on Day 3 Route A, or in the 30-day plan.

Why a separate program. Ministry burnout has features that ordinary burnout does not. The work is bound up with faith, so exhaustion feels like spiritual failure. The role is bound up with identity, so weakness feels like disqualification. The person who would normally help, a pastor, is you. And there is rarely anyone in the congregation to whom you can say, "I am not sure I believe any of this at the moment," without consequences. This program takes those things seriously.

Who to tell. Before you go, decide who will know that you are on retreat and why. You do not owe the congregation an explanation; "taking some days for prayer" is true and sufficient. But someone in leadership should know that you are struggling, if you are, and that this retreat is part of addressing it. If you have no one in your own church who can hear that safely, find a pastor in another church, a denominational officer, or a professional supervisor, and tell them. Leaders who carry burnout alone tend to break alone.

Location. If at all possible, do not use your own church building or a place where members will expect access to you. A colleague's church, a retreat centre, a borrowed house in another town. If you must use your own building, arrange for another leader to protect it and make clear that you are unavailable. The retreat needs you to be nobody's pastor for three days.

Additions to the sessions.

Session One. When you write the ordinary demands on the cards, include the sermon, the meeting, the pastoral situation, the building project, and the person who is angry with you. All of it. It goes in the envelope like everyone else's.

Session Three. Pay particular attention to the Share and Seek-counsel marks. Ministers habitually mark everything Act. The structural answer God gave Moses in Exodus 18 was shared load, and Session Three's exercise is where you begin to admit which loads should have been shared years ago.

Session Four. Ministers carry a specific grief that rarely gets named: the ministry that did not become what you hoped, the people who left, the vision that failed, the church that hurt you while you were serving it. Give it a card. Use the general lament if the specific one is too close.

Session Five. Two cautions. First, the temptation to confess pastorally, that is, to produce a confession that would sound right from a pulpit, is strong in ministers. Use Hannah's honesty. Second, if what surfaces involves misconduct, the safeguarding guidance in Session Five applies to you with particular force, and the paragraph in Session Nine's teaching on restoration and reinstatement, which says that forgiveness and fellowship are not the same as return to office, is not optional reading. Seek qualified guidance about accountability and reporting, and do not use the retreat to manage it privately.

Session Seven. Ministers are practised at hearing God for other people and often out of practice at listening for themselves. Bring your own question, not the church's. And apply the "responsibilities" test honestly: some ministry roles need to change or end for reasons of health, truth, or safety, and "God has called me here" is sometimes a way of not asking whether he is calling you on. A personal impression received on retreat does not bypass elders, boards, denominational oversight, or the ordinary processes by which church decisions are tested.

Session Nine. Do it. The question "Do you love me?" asked of someone who has been feeding sheep professionally for years is the retreat's most important question for ministers. Answer it without the pulpit voice.

Session Ten. When you choose your one rhythm, consider a weekly protected period of ceasing that the congregation knows about and a leadership team protects. Ministers who rest in secret do not rest. And when you choose your one person, consider a supervisor or a peer group outside your church, not a member of it.

The re-entry problem for ministers. You will return to a pulpit within days, and there is a temptation to preach the retreat. As a strong rule, do not preach or publicly announce what you believe you heard for at least a month. Let it be tested first through time, Scripture, trusted counsel, and the church's proper leadership processes. A congregation that hears its pastor announce a new direction on the Sunday after a retreat has good reason to be wary. Tell the person in leadership who knew you were going. Keep the rest until it has become real.

When the retreat shows you it is more than tiredness. Some ministers will discover on retreat that what they are carrying is not burnout but depression, or trauma, or a marriage in serious trouble, or a crisis of faith they have been hiding. Lesson 5 applies to you exactly as it applies to a layperson, with one addition: find help outside your own church. A minister receiving pastoral care from their own congregation is rarely free to be honest. Your denomination, a professional counsellor, or a pastor in another town can hold what your congregation cannot.

A last word. The retreat's central image has been Elijah, who had the greatest public ministry of his generation and asked to die the next day. God fed him, let him sleep, listened to him, reoriented him, and gave him a companion and a successor. He did not tell him to try harder. That is the pattern, and it is for you.

## Choosing your program

| Program | Format | Approximate length | Best for | Deeper sessions included? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Standard personal retreat | 3 days | A full individual retreat | Yes |
| 2 | One-day retreat | 8–9 hours | One protected day | No lament, confession, or forgiveness |
| 3 | Spiritual reset | 3 hours | An immediate pause | No |
| 4 | At-home retreat | 8 or 12 days | Ongoing home responsibilities | Yes, with rest days |
| 5 | Couples | 3 days | Two personal retreats taken together | Yes, privately |
| 6 | Group or church | Weekend or weekly | A facilitated community setting | Carefully limited |
| 7 | Pastors and leaders | 3 days | Ministry-specific burnout | Yes, with external support |

Choose the format that gives you enough protection and support, not the one that appears most demanding. A shorter retreat completed honestly is better than a fuller program that exceeds your present capacity.
