# Welcome

## A welcome from Richmond

Hello, and welcome.

My name is Richmond Kobe. I have served as a pastor for more than twenty years, in different countries and different kinds of churches.

During that time, I have sat with many people who were running on empty. Some were pastors and leaders. Some were parents or caregivers. Others were people whose faith had quietly grown dry without anyone noticing.

Almost none of them needed another sermon or another spiritual assignment. What they needed was permission to stop, and a safe, practical way to do it.

That is what this course is designed to provide.

It is not simply a collection of lectures, and it is not a programme for becoming more disciplined. It is a guided spiritual retreat: an opportunity to step away from the noise, become honest with God about what you are carrying, and return to ordinary life with something you can genuinely sustain.

I want to be clear about what you can expect, because I do not want to promise more than I can responsibly promise.

I cannot guarantee that you will feel God's presence in a particular way. I cannot promise that God will answer a specific question during your retreat. Your experience may be quiet and gentle rather than dramatic.

What I can tell you is that this course is designed to help you return with four things: one burden to entrust to God, one responsible step to take, one person to involve, and one sustainable rhythm of rest to carry home.

Those four things are the practical aim of the course. Everything else supports them.

The journey has three main phases.

The first is preparation. You will explore what you may be experiencing and make practical preparations that will help you protect your retreat time.

The second phase is the retreat itself. The course includes ten guided sessions. The core three-day retreat uses seven of them, while three are optional and can be completed when they are appropriate for you. You will also find shorter retreat formats if three uninterrupted days are not possible.

The third phase is your return. You will learn how to test what you believe you heard, turn insight into responsible action, and build a spiritual rhythm that can survive ordinary life. You will then have a gentle plan to guide your first thirty days after the retreat.

If you are already exhausted, the word "preparation" may make you want to close this page. Please hear this: you do not have to complete every teaching lesson before receiving help from the course.

The next lesson will help you choose your route. Some of you will complete the full preparation pathway, and that will be exactly what you need. Others need a shorter reset first and can return to the deeper teaching afterwards.

Both are legitimate paths. Neither is the lazy option.

There is one more thing I need to say, and it is the most important.

A spiritual retreat is not a substitute for medical care, professional counselling, trauma treatment, addiction treatment, or crisis support. If you are in immediate danger, thinking about harming yourself, experiencing abuse, or carrying something that has gone beyond ordinary tiredness, please do not enter an extended period of silence alone.

This module includes guidance about recognising when you may need additional help. Seeking that help is not a failure of faith. It is an act of wisdom and courage.

One biblical pattern will accompany us throughout this course: the story of Elijah.

After one of the greatest public victories of his ministry, Elijah became overwhelmed, ran into the wilderness, and asked God to let him die. God did not begin by telling him to work harder or have more faith. He gave him food, allowed him to sleep, listened to him, and then gave him direction.

Rest came before direction.

That story reminds us that exhausted people do not always need another demand. Sometimes they need a safe place to stop, receive care, tell the truth, and become available to God again.

You do not need to arrive strong. You do not need to manufacture a spiritual experience. You only need to come honestly.

So, come as you are.

Let's begin.

## How the course is arranged

| Module | What it is | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Start Here | Welcome, choosing your route, and where to find help | 20 minutes |
| Module 1: Why Your Soul Needs a Retreat | Understanding burnout, spiritual dryness and emotional overload, and what a retreat can and cannot do | 2–3 hours of reading and reflection |
| Module 2: Preparing to Meet With God | Practical preparation: length, location, intention, digital boundary, what to bring, food and health, silence | 2–3 hours |
| Module 3: Choose Your Retreat | Seven ready-to-use formats, from three hours to three days, at home or away | 30 minutes |
| Module 4: The Retreat Journey | Ten guided sessions; the core retreat uses seven over three days | 3 days, or the shorter format you choose |
| Module 5: After the Retreat | Testing what you heard, turning insight into action, a rule of life, when to seek help, and your 30-day plan | 30 days, lightly |
