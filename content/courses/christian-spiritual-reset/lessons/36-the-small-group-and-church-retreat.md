# Program 6: The Small-Group and Church Retreat

The retreat run for a group of adults, with a leader, over a weekend or a series of weekly meetings.

Sessions used: the standard program (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10) for a residential weekend; or Sessions 1, 2, 3, 7, and 10 for a one-day church retreat; or one session per week for a small group meeting over seven to ten weeks.

This program is written for adults. Retreats involving minors require age-appropriate material, parental arrangements, trained safeguarding personnel, and the church's youth-protection procedures. Do not adapt this program for young people without that framework.

What the leader is, and is not. The leader is a facilitator: someone who holds the schedule, reads the passages, times the silences, and keeps the group safe. The leader is not a counsellor, not a therapist, and not the person to whom everyone's confessions and griefs are addressed. If you are leading, say this at the start, and mean it. The most important thing you will do all weekend is protect people from being pushed further than they can safely go, including by you.

Group size. Six to twelve is workable for a small group. For a church retreat with more than twelve, break into groups of six to eight for any sharing, with a facilitator in each. Do not run sharing in a group larger than that; people will either stay silent or say too much.

**Before the retreat: the leader's preparation.**

- Complete the whole course, including Lesson 5, before you lead anything. Do the retreat yourself first if at all possible.
- Know your church's safeguarding policy and your reporting obligations, and tell the group what they are before any sharing begins. A leader cannot promise absolute confidentiality, and should not.
- Appoint a safeguarding lead who has accepted the role, and arrange an external professional or crisis contact before the retreat. Do not assume that a participant who happens to be professionally qualified will act as the emergency responder. Know the local crisis and emergency numbers, and have a quiet room available where someone can go with a companion if a session overwhelms them.
- Ask participants to complete Module 2 in advance and ask them to do Lessons 7 and 9 before they come. A group of people who arrive without an intention or a phone plan will spend Day 1 finding one.
- Ask participants to read each session's teaching privately before the group gathers for it. Reading every teaching aloud turns the retreat into a seminar and lengthens the schedule considerably.
- Decide in advance what will be shared and what will not, and tell the group. The default below is that almost nothing from the sessions is shared aloud.

- At the first gathering, agree how participants want to be checked on if they leave a session: a quiet message, a designated companion, or being given space unless there is a safety concern.

The rules for sharing. State these at the first gathering and repeat them before each sharing time.

1. Sharing is always optional. "Pass" is a complete contribution, and no one will be asked why.
2. Share what you noticed, not what you concluded, and never what someone else in the room wrote or said.
3. No advice, no fixing, no interpreting another person's experience. The response to a share is "thank you," or silence.
4. Everyone agrees not to repeat another person's story outside the group. The leader will explain that this is a serious commitment, subject to the safeguarding limits already stated, though no group can guarantee absolute confidentiality.
5. Anyone may leave any session, any silence, or any sharing, without explanation, and will be checked on in the way agreed at the start.

The shape of each session in a group. Participants read the teaching privately beforehand. The group gathers for the passage, a brief introduction from the leader, the guided prayer, and the silence. Participants then go to separate places for the self-assessment, journaling, and exercise. The group regathers only for optional sharing where the schedule includes it. In Session Five, the leader introduces the passage and then gives participants complete privacy for the prayer, journaling, and exercise; no personal confession is spoken to the group or the facilitator.

For a Friday-to-Sunday weekend, drop Session Five and Session Seven, and do Session Ten on Sunday afternoon; participants take Five and Seven into the 30-day plan.

The one-day church retreat. Follow Program 2, with the same pattern: passages and silences together, journaling and exercises apart, brief optional sharing after Sessions Two and Three, and a closing with the blessing.

The weekly small group. One session per meeting, over seven to ten weeks, with the leader reading the passage, a shortened silence together, and the teaching, journaling, and exercise done at home before the next meeting. Open each meeting with brief optional sharing about the previous session's exercise. Take a week off between Sessions Four and Five, and between Five and Six if you include Six. Do not include Six unless the group is small, well-established, and the leader has considered Session Six's conditions carefully. The 30-day plan can be done together as the final weeks.

When someone is in trouble. If a participant becomes distressed, the safeguarding lead or a designated leader goes with them to the quiet room, sits with them, uses Lesson 5's orientation exercise if needed, and does not try to counsel them. If the distress is severe, involves safety, or does not settle, the leader contacts the arranged professional or emergency services, and does not leave the person alone. Afterwards, the leader follows up within a day and helps them find ongoing support, seeking the person's agreement wherever possible; if there is immediate danger or a safeguarding obligation, follow the agreed emergency and reporting procedure even when consent cannot first be obtained. None of this is announced to the group.

A word to pastors leading this. You may be more tired than anyone in the room. Do not lead this retreat as a substitute for taking it. Program 7 is for you.
