## One truth to remember

> **A retreat is not running away from life. It is stepping away to meet with God before returning to ordinary life.**

## Key Scripture

> “But he would withdraw to desolate places and pray.”
>
> — Luke 5:16 (ESV)

Crowds were coming to Jesus in great numbers. Even then, he regularly withdrew to quiet places to pray. Stepping away was part of the way he lived, prayed and served.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About five to six minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

After the last lesson, a voice may have started arguing with you.

It may sound sensible.

People are counting on you.

You can rest when things calm down.

Taking time for yourself is selfish.

Real faith keeps going.

If you are a parent, carer, leader or simply the person everyone depends on, that voice may be very loud.

Let us answer it.

First, rest is part of God’s design.

Genesis tells us that God rested on the seventh day. He was not exhausted. His rest established a pattern.

Later, God gave Israel the Sabbath—a regular time to stop working. He gave this command to people who had been slaves in Egypt and had lived under the control of others.

They needed to learn that they were no longer slaves who could never stop.

This teaches us something important. Rest is not only a reward for completing every task. It is also an act of trust. We stop because God—not us—holds the world together.

Second, look at Jesus.

Luke tells us that great crowds came to hear Jesus and to be healed. If anyone could have said, “People are counting on me,” it was Jesus.

Yet Luke says, “He would withdraw to desolate places and pray.”

This was not a single event. Withdrawing to pray was part of Jesus’ way of life.

He prayed after demanding days. He sought solitude while grieving. He spent extended time in prayer before important decisions.

Then he returned to ordinary life and the people around him.

A healthy retreat follows a similar movement. You step away for a time. You turn towards God. You become honest about what you are carrying. Then you return.

A retreat is not abandoning the people who depend on you. Responsible preparation matters. You may need to arrange childcare, cover work, prepare medication, tell someone where you will be or choose a shorter retreat.

Stepping away safely and responsibly is not selfish.

There is another important difference to understand. Leaving an unsafe or abusive situation is not “running away” in the sense discussed in this lesson. Moving towards safety is wise. If someone is hurting, threatening or controlling you, use the Safety and Support page and seek appropriate help.

The Bible gives us an example of someone who did try to escape responsibility.

God told Jonah to go to Nineveh. Jonah boarded a ship travelling in the opposite direction because he did not want to obey God.

Elijah’s situation was different. Elijah fled because he was afraid and exhausted. In the wilderness, he spoke honestly to God and received the care God provided.

The important question is not simply whether you step away. It is why you are stepping away and what you are turning towards.

A Christian retreat is not a way to hide from truth or avoid a responsibility that needs to be faced. It is a planned pause in which you turn towards God and become more honest about your life.

Over the years, I have often heard three sentences from people who needed rest but struggled to receive it.

The first is, “If I stop, everything will fall apart.”

Some responsibilities are real, and you should plan for them. But this sentence can also hide the belief that everything depends on you.

Psalm 127 says that God “gives to his beloved sleep.”

The second sentence is, “Other people have it harder, so I have no right to be tired.”

Another person’s suffering does not make your tiredness unreal. Jesus did not invite only the most exhausted person to come to him.

The third sentence is, “I will rest when this busy season is over.”

Sometimes that is reasonable. But busy seasons often lead into other busy seasons. If rest always has to wait until everything is finished, it may never come.

So hear this plainly: you have permission to pause.

You do not have to earn rest by reaching complete exhaustion. Rest is part of God’s good design, and Jesus showed that withdrawing to pray can belong within a faithful life.

This does not mean that everyone must take a three-day retreat. Your responsibilities, health and circumstances matter. That is why this course also includes a one-day retreat and a three-hour reset.

Jesus did not always go away alone. On some occasions, he took close disciples with him.

You do not have to prepare alone either.

Now consider this question.

Which sentence sounds most like you?

“If I stop, everything will fall apart.”

“Other people have it harder, so I have no right to be tired.”

“I will rest when this busy season is over.”

Or is there another sentence that makes it difficult for you to pause? <#5#>

Do not argue with the sentence yet. Simply notice it.

Then take one small step.

Tell one safe, trustworthy person that you plan to take a retreat. You do not need to explain everything. Simply tell them what you are planning.

If no safe person comes to mind, write down the name of a suitable pastor, mature Christian, counsellor or support service you could contact.

When you have done that, mark this lesson complete.
