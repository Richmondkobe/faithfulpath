# Lesson 22 — Choose a Rhythm You Can Keep

## One truth to remember

> **A good rhythm is like a trellis, not a cage. It supports life. It does not earn God's love.**

## Key Scripture

> “And he said to them, ‘The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath.’”
>
> — Mark 2:27 (ESV)

Jesus rejected ways of keeping the Sabbath that turned rest into a burden. God's gift of rest is meant to serve people, not to crush them.

## Start with your Session 10 card

Take out the rhythm card you made in Session 10. Use it as the starting point for these three lines. You may keep it, make it smaller or change it.

## Your rhythm in three lines

* **When I will pray:** a short, regular time most days. Five or ten minutes with a psalm is enough.
* **When I will rest:** a regular time each week when I stop ordinary work, such as a day, half a day or an evening.
* **Who will support me:** one safe person I will share this rhythm with.

*Choose what you can keep in a difficult ordinary week, not what you wish you could manage. If you share your home with others, plan the rest time together where it is safe to do so.*

## Audio script and transcript

*(About five to six minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

In this lesson, you will write a small pattern that can help carry you through ordinary weeks. Christians have sometimes called this a “rule of life.” The word “rule” here does not mean a list of strict laws. It comes from an old word for a measuring stick, something that gives shape.

A helpful picture is a trellis in a garden. A trellis is a simple frame that helps a plant grow upwards without being crushed. That is what a good rhythm does. It supports your life with God.

The Bible gives us examples. Daniel prayed three times a day at his window, as he had always done. And the early church devoted themselves to teaching, fellowship, sharing meals and prayer.

But a rhythm must never become a way of earning God's love, or a stick to beat yourself with. Paul wrote, “For freedom Christ has set us free.” If your rhythm becomes a cage, it needs to change.

For many tired people, the bigger danger is not being too strict. It is drift. Without a chosen pattern, the loudest demand wins every day. A rhythm helps you decide ahead of time what will not be squeezed out.

At the centre of a healthy Christian rhythm is regular rest. In the Bible, this is called the Sabbath: a regular time to stop working, rest and enjoy God.

God gave the Sabbath to people who had been slaves, people who had never been allowed to stop. Rest is a way of saying that God holds the world, and that we are not slaves to our work, our phones or other people's expectations.

Jesus said, “The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath.” He refused to let rest become a heavy burden. Christians have different views about which day to keep, and Paul warned believers, in Romans chapter 14, verses 5 and 6, not to judge one another about special days. So this course does not tell you which day to choose. It simply encourages the principle: God built a rhythm of stopping into life.

How can you practise this? Choose a regular time each week when you stop your ordinary work. Not only paid work, but also managing, fixing and catching up. Ideally a whole day. If that is not possible, half a day, or an evening. Fill it with worship, rest, enjoyment and people you love. Necessary care and emergencies still come first.

If you work on Sundays, choose another time. If you are a carer and cannot stop for a whole day, look for smaller pieces of rest, such as a protected hour, or help with one task. A half-day kept for a year is worth more than a full day planned and never taken.

Take out the rhythm card you made in Session 10. Use it as the starting point for these three lines. You may keep it, make it smaller or change it.

So your rhythm has three simple lines. When you will pray: a short, regular time most days. Five or ten minutes with a psalm is enough. When you will rest: a regular time each week. And who will support you: one safe person you will share this with. A rhythm nobody knows about is easy to drop quietly.

Choose what you can keep in a difficult ordinary week, not what you wish you could manage. And if you share your home with others, plan your rest time together where it is safe to do so.

You will miss things. Everyone does. When that happens, do not punish yourself, and do not try to make up for it with double effort. Simply start again at the next opportunity.

In two weeks, look at your rhythm again. If it already feels like a cage, make it smaller.

Now, a question to think about. What usually wins when you try to rest: work, your phone, other people's needs or something else? <#5#>

And here is your step. Write your three lines: when you will pray, when you will rest and who will support you. Then write underneath: “When I miss something, I will start again at the next opportunity. I will not punish myself or try to make up for it with extra effort.”
