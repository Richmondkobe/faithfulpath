## Audio script and transcript

*(About five to six minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Before we begin, one important point. If you are in danger, being harmed, unable to manage daily life, in severe distress, or struggling with alcohol or drug dependence, do not wait for any part of this plan. Get help today. Safety and professional help come first.

This lesson gives your first month after the retreat or reset a gentle shape, so that you do not have to keep deciding what to do next.

The month is not for becoming a different person in thirty days. It is for three simple things: letting your rhythm become familiar, taking your next steps at a pace that does not bring back your exhaustion, and giving important questions the time they need.

Paul wrote, “He who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ.” Notice who finishes the work. God does. And the day of completion is not Day 30. Day 30 is simply a review.

Through the month, keep returning to the rhythm from your Session 10 card, imperfectly. That is your main practice. When you miss something, begin again at the next opportunity, without trying to make up for it.

Now, the four weeks.

Week 1 is landing. Do very little. Keep returning to your rhythm. Check in briefly with your support person. You do not need to give a long report. Do not make big decisions or redesign your life this week, unless safety or an urgent responsibility requires action.

Week 2 is doing. Take one step from your cards. That might be one conversation, or one appointment. Then check your rhythm. If it already feels too heavy, make it smaller. Do not give it up; just shrink it.

Week 3 is shaping. Look again at your three lines from Lesson 22: when you will pray, when you will rest, and who will support you. If it would help, ask one safe and appropriate person who may be able to help change your workload for one change.

Week 4 is settling. Notice what is working. If you are testing a major direction, look at it again with your wise person. Day 30 is not a deadline for decisions.

Each week, there is also an optional short video of encouragement.

Then, on Day 30, you can open the Day 30 Review. It is not a test. It is a chance to notice where you received help, rest or grace that you did not produce yourself, what has become clearer, and what is still unresolved. You can look at it at any time, but it is best after thirty days.

After Day 30, keep what is helping, make smaller what is too heavy, and seek help with anything that remains serious or unresolved.

And if the month does not go to plan, because of illness, a family crisis, or a longer dip than you expected, please hear this. You do not need to start again from the beginning. Find the next step that still matters, and begin there. The small things you did manage, a rhythm kept on some days, one appointment, one honest conversation, are not failures. They are the plan, in the form your month allowed.

Now, a question to think about. What would help you most in your first week back: more rest, fewer tasks, or one conversation? <#5#>

And here is your step. Choose one weekly practice to protect this month. Then set a reminder for Day 30 in your calendar or phone, so you remember to open the Day 30 Review.
