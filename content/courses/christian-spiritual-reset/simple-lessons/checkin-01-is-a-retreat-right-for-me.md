# Check-in 1 — Is a Retreat Right for Me Now?

*Part 1 — Understand Your Need · Check-in*

**What you will do here:** Answer three short questions, read the guidance and choose your next step.

This is not a test, medical assessment or diagnosis. It cannot determine whether a retreat is safe for you. It offers simple guidance based only on the three answers you give.

Your answers and guidance are not saved. Nothing you select contacts anyone or asks another person to check on you.

## Your guidance

*Show only one of the following messages. This guidance is not a professional assessment.*

### A. Get support before retreating alone

Your answers suggest that spending extended time alone in silence may not be safe or suitable for you right now.

This is not a failure of faith, and you are not being turned away. The order matters: **support first, retreat later or with suitable help.**

If you are in immediate danger, thinking about suicide, believe you may harm yourself or cannot keep yourself safe, call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department now. Tell a safe person and ask them to stay with you.

If the danger is not immediate, contact a doctor, qualified mental health professional or appropriate support service before planning a retreat. Tell them that you are considering spending time alone in silence.

**[ Get Help Now ]**

**[ Find support where I live ]**

*You are welcome to return when you have appropriate support.*

### B. Speak with a professional before retreating alone

What you described is affecting your daily life and may need more support than a pastoral course can provide.

Please speak with a doctor or qualified mental health professional before beginning a retreat alone. Tell them that you are considering a retreat that includes silence, prayer and time away from your normal routine.

Ask whether a retreat is appropriate now and whether it should be shorter or include support.

You may continue reading the preparation lessons while arranging that conversation. Do not treat continued access to the course as professional approval to retreat alone.

### C. Consider a shorter or supported retreat

You appear to be carrying pressure that deserves care.

A shorter retreat or one planned with support may be more suitable than three days alone. Tell a safe person what you are planning, agree when you will make contact and keep support details nearby.

If your condition becomes worse, daily life becomes difficult to manage or being alone begins to feel unsafe, seek professional help before continuing.

This guidance is cautious, not diagnostic. You may speak with a doctor or counsellor at any time, even if your difficulties do not feel severe.

### D. You may continue preparing

Nothing you selected points to the urgent concerns covered by this short check-in.

You may continue preparing for your retreat. Stay honest about how you are feeling, involve a safe person and remember that you may pause at any time.

This message is not medical clearance or a promise that retreating alone is safe. If your situation changes or you become concerned, repeat the check-in and seek appropriate advice.
