# Lesson 20 — Test What You Think You Heard

*Part 4 — Bring It Home · Lesson 20*

**What you will do here:** Sort what you brought home from your retreat, and learn a simple, safe way to test it before you act.

## Important: safety comes first

Waiting and testing are for ordinary decisions. They do **not** apply if you are in immediate danger, facing abuse, needing urgent medical care, under a legal or safeguarding duty to report, or facing a deadline that cannot safely wait. In those situations, seek qualified help and take the safest necessary action now. Use **Get Help Now** if anyone is in immediate danger.

## One truth to remember

> **Testing what you sensed is not doubting God. It is taking him seriously.**

## Key Scripture

> “Do not despise prophecies, but test everything; hold fast what is good.”
>
> — 1 Thessalonians 5:20–21 (ESV)

Paul does not tell Christians to ignore what may be from God. He tells them to test it, and then hold on to what is good.

## Sort your card

Look at your **What I sense** card from Session 7, and any **To test** card from Session 9. Which of these fits?

* **Nothing significant:** a sense of peace, or a familiar verse with no direction attached. *There is nothing to test. That is a complete and good outcome.*
* **A small, reversible step:** such as booking an appropriate appointment, gathering information, asking a safe person for advice or protecting time to rest. *Use the six tests. If no serious concern appears, you may take it.*
* **A major or hard-to-reverse direction:** such as leaving a job, church or country, starting a ministry, a large financial commitment, a confrontation, or contact with someone who may be unsafe. *Test it carefully, with other people, before you act.*

## Six simple tests

* Does it agree with the whole of Scripture, read in context?
* Does it reflect the character of Jesus?
* Is it based on facts, not only feelings?
* Does it still seem right after time and honest questions?
* What do wise and, where needed, qualified people say?
* Does it respect safety and accountability, or does it demand secrecy and speed?

*If a direction clearly conflicts with Scripture, requires harm, breaks appropriate accountability or creates serious danger, let it go. If the direction passes those essential tests but the remaining evidence is mixed, the answer is “not yet,” not necessarily “never.”*

## Choosing a wise person

Choose someone who is mature, trustworthy, able to disagree with you and not personally invested in controlling the outcome. For decisions involving health, law, money, abuse, employment or relocation, include someone with relevant professional knowledge. One conversation is a minimum, not automatic confirmation.

Listen carefully without immediately defending your conclusion. Write down what they say and consider it. Their opinion is part of the testing; it does not replace your responsibility to decide wisely.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About five to six minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Welcome home.

Ordinary life may already feel loud again. The quiet of the retreat may feel like something that happened a long time ago. That is normal.

This lesson is about one card in particular: the card from Session 7 marked “What I sense,” and any card from Session 9 marked “To test.” In Session 7, you wrote down what you sensed, and promised to test it before acting. Now we will do that.

First, some people feel that testing is a lack of faith. If God spoke, why check it? But the Bible tells us to test, because God does speak, and because we can easily mishear.

Paul wrote, “Do not despise prophecies, but test everything; hold fast what is good.” And in the book of Acts, the people of Berea were praised because they checked the Scriptures to see whether what they were taught was true.

Testing does not insult God. It honours him, because it refuses to put his name on something that may really come from our own fear, desire or tiredness.

Before we go on, one important point. Waiting and testing are for ordinary decisions. If you are in immediate danger, facing abuse, needing urgent medical care, or facing a deadline that cannot safely wait, do not wait. Seek qualified help and take the safest necessary action now.

Now, look at your card, and ask what kind of thing it holds.

It may hold nothing significant: a sense of peace, or a familiar verse. If so, there is nothing to test. That is a good and complete outcome. Many people will be here.

It may hold a small, reversible step. For example, booking an appropriate appointment, gathering information, asking a safe person for advice, or protecting time to rest. These steps are easy to undo if you were mistaken, and they harm no one. Use the six tests. If no serious concern appears, you may take the step.

Or it may hold something major and hard to undo. Leaving a job or a church. Moving to another country. Starting a ministry. A large financial commitment. A confrontation, or contact with someone who may be unsafe. These need careful testing, with other people, before you act. If that is your card, be glad you wrote it down instead of acting on it straight away.

Here are six simple tests.

Does it agree with the whole of Scripture, read in context, not just one matching verse?

Does it reflect the character of Jesus? If a direction makes you harder, more secretive or more self-important, that is a warning.

Is it based on facts, not only feelings?

Does it still seem right after time and honest questions? For a major direction, set a reasonable review date rather than an immediate decision date. The right period depends on the decision, its risks and any real deadlines. Time is one test; it is not proof by itself.

What do wise people say? For matters of health, money, law, work or abuse, you will also need someone with professional knowledge.

And does it respect safety and accountability, or does it push you to act in secret and in a hurry? A rushed feeling is a reason to slow down.

When you look for a wise person, choose someone who is mature, trustworthy, able to disagree with you and not personally invested in controlling the outcome. For decisions involving health, law, money, abuse, employment or relocation, include someone with relevant professional knowledge.

Do not ask them simply to agree with you. Ask them to help you test it. Ask: What seems wise to you? What concerns you? What have I not considered? Then listen carefully without immediately defending your conclusion. Their opinion is part of the testing. It does not replace your responsibility to decide wisely.

If a direction clearly conflicts with Scripture, requires harm, breaks appropriate accountability or creates serious danger, let it go. That is the testing doing its job. But if it passes those essential tests and the rest of the evidence is mixed, the answer is “not yet,” not necessarily “never.” Keep testing, and look at it again on your review date.

Now, a question to think about. Is there anything you feel reluctant to test? Why? <#5#>

And here is your step. Sort your card. If it holds a small, safe and reversible step, use the six tests, and if no serious concern appears, you may take it. If it holds a major direction, arrange a conversation with at least one wise person before deciding. And if it holds nothing significant, there is nothing further to test.
