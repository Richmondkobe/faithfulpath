# Start Here 4 — Safety and Support

**What you will do here:** Learn when to pause, when not to retreat alone, and where to find help.

## If you need help right now

**If you are in immediate danger, thinking about suicide, believe you may harm yourself, or cannot keep yourself safe:**

* call the emergency number where you are or go to the nearest emergency department;
* tell a safe person nearby what is happening; and
* ask that person to stay with you. Do not remain alone.

**To find a crisis or support line in your country:** visit [Find A Helpline](https://findahelpline.com/) and choose your country.

*Find A Helpline is a directory of support services. It is not an emergency service.*

## One truth to remember

> **Asking for help is not a lack of faith. It is wise and brave.**

## Do not retreat alone if…

* you are thinking about suicide, harming yourself or not wanting to be alive;
* someone is hurting, threatening or controlling you;
* stopping or reducing alcohol or drugs may cause withdrawal;
* silence or difficult memories often bring panic, flashbacks or a feeling that you or your surroundings are not real;
* you are in severe distress or do not feel able to keep yourself safe; or
* a doctor or counsellor has advised you to wait or to have support with you.

If any of these applies to you, seek appropriate help before retreating alone. You may be able to return later or plan your retreat with professional guidance and a safe support person.

**A retreat never requires you to stop prescribed medication.** Continue taking medication as directed unless the qualified healthcare professional responsible for your care tells you otherwise.

## When to stop a session

If a session brings up more than you can manage, **stop the session**.

1. Put the exercise aside.
2. Sit or stand somewhere you feel physically safe.
3. Notice where you are and name a few things you can see.
4. Drink some water or eat something if that is safe and appropriate for you.
5. Pray simply: “Lord, help me.”
6. Contact your support person.

If you feel unsafe, severely distressed or at risk of harming yourself or someone else, contact an emergency service or qualified professional immediately.

You do not have to finish an exercise. **A paused retreat is not a failed retreat.**

## Good to know

* **Speak with your doctor or counsellor** before the retreat if you are receiving care and are unsure whether retreating alone is suitable. Their instructions take priority over this course.
* **Your answers are private.** No one monitors them, and nothing you write or select contacts anyone for you. You must contact support yourself when you need it.
* **You may involve a safe person.** A pastor, counsellor, doctor or trusted person may support you before, during or after the retreat.
* **You may stop at any time.** Rest, safety and appropriate care matter more than completing a page.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About four to five minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Before we go any further, I want to speak with you about safety.

This is not meant to frighten you. A retreat can be a good and helpful experience. But even a good thing can cause harm if we ask it to do something it cannot do.

I want this retreat to support you, not place you at greater risk.

First, if you need help right now, please do not wait.

If you are in immediate danger, thinking about suicide, believe you may harm yourself, or cannot keep yourself safe, call the emergency number where you are or go to the nearest emergency department.

Tell a safe person nearby what is happening. Ask that person to stay with you. Do not remain alone.

At the top of this page, you can also find a link to crisis and support lines in different countries. That directory does not replace emergency services when danger is immediate.

Now let me explain what a retreat can and cannot do.

A retreat can give you space to rest. It can help you slow down, pray and become honest with God. It may help you recognise what you have been carrying.

But a retreat cannot replace medical treatment, counselling, trauma care, addiction treatment, safeguarding help or crisis support.

If you live with depression, anxiety, addiction or the effects of trauma, a retreat may sometimes support the care you are already receiving. It is not a replacement for that care.

Trusting God and receiving help are not opposites. God often cares for us through doctors, counsellors, pastors, family members, friends and other people who are equipped to help.

Please do not retreat alone if you are thinking about suicide, harming yourself or not wanting to be alive.

Do not retreat alone if someone is hurting, threatening or controlling you.

Seek medical advice if stopping or reducing alcohol or drugs may cause withdrawal. A retreat does not require you to stop taking prescribed medication. Continue taking it as directed unless the qualified healthcare professional responsible for your care tells you otherwise.

You should also seek guidance before retreating alone if silence or difficult memories often bring panic, flashbacks or a feeling that you or your surroundings are not real.

The same applies if you are in severe distress, cannot keep yourself safe, or a doctor or counsellor has advised you to wait.

If any of these applies to you, getting help first does not mean your retreat is over before it begins. You may be able to return later. You may also be able to plan the retreat with professional guidance and a safe support person.

Here is one simple rule for every session: if something brings up more than you can manage, stop.

Put the exercise aside. Sit or stand somewhere you feel physically safe. Notice where you are and name a few things you can see. Drink some water or eat something if that is safe and appropriate for you.

You can pray simply, even if your only words are, “Lord, help me.”

Then contact your support person.

If you feel unsafe, severely distressed or at risk of harming yourself or someone else, contact an emergency service or qualified professional immediately.

You do not have to finish an exercise. A paused retreat is not a failed retreat.

Remember Elijah. He rested and ate before God gave him further direction.

There are three more things I want you to know.

If you already receive care from a doctor or counsellor, speak with them before the retreat if you are unsure whether retreating alone is suitable. Their instructions take priority over anything in this course.

Your answers in this course are private. I do not monitor them, and neither does a counsellor. Nothing you write or select will contact anyone for you. If you need help, you must reach out yourself.

You may also involve a safe person at any time. That person could be a pastor, counsellor, doctor, family member or trusted friend.

Asking for help is not a lack of faith. It is wise and brave.

At the end of this page, you will see two buttons.

If you understand these boundaries and feel able to continue, choose “I understand and want to continue.”

If you need support first, choose “Help me find support where I live.”

Either choice can be a wise choice. <#3#>

Thank you for taking your safety seriously. Your life matters, and you do not have to carry everything alone.
