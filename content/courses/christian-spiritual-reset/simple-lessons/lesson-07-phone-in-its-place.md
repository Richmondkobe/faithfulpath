# Lesson 7 — Put Your Phone in Its Place

*Part 2 — Prepare Your Retreat · Lesson 7*

**What you will do here:** Choose a phone plan that protects your attention without cutting you off from help.

## One truth to remember

> **The goal is not to reject your phone. It is to stop it being the centre of your attention for a while.**

## Key Scripture

> “But the Lord answered her, ‘Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her.’”
>
> — Luke 10:41–42 (ESV)

Martha was distracted by many responsibilities. Jesus addressed her anxious distraction and affirmed Mary’s choice to sit and listen.

A phone can pull our attention in many directions too.

## Take one step

**Choose your phone plan and test your emergency contact route.**

### Plan A — Stay reachable *(best for most people)*

* Turn on Do Not Disturb, Focus or a similar setting.
* Allow calls from one or two chosen people.
* Turn off message previews and unnecessary notifications.
* Keep the phone out of sight except when using it for the course or contacting support.

**Test Plan A:** Ask your emergency contact to call while the setting is active. Confirm that the call comes through. Then trigger an ordinary notification and confirm that it remains quiet.

### Plan B — Disconnect

* Use airplane mode or switch off the phone.
* Choose specific times to reconnect and check for urgent messages.
* Make sure a safe person can reach you another way in an emergency.

Choose this only if:

* you do not need the phone for health or safety;
* an emergency contact route remains available; and
* you have another way to use the retreat material.

**Important:** Course recordings require internet access at launch. Do not choose Plan B if this phone is the device you need to stream the audio.

**Test Plan B:** Test the alternative emergency route. For example, ask your contact to call the retreat centre, guesthouse, church office or safe person who can reach you.

☐ I have chosen my phone plan and tested my emergency contact route.

### If someone monitors your device

If someone checks your phone, controls your accounts or may react dangerously when settings change, do not make changes that could put you at risk.

Use the Safety and Support page and seek specialist advice from a safe device if possible.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About five minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Of all the practical arrangements, your phone may be the one that most affects whether your retreat remains protected.

You can choose the right place and set aside the time, but still spend much of the retreat checking messages.

This is not a lesson against technology.

Your phone may contain your Bible, your support person’s number and a way to call for help. You may also use your phone—or another device—to play the recordings in this course.

The goal is not to reject your phone.

The goal is to stop it being the centre of your attention for a while, so that you can give attention to God, rest and what is happening inside you.

Think about how often a phone fills the small gaps in an ordinary day.

We reach for it while waiting, before a meeting, when we feel bored or when an uncomfortable thought begins to appear.

Those small gaps are often where honest thoughts rise and simple prayer begins.

A retreat opens some of those spaces again.

In our Key Scripture, Martha was distracted by many responsibilities while her sister Mary sat and listened to Jesus.

Jesus did not say that caring for people was wrong. He addressed Martha’s anxious distraction and affirmed Mary’s choice to give her attention to what mattered in that moment.

A phone can pull our attention in many directions too.

For your retreat, choose between two simple plans.

Plan A is to stay reachable.

This is the best plan for most people.

Turn on Do Not Disturb, Focus or a similar setting. Allow calls from one or two chosen people. Turn off message previews and unnecessary notifications.

Keep the phone out of sight except when you need it for the course, a planned check or contact with your support person.

Plan A is especially important for parents, carers and anyone with health or safety responsibilities.

Test this plan before the retreat.

Ask your emergency contact to call while the setting is active. Make sure the call reaches you.

Then trigger an ordinary notification and make sure it remains quiet. Phone settings work differently, so do not assume the plan works until you test it.

Plan B is to disconnect.

You might use airplane mode or switch the phone off. You then reconnect only at planned times.

Choose this plan only if you do not need the phone for health or safety and someone can reach you by another route in an emergency.

For example, your contact may be able to call the retreat centre, guesthouse, church office or another safe person nearby.

Test that alternative route before the retreat.

There is one more practical limit. The course recordings require internet access at launch. If this phone is the device you need for the audio, do not disconnect it from the internet.

Use Plan A instead.

Whichever plan you choose, agree on what counts as an emergency.

A serious health or safety problem, or an urgent situation involving someone who depends on you, may require contact.

An ordinary question, social message or routine work decision can usually wait.

Your phone boundary is not a test of willpower.

If you pick up the phone without thinking, you have not failed. You have noticed a habit.

Put the phone down and return to the retreat.

During the first hours, you may reach for it many times. Your mind may also seem louder when the normal stream of messages stops.

If an ordinary thought comes up, you may write it down briefly and return to the session.

If what comes up feels overwhelming or unsafe, stop and use the Safety and Support page.

There is also an important safety exception.

If someone monitors your device, controls your accounts or may react dangerously when settings change, do not follow these instructions if doing so could place you at risk.

Use the Safety and Support page. Seek specialist advice from a safe device if possible.

Now consider one question.

When do you usually reach for your phone without deciding to? <#5#>

Then take one practical step.

Choose Plan A or Plan B and test the emergency contact route that belongs to that plan.

When you know that your attention is protected and urgent help can still reach you, mark this lesson complete.
