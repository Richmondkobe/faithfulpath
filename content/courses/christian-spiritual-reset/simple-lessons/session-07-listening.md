# Session 7 — Listening for God's Direction

## FULL VERSION (three-day retreat)

**Three-day retreat · Day 3, morning · Core session 6 of 7**

*Optional: Watch Richmond's Day 3 introduction.*

### Before you begin

**Time needed:** about 60–90 minutes.

**Have nearby:** your Bible, your journal, a pen, a card or small piece of paper, and your cards from Session 3.

If you did Session 5 this morning, rest for at least an hour before you begin.

Day 3 turns towards listening and returning home. If some of the earlier work feels unfinished, that is expected.

**Before you listen, remember:** silence does not guarantee a message from God. Whatever you sense today will be written down and tested. **Do not make a major decision during your retreat** unless immediate safety requires it.

You may pause or stop at any time.

### Listen

**▶ Listen — about 7–8 minutes**

**[ Read the transcript ]**

### Pray

**▶ Guided prayer — about 2 minutes**

Use only the lines that fit you. You may leave any blank empty.

### Be still

**Suggested: 20 minutes.** This is the longest silence of the retreat. Ten minutes is a complete practice, and so is five.

**[ Timer: 5 · 10 · 15 · 20 · 30 minutes ]**

Prayer phrase: **“Speak, Lord, for your servant hears.”** Or, shorter: **“Here I am.”**

Bring your question to God, then let go of the demand for an answer. You are not trying to catch a message. If something seems important, write one or two words and return to your phrase. If nothing comes, stay with your phrase. That is still faithful listening.

A thought is not from God just because it is vivid, repeated, emotional or sudden.

### Respond

**Write *(optional)*:** God asked Elijah, “What are you doing here?” Answer that question for yourself, honestly, in a few sentences.

**Do: listen with Scripture** *(20–30 minutes)*

1. **Choose a passage.** If you are not sure, use one of these: Psalm 25:4–10, Proverbs 3:5–8 or James 1:5–8.
2. **First reading:** read slowly. Write down one word or phrase that stands out.
3. **Second reading:** ask what the passage plainly says. Who is speaking? What is happening? What instruction, promise or truth is actually present? Do not make it mean something unrelated to its context. If you are unsure, write: “I need help studying this.”
4. **Third reading:** ask what it might be saying to you. Write it down, and label it clearly: **what I sense, not what God said.**

**Then write one card:**

* **What I sense:** ______
* **I will test it with:** Scripture, time and these people: ______

Keep this card with your Session 3 cards. You will use it in Session 10 and in Lesson 20.

**Important:**

* An impression is only an impression until it has been tested.
* Do not act on anything that goes against Scripture, encourages harm, demands secrecy, makes you feel superior to others or pushes you to act in a rush.
* Small, reversible steps—such as arranging qualified advice, gathering information or asking for support—may be appropriate. Do not treat those steps as proof that the impression came from God. Big decisions can wait.
* If you hear a voice as though someone is speaking when no one is there, especially if it is repeated, commanding, frightening, confusing or affecting your daily life, do not treat it as guidance to obey. Stop the discernment exercise and speak with a qualified medical or mental-health professional. If a voice tells you to harm yourself or someone else, or you feel unable to stay safe, use **Get Help Now** and do not stay alone.
* If you sense nothing, you have not failed. God's direction may come later, through ordinary life.

### Close

Read these words slowly. Change any line you cannot say honestly.

> God has spoken in Scripture, and I will begin there.
> I have brought my question honestly, without demanding an immediate answer.
> Anything I sensed will be tested before I act.
> I will seek wise and qualified advice.
> I will not use God's name to justify harm, haste, secrecy or escape.

☐ I have finished this session.

### What comes next

**Take a break:** eat, rest and walk if you can.

**If you did Session 5 yesterday and still feel rested,** you may choose one flex session this afternoon: Session 8 — Remember Who You Are **or** Session 9 — Renewing Your Purpose. Not both. Resting instead is a good choice.

**Otherwise, rest until late afternoon, then begin Session 10.** Do not go into Session 10 tired.

**Continue: Session 10 — Going Home With a New Rhythm** *(or a flex session, if you have energy)*

## Audio script and transcript (shared by both versions)

*(About seven to eight minutes including the Bible reading. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Welcome to this session.

Before we begin, please remember two things. Silence does not guarantee a message from God. And whatever you sense today will be written down and tested before you act on it.

We have followed Elijah through this whole course. Today, we reach the most important part of his story.

Our Bible passage is First Kings chapter 19, verses 9 to 18, from the English Standard Version. Listen for what God says, and what God does.

There he came to a cave and lodged in it. And behold, the word of the Lord came to him, and he said to him, “What are you doing here, Elijah?” He said, “I have been very jealous for the Lord, the God of hosts. For the people of Israel have forsaken your covenant, thrown down your altars, and killed your prophets with the sword, and I, even I only, am left, and they seek my life, to take it away.” And he said, “Go out and stand on the mount before the Lord.” And behold, the Lord passed by, and a great and strong wind tore the mountains and broke in pieces the rocks before the Lord, but the Lord was not in the wind. And after the wind an earthquake, but the Lord was not in the earthquake. And after the earthquake a fire, but the Lord was not in the fire. And after the fire the sound of a low whisper. And when Elijah heard it, he wrapped his face in his cloak and went out and stood at the entrance of the cave. And behold, there came a voice to him and said, “What are you doing here, Elijah?” He said, “I have been very jealous for the Lord, the God of hosts. For the people of Israel have forsaken your covenant, thrown down your altars, and killed your prophets with the sword, and I, even I only, am left, and they seek my life, to take it away.” And the Lord said to him, “Go, return on your way to the wilderness of Damascus. And when you arrive, you shall anoint Hazael to be king over Syria. And Jehu the son of Nimshi you shall anoint to be king over Israel, and Elisha the son of Shaphat of Abel-meholah you shall anoint to be prophet in your place. And the one who escapes from the sword of Hazael shall Jehu put to death, and the one who escapes from the sword of Jehu shall Elisha put to death. Yet I will leave seven thousand in Israel, all the knees that have not bowed to Baal, and every mouth that has not kissed him.”

<#2#>

After God fed Elijah and let him sleep, Elijah travelled to a mountain called Horeb, and went into a cave. God's first words to him were a question: “What are you doing here, Elijah?”

Elijah answered that he alone was left, and that people were trying to kill him. The danger was real. But his tiredness and fear had narrowed what he could see.

Then came a powerful wind, an earthquake and a fire. And three times the Bible says the LORD was not in them. After the fire came something quiet. Our reading calls it “the sound of a low whisper.” Other Bible translations say “a still small voice” or “a sound of sheer silence.”

Then God asked the same question again, and Elijah gave the same answer.

Notice what God did next. He did not give Elijah a full explanation. He did not remove every danger. Instead, he gave him work to do. He gave him a helper, Elisha, to share the work. And he gently corrected him. There were seven thousand others in Israel who had stayed faithful. Elijah was not alone.

So when we listen for God, we may hope for an explanation, and receive a next step instead. We may hope to be told we were right, and discover that our picture was incomplete.

This passage does not teach that God always speaks quietly. In the Bible, God speaks in many ways. But it does warn us not to confuse something dramatic with something from God.

Here is another important point. God's correction is not the same as harsh self-criticism. If a thought says that you are hopeless, worthless or beyond help, that is condemnation, and it is not the voice you are listening for. God's correction is truthful, and it leads towards repentance and life.

So how does God guide us? Christians seek God's guidance through Scripture, prayer, conscience, wise advice from others, and careful attention to circumstances. Conscience can help us notice something, but conscience is not infallible. It can be shaped by fear, shame, culture, past teaching or scrupulosity, so it must also be tested. Of all these, only Scripture is our authoritative written guide. Much of what God wants us to know about how to live is already written. So before you listen for something new, ask what the Bible already says.

Everything else we sense needs to be tested. When the boy Samuel heard God calling him, he did not recognise it. He needed the older priest, Eli, to help him understand. Eli told him to say, “Speak, LORD, for your servant hears.” You are not meant to work out God's guidance alone either.

So test what you sense. Does it agree with the Bible, read in context? Does it reflect the character of Jesus? Does it hold up over time? What do wise people say? Does it respect safety and justice? And does it push you to act in a rush, or to keep it secret? A rushed feeling is a reason to slow down.

And please remember this. If you hear a voice as though someone is speaking when no one is there, especially if it is repeated, commanding, frightening, confusing or affecting your daily life, do not treat it as guidance to obey. Stop the discernment exercise and speak with a qualified medical or mental-health professional. If a voice tells you to harm yourself or someone else, or you feel unable to stay safe, get help straight away and do not stay alone.

Finally, if you listen and sense nothing at all, you have not failed. Faithful listening that hears nothing is still faithful. God's direction may come later, through ordinary life.

Take a slow breath. <#3#> Speak, Lord, for your servant hears.
