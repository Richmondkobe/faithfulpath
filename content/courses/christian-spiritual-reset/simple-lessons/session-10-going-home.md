# Session 10 — Going Home With a New Rhythm

*This file holds three versions of the same session. The learner sees only the version for the retreat they chose.*

---

## FULL VERSION (three-day retreat)

**Three-day retreat · Day 3, late afternoon · Core session 7 of 7**

### Before you begin

**Time needed:** at least an unhurried hour. If you become tired, take a short break rather than rushing.

**Have nearby:** your journal, a pen, a few blank cards, the envelope from Session 1 marked **Not until Day 3**, and your cards from Sessions 3 and 7 (and 8 or 9 if you did one).

Eat and rest before you begin. Do not come straight from another session.

**Keep private envelopes sealed.** This session does not reopen the Forgiven or Placed in God's care envelopes.

This session does not assume you completed every session. It recognises that you came, you stayed, and you are now preparing to return to ordinary life.

You may pause or stop at any time.

### Listen

**▶ Listen — about 8–9 minutes**

**[ Read the transcript ]**

### Pray

**▶ Guided prayer — about 2 minutes**

Use only the lines that fit you. You may leave any blank empty.

### Open the envelope

Now open the envelope from Session 1. Read the cards: the ordinary things you set down at the start.

Some may feel just as heavy. Some may feel lighter or clearer. Sort them using the same letters as Session 3:

* **A — Act:** mine to do
* **S — Share:** I should not carry this alone
* **E — Entrust:** I cannot control this
* **? — Ask for advice:** I am not sure

Not every card has to become a task again. Anything that no longer needs doing can be set aside.

**If a card concerns immediate danger, thoughts of suicide, serious medical needs, abuse or another crisis,** stop this exercise and use **Get Help Now** or contact the appropriate emergency or safeguarding service. Do not place an urgent safety matter on an Entrust card and leave it there.

### Be still

**Suggested: 10 minutes.** Choose a shorter time if you need to.

**[ Timer: 5 · 10 · 15 · 20 · 30 minutes ]**

Prayer phrase: **“Go, return on your way.”** Or: **“I am not alone.”**

This is the last silence of your retreat. For these few minutes, stop reviewing the retreat and stop planning what comes next. Simply be with the God who has been with you throughout it.

### Respond

**Do: four things to take home**

Write each on its own card.

1. **One burden to entrust:** the one thing you most need to stop trying to control. Add the words: *placed in God's care.*
2. **One action to take:** one small, faithful step you can do in the first two weeks home.
3. **One person or service to contact:** who you will contact in your first week, when you will do it, and how.
4. **One rhythm to keep:** one small practice for the next 30 days. Choose one:
   * a daily quiet of up to ten minutes with a psalm;
   * one protected hour each week with God, phone away;
   * one phone boundary you will keep;
   * a weekly time of rest from ordinary work;
   * a regular honest conversation with a trusted person.

   Write the day, time and place. Underneath, write: **“When I miss it, I will start again the next day without guilt.”**

If you are ready, read the four cards to God, aloud or silently.

**Keep the four cards somewhere you will see them.** Keep sensitive cards and sealed envelopes private and secure. If someone checks your belongings, do not carry anything that could put you at risk.

### Close

Read these words slowly. Change any line you cannot say honestly.

> I came as honestly as I could, and grace met me here.
> I am returning with some clarity and some unfinished questions.
> I will not carry ordinary life alone.
> I take one small rhythm and one next faithful step.
> My retreat experience is not the measure of God's faithfulness.

### A blessing

Receive these words from Scripture, aloud if you can:

> “The LORD bless you and keep you; the LORD make his face to shine upon you and be gracious to you; the LORD lift up his countenance upon you and give you peace.”
> — Numbers 6:24–26 (ESV)

☐ I have finished this session.

### What comes next

**Your retreat is over. Do not add anything to it.** Have a simple meal and pack slowly.

**Travel safely.** If you are tired, overwhelmed or unwell, delay travel or ask for help. Tell your support person your travel plan and when you arrive.

**A dip may happen.** Some people feel flat, irritable or discouraged during the first few days after a retreat. Others do not. Either response is possible, and neither determines whether the retreat was worthwhile. If you notice severe distress or safety concerns, seek help.

**Begin Part 4 on your first morning home, not before.**

**Continue: Lesson 20 — Test What You Think You Heard** *(tomorrow)*

## Audio script and transcript (shared by all three versions)

*(About eight to nine minutes including the Bible readings. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Welcome to the final session of the retreat plan or reset you chose.

This session does not assume that you completed everything. It simply recognises that you came, you stayed, and you are now preparing to return to ordinary life.

Our Bible passages are from the English Standard Version. First, First Kings chapter 19, verses 19 to 21, where Elijah leaves the mountain. Then Luke chapter 10, verses 17 to 20, where Jesus' disciples return from their mission.

So he departed from there and found Elisha the son of Shaphat, who was plowing with twelve yoke of oxen in front of him, and he was with the twelfth. Elijah passed by him and cast his cloak upon him. And he left the oxen and ran after Elijah and said, “Let me kiss my father and my mother, and then I will follow you.” And he said to him, “Go back again, for what have I done to you?” And he returned from following him and took the yoke of oxen and sacrificed them and boiled their flesh with the yokes of the oxen and gave it to the people, and they ate. Then he arose and went after Elijah and assisted him.

The seventy-two returned with joy, saying, “Lord, even the demons are subject to us in your name!” And he said to them, “I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven. Behold, I have given you authority to tread on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing shall hurt you. Nevertheless, do not rejoice in this, that the spirits are subject to you, but rejoice that your names are written in heaven.”

<#2#>

We have followed Elijah through this whole course. He was exhausted. God gave him food and sleep. Then God met him on the mountain, and told him, “Go, return on your way.”

And Elijah's very next step was to find Elisha, a companion to share the work. Elijah returned from the wilderness knowing that he was not alone.

Earlier in the course, we said that a retreat is different from running away because it intends to return. This is the returning. It matters just as much as the leaving.

Here is something important. When you return to ordinary life—or continue with the rest of your day—your usual demands will still be there. Old habits can return quickly. So this session is not about big resolutions. It is about a small rhythm, something simple you can repeat in ordinary weeks.

Jesus lived with this rhythm. He taught, healed and served. Then he withdrew to pray. Then he returned to the people again. That was a pattern for life, not a one-time event.

Now think about the second passage. The disciples returned from their mission full of excitement. Even the demons had obeyed them. Jesus did not dismiss their joy, but he redirected it. He said, “Do not rejoice in this, that the spirits are subject to you, but rejoice that your names are written in heaven.”

That matters for you today. Your deepest reason for joy is not how good your retreat felt. It is that, in Christ, you belong to God. If this retreat felt dry, that belonging has not changed. And if it felt rich, that feeling is not the foundation to build on. God's grace is.

Elijah went back with a companion. You also need safe and trustworthy support around you. That may be a friend, spouse, pastor, counsellor, doctor, support group or specialist service.

Two things to know as you return.

First, a dip may happen. Some people feel flat, irritable or discouraged during the first few days after a retreat. Others do not. Either response is possible, and neither determines whether the retreat was worthwhile. But if you notice severe distress, or any safety concern, please seek help.

Second, share carefully. You may want to tell everyone what changed. But some things still need testing. Tell your support person what would help them support you. Keep private things private, but bring anything significant to wise, qualified people rather than keeping it secret.

In this session, you will open the envelope you sealed at the beginning, sort what is inside, and choose four simple things to take home. One burden to entrust to God. One action to take. One person or service to contact. And one rhythm to keep.

Take a slow breath. <#3#> Go, return on your way. You are not alone.
