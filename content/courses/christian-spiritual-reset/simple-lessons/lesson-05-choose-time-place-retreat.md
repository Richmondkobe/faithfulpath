## Audio script and transcript

*(About five minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Many people sincerely want to spend unhurried time with God, but the retreat never happens.

Usually, this is not because they do not care.

It is because they never settle three ordinary questions.

Which retreat will I take?

Where will I take it?

And when will it happen?

In this lesson, you will answer those three questions.

Here is the main idea: a retreat does not need to be impressive. It needs to be protected.

Let us begin with the retreat plan.

The full retreat in this course lasts three days. If you can take three days safely and responsibly, I recommend it.

The first day gives you time to slow down and become present. The second day makes room for deeper honesty with God. The third day turns towards listening, responding and returning home.

The sessions are separated by meals, sleep, walks and generous periods of rest. The retreat is not three days of constant teaching or difficult exercises.

Ideally, the three days should be together, without your normal work between them.

If three days are not possible, that is all right.

The one-day retreat includes five shortened sessions with generous breaks. It gives you a meaningful retreat without trying to squeeze the entire three-day journey into one day.

The three-hour reset is a shorter first pause. It uses four brief movements from the retreat. It can help you stop, become present and notice what you are carrying.

The three-hour reset is valuable, but it is not the full retreat course. You may return later for the longer journey.

Choose the retreat you can genuinely protect—not the one that sounds most impressive.

A one-day retreat that happens is better than a three-day retreat that remains on your calendar for a year.

There are also four other ways to use this material.

You can spread the retreat across several days at home. You can use the couples format, take part with a group or church, or follow the version designed for pastors and Christian leaders.

These choices are kept under “Other retreat formats” so that this page remains simple. Open that section only if one of those formats fits your situation.

Next, choose your place.

Jesus often went to ordinary places to pray: a hillside, a garden or a quiet place away from the crowd.

You do not need an expensive retreat centre.

You may be able to use a quiet room at home, especially if you can set aside chores and interruptions. You might use a room at church, stay in a safe place offered by someone you trust, or choose a retreat centre or guesthouse if that is affordable.

The surroundings do not have to be beautiful. They need to support rest, prayer and safety.

Ask yourself four questions.

Can I be left mostly undisturbed here?

Do I feel safe here?

Can I meet my needs for sleep, food, medication and physical access?

Can I contact help if I need it?

If one answer is no, do not ignore it. Change the plan, solve the problem or choose another place.

The most important parts of a retreat do not require special accommodation. You need protected time, honesty with God, Scripture, rest and a suitable place to be quiet.

Finally, choose when the retreat will happen.

A retreat without a date is easy to postpone.

Choose a date and place it in your calendar. If you cannot choose an exact date yet, choose a target week and write down what must happen before you can confirm it.

If possible, make the hours before your retreat gentler. Finish urgent work, prepare what you need and avoid arriving already rushed.

Try to keep the hours after the retreat gentle too. Do not fill them immediately with demanding appointments if you can avoid it.

This may not be possible for everyone. Work, school, caregiving and family responsibilities are real. Do what is reasonable within your circumstances.

If you arrive tired, you are allowed to rest. Remember Elijah. God cared for his body before giving him further direction.

Now consider one question.

What could stop your retreat from happening, and what would help protect the time? <#5#>

Then take one practical step.

Choose your retreat plan: three days, one day, three hours or one of the other available formats.

Choose a place that supports your safety and practical needs.

Then choose a date or target week and write it somewhere you will see it.

You may change your plan later without losing anything you have already completed.

When you have made those choices, mark this lesson complete.
