You are not producing, solving or proving anything. When your mind reaches for a task or a reason to be useful, notice it, and return to your phrase. You may sit, stand or walk slowly.

### Respond

**Do: what I do, and what remains true**

1. **List your roles:** at home, at work, at church and in your community. Include informal ones, such as “the one who holds everyone together.”
2. **Beside each role, write why it matters.** Your roles are real callings. Do not skip this.
3. **Then write what remains true of you in Christ if that role changed or ended.**

**Truths from Scripture to help you:**

*The first truth belongs to every person as someone made in God's image. The remaining truths describe those who belong to Christ.*

* I am made in God's image. *(Genesis 1:27)*
* I am called a child of God. *(1 John 3:1)*
* God loved me before I improved. *(Romans 5:8)*
* In Christ, I am not condemned. *(Romans 8:1)*
* I am not a slave; I am an heir through God. *(Galatians 4:7)*
* Nothing can separate me from God's love in Christ. *(Romans 8:38–39)*

**Then write one card:** copy the three or four truths you most needed to read. Keep the card somewhere private where you will see it on a hard day.

*If these truths feel out of reach, do not force yourself to feel certain. Read them as what Scripture says. Consider bringing your page to a mature, safe Christian who will listen without pressuring you.*

**Important:** If feelings of worthlessness are persistent, affect much of your life, or come with hopelessness, loss of interest, major changes in sleep or appetite, or thoughts of death, please speak with a doctor or qualified mental-health professional. You do not need to wait until it becomes severe. If you are thinking about suicide or cannot keep yourself safe, use **Get Help Now** and do not stay alone.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About seven to eight minutes including the Bible readings. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Welcome to this session.

This session is gentler than the harder sessions of Day 2. Its question is simple. Who are you when your roles are set aside for a moment?

Our Bible passages are from Matthew's Gospel, from the English Standard Version. First, the end of the account of Jesus' baptism, in Matthew chapter 3, verses 16 and 17. Then, what happened straight afterwards, in Matthew chapter 4, verses 1 to 7.

And when Jesus was baptized, immediately he went up from the water, and behold, the heavens were opened to him, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and coming to rest on him; and behold, a voice from heaven said, “This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased.”

Then Jesus was led up by the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted by the devil. And after fasting forty days and forty nights, he was hungry. And the tempter came and said to him, “If you are the Son of God, command these stones to become loaves of bread.” But he answered, “It is written,
“‘Man shall not live by bread alone,
but by every word that comes from the mouth of God.’”
Then the devil took him to the holy city and set him on the pinnacle of the temple and said to him, “If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down, for it is written,
“‘He will command his angels concerning you,’
and
“‘On their hands they will bear you up,
lest you strike your foot against a stone.’”
Jesus said to him, “Again it is written, ‘You shall not put the Lord your God to the test.’”

<#2#>

Notice the timing. When Jesus was baptised, a voice from heaven said, “This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased.” This came at the beginning of his public ministry—before the recorded sermons, healings and miracles.

Jesus did not earn his place as the Son through public success. The Father's words declared who he already was. What Jesus did later expressed who he was. It did not create it.

Now notice what happened straight afterwards. In the wilderness, the tempter twice said, “If you are the Son of God…” He pressed Jesus to use his sonship on the tempter's terms: satisfy himself through power, put on a spectacle and test the Father's protection. In the third temptation, which Matthew records next, the tempter offered Jesus all the kingdoms of the world if he would bow down and worship him.

Jesus refused to let the tempter define what being the Son meant. He remained obedient to his Father and answered from Scripture.

This matters for tired people. Burnout can show how closely our worth has become tied to being useful, successful or needed. That does not mean your sense of identity caused your burnout. Burnout often has practical causes too. But when we are exhausted, we may discover how much we depended on our roles to feel we were worth something.

While the work goes well, we feel secure. But when the work stops, through tiredness, illness, losing a job, children leaving home, retirement, or the end of a ministry, a frightening question can appear. Who am I, if I am not doing this?

The words spoken at the baptism belong to Jesus in a unique way. He is God's eternal Son. But through Jesus, those who belong to him are truly welcomed into God's family.

The apostle John wrote, “See what kind of love the Father has given to us, that we should be called children of God; and so we are.” Notice those last words. Not “so we will be, once we have done enough.” So we are.

Paul says that those who belong to Christ are no longer slaves but children and heirs through God. Our place in God's family is received through Christ, not maintained by proving that we are useful.

This does not mean your roles are unimportant. You may be a parent, a pastor, a nurse, a carer or a provider. These are real callings. If you lose one, it is a real loss, and it is right to grieve it.

But your identity in Christ can hold you steady when a role changes or ends. It does not remove the grief. It reminds you that the role was never the whole of who you are.

This is not positive thinking, or repeating nice words until they feel true. It is what Scripture says about those who belong to Christ, whether or not we feel it today.

If calling God “Father” is painful because of your own history, you can use other words from Scripture, such as, “Nothing can separate me from the love of God in Christ.”

And if feelings of worthlessness are heavy and do not lift, please speak with a doctor or counsellor. Seeking help is not a lack of faith.

In a moment, you will spend some quiet time simply being with God. You are not producing, solving or proving anything.

Take a slow breath. <#3#> See what love the Father has given us.
