## Your cards, one kind at a time

*The descriptions below explain what each kind of card is for. You are not acting on every card now. For this lesson, choose only the action card from Session 10. Use the rest as guidance for later.*

* **Act:** complete one Act card during the next two weeks, beginning with the action chosen in Session 10.
* **Share** *(for later)*: ask a safe person or appropriate service for specific help. Share only the information needed to make the request safely. If the first person cannot help, that is information—not proof that you should carry it alone. Consider another safe person or qualified service.
* **Ask for advice** *(for later)*: make the appointment. The card is done when a date is booked, not when the decision is made. If you cannot book yet, take the next step, such as asking for a referral or joining a waiting list.
* **Entrust** *(for later)*: there is no task. Read it, pray it, and put it back.

**Important:** If an action involves apologising or contacting someone you have harmed, focus on the harm done to them, do not pressure them to respond, and **do not make contact if it would be unsafe, unwanted or legally restricted.** Seek advice first if you are unsure.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About five minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

A retreat or reset can begin a real change. But that change takes root through what you practise afterwards. The work of these next two weeks does not need to be dramatic. It simply needs to be real.

James wrote, “Be doers of the word, and not hearers only.” He compared someone who hears but does nothing to a person who looks in a mirror, walks away, and forgets what they saw.

That describes a common experience after a retreat or reset. For a short time, you see yourself clearly. Then ordinary life returns, and the picture fades, because nothing was done with it.

Jesus said something similar. He described two houses. One stood firm in a storm, and one fell. The difference was not what the builders heard. It was that one of them heard his words and did them.

During your retreat or reset, you heard Scripture and noticed possible next steps. This lesson is about responding to what is clearly wise and faithful—not treating every retreat impression as a command from God.

Think about Nehemiah in the Old Testament. When he heard that Jerusalem's walls were broken down, he wept and prayed for days. But he did not rush. He waited, he prayed, and when the moment came, he asked for exactly what he needed. Then he quietly looked at the walls himself before telling anyone. And the rebuilding was divided into small sections that families could manage. Prayer, then a plan. Small, specific steps.

Let us look at what each kind of card is for. You are not acting on every card now. For this lesson, you will choose only the action card from Session 10. The rest are guidance for later.

Your Act cards. During the next two weeks, complete one, beginning with the action you chose in Session 10. Not all of them. One. When it is done, you might take the next one later. The goal is not speed. It is for the cards to become things that actually happened.

Your Share cards. Burdens you should not carry alone will not share themselves. Ask a safe person or appropriate service for specific help. Share only the information needed to make the request safely. If the first person cannot help, that is information, not proof that you should carry it alone. Consider another safe person or qualified service.

Your Ask for advice cards. Make the appointment. The card is done when the date is booked, not when the decision is made. If you cannot book straight away, take the next step. Ask for a referral, join a waiting list, or find out the cost. If you have been putting off a medical or mental-health appointment, this may be the most important card you have.

Your Entrust cards have no task. That is the point. Read the card, pray it, and put it back.

If one of your actions is an apology to someone you have harmed, please take care. Focus on the harm done to them. Do not pressure them to respond. And do not make contact if it would be unsafe, unwanted or legally restricted. If you are unsure, seek advice first.

One more thing. Rest does not fix a structure. Many tired people rest well, and then walk straight back into the same pattern that emptied them. Remember Moses. His father-in-law, Jethro, told him that he was carrying too much, and helped him share the work. On this page, there are five questions to help you think about a heavy role. Then you can identify one safe and appropriate person who may be able to help change it. If raising it directly could expose you to retaliation, control or danger, seek independent advice first.

And if some of this does not work, that does not undo your retreat or reset. A missed plan can be restarted. A refused request is useful information. An appointment weeks away is still booked. Habits are built by starting again, not by never failing.

Now, a question to think about. What is one thing you are doing that you do not need to do, or that someone else could do? <#5#>

Before you schedule your action, check that it is small, safe and reasonably reversible. If it involves a major decision, significant money, leaving a role, confronting someone, reporting harm or contacting someone who may be unsafe, do not schedule the final action yet. Schedule a conversation with a wise or qualified person instead.

Then here is your step. Put the action from your Session 10 card into your calendar, with a day and time in the next two weeks. If it does not happen, reschedule it without guilt.
