> — 1 Kings 19:7 (ESV)

Elijah was exhausted. Before God gave him new direction, God gave him sleep, food and water. Your body also needs care while you rest and pray.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About five minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

When people imagine a spiritual retreat, they often think first about the spiritual things they should bring: a Bible, a journal, prayer notes and perhaps a book.

Those things can help. But your body is coming on the retreat too.

You will still need food, water, sleep, medication, warmth and safety. Caring for those needs is not a distraction from meeting with God. It is part of preparing to meet with him.

Think again about Elijah.

When Elijah reached the wilderness, he was frightened and exhausted. He lay down under a tree and wanted to die.

God did not begin with a long message. He let Elijah sleep. Then an angel gave him bread and water. Elijah ate, drank and slept again.

The angel returned and said, “Arise and eat, for the journey is too great for you.”

Only later did God speak to Elijah about what came next.

This story reminds us that we are whole people. What happens in the body can affect our thoughts, emotions and prayers. Hunger, pain, dehydration or a lack of sleep can make quiet reflection much harder.

So prepare simply.

For the retreat sessions, bring a Bible, a journal or notebook and two pens. If you are using printed retreat cards or workbook pages, put them together before you begin. You might also want an envelope for anything you choose to seal and read again later.

Next, prepare for your health.

Bring the medication and health equipment you normally use. Keep taking prescribed medication exactly as directed. A retreat is not the time to stop it, reduce it or experiment with a different dose.

If a doctor, pharmacist or other health professional has given you instructions, those instructions come before anything in this course.

Then plan your food.

Choose simple meals that are easy to prepare. Make sure they meet your allergies, dietary needs and medical needs. Bring enough food, even if you expect to have a quiet or less active day.

You do not need special retreat food. You do not need to make the meals impressive. The aim is to care for your body without turning meal preparation into another large task.

Keep drinking water where you can see and reach it.

Also think about caffeine. If you normally drink tea or coffee, suddenly stopping may cause headaches or leave you feeling more tired. Your retreat does not need to become a test of how many ordinary comforts you can remove. Unless a health professional has advised you differently, it may be wiser to keep your normal routine.

And let me say this clearly.

You do not need to fast to complete this retreat. Do not fast if it conflicts with medical advice, medication, pregnancy, recovery from an eating disorder, or your health needs.

Fasting can be meaningful for some people in the right circumstances, but it is not required here. For many people, setting a boundary around their phone will be a more helpful kind of fast.

Now think about rest.

Pack comfortable clothes, suitable shoes and something warm. Bring ordinary items that safely help you sleep, such as earplugs, an eye mask or prescribed equipment.

Do not cut down your sleep to fit in more sessions. More prayer exercises do not automatically create a better retreat. If your body needs rest, let it rest.

You may discover that you sleep more than expected, especially at the beginning. That does not mean you are wasting the retreat. Your body may be showing you how tired it has become.

If you feel well enough, a gentle walk can help you slow down and notice what is around you. But this is not the time to begin a demanding exercise plan or push through pain.

If you become unwell, pause the retreat. Follow your normal care plan and seek medical help when needed. Your retreat is paused, not ruined.

Finally, prepare for safety.

Keep your phone charged and make sure you can reach your support person. Write the retreat address, your support person’s number and the local emergency number on paper. That way, you can still find them if your phone stops working.

The optional workbook includes a Packing Checklist and an Emergency Information Card. You can print them if they would help. Nothing you write on them is stored by this course.

Now think about this question: What does your body usually need that you tend to ignore when you are busy? <#5#>

Then take one step. Work through the checklist on this page. You do not need to pack everything now. Simply gather what you can and make a plan for the rest.

Pack simply. Eat enough. Drink water. Take your medication as directed. Allow yourself to sleep.

Caring for your body is part of your retreat, not a distraction from it.
