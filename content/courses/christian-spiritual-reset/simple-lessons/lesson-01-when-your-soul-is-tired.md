## One truth to remember

> **Your tiredness does not make you a failure. Jesus invites tired people to come to him.**

## Key Scripture

> “Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.… and you will find rest for your souls.”
>
> — Matthew 11:28–29 (ESV)

Jesus speaks to people who are worn out and weighed down. He does not tell them to prove themselves before approaching him. He invites them to come and receive rest—including rest for their souls.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About five minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

You are still showing up.

You go to work. You answer messages. You care for people. You go to church.

If someone asked how you were, you might say, “Fine.” That would not exactly be a lie. Nothing may have fallen apart.

But somewhere underneath, something has gone quiet.

Prayer may feel like talking into an empty room. Things that once strengthened your faith may no longer move you. You may not be angry with God. You may simply feel tired in a place that sleep does not seem to reach.

If that sounds like you, please hear this first.

You are not a failure. You are not a fake Christian. You may be a tired person who has kept going for longer than you realised.

There is a difference between ordinary physical tiredness and what we might call soul-tiredness.

Ordinary tiredness may improve after sleep or a quieter day. Soul-tiredness can remain even after your body has rested.

It may appear in small ways.

You stop enjoying things that once helped your faith.

Worship music plays, but you feel little or nothing.

You become impatient with people you love.

You pray for other people out of habit, but you cannot remember the last time you spoke honestly to God about yourself.

These signs do not prove that your tiredness is spiritual. Exhaustion and emotional numbness can also be connected to stress, grief, physical illness or mental-health difficulties. This course cannot diagnose the cause.

If your exhaustion continues, affects your daily life or concerns you, speak with a doctor, counsellor or another qualified professional.

Many Christians respond to tiredness by trying harder.

They begin a new Bible-reading plan. They set an earlier alarm. They accept another church responsibility.

Sometimes those things help. At other times, they become more tasks that the tired person feels unable to complete.

What you need may not be another assignment. You may need unhurried time with God, with nothing to prove and nothing to perform.

The Bible is honest about deep exhaustion.

Think again about Elijah. He had just experienced one of the greatest victories of his life. Soon afterwards, he was sitting under a tree in the wilderness, asking God to let him die.

His collapse came after a victory, not a defeat.

Elijah had been living under danger and intense pressure. When he finally stopped, everything he had been carrying appeared to catch up with him.

Something similar can happen to us. We may not recognise how tired we are until we finally slow down.

Notice how God cared for Elijah.

God did not begin by telling him to try harder. He gave Elijah bread and water. He allowed him to sleep. Then he fed him again.

Only later did God speak to Elijah about what he should do next.

In Elijah’s story, rest came before direction.

Jesus also made room for rest and prayer. When his disciples were so busy that they did not even have time to eat, he said, “Come away by yourselves to a desolate place and rest a while.”

The Gospels also tell us that Jesus regularly withdrew to quiet places to pray.

If Jesus and his disciples made room to rest and pray, you are not weak for needing to do the same.

In today’s Key Scripture, Jesus says, “Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

He offers more than physical rest. He speaks about rest for your soul.

This retreat is one way of responding to that invitation.

It is not a way to force yourself to feel something. It cannot force God to answer a question. It is not a test of how spiritual you are.

It is a planned pause in which you can rest, pray and become honest with God again.

Now consider one question.

What is one thing you keep doing for God, even though you feel little or nothing when you do it?

Do not judge yourself or decide immediately that you should stop. Simply notice and name it. <#5#>

Then take one small step.

Write one honest sentence to God about how you are today. Write what you would say if nobody else could hear you.

Keep your sentence somewhere private and safe. You may return to it later in the retreat.

When you have written it, mark this lesson complete.

If you are thinking about suicide, believe you may harm yourself, or are in danger, please do not wait. Go to the Safety and Support page and seek help now.
