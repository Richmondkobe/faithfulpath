# Session 9 — Renewing Your Purpose

*This is a flex session. It has one version.*

---

**Flex session · Day 3 afternoon if you have energy, or after your retreat**

### Before you begin

**Time needed:** about 60–90 minutes.

**Have nearby:** your Bible, your journal, a pen, one or two cards or small pieces of paper, and your cards from Sessions 3, 7 and 8 if you have them.

It is optional. On Day 3, choose **Session 8 or Session 9, not both**, and only if you feel rested. Resting instead is a good choice. If you return to this session after the retreat, it can follow Session 8 or be used on its own.

**This is not a session for making a final decision about leaving your job, ministry or role.** Unless immediate safety requires action, record any larger question and test it after the retreat with wise and qualified people. This session is about something quieter: where purpose comes from, and how to renew it, or narrow it, when it has turned into heavy obligation.

You may pause or stop at any time.

### Listen

**▶ Listen — about 7–8 minutes**

**[ Read the transcript ]**

### Pray

**▶ Guided prayer — about 2 minutes**

Use only the lines that fit you. You may leave any blank empty.

### Be still

**Suggested: 15 minutes.** Choose a shorter time if you need to.

**[ Timer: 5 · 10 · 15 · 20 · 30 minutes ]**

Prayer phrase: **“Lord, you know everything.”** Or, if it is honest today: **“Lord, you know that I love you.”**

Let Jesus' question, “Do you love me?”, be asked once. Notice what rises: warmth, resistance, grief, longing or nothing. Whatever comes is information, not a verdict.

*If checking your own feelings or faith tends to become compulsive for you, do not keep repeating the question. Stay with “Lord, you know everything.”*

### Respond

**Do: a purpose for this season**

You are not writing a mission statement. Answer four short questions, then write one sentence.

1. Who or what is truly within my care right now?
2. What is not my responsibility?
3. What does love look like within my present strength and limits?
4. What support or boundary would help me keep going?

**Then write one card:**

> “In this season, I want to practise love for God and my neighbour by ______, within the limits and responsibilities I have now.”

Keep the blank **small and specific**. For example:

* “by being fully present with my children in the evenings”
* “by doing my work honestly and going home on time”
* “by caring well for my mother while she is still here”
* “by resting while my body heals and letting others help me”

Underneath, write: **One faithful thing:** one small, safe action for the next month. Resting, asking for help, booking an appointment or saying no to something non-essential can count.

Keep this card with your other cards for Session 10.

**If something larger came up,** such as a sense that a role should end or change, write it on a separate card marked **To test**, and keep it with your Session 7 card. It is not a decision. It is a question to test with wise people after your retreat.

**Important:** If you are carrying serious harm you caused, such as abuse, exploitation or a serious breach of trust, God's forgiveness does not automatically mean returning to a particular role or position. Seek qualified guidance before taking on any responsibility over others.

### Close

Read these words slowly. Change any line you cannot say honestly.

> My purpose begins in my relationship with Christ, not in proving my worth.
> I will practise love within the responsibilities and limits of this season.
> What has become too heavy, I will bring honestly for wise discernment.
> I do not have to change the world. I can take one faithful step.

☐ I have finished this session.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About seven to eight minutes including the Bible reading. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Welcome to this session.

Before we begin, please know that this is not a session for making a final decision about leaving your job or your ministry. Major decisions normally should not be made during a retreat. If immediate safety is involved, however, getting to safety comes first. This session is about something quieter: where our purpose comes from, and how it can be renewed.

Our Bible passage is John chapter 21, verses 15 to 19, from the English Standard Version. Listen for the questions Jesus asks, and notice what he does not ask.

When they had finished breakfast, Jesus said to Simon Peter, “Simon, son of John, do you love me more than these?” He said to him, “Yes, Lord; you know that I love you.” He said to him, “Feed my lambs.” He said to him a second time, “Simon, son of John, do you love me?” He said to him, “Yes, Lord; you know that I love you.” He said to him, “Tend my sheep.” He said to him the third time, “Simon, son of John, do you love me?” Peter was grieved because he said to him the third time, “Do you love me?” and he said to him, “Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you.” Jesus said to him, “Feed my sheep. Truly, truly, I say to you, when you were young, you used to dress yourself and walk wherever you wanted, but when you are old, you will stretch out your hands, and another will dress you and carry you where you do not want to go.” This he said to show by what kind of death he was to glorify God. And after saying this he said to him, “Follow me.”

<#2#>

On the night Jesus was arrested, Peter denied knowing him three times. Now, after the resurrection, on a beach, after breakfast, Jesus asks Peter three times, “Do you love me?”

The three questions seem to echo Peter's three denials. By the third question, Peter is deeply grieved. He answers, “Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you.”

And each time, Jesus gives Peter something to do. “Feed my lambs. Tend my sheep. Feed my sheep.”

Notice what Jesus does not ask. He does not ask, “Will you try harder?” He does not ask for a plan. He asks about love. Peter's renewed purpose grows out of his relationship with Jesus. His work does not buy that relationship. It flows from it.

And notice who receives this purpose. Someone who had just failed, publicly and badly. So a purpose from God is not a reward for success.

There is one important safeguard here. Peter's restoration does not mean that everyone who has caused serious harm should return to leadership. Forgiveness is real. But abuse, exploitation or a serious breach of trust may mean that some roles are no longer right, even when repentance is genuine.

Now notice what Peter's purpose was. “Feed my sheep.” It was not a title or a big strategy. It was care for people who belong to Jesus.

That was Peter's particular calling. Most of us should not call other people “my sheep.” But the principle still holds. Jesus said the greatest commandments are to love God with all your heart, and to love your neighbour as yourself. None of this needs a big platform. It can begin with small, faithful acts of love.

Burnout can change how we experience our purpose. Work that once felt meaningful can start to feel mechanical. Ministry becomes programmes. Parenting becomes a list of tasks. Care becomes a caseload.

Exhaustion, depression, trauma, illness and prolonged stress can numb our feelings. Caring while feeling little does not automatically mean that love has disappeared. It may mean that you need rest, treatment, support or a different way of carrying the responsibility.

Sometimes faithful love continues even when warm feelings are absent.

So renewing your purpose may mean finding meaning again in the work you already have. But it may also mean narrowing your role, sharing it, changing how it works, or, after careful discernment, eventually letting it go. Renewed love cannot make an unhealthy or unsafe situation healthy on its own.

Purpose renewed is sometimes purpose narrowed. Peter was told to feed sheep, not to run the world.

And for some people, this season's purpose is mainly to receive: to rest, to heal, and to let others help. Remember Elijah. He was fed before he was sent.

Jesus also tells Peter that following him will carry a real cost. Renewed purpose is not a promise that the path will be easy or successful. It is a call to follow Christ faithfully, including through limits and suffering.

The passage ends with two words from Jesus to Peter: “Follow me.” Our purpose is never a separate project. It stays connected to following Jesus.

In a moment, you will be quiet with Jesus' question: “Do you love me?” You do not need to produce a perfect answer or examine your feelings until you feel certain. Jesus already knows what is true. You may simply remain with the words, “Lord, you know everything.”

Take a slow breath. <#3#> Lord, you know everything.
