## FULL VERSION (three-day retreat)

**Three-day retreat · Day 1, afternoon · Session 2 of 7**

### Before you begin

**Time needed:** about 60–90 minutes, including a short walk.

**Have nearby:** your Bible, your journal, a pen and a drink.

You have had a break, a meal, and perhaps a rest. Your mind may drift back to the envelope. That is normal. Leave it sealed until Day 3, unless you realise you put an urgent safety, health or caregiving matter inside.

This session has the longest silence so far. If ten minutes was your limit this morning, stay there. Nothing here requires more.

You may pause or stop at any time.

### Listen

**▶ Listen — about 7–8 minutes**

**[ Read the transcript ]**

### Pray

**▶ Guided prayer — about 2 minutes**

### Be still

**Suggested: 15 minutes.** Choose a shorter time if you need to.

**[ Timer: 5 · 10 · 15 · 20 · 30 minutes ]**

Prayer phrase: **“Be still, and know that I am God.”** Or, shorter: **“God is here.”**

For the first minute, simply notice where you are: the chair beneath you, the light, the sounds around you. If noticing your body feels uncomfortable, look at an object in the room instead. Then turn to your phrase.

When your mind wanders, do not scold yourself. Notice where you went, and come back. The coming back is the practice.

### Respond

**Write *(optional)*:** Finish this sentence: “If I gave this moment my full attention, without trying to fix the past or plan the future, I would notice…”

**Do: a walk of noticing** *(20–30 minutes)*

Walk slowly outside if it is safe, or sit by a window or in a different room. Leave your phone out of your hand. If you carry it for safety, do not use it for photos, messages or music.

1. For a few minutes, notice only what you can **see**.
2. Then notice only what you can **hear**.
3. Then notice what you can **feel**: the ground, the air, the temperature.
4. If you are outside, notice what you can **smell**.
5. For the last few minutes, simply be where you are. Repeat your prayer phrase if it helps.

If focusing on one sense feels uncomfortable, choose a different one, or simply look around. You do not have to complete every step.

**If you feel unsafe, faint, confused or disoriented, stop.** Sit down or go to the nearest safe, familiar place and contact your support person if needed. Do not drive while you feel faint, confused or disoriented.

### Close

Read these words slowly. Change any line you cannot say honestly.

> God is a very present help.
> The storm is real, and Christ is with me in it.
> I am learning to be here, in this hour, in this place.
> Every time I wander, I will come back.

☐ I have finished this session.

### What comes next

**Day 1 is finished.** Have a simple meal and rest. Do not add another session. Sleep when you are tired.

Session 3 begins tomorrow morning.

**Continue: Session 3 — Put Down What You Are Carrying** *(tomorrow)*

## Audio script and transcript (shared by all three versions)

*(About seven to eight minutes including the Bible reading. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Welcome to the second session of your retreat.

Take a moment to settle. Notice the place where you are sitting. Notice the light, and any sounds around you. You are here.

Our Bible passage is Psalm 46, from the English Standard Version. Listen to it slowly. As you listen, notice how much noise and trouble there is in it.

God is our refuge and strength,
a very present help in trouble.
Therefore we will not fear though the earth give way,
though the mountains be moved into the heart of the sea,
though its waters roar and foam,
though the mountains tremble at its swelling. Selah
There is a river whose streams make glad the city of God,
the holy habitation of the Most High.
God is in the midst of her; she shall not be moved;
God will help her when morning dawns.
The nations rage, the kingdoms totter;
he utters his voice, the earth melts.
The Lord of hosts is with us;
the God of Jacob is our fortress. Selah
Come, behold the works of the Lord,
how he has brought desolations on the earth.
He makes wars cease to the end of the earth;
he breaks the bow and shatters the spear;
he burns the chariots with fire.
“Be still, and know that I am God.
I will be exalted among the nations,
I will be exalted in the earth!”
The Lord of hosts is with us;
the God of Jacob is our fortress. Selah

<#2#>

“Be still, and know that I am God” is one of the best-known lines in the Bible. People often put it on calm pictures of lakes and sunsets.

But listen again to the psalm. It is not calm at all. The earth gives way. Mountains fall into the sea. The waters roar. Nations rage, and kingdoms fall.

The words “Be still” come right in the middle of all that trouble.

The word translated “be still” can also mean “stop,” or “let go.” In the context of the psalm, the command appears to confront the raging nations and all human striving against God. It tells them to stop fighting against God, and to recognise who he is.

So this kind of stillness is not a calm feeling in a calm world. It is the stillness of a person who knows something true, even when the world is not calm.

And here is what the psalm says is true. “God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.”

A very present help. Not far away. Not waiting until the trouble is over.

This does not promise that you will always feel calm, or always sense God strongly. It means that no trouble puts you out of God's sight or care.

Now think about your own attention. Most of us are rarely in one place. Our body is in the room, but our mind is somewhere else. We think about tomorrow's meeting, or last week's conversation, or the people at home.

That is very normal. But it is also tiring, because we are in two places, and fully present in neither.

Jesus said, “Do not be anxious about tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself.” He was not shaming anxious people, or telling us not to plan. He was inviting us to stop carrying tomorrow's weight before tomorrow comes.

If you live with anxiety, becoming present may need professional help as well as prayer. There is no shame in that.

There is another picture of stillness in the Gospels. One night, Jesus and his disciples were crossing a lake in a boat. A great storm came, and the boat began to fill with water. The disciples were terrified. And Jesus was asleep.

They woke him, and he spoke to the wind and the sea: “Peace! Be still!” And there was a great calm.

The danger was real, and their fear was understandable. But the calm came from who Jesus is, not from a special technique.

So, during this session, your task is simple. You are not trying to make every storm stop. You are not pretending the storms are not there. You are bringing your attention back into this place, this hour and this body, and remembering that God is here too.

When your mind wanders, and it will, you do not need to scold yourself. Notice where you went, and come back. Each return is part of the practice.

In a moment, you will spend some time in quiet. If your retreat plan includes the guided prayer, listen to that first. After that, you will take a slow walk of noticing, or sit somewhere new, and pay attention to what is around you.

Take one slow breath. <#3#> God is here.
