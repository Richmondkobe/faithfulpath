## Key Scripture

> “Cast your burden on the LORD, and he will sustain you.”
>
> — Psalm 55:22 (ESV)

David wrote this psalm while deeply troubled. He does not say that every burden will disappear immediately. He trusts that God will sustain him so that he does not carry the burden alone.

## Three kinds of tiredness

* **Burnout or feeling worn out:** feeling drained after carrying too much stress or responsibility for too long.
* **Spiritual dryness:** a season when God feels far away and prayer feels difficult.
* **Emotional overload:** having more feelings or pressure than you can manage right now.

*These are pastoral descriptions, not medical diagnoses. You may recognise more than one.*

## Audio script and transcript

*(About five to six minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

So far, we have used words such as tired, dry and overloaded.

They can sound as though they mean the same thing. They do not.

Different kinds of tiredness may need different kinds of care. Before your retreat, it can help to notice what may be happening.

Naming what you are carrying is an honest first step.

Before we continue, I need to give you one important caution.

What I am sharing here is a pastoral description. It is not a medical diagnosis. Some of the experiences in this lesson can also be connected to grief, depression, anxiety, trauma, physical illness, medication, lack of sleep or other causes.

This lesson cannot tell you which cause applies to you. A retreat cannot diagnose or treat a health condition.

With that boundary in place, let us look at three kinds of tiredness.

The first is burnout, or simply feeling worn out.

In everyday conversation, people often use the word burnout when they feel drained after carrying too much stress or responsibility for too long.

You may have been giving more than you have been receiving. You may dread responsibilities that once felt meaningful. You may feel irritable or resentful. You may continue serving, working or caring for people while quietly wishing you could walk away.

Moses knew what it was like to carry too much.

He said to God, “I am not able to carry all this people alone; the burden is too heavy for me.”

God did not shame Moses for admitting this. He told Moses to gather seventy elders who could help carry the responsibility.

That matters.

If you are worn out, rest may help. But rest may not be the only thing you need. You may also need help, clearer limits or a change in the way responsibilities are shared.

Otherwise, you could rest for a few days and return to the same pattern that exhausted you.

The second kind of tiredness is spiritual dryness.

Spiritual dryness is a season when God feels far away and prayer feels difficult.

You may be physically rested and still feel spiritually dry. Prayer may seem to go nowhere. The Bible may feel like words on a page. Worship may stir little emotion.

The Bible is honest about experiences like this.

In Psalm 13, David asks, “How long, O LORD? Will you forget me forever?”

This is not the prayer of someone pretending everything is fine. It is the prayer of someone continuing to speak to God while feeling forgotten.

God preserved prayers like this in Scripture. They show us that honest faith does not always feel strong or certain.

Spiritual dryness is not always caused by personal sin. There may sometimes be something to confess, but you should not search endlessly for a hidden failure simply because God feels distant.

A retreat cannot force God to feel close. It cannot produce a spiritual experience on demand.

What it can give you is unhurried space to speak honestly with God and continue turning towards him, even if your feelings do not change quickly.

The third kind of tiredness is emotional overload.

Emotional overload means having more feelings or pressure than you can manage right now.

It may build slowly. You may be carrying grief you have not had time to feel, disappointment you have not spoken about or anger you have pushed aside.

People who are emotionally overloaded may still appear strong. They may continue working, serving and caring for others.

The pressure may appear through unexpected tears, strong reactions to small problems, difficulty thinking clearly or a flat feeling where both joy and sadness used to be.

The apostle Paul once described a period of suffering by saying that he and his companions were “so utterly burdened beyond our strength.”

Paul was describing severe hardship, not giving us a medical label. But his words remind us that faithful people can experience more pressure than they feel able to carry.

Psalm 55 says, “Cast your burden on the LORD, and he will sustain you.”

That does not mean every feeling will disappear. It means you can bring the truth of what you are carrying to God. You do not have to pretend that the weight is small.

You may recognise yourself in more than one of these descriptions.

You may be physically tired and worn out. You may feel spiritually dry and emotionally overloaded. You may also be unsure.

That is all right. You do not need to label yourself perfectly.

Remember that these descriptions cannot tell you whether you have a physical or mental health condition.

If you have felt low, empty or uninterested in life on most days for two weeks or more, speak with a doctor or qualified mental health professional.

You should also seek help if these feelings are affecting your work, studies, relationships, sleep, eating or ability to manage daily life. You do not need to wait two weeks if you are struggling to cope or want help sooner.

If you are thinking about suicide, believe you may harm yourself or cannot keep yourself safe, do not continue alone. Go to the Safety and Support page and seek help now.

A retreat can wait. Your safety cannot.

Now consider one question.

When did this tiredness begin?

Did it follow a particular event, or did it build slowly? <#5#>

You do not need to understand everything yet. Simply notice what you can.

Then take one small step.

Choose the words on this page that fit you best today: physically tired, worn out, spiritually dry, emotionally overloaded or unsure.

You may choose more than one.

Your choices are not saved, sent or monitored. They are only here to help you notice.

When you have chosen your words, mark the lesson complete.
