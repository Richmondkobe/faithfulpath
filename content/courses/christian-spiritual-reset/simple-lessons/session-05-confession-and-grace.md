# Session 5 — Confession and Grace

*This session is part of the three-day retreat only. It has one version.*

---

**Three-day retreat · Day 2 evening (only if settled) or Day 3 morning · Session 5 of 7**

### Before you begin

**Time needed:** about 60–90 minutes.

**Have nearby:** your Bible, your journal, a pen, one or two cards or small pieces of paper, an envelope and your cards from Session 3.

Begin only after real rest since Session 4, and when you feel settled enough. Eat, drink and take any medication as planned.

**This session is about grace.** It is not an interrogation. It is not a search for hidden sins to explain why God feels far away. Confession does not persuade God to become merciful. It is how we step honestly into the mercy God has already shown.

**If you live with scrupulosity** (repeated confessing, checking or seeking reassurance about sin), you may skip the written exercise and use only a short, familiar prayer that fits your treatment plan. Please follow the guidance of your clinician.

If your treatment plan tells you not to repeat confessions or seek reassurance, do not make the card or the Forgiven envelope. Follow your treatment plan instead. Skipping the exercise is not disobedience.

You may pause or stop at any time.

### Listen

**▶ Listen — about 7–8 minutes**

**[ Read the transcript ]**

### Pray

**▶ Guided prayer — about 2 minutes**

Use only the lines that fit you. You may leave any blank empty.

### Be still

**Suggested: 15 minutes.** Choose a shorter time if you need to.

**[ Timer: 5 · 10 · 15 · 20 · 30 minutes ]**

Prayer phrase: **“According to your steadfast love.”** Or, shorter: **“Have mercy on me.”**

Do not search for sins during the silence. Rest in God's mercy. An unwanted thought, image or memory is not automatically a sin. You do not need to confess a thought simply because it appeared.

### Respond

**Before you write, ask yourself:**

* What did I choose, and who was affected?
* Was this wrongdoing, a human limitation, a symptom of illness or stress, or a mix?
* Do I need confession, care, boundaries, treatment, repair, or more than one of these?

*Not every limitation is sin. Exhaustion, numbness, fear, illness, trauma responses and the need for boundaries are not automatically moral failures.*

If nothing comes to mind, that is an honest answer. Rest in God's mercy. Do not keep searching until you find something. Move to the closing words.

**Do: confession and assurance** *(optional)*

**[ Skip the written exercise ]**

1. On a card, write what you need to confess, in plain words, without excuses and without punishing yourself. Use general words. Do not record names or identifying details on the confession card.
2. If you are ready, read the card to God, aloud or silently.
3. Read these promises slowly:

> “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”
> — 1 John 1:9 (ESV)

> “As far as the east is from the west, so far does he remove our transgressions from us.”
> — Psalm 103:12 (ESV)

4. If your confession points to an action, such as an apology, a conversation or contacting a qualified person, write it on an **Act** or **Ask** card from Session 3, not on the confession card. On that card, write only the name or role of the person you will contact.
5. Put the confession card in an envelope, seal it, and write on the front: **Forgiven** and today's date.

The envelope is a reminder of God's promise. It does not have power of its own. You do not need to open it again. Nothing is destroyed.

**Important:** If what you remember involves harm to another person, abuse, criminal conduct, professional misconduct or someone's present safety, confession to God is not the end of your responsibility. If anyone is in immediate danger, use **Get Help Now** or contact the appropriate emergency or safeguarding service. Otherwise, seek qualified guidance about accountability, repair and any reporting duties. Before sharing details, ask what the person can and cannot keep confidential.

Do not record identifying details on the confession card. On an **Act** or **Ask** card, write only the name or role of the qualified person you will contact.

If this session brings panic, overwhelming shame or thoughts of harming yourself, stop. Sit down, name where you are and what you can see, and contact your support person. If there is immediate danger, use **Get Help Now**.

### Close

Read these words slowly. Change any line you cannot say honestly.

> God's steadfast love is the reason I can confess honestly.
> I have named what I can see, without excuses and without exaggeration.
> My forgiveness rests on God's faithfulness in Christ, not on my feelings.
> I will accept both mercy and the faithful steps that responsibility requires.

☐ I have finished this session.

**If you did this session on the morning of Day 3:** rest for at least an hour before continuing. Eat something and move gently.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About seven to eight minutes including the Bible reading. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Welcome to this session.

Before anything else, please hear this. This session is about grace. It is not an interrogation. It is not a search for hidden sins to explain why you feel tired or far from God. Confession does not persuade God to become merciful. It is how we step honestly into the mercy he has already shown.

Our Bible passage is Psalm 51, verses 1 to 12, from the English Standard Version. As you listen, notice how much of it is about God's character.

Have mercy on me, O God,
according to your steadfast love;
according to your abundant mercy
blot out my transgressions.
Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity,
and cleanse me from my sin!
For I know my transgressions,
and my sin is ever before me.
Against you, you only, have I sinned
and done what is evil in your sight,
so that you may be justified in your words
and blameless in your judgment.
Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity,
and in sin did my mother conceive me.
Behold, you delight in truth in the inward being,
and you teach me wisdom in the secret heart.
Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean;
wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.
Let me hear joy and gladness;
let the bones that you have broken rejoice.
Hide your face from my sins,
and blot out all my iniquities.
Create in me a clean heart, O God,
and renew a right spirit within me.
Cast me not away from your presence,
and take not your Holy Spirit from me.
Restore to me the joy of your salvation,
and uphold me with a willing spirit.

<#2#>

The heading of this psalm connects it with King David, after the prophet Nathan confronted him. David had abused his power as king. He took Bathsheba, and then arranged for her husband, Uriah, to be killed to hide what he had done. This was real and serious harm.

So Psalm 51 is not the prayer of someone with a vague feeling of being not good enough. It is the prayer of someone who did serious wrong and knew it.

And notice how David begins. Not with excuses. Not with a list of his failures. He begins with who God is. “Have mercy on me, O God, according to your steadfast love.”

That order matters. Our confession rests on God's character and the mercy he has given in Jesus, not on how strongly we feel or how perfectly we find the words.

Two things in the psalm need care.

First, David says to God, “Against you, you only, have I sinned.” That does not mean Bathsheba and Uriah were not harmed. Confessing to God and taking responsibility for harm done to people belong together. And forgiveness does not automatically remove the consequences of what we have done.

David also says that he was brought forth in iniquity and conceived in sin. He is not blaming his mother or saying that conception is sinful. He is acknowledging how deeply sin reaches into human life.

David also prays, “Take not your Holy Spirit from me.” This comes from David's particular history as Israel's king. It is not an instruction to keep checking anxiously whether God has abandoned you. The purpose of this session is honest confession and trust in God's mercy, not repeated reassurance-seeking.

Second, the psalm does not end in despair. David asks, “Create in me a clean heart, O God.” And he asks, “Restore to me the joy of your salvation.” He does not ask only to be tolerated. He asks to be made new. Joy may return slowly, and that is all right. If you do not feel relief straight away, it does not mean your confession did not count.

Now, a very important point, especially for tired people. Not every limitation is sin.

Exhaustion is not sin. Feeling numb is not sin. Fear, illness, unwanted thoughts and the need for healthy boundaries are not automatically moral failures. So please do not confess your tiredness as though it were cruelty.

Instead, ask simple questions. What did I choose? Who was affected? Was this wrongdoing, a human limitation, a symptom of illness or stress, or a mix of these? What do I need: confession, care, boundaries, treatment, repair, or more than one?

If nothing comes to mind, that is an honest answer. Rest in God's mercy.

The Bible gives us a clear promise. First John chapter 1 says, “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

If you live with scrupulosity, where confessing, checking or seeking reassurance becomes something you feel you must repeat, please do not use this session to create a new rule. You may skip the written exercise and pray a short, familiar prayer that fits your treatment plan.

And if what you remember involved harm to another person, abuse, crime, or someone's safety, confession to God is not the end of your responsibility. If anyone is in immediate danger, get help straight away. Otherwise, seek qualified guidance about responsibility, repair and reporting. Confession to God should never become a way of avoiding what needs to be faced openly.

In a moment, you will spend some time in quiet. Do not search for sins. Simply rest in God's steadfast love. If you remember something that needs attention, you can write it down afterwards.

Take a slow breath. <#3#> God's mercy was there before you began.
