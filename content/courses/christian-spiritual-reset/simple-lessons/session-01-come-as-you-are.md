## FULL VERSION (three-day retreat)

**Three-day retreat · Day 1, morning · Session 1 of 7**

*Optional: Watch Richmond's Day 1 introduction.*

### Before you begin

**Time needed:** about 60–90 minutes.

**Have nearby:** your Bible, your journal, a pen, a few cards or small pieces of paper, an envelope, and a drink.

You have arrived. Before you start, do the ordinary things. Unpack. Find where you will sit. Put your phone where you planned. If you are very tired, rest for an hour first. There is no prize for starting quickly.

Have your sentence from Lesson 6 nearby: "Lord, what I am carrying is…"

You may pause or stop at any time.

### Listen

**▶ Listen — about 7–8 minutes**

**[ Read the transcript ]**

### Pray

**▶ Guided prayer — about 2 minutes**

After the prayer, read your sentence from Lesson 6 to God, exactly as you wrote it. It does not need to be improved.

### Be still

**Suggested: 10 minutes.** Choose a shorter time if you need to.

**[ Timer: 5 · 10 · 20 · 30 minutes ]**

Prayer phrase: **"I am here."**

You are not trying to feel anything. You are letting yourself be seen. When your mind wanders, gently return to the phrase.

### Respond

**Write *(optional)*:** Finish this sentence: "The thing I most need to tell God honestly at the start of this retreat is…"

**Do: set things down**

1. Take three or four cards.
2. Write down the ordinary things that can wait: work tasks, emails, errands, and decisions that do not need to be made this week. Write one item per line.
3. Read the list once.
4. Put the cards in an envelope and seal it.
5. Write on the front: **Not until Day 3.**
6. Put the envelope away in your bag or a drawer. You will open it in Session 10.

**Important:** The envelope is only for things that can safely wait. Do not put a safety concern, a medical need, a crisis, or thoughts of harming yourself in the envelope. Deal with those now, as the Safety and Support page explains.

Your burdens, questions and grief do **not** go in the envelope. They are what the rest of the retreat is for.

### Close

Read these words slowly. If a line feels too much, say "Help me receive this" instead.

> I have come as I am.
> God sees me with compassion.
> I do not have to earn this time.
> I will practise putting down my need to perform.

☐ I have finished this session.

### What comes next

**Take a long break, ideally an hour or more.** Eat, rest, and move gently if you can. Then begin Session 2.

**Continue: Session 2 — Be Still and Become Present**

## Audio script and transcript (shared by all three versions)

*(About seven to eight minutes including the Bible reading. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed "Read the transcript" section.)*

Welcome to the first session of your retreat.

Before we begin, take a slow breath. You do not need to be ready in any special way. You only need to be here.

Our Bible passage is a story Jesus told, from the English Standard Version. It is often called the parable of the prodigal son. A parable is a short story that teaches something about God. Listen to it slowly.

And he said, “There was a man who had two sons. And the younger of them said to his father, ‘Father, give me the share of property that is coming to me.’ And he divided his property between them. Not many days later, the younger son gathered all he had and took a journey into a far country, and there he squandered his property in reckless living. And when he had spent everything, a severe famine arose in that country, and he began to be in need. So he went and hired himself out to one of the citizens of that country, who sent him into his fields to feed pigs. And he was longing to be fed with the pods that the pigs ate, and no one gave him anything.
“But when he came to himself, he said, ‘How many of my father's hired servants have more than enough bread, but I perish here with hunger! I will arise and go to my father, and I will say to him, “Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Treat me as one of your hired servants.”’ And he arose and came to his father. But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him. And the son said to him, ‘Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son.’ But the father said to his servants, ‘Bring quickly the best robe, and put it on him, and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet. And bring the fattened calf and kill it, and let us eat and celebrate. For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found.’ And they began to celebrate.

<#2#>

Jesus told this story to people who complained that he welcomed sinners and ate with them. He wanted them to see how God feels when someone who was lost comes home.

Think about the younger son. He took his share of his father's money and wasted it in a far country. When the money ran out, he ended up feeding pigs, hungry and alone.

Then he decided to go home. But notice what he did first. He prepared a speech.

He planned to say, "Father, I have sinned. I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Treat me as one of your hired servants."

Part of that speech was honest. He had done wrong. But part of it was a plan. He hoped to earn a place in the house as a worker. He could imagine being useful. He could not yet imagine being welcomed as a son.

Now listen again to verse 20. "While he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him."

The father did not wait for the speech. He ran. When the son spoke, he confessed his sin. But the line about becoming a servant is never recorded. Before any bargaining could begin, the father called for a robe, a ring, shoes, and a feast.

The father did not want a useful worker. He wanted his son.

Many of us come to God like that son. We arrive with a plan to make ourselves acceptable. We plan to be very disciplined, or very sorry, or very focused, so that by the end of the retreat we will have earned God's welcome.

But this story says something simpler. God moves toward us first, with compassion. His welcome is grace. Grace means receiving kindness from God that we could never earn. The welcome does not depend on your speech.

That is what "come as you are" means.

It does not mean that your struggles do not matter. It means that change begins when you are received honestly, not when you pretend to be better than you are.

Jesus said, "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden." That describes people. It is not a test they must pass. The tired version of you is invited. So is the distracted version, the doubting version, and the numb version. There is no other version of you to bring.

So in this first session, you do not need to create a special feeling. You do not need a perfect prayer.

If you already know of something wrong that you need to confess, you do not have to wait. Tell God honestly. But remember, your sorrow does not buy God's welcome. You are coming back to a God whose grace has already opened the door.

For now, only one thing is asked of you. Arrive honestly, just as you are, before a God who has seen you coming for a long time.

In a moment, you will pray. Then you will set down the ordinary things that can wait, so you can give your attention to God.

Take one more slow breath. <#3#> You are welcome here.
