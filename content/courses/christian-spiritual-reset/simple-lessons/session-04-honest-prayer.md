# Session 4 — Honest Prayer When It Hurts

*This session is part of the three-day retreat only. It has one version.*

**Three-day retreat · Day 2, afternoon · Session 4 of 7**

### Before you begin

**Time needed:** about 60–90 minutes.

**Have nearby:** your Bible, your journal, a pen, a drink and some tissues.

Take a real break after Session 3 before you begin. Do not start this session tired, hungry or straight after the cards.

This session may bring grief or anger closer to the surface. You do not have to force anything. **You are in charge of how far you go.** You may stop at any point and leave anything untouched.

**You may skip this session** if your loss is very recent, traumatic or overwhelming, or if memories and silence often bring panic, flashbacks or a feeling of being unreal. Choose rest, a safe person's company or supported prayer instead. You do not need to work through grief on the retreat's timetable.

### Pray

**▶ Guided prayer — about 2 minutes**

Use only the lines that fit you. You may leave any blank empty.

### Be still

**Suggested: 10 minutes.** This is shorter than before. If you are already tired, five minutes is enough, or none.

**[ Timer: 5 · 10 · 15 · 20 · 30 minutes ]**

Prayer phrase: **“How long, O LORD?”** Or, if that feels too raw: **“Consider and answer me.”**

If tears come and you feel safe enough, you may allow them. If you begin to feel overwhelmed, stop and ground yourself. If nothing comes, that is also all right.

### Respond

**Do: write your own lament** *(optional)*

A lament is honest prayer that tells God what hurts. Choose the safest level for you today. A longer prayer is not a better prayer.

* **One or two sentences:** tell God what is wrong, and make one request.
* **Broad words:** use the four parts below, with general words and no names or details.
* **More detail:** use the four parts below, in whatever detail feels safe.

**The four parts:**

1. **Speak to God.** Address him honestly—with a biblical name such as God, Lord, Father or Jesus. Use the form of address that helps you speak truthfully.
2. **Say what is wrong.** You may ask, “How long?” You do not have to soften it.
3. **Ask for what you want.** Say what you truly want, not what you think you should want. Naming a desire is not acting on it.
4. **Trust, if you can.** If you cannot, write “not yet,” or the smallest truth you can hold, such as “I am still speaking to you.” A blank fourth part is still an honest prayer.

If you are ready, read your lament to God aloud or silently. You may also close your journal and leave it for another day. Either way, **keep it.** Do not tear it up. You may want to read it again in the weeks ahead.

You do not have to finish it today. An unfinished lament is still prayer.

**Important:** If this session brings panic, a feeling of reliving a memory, or a feeling of being unreal, stop. Sit down, open your eyes, name where you are and what you can see, and contact your support person if needed. If you are thinking about suicide, may harm yourself or someone else, or cannot keep yourself safe, stop the retreat and use **Get Help Now**. Do not stay alone.

### Close

Read these words slowly. Change any line you cannot say honestly.

> I have spoken as honestly as I can today.
> My grief does not keep me out of God's presence.
> I do not have to be finished with sorrow in order to pray.
> Where trust is hard, I will stay with one honest word, or receive support until words return.

☐ I have finished this session.

## Audio script and transcript

*(About seven to eight minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Welcome to this session.

Before we begin, please hear this. You are in charge of how far you go today. You may stop at any time. You may leave anything untouched.

This session is about lament. A lament is honest prayer that tells God what hurts.

Our Bible passage is Psalm 13, from the English Standard Version. It is only six verses. Listen for the moment when the psalm turns.

“How long, O LORD? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me? How long must I take counsel in my soul and have sorrow in my heart all the day? How long shall my enemy be exalted over me?

Consider and answer me, O LORD my God; light up my eyes, lest I sleep the sleep of death, lest my enemy say, ‘I have prevailed over him,’ lest my foes rejoice because I am shaken.

But I have trusted in your steadfast love; my heart shall rejoice in your salvation. I will sing to the LORD, because he has dealt bountifully with me.”

<#2#>

Many Christians are surprised to learn how many psalms are laments. They are one of the largest groups in the book of Psalms. The prayer book of the Bible is full of complaint, grief and hard questions. And God kept every one of them.

So if you have been taught, in words or by example, that your prayers should always sound positive, the psalm writers were not taught that.

Psalm 13 shows a simple pattern. First, the writer speaks to God directly. Then he says what is wrong. Four times, he asks, “How long?” Then he asks God for help: “Consider and answer me. Light up my eyes.” And finally, he turns to trust: “But I have trusted in your steadfast love.”

Notice what the psalm does not do. It does not skip the hard questions to reach the happy ending. It does not apologise for them. And the trust at the end does not mean the pain has gone. It shows that trust can be spoken while things are still not resolved.

Not every lament reaches that turn. Psalm 88 ends in darkness. There is no happy ending, and God kept that psalm too. So if your prayer today does not reach trust, you have not prayed wrongly.

Many of us carry grief that has never had time or permission. Some losses are obvious: a death, a diagnosis, a divorce, a miscarriage. Others are harder to see. A ministry that did not become what you hoped. A friendship that faded. A church that hurt you. A body that no longer does what it used to do. The person you expected to be by now. These losses rarely have a funeral. So they are carried quietly, sometimes for years.

Jesus wept at the tomb of his friend Lazarus, even though he knew he would raise him. And on the cross, Jesus prayed the words of Psalm 22: “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?” If lament was right for Jesus, it is right for you.

The Bible also lets us bring anger and confusion to God, instead of hiding them behind religious words. Job argued with God. Jeremiah said he felt deceived. A psalm even asks God, “Why are you sleeping?”

Honest prayer does not mean every conclusion we reach in pain is correct. It means we bring what we really think to God, where it can be heard and, over time, changed.

If you cannot find words at all today, that is all right. Your silence does not put you out of God's reach. You may need another person's company before words come.

In this session, you may write your own short lament. You can choose how much to write. You can keep it very general. And you can leave the ending blank.

If at any point this becomes more than you can manage, stop. Sit down, open your eyes, and notice where you are. Contact your support person if you need to. And if you are thinking about suicide, or cannot keep yourself safe, stop the retreat and get help straight away. Please do not stay alone.

Take a slow breath. <#3#> God is not afraid of your honest prayer.
