# Session 3 — Put Down What You Are Carrying

*This file holds three versions of the same session. The learner sees only the version for the retreat they chose.*

## FULL VERSION (three-day retreat)

**Three-day retreat · Day 2, morning · Session 3 of 7**

*Optional: Watch Richmond's Day 2 introduction.*

### Before you begin

**Time needed:** about 60–90 minutes.

**Have nearby:** your Bible, your journal, a pen, several cards or small pieces of paper, an envelope and a drink.

Good morning. If you did not sleep well, that is not a failure. Eat, drink and take any medication as planned before you begin. Do not start this session hungry or unwell.

This session begins the deeper part of the retreat. It may bring up heavy things. If it becomes more than you can manage, stop. Let safety, not finishing, decide your next step.

You may pause or stop at any time.

### Listen

**▶ Listen — about 7–8 minutes**

**[ Read the transcript ]**

### Pray

**▶ Guided prayer — about 2 minutes**

### Be still

**Suggested: 15 minutes.** Choose a shorter time if you need to.

**[ Timer: 5 · 10 · 15 · 20 · 30 minutes ]**

Prayer phrase: **“Come to me, and I will give you rest.”** Or, shorter: **“Gentle and lowly in heart.”**

You do not need to solve anything during the silence. Simply be with Jesus, who said, “Come.”

### Respond

**1. Make a list** *(about 10 minutes)*

Write down everything you are carrying right now: responsibilities, worries, fears, guilt, hopes for other people and situations that are not settled. Include small things. Do not sort them yet.

If something feels too large or painful to write in detail, write only one general word, such as “grief” or “something for professional support.”

**2. Sort your list.** Beside each item, write the letter that describes your next step. You may use more than one letter if necessary.

* **A — Act:** something that is mine to do. *What is the next faithful step?*
* **S — Share:** something I should not carry alone. *Who should help me?*
* **E — Entrust:** something I cannot control. *What am I trying to guarantee that belongs to God or to someone else?*
* **? — Ask for advice:** I am not sure whose responsibility this is. *Whose wise help do I need?*

**3. Write your cards.** Write one card for each next step you intend to take. One concern may therefore appear on more than one card. You do not have to make a card for everything today. Use these starting words, or your own:

* **Entrust:** “I cannot control ______. I give the outcome to God. Show me any faithful step that is still mine.”
* **Share:** “I need help carrying ______. I will talk to ______.”
* **Act:** “My next faithful step is ______. I do not have to solve everything today.”
* **Ask:** “I will not decide this alone. I will ask ______ for advice.”

**4. Hand them over.** Read each card to God. Then place the **Entrust** cards face down under your Bible or in an envelope marked with today's date. Keep the other cards together. You will use them in Session 10.

Placing the cards face down is simply a physical reminder of the prayer you have made. You may pick them up again whenever you need to act, seek help or reconsider what is required. Nothing is destroyed.

**Write *(optional)*:** Was anything on your list placed on you “in God's name” that feels like a heavy burden Jesus did not give you?

**Important:** Putting something down is not pretending that harm did not happen. If you are carrying abuse, injustice or serious harm, this session does not ask you to let it go, stay silent, remove a safety plan or excuse the person responsible. Write only a general word if that feels safer. If there is immediate danger or urgent risk, stop this exercise and use **Get Help Now**. Otherwise, speak with an appropriate trusted or qualified person.

If a card brings up more than you can manage, stop and set it aside. It can wait.

### Close

Read these words slowly. Change any line you cannot say honestly.

> I have come to Christ, who gives rest.
> I let go of trying to control what is beyond me.
> I will ask for help with what I should not carry alone.
> I will take the next faithful step in what belongs to me.
> Christ is gentle and lowly in heart, and I am learning his way.

☐ I have finished this session.

### What comes next

**Rest for at least an hour before Session 4.** Session 4 is about honest prayer and grief, and it should not follow straight after this one. Eat, drink, move gently or sleep. Begin Session 4 only when you feel settled.

**Continue: Session 4 — Honest Prayer When It Hurts** *(after a long break)*

## Audio script and transcript (shared by all three versions)

*(About seven to eight minutes. Read calmly and naturally. On the course page, place this inside the closed “Read the transcript” section.)*

Welcome to this session.

Before we begin, take a slow breath. If you are hungry or thirsty, it is all right to pause this recording and look after yourself first.

This session asks you to look honestly at what you have been carrying. Some of it may feel heavy. You may stop at any time.

Our Bible passage is Matthew chapter 11, verses 28 to 30, from the English Standard Version. You heard part of it in Lesson 1. Listen to it again, slowly.

“Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.”

<#2#>

Sometimes a verse is so familiar that we stop really hearing it. So let us listen to it in three parts.

First, Jesus says, “Come to me.” Rest begins with a person, not a technique. And Jesus does not list which burdens are allowed. All who are weary are invited.

Some of what you carry may even have been placed on you in God's name, by people who asked too much and helped too little. Jesus spoke against religious leaders who did that. Whatever you are carrying, he still says, “Come.”

Second, Jesus says, “Take my yoke upon you.” A yoke was a wooden frame that joined two animals together so they could pull a load. Notice that Jesus does not say, “I will take away every yoke.” He invites us to take his yoke, to become his disciples, and to live under his gentle leadership.

So the difference is not that there is nothing to carry. The difference is the kind of master we serve. Jesus says, “I am gentle and lowly in heart.” He does not drive his followers with fear, shame, or endless pressure to prove themselves. If you have been driving yourself that way, you may have been carrying someone else's yoke.

Third, Jesus says, “Learn from me.” His rest is not only a short break. It is a new way of living, learned slowly with him. You will not learn it all today. But you can begin.

Now, a word about what it means to put something down. Some people hear that and think, “I must drop all my responsibilities.” Then they feel guilty, because their children still need feeding and their work still needs doing. That is not what Jesus asks.

In this session, putting things down means four things.

Some things are yours to do. For those, you take the next faithful step.

Some things are too heavy to carry alone. For those, you ask someone to help you.

Some things are outside your control, such as another person's choices or the final outcome of a situation. For those, you entrust the outcome to God. That means you stop trying to guarantee what only God, or another person, can decide.

And some things are unclear. For those, you ask for wise advice before you decide.

The apostle Peter wrote that we can cast all our anxieties on God, because he cares for us. That does not mean our responsibilities disappear. It means we no longer carry them as if everything depends on us. And if you live with an anxiety disorder, needing treatment does not mean you have failed to trust God. Medical care, practical help and faith can all belong together.

There is one more important point. Putting something down is not the same as pretending that harm did not happen. If someone has abused you or seriously wronged you, this session is not asking you to let that go, stay silent, remove a safety plan, or excuse the person responsible. Those wounds deserve their own care, with the right people.

On this page, you will find simple steps to follow. You will write down what you are carrying, sort it, and bring it to God.

Take one slow breath. <#3#> Jesus says, “Come to me.” You are welcome here.
