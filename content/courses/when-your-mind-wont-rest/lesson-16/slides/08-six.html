THE TOOL · THE SIX-MOVEMENT PRAYER

1 · Be present

Be still before you speak.

2 · Name the reality

What is known. The event, not the chain.

3 · Pour out your heart

Fear, grief, anger, desire. Untidied.

4 · Make your request

Specific, and open to God's will. Once.

5 · Remember and discern

What Scripture says. What is mine today.

6 · Entrust the outcome

In words. Then rise and act.

Lesson 16 · Prayer That Does Not Become More Worry · Slide 8 of 12

So here is the shape, drawn from the themes Psalm 62 holds together. It is not a formula. It is somewhere for a prayer to go when it keeps turning into rehearsal. One: become present. Be still for a moment before you speak; you are coming before a person who already knows what you will say. Two: name the reality. State what is actually known, in a sentence or two. Not the chain of consequences; the event. Three: pour out your heart. Say honestly what you feel: fear, grief, anger, desire. Do not tidy it. God has read the psalms too. Four: make your request. Ask specifically, and remain open to God's will. You may ask again tomorrow; that is intercession. What you need not do is ask again within the same prayer to feel that the asking has taken. Five: remember and discern. Return your attention to what Scripture clearly says, and name what is yours to do today: act, wait, seek help, or surrender. Six: entrust the outcome. Place what you cannot control into God's care, deliberately and in words, and then rise and do the thing from movement five. The refuge is real whether or not you feel it yet.
