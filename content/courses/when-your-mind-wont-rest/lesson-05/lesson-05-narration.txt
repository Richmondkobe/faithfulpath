Welcome to Lesson 5, the last lesson in Module 1. Here is today's question. When did you last ask what your body is telling you? Most overthinkers have examined their thoughts, their faith and their fears in great detail, and have never once asked whether their body is part of the noise. The teaching takes about eight minutes. Near the end, you will pause for one small practice, so have a pen nearby.

Here is what you will learn today: how to check your body before you interpret your mind, and when a pattern belongs to a doctor rather than a journal. And one thing I must say before we begin, because the story in this lesson includes a man who asked God to let him die. If you have had thoughts of not wanting to be alive, or of harming yourself, please do not treat that as tiredness. Use the "Get help now" link under this lesson today, and tell someone safe who is near you. This lesson can wait. You cannot.

Our verse is 1 Kings 19, verse 5, in the English Standard Version. "And behold, an angel touched him and said to him, 'Arise and eat.'" This is Elijah, straight from Mount Carmel, where fire fell from heaven. Then Jezebel threatens his life, he runs into the wilderness, sits down under a broom tree, and asks God to let him die. And the first thing God sends is not a sermon. It is a touch, bread, water, and sleep.

Let me tell you about a pastor I will call Joseph. He phoned me late one evening in what he called a spiritual crisis. He had been questioning his calling for a week. He felt distant from God. He had begun to wonder whether the last fifteen years had been a mistake. I listened for a long time. Then I asked some questions that had nothing to do with his calling. How much had he slept? Four or five hours a night, maybe less. When had he last taken a day off? He could not remember. Had he eaten properly? Something, at some point. And he had been fighting a chest infection for two weeks, drinking a great deal of coffee to push through it. I did not tell him his crisis was imaginary. I told him I did not think either of us could hear his questions clearly until he had slept, eaten, and let his body recover, and I asked him to see a doctor about the infection.

Look at the order of what God did for Elijah. He did not rebuke him for his despair. He did not correct his theology or remind him of Carmel. Elijah slept. An angel woke him with bread and water. He ate and slept again, and was fed a second time, "for the journey is too great for you." Only after that, and forty days of travel, did he arrive at Horeb, where God finally asked, "What are you doing here?" Only then did the conversation about his fear and his future begin. The passage does not teach that food and sleep cure despair; Elijah's questions still had to be answered, and they were. But the order matters. God did not treat his prophet as a soul without a body.

Here is the main truth. You are not only a mind. God made your body and called it good. Jesus grew tired, slept in boats and asked for water. Scripture is not embarrassed by the fact that people who are hungry, exhausted or ill think and feel differently from people who are fed and rested. So be slow to draw conclusions about your faith or your future from an exhausted, hungry or unwell body. Ask first: what is my body telling me?

So, without pretending to be a doctor, here are the things to check. Sleep: a problem that looks manageable at noon can look overwhelming at three in the morning. Weeks of stress: a body braced for a long time produces a scanning, vigilant mind. Skipped meals. Illness or pain, which drain the reserves that keep worry in proportion. Caffeine, which in some people adds restlessness and a faster heartbeat. Medication, which can affect mood and sleep, especially when it is started, stopped or changed; and please, never change a medication because of anything in this course. Speak to the prescriber or a pharmacist. And long seasons without rest, the tiredness that one night's sleep does not fix. None of these is a sin. Nobody has failed God by being tired or unwell. But each one can make a restless mind more restless.

I want to be as clear here as anywhere in this course. Not every spiritual struggle has a physical cause. Some anxious thinking comes from real spiritual questions, real sin, a wound, grief, or the fears we met in Lesson 4, and caring for the body does not replace caring for those. Elijah still needed Horeb. And food and sleep do not cure anxiety disorders. If you live with an anxiety condition, depression, obsessive-compulsive symptoms or trauma, better sleep and regular meals may help you cope, but they are not treatment. Treatment is what qualified professionals provide, and it may include therapy, medication or both. Please do not let anyone, including this lesson, tell you that you have simply not been resting enough. Physical causes and spiritual realities are not rivals.

Joseph slept eleven hours that night and saw his doctor the next day. The questions about his calling did not disappear. But when we spoke the following week, they had become smaller and more specific, and he could think about them without despair. His body had been part of the story all along. He had simply not been reading that part.

Now it is your turn. Take your pen. Write down one thing for your body that you have been putting off. An earlier night. A proper meal. A walk. The appointment you have been avoiding. Just one. Then do it today, and treat it as an act of faith, not an interruption to it. If you are unsure whether something belongs to a doctor, that is a reason to book the appointment, not to think about it more. Pause the audio now and write your one thing. Press play when you are ready. ...

One thing to remember. The body is part of the story, not the whole of it. If God gave his exhausted prophet bread and sleep before he gave him a conversation, then you may care for your body without guilt, and you should not treat yourself, or anyone else, as though the body were irrelevant to the mind. That is the end of Module 1. You have learnt to notice what is happening: the circling, the seven kinds, the shame, the fear beneath, and the body. Module 2 will name the patterns one by one.

Let us pray. Father, you made my body and called it good. You fed Elijah before you questioned him. Forgive me for treating my tiredness as a failure and my body as an inconvenience. Teach me to rest, to eat, to ask for help, and to call it faith. And where my struggle is more than tiredness, give me the courage to seek the help I need. In Jesus' name. Amen. ...
