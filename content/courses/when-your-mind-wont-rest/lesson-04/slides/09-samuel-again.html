Lesson 4 · The Fear Beneath the Thought · Slide 9 of 12

Finding the fear changed what Samuel's work was. He still finished the spreadsheet, made his adjustments and gave his presentation. What changed was that he stopped expecting the spreadsheet to solve a fear of rejection. For the fear, he began asking different questions. Is this fear telling the truth? Who has actually rejected me, and who has not? What does God say about it? "Will the elders be happy with the numbers?" cannot be settled in advance, so the mind circles it. "Am I secure in Christ whether or not the elders are happy?" can be answered, and Scripture answers it. That does not make rejection painless. It means another person's response is not the final verdict on your worth. When Samuel finally said the sentence out loud, "They wouldn't want me," he could see it did not match what he actually knew. His elders had shown him nothing but confidence for years. The fear had been enormous in the dark, and became something he could examine in the light.
