# The 30-Day Mind-Renewal Journey

This journey is for the member who has completed the foundation course or read the book, and now wants to live it, a little at a time. It takes the practices from the twenty-one lessons and spreads them across four weeks: a week of noticing, a week of testing, a week of releasing, and a week of practising, with two days at the end for review and commitment.

It is best used after the foundation course, but it is not locked, and prior completion is recommended rather than required.

> **Please read before Day 1**
>
> This is a pastoral formation plan, not a mental-health treatment programme. It is not designed to function as self-directed cognitive behavioural therapy, exposure therapy, or treatment for OCD, trauma, depression or an anxiety disorder. If a qualified professional is already supporting you, their guidance takes priority over the exercises and the limits in this journey. If at any point you cannot keep yourself safe, stop and use "When This Course Is Not Enough" in Start Here.

*Displayed before Day 1 can open on a member's first entry, with a single **Continue to Day 1** button. No agreement, checkbox or personal response is required, and the support pages and course home remain open. The notice stays accessible from the journey home afterwards.*

## How to use the journey

**Not every day is a worksheet.** Each week has roughly four practice days, two light days and one full rest day, and only two or three of the practice days ask for a written worksheet. The others ask for a brief observation, a single practical action, or ordinary life with no analysis at all. That is deliberate. A course about a restless mind should not ask you to examine your mind every day for a month. Each day is marked **Worksheet**, **Brief**, **Action**, **Ordinary** or **Rest**.

**Fifteen to thirty minutes.** Brief days need only a few minutes. Full worksheets may take longer. Stop at thirty minutes unless you are completing a practical safety or professional-care step; you may continue another day without treating the exercise as incomplete.

**Days do not have to be consecutive.** Thirty days may take you six weeks or longer, and that is still the journey. If you miss a day, do the next one; do not go back and catch up, and do not restart from Day 1. The grace clause of Lesson 20 applies here: a missed day is a day.

**The journey is adaptable, not compulsory.** You do not need to complete all thirty days to benefit. If daily attention to your thoughts increases distress or self-monitoring, reduce it to three days a week, use only the practices your professional recommends, or stop and seek support. Stopping is a legitimate use of the journey.

**Once for today, then stop.** Unless a day specifically invites continued practice, complete the written exercise once for that day and then stop. This is a practical boundary, not a spiritual rule, and a professional treatment plan may instruct you differently. Persistent prayer is never limited by it.

**Some days are optional.** A few exercises, marked as optional, touch on memory, fear or decisions that not every member has or should reach for. If nothing comes to mind, or if the exercise is not for you this month, write "not today" and move on. That is a legitimate result.

**The tracker is optional.** The toolkit has a 30-Day Tracker: one line per day for the date and a word or two. Use it only if it helps you see the month without scoring yourself. If you find yourself repeatedly checking, correcting or analysing it, stop using it.

## How the days are marked

Each day can be marked **Complete**, **Skip for now**, **Not appropriate for me**, or **I need support**. You can change or clear any of these at any time. "I need support" opens Finding Help Where You Live; it does not send a notification, create a support request or alert Faithful Path.

Your progress reads as *"x of 30 days visited"*. A day counts as visited when you open it, whatever you mark afterwards, and opening a day never marks it complete. There are no streaks, no overdue notices and nothing to catch up.

## The weeks

- **Week One: Notice** (Days 1–7). The first week asks nothing of you except attention. You are not fixing anything yet.
- **Week Two: Test** (Days 8–14). The second week takes what you have noticed and asks whether it is true.
- **Week Three: Release** (Days 15–21). Examining the claims the mind makes, replacing false reassurance with truth, and handing over what is not yours.
- **Week Four: Practise** (Days 22–28). Building a life in which the tools have somewhere to live.
- **Review and Commitment** (Days 29–30).

*Optional, any day in Week Four: if there is a decision that has been waiting, and you have the time, complete The Faithful Decision Sheet from Lesson 18. If the response is act and the decision is difficult to reverse, arrange the review the subject requires before doing anything. If there is no such decision, skip this entirely.*
