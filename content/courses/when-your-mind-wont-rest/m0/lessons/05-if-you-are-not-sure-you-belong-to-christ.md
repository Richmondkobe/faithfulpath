# If You Are Not Sure You Belong to Christ

*This page is here for anyone who needs it. It is not a lesson to complete, and it is not a page to reopen repeatedly to check whether you believed correctly or whether your prayer was good enough.*

This course speaks throughout as though its reader belongs to Christ. It talks about a Father who knows what you need, about the peace Christ gives his people, and about a Spirit who is present in the waiting. If you are unsure whether any of that is true of you, I want you to know clearly what this course assumes. You do not have to settle every question before you may read on. So here, briefly, is what the course assumes.

God made you, and he made you for himself. Beneath the many different experiences human beings call restlessness lies a deeper spiritual truth: we were made to know God and cannot find our final rest apart from him. That does not mean every anxious thought or bodily symptom is caused by personal sin or spiritual failure. The restlessness addressed in this course may involve temperament, habit, physical health, painful circumstances or a condition requiring professional care. But no account of human peace is complete without the God for whom we were made. Augustine expressed it memorably: our hearts are restless until they rest in God (Confessions 1.1).

We have turned from him. Every one of us, in ways large and small, has chosen to live as though God were not God: trusting ourselves, serving created things and resisting his rightful rule. Scripture calls this sin. It includes our wrong actions but reaches deeper, into the orientation of the heart, and it separates us from the God for whom we were made.

God has come to us in Jesus Christ. Jesus, fully God and fully human, lived the life of trust we have not lived, and died the death our turning-away deserved, bearing on the cross the judgement that belonged to us. On the third day God raised him from the dead. Through his life, death and resurrection, God has provided everything necessary to reconcile us to himself. This salvation is received through repentance and faith in Christ, not earned by our effort.

What God asks of you is not a performance but a turning. To repent is to turn from the life lived as though God were not God. To believe is to trust, not in your ability to trust, but in Jesus: that what he did was enough, that it was for you, and that the Father who raised him receives all who come to him through his Son. There is no qualifying standard of calm, of certainty, or of feeling. Jesus met troubled and confused disciples with words of peace. The man who said, “I believe; help my unbelief” was not turned away (Mark 9:24).

If you want to turn to Christ, you can do it now, in your own words, or in these:

Lord Jesus, I have lived as though God were not God. I believe that you died for my sin and rose from the dead, and that what you did is enough. I turn to you. Receive me, forgive me, and make me yours. Amen.

You do not need to repeat this prayer until it feels perfect or until you feel certain enough. The prayer is an expression of turning to Christ; its wording and the intensity of your feelings are not what save you.

This prayer does not save you because its words are perfect. Christ saves all who come to him; he has said that whoever comes to him he will never cast out (John 6:37). If you have turned to him, rest your assurance in who he is and what he has promised, not in the strength of your feelings or the quality of your prayer. Tell a trustworthy Christian, and seek a healthy, accountable church where the gospel is faithfully taught and where you can be known, supported and encouraged.

If you are not there yet, you are welcome to read on. The course’s practical guidance remains open to you, and its Christian teaching will help you understand the life and peace it describes. Where a promise is specifically given to those who belong to Christ, the course will not pretend otherwise. The God it describes is not waiting for you to be certain before he is kind.

One more thing, because this course is about restless minds. Some readers will find that the question of whether they truly belong to Christ becomes itself a subject the mind will not leave alone: whether they believed correctly, whether they meant it, whether it took. If that is you, please do not open this page every night to check. Assurance rests in Christ and his promises, not in the quality of your feeling about them. A trustworthy pastor who understands both the gospel and the anxious conscience can help you, and Lessons 3, 10 and 16 speak to this pattern directly. The invitation of the gospel is to come, not to keep verifying that you came.
