# When This Course Is Not Enough

This course is built from my pastoral book *When Your Mind Won’t Rest*. It can help you recognise what your restless mind may be doing and practise faithful responses. It cannot treat a condition, and it is not designed to. Some of what troubles a mind is beyond what any course can reach, and recognising that is not a failure of faith. It is wisdom, and it may be the most important thing this course helps you to do.

Please seek help from a doctor, a qualified mental-health professional or an appropriate specialist service, rather than relying on this course, if any of the following is true of you.

## Thoughts of suicide or self-harm

You have had thoughts of ending your life, or of harming yourself. This includes thoughts of not wanting to be here, or of others being better off without you, even if you have no plan. Please tell someone today. If you may act on such thoughts, cannot keep yourself safe, or are in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services or an urgent crisis line now, go to a safe place, involve a trusted person if you can, and do not remain alone while you seek help. Do not wait for a routine appointment. The Finding Help Where You Live page explains how to find the numbers for your country; they change, so please check them.

> **Get help now:** open **Finding Help Where You Live** for emergency numbers and crisis directories.

## Panic or urgent physical symptoms

You are experiencing panic attacks or unexplained episodes of intense physical fear. Symptoms may include a pounding heart, breathlessness, dizziness, chest discomfort or a sense of impending disaster. A healthcare professional can assess these symptoms, rule out other causes and discuss effective treatment; panic disorder is treatable through psychotherapy, medication or both, and it is not what this course addresses. New, severe or persistent chest pain, particularly with breathlessness, sweating, nausea, faintness or spreading pain, requires urgent medical assessment.

## Severe disruption to daily life

Your ability to function has been significantly affected. If anxiety or low mood is preventing you from working, sleeping, eating, caring for yourself, caring safely for others or maintaining ordinary daily life, seek professional help promptly. If you cannot meet immediate basic or safety needs, seek urgent help.

## Severe sleep disruption

**Your sleep is severely disrupted.** Poor sleep can make a restless mind more restless, and Lessons 5 and 20 offer ordinary habits that can support sleep. But those habits are not a guaranteed solution and are not a substitute for assessment or evidence-based treatment of insomnia. If sleep problems are severe, persistent, worsening or affecting safety and daily functioning, speak with a healthcare professional. Persistent insomnia, or sleep problems with other symptoms, should be assessed by a doctor rather than managed by habit alone.

## Trauma

You have experienced trauma. If your restlessness includes intrusive memories, nightmares, flashbacks, feeling constantly on guard, or numbness following something that happened to you, the exercises in this course are not designed for that, and some of them could make it worse without the right support. Please seek a professional experienced in trauma.

## Abuse, danger and safeguarding

You are being abused, or you are not safe. If someone is harming, controlling, threatening or frightening you, or you are in danger, this course is not the response. Contact a domestic-abuse or other specialist service, or the emergency services if the danger is immediate. Safety advice may need to come before you tell family or church leaders. If a child or a vulnerable adult is at risk, please tell someone with the authority to act.

## Severe or persistent anxiety or low mood

Your anxiety or low mood is severe, persistent, worsening, or has not responded to ordinary support. Persistent, hard-to-control worry about many things, or a low mood that has settled in and will not lift, may be a condition that responds well to treatment. A doctor is the place to start.

## Pregnancy and the period after birth

**You are pregnant or have recently given birth.** New parents may experience distressing, unwanted thoughts or images about harm coming to their baby. When these thoughts are unwanted, frightening and inconsistent with what the parent wants, they may occur as intrusive thoughts, including in perinatal OCD, and should be discussed with a doctor, midwife or qualified perinatal mental-health professional. They are not the same as an intention to harm. However, thoughts accompanied by an urge or intention to act, inability to keep the baby or parent safe, severe confusion, extreme mood changes, hallucinations, delusional beliefs or loss of contact with reality require urgent medical help. Postpartum psychosis is a psychiatric emergency. Do not remain alone with the concern; contact emergency or urgent perinatal mental-health services immediately.

## Obsessive or compulsive patterns

You are caught in obsessive or compulsive patterns. If intrusive, unwanted thoughts, images or urges repeatedly cause significant distress, or you feel driven to check, repeat, confess, seek reassurance or perform mental or physical rituals until things feel “right,” that may be obsessive-compulsive disorder, including the religious form often called scrupulosity. It has established treatments, but it is not treated by more effort or more prayer, and some of this course’s exercises should not be used without professional guidance.

## When a course exercise becomes a problem

An exercise in this course has itself become a problem. If you find yourself redoing worksheets to feel certain, unable to stop a practice, or in disproportionate distress when you miss one, stop, and speak with a professional.

## If you are already receiving care

If you are already receiving care, please continue it, and let your clinician’s guidance take priority over anything here. Never start, stop or change medication because of anything in this course.

## When services are difficult to access

Seeking help may be difficult where you live: services may be scarce, expensive or far away. Where preferred care is unavailable, begin by asking a primary-care doctor, a recognised health service or a reputable community organisation what options exist. A pastor may be able to help you find and reach that help, and with your consent pastoral and professional care can sometimes be coordinated, but pastoral care should not take the place of clinical treatment that you need.

None of the above puts you outside the reach of this course, or of God. If any of it is true of you, you may still read on. You may find parts of the teaching helpful alongside appropriate care, but this does not mean that every exercise is suitable for you. Follow the guidance of the qualified professional supporting you, and skip anything they advise you not to use. And please get the care. The Christian life is not lived by carrying alone what was never meant to be carried alone, and that includes this.

## Three boundaries to acknowledge

Please read and acknowledge these three boundaries before continuing with the foundation journey. They protect the purpose of the course and remind you where additional help is available. The course records only whether each statement has been checked and the acknowledgement date. The boxes are not a safety assessment, consent to treatment or a waiver of any right. Ticking them does not tell Faithful Path that you are safe, and it does not contact help.

- I understand this is pastoral formation, not medical or psychological treatment.
- I know where to find this page.
- I understand I can stop or skip any exercise.

*You can leave this page at any time. Nothing in the course is locked, and Finding Help Where You Live and My Mind Is Restless Right Now are always open.*
