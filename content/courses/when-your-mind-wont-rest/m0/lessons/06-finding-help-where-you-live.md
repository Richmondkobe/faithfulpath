# Finding Help Where You Live

> **Get help now**
>
> **If you are in immediate danger, or you may act on thoughts of ending your life, use your country's emergency number now.** 112 in most of Europe · 911 in the United States and Canada · 999 in the United Kingdom · 000 in Australia · in Thailand, 1669 for a medical emergency and 191 for the police.
>
> If you do not know your country's number, go to the nearest emergency department, or ask a trusted person nearby to help you contact the appropriate local emergency or crisis service. Contact police when police assistance is needed and it is safe to do so.
>
> **To find a crisis line for your country tonight:** [Find A Helpline](https://findahelpline.com/) provides verified helpline information for more than 150 countries, by phone, text and online chat. The [International Association for Suicide Prevention](https://www.iasp.info/crisis-centres-helplines/) also directs people seeking crisis support to Find A Helpline. Availability and hours vary by country.

*Journal entries, reflections and check-ins are not monitored. Writing that you need help does not alert Faithful Path or contact any of the services on this page. If you need a person, contact one of the appropriate services directly.*

Several times in this course I have said that some of what troubles a mind needs more than a course, and that this page would explain how to find that help. This is that page. I have not filled it with telephone numbers, because I have learned that numbers change: a crisis line I once gave to people in Bangkok has since moved to a different number, and a page of numbers printed in a book becomes wrong one line at a time without anyone knowing. What follows instead is how to find the right service for your country, tonight if necessary, with a few examples that were correct when checked.

**Contact information last checked: 15 September 2026. Services, hours and numbers can change.**

## To find a crisis line or helpline in your country

These directories cover most of the world:

- [Find A Helpline](https://findahelpline.com/) — verified helpline information for more than 150 countries, by phone, text and online chat, filtered by what you need: suicidal thoughts, anxiety, depression, abuse and more.
- [IASP crisis centres and helplines](https://www.iasp.info/crisis-centres-helplines/) — the International Association for Suicide Prevention's crisis page, which directs people to Find A Helpline.
- [Befrienders Worldwide](https://befrienders.org/) — volunteer emotional-support centres in dozens of countries.
- [Child Helpline International](https://childhelplineinternational.org/) — helplines for children and young people, by country.
- [Lila.help](https://lila.help/) — for domestic abuse: a directory of trustworthy gender-based-violence helplines and organisations, run by the Global Network of Women's Shelters, covering almost every country.

If you have no internet, a doctor, pharmacist, hospital or church office may be able to tell you what help exists locally. It is an ordinary thing to ask.

## Some examples, correct when checked

Services change; if a number does not connect, use the directories above.

- **Thailand:** medical emergency, 1669; police, 191; Department of Mental Health hotline, 1323 (free, 24 hours); Social Help Centre, 1300. Samaritans Thailand, 02 113 6789 — press 1 for the Thai line (12.00–22.00 daily when checked), press 2 for the English callback service. The English callback is not a substitute for emergency assistance; check its current hours and callback arrangements before relying on it.
- **United Kingdom and Republic of Ireland:** Samaritans, 116 123, free day or night. In England, Scotland and Wales, 111 can help with urgent health needs that are not life-threatening; check the appropriate NHS service for your location.
- **United States:** call or text 988.
- **Canada:** call or text 9-8-8.
- **Australia:** Lifeline, 13 11 14 (24 hours).
- **New Zealand:** call or text 1737.

## To find ongoing care

For anything in "When This Course Is Not Enough" that is not an emergency, the usual first step is a primary-care doctor, who can assess what is happening, consider whether physical factors may be contributing, discuss appropriate care and make a referral where necessary. Where a doctor is hard to reach, a recognised health service, a reputable community organisation or a mental-health charity in your country will know the routes that exist. When you look for a counsellor or therapist, ask about their qualifications and whether they are registered with a recognised professional body; a Christian counsellor should have both a faith you can trust and a training you can check. Cost and distance are real obstacles in many places, and I will not pretend otherwise. Ask what is free or subsidised, what can be done by telephone or video, and whether a church or charity near you can help you reach it.

## A word about your church

A pastor can be one of the best people to help you find and reach professional help, and with your consent pastoral and professional care can sometimes be coordinated. But pastoral care is not clinical treatment and should not be asked to take its place. If a church tells you that medication is a failure of faith, that therapy is unnecessary for a believer, or that your restlessness would end if you prayed enough, that church is not applying the teaching of this course, and you may need to find help elsewhere while still belonging to Christ.

If you are reading this page because you may be in immediate danger or may act on thoughts of harming yourself, contact emergency or crisis support now. If you are frightened but not in immediate danger, contact a trusted person or an appropriate helpline and tell them plainly that you need support. Everything else on this page can wait.
