# Lesson 21: You Do Not Have to Solve Your Whole Life Tonight

**What this lesson will do for you.** By the end of this lesson you will be able to name what this course has and has not promised, choose one practice to carry forward, and identify which of the five faithful responses the present actually requires.

## Before you begin

This is the last lesson of the foundation course, and it gathers rather than adds. If the help notes in these lessons have spoken to you more than once, the next faithful step may be to contact a doctor, a qualified mental-health professional or another appropriate service; that is a good outcome of the course, not a failure of it. If you are in immediate danger or unable to keep yourself safe, do not wait for a routine appointment: use Finding Help Where You Live now. And if you remain unsure what it means to belong to Christ, the Start Here page "If You Are Not Sure You Belong to Christ" is there for you; do not turn the question into another nightly examination of whether you believed correctly enough.

## Key Scripture: Matthew 6:34 (ESV)

> Therefore do not be anxious about tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble.

The sentence Lesson 17 worked through, returned to as the course closes. Jesus assumes trouble; what he declines to do is let tomorrow's be added to today's.

## One truth to carry with you

You do not have to solve your whole life tonight. Your mind may still try: it may gather work, family, money, health, conversations, regrets and questions about God into one impossible night-time assignment. But no single night was made to carry the whole of a life, and God has not arranged it that way. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble. Each day contains responsibilities, conversations, decisions, prayers and acts of trust, but only those that genuinely belong to that day. Faithfulness is not solving everything. It is responding truthfully to what is actually before you and refusing to make tonight responsible for every day that may follow.

## The lesson

This course began with a woman I called Grace, lying awake with a mind that would not switch off. It has passed through a good many others since: Daniel stalled on a decision, Ruth ashamed of her fear, Samuel and the budget, Esther and the one-line email, Rebecca and the job offer that lapsed, Lydia carrying six people, Elizabeth praying herself more frightened, Philip living through a redundancy that had not happened, Caleb at his post, Jonathan with a set of good tools and no life to put them in. The people in these pages are illustrative figures, and no story should be read as a complete account of a particular person. But nothing about their restlessness was invented. If you have recognised yourself in some of them, you have recognised something true.

Let me say plainly what this course has and has not offered. **It has not promised that your mind will become quiet.** Some will find their minds quieter, or their difficult periods easier to navigate. Others will find that the practices help only at the edges, while the central difficulty needs individual professional care, and for them the most important sentence may be one of the help notes. None of those outcomes is a failure of faith: a mind is part of a body, bodies are not all the same, and God's grace is not measured out according to how calm we manage to be. **It has not promised that trusting God will remove fear.** Lesson 3 said that fear is an experience and trust is a direction, and the two can travel together for a long way. **It has not promised certainty** — not about tomorrow, the decision, the scan, or whether you were right. **And it has not promised that you can do this alone, or that you should.** The help notes have been there in every lesson, early, because for some this course is a companion to treatment and not a replacement for it.

What it has offered is a way of understanding what your mind is doing when it will not rest: the difference between thinking and circling, the fear beneath the thought, the body that is part of the story. Ways of recognising the particular shapes overthinking takes: the predicted disaster, the replayed past, the rehearsed conversation, the mind-reading, the certainty-seeking, the perfectionism, the carrying of everyone. Practices for renewing the mind with what is true rather than what is merely reassuring: examining the claims your mind makes, praying in a shape that does not become more worry, giving tomorrow back, deciding when discernment is finished. And a way of living: receiving peace before the answers, and building a small rhythm that holds the rest.

Underneath all of it there has been one pattern, and if you take nothing else, take this. For much of what troubles a restless mind, there are a few faithful responses, and the work is to find which one the present moment actually requires. **Act**: something is mine to do now. **Wait**: something will be mine, but not yet, and I will write down when. **Seek help**: I lack what I need, and I will ask someone who has it. **Move towards safety**: there is credible danger, so ordinary reflection stops and specialist or emergency guidance comes first; this may require a safety plan rather than an unplanned confrontation or departure. **Surrender**: this is not mine, and I will give it to God. Circling often continues because the mind has not identified the response presently required, and is trying to do all five at once, in the dark, without a pen. In other situations a person may know exactly what is required and still be unable to disengage, because of anxiety, trauma, depression, OCD or another condition. The five responses are a pastoral framework. They are not an explanation of every restless mind, and they are not a substitute for the help some restless minds need.

Here, then, is what the whole course has been trying to say, and it is the sentence I have used more than any other with the people in these pages. **You do not have to solve your whole life tonight.** Your mind may still try. It may gather work, family, money, health, conversations, regrets and questions about God into one impossible night-time assignment. But no single night was made to carry the whole of a life, and God has not arranged it that way. "Sufficient for the day is its own trouble." Each day contains responsibilities, conversations, decisions, prayers and acts of trust, but only those that genuinely belong to that day. Faithfulness does not require carrying every possible future responsibility at once.

So ask what the present genuinely requires. Is there a proportionate action to take, a defined reason to wait, qualified help to seek, credible danger requiring specialist safety guidance, or an outcome to entrust to God? Sometimes the answer will be clear and sometimes it will not, and sometimes you will know the response and still need professional help to disengage from the cycle. That is not a failure of the question; it is one of its honest answers. Faithfulness is responding truthfully to what is actually before you and refusing to make tonight responsible for every day that may follow. Every lesson in this course has ended with one action for today, and that was not a device. It was the point.

This is why the practices are small. Six movements, not an attempt to control every prayer. Three columns, not a complete life plan. One small commitment tied to a realistic cue. The smallness is not because your troubles are small. It is because the present is the only place any of us can respond, and the present is small enough to hold.

And this is why the final word is not a technique but a prayer, and the prayer is one of surrender. Not the surrender of giving up, which is the resignation Lesson 19 warned against, and not the surrender of pretending, which is the denial Lesson 14 warned against. It is the surrender of the disciples in the upper room, who did not understand what was coming and were given peace anyway; of the psalmist who poured out his heart and then took refuge; of the Lord himself in the garden, who asked for the cup to pass and then said, "not my will, but yours." It is placing what you cannot carry into hands that can, tonight, and again tomorrow, as often as it needs to be done.

## Guided prayer

*You may use the words that are true for you, adapt them or remain silent.*

If you belong to Christ, you may pray the following words as an expression of trust. If you are uncertain, pray only what you can say honestly, and return to the gospel section at the front without forcing certainty.

Father,

I have carried more than I can hold. I have taken up tomorrow’s trouble, yesterday’s regret, other people’s responsibilities and outcomes I cannot control. Some of it came from love, some from fear, and much of it became too tangled for me to separate alone. I bring it to you without pretending that I have sorted it perfectly.

Show me what faithfully belongs to me now. Give me courage to act where action is appropriate, patience where waiting has a real purpose, and humility to seek qualified help where I lack what I need. Where there is danger, lead me toward safety and toward people equipped to help me take the next possible step.

What lies beyond my role and control—the outcome, another person’s heart, the scan, the verdict, the past I cannot change and the future I cannot see—I entrust to you. When I take it up again tomorrow, receive it again without reproach.

I do not need to prove my faith by feeling calm. My hope rests in Christ, not in the condition of my mind. Give me the peace Christ gives his people: not a feeling I must produce, but the assurance that I am not left alone while questions remain unanswered.

When my mind circles again, remind me that its return is not failure. Help me notice what is happening, remember what is true, and return to the next faithful thing.

Sufficient for the day is its own trouble. Let this day be enough. Give me whatever rest is possible tonight, and if sleep does not come, keep me from turning wakefulness into condemnation.

In the name of Jesus, who prayed honestly in the garden, rested in the boat, and has not left his people orphaned.

Amen.
