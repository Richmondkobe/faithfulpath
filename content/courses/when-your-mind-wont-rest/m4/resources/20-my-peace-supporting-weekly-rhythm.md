# My Peace-Supporting Weekly Rhythm

*Worksheet 20 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 20*

> **Before you begin.** Acute crisis is not the time for an ambitious new rule: keep only what is necessary for safety and basic functioning, and come back to this later. Write it once, for the season you are in, during the day. If you find yourself rewriting it repeatedly in order to get it right, stop, keep the version you have, and review it at the date you set. If the rule becomes rigid — if missing an item produces disproportionate distress, or items must be completed in a particular way or order for the day to feel safe — stop using it and speak with a qualified professional. Medication is never changed because of anything on this page.

Use this to write a rule of life for the season you are in. Write it out. Do it once, in ordinary time; if you are in acute crisis, keep only what is necessary for safety and basic functioning, and come back to the rest later. If you find yourself rewriting the rule repeatedly in order to get it right, stop, keep whatever version you have, and review it at the date you set rather than now.

Step 1. My actual week.

Write, honestly, what an ordinary week currently contains. Do not write what you intend; write what happens.

When I pray:

When I read Scripture:

When I sleep, and how:

When my work ends, and where my phone is in the evening:

When I move my body:

Whom I see, and when:

When I rest:

What Christian community I am part of, and how:

What professional support I have, if any:

Step 2. My rule for this season.

Write one line for each. Tie each to a time, a place or a reliable cue. Keep them small enough to survive a bad week.

One practice that turns me toward God:

One practice that supports my body (sleep, movement or rest):

One boundary that protects my attention or my rest:

One form of safe relationship or support, including professional care if I need it:

Step 3. The grace clause.

Beside each line, write what I will do when I miss it.

Step 4. Where the tools live.

Write when and where, during waking hours, I will use the practices from this course that have helped me most: the Thought-Capturing Record, the Six-Movement Prayer, the later list, or another. Note that night-time gets only the brief note, never the full exercise.

Step 5. What support, if any, would help?

If there is a safe and trustworthy person, write which lines of this rule I would like their support with, and when I will ask. Write only what I am comfortable sharing. If there is no such person yet, write the first name on my Support Map.

Step 6. Review.

Write the date, about a month from now, when I will look at this again and adjust it. Write also the season this rule is for, so that when the season changes I rewrite it rather than carry it as a reproach.

> **If this rule becomes rigid—if missing an item produces**
>
> disproportionate distress, or you must complete items in a particular way or order for the day to feel safe—stop using it and speak with a qualified professional, who can help assess

what is happening. A rule of life is a trellis, not a ritual, and a trellis that has started to strangle the plant is not doing its work. Medication, if you take it, is never changed because of anything on this page. Keep this page. The weekly rhythm worksheet is in the Course Journal and Practical Toolkit, and the 30-day plan will invite you to write your rule in its final days.

---

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
