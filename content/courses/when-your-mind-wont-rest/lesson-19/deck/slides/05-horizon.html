Lesson 19 · Peace Before Answers · Slide 5 of 12

Caleb's sentence was "I can't rest until we know," and most of us have a sentence like it: I will permit myself peace when I know that such-and-such. That structure feels responsible, but it has real problems. The answer keeps moving: one scan leads to another, and each answer reveals the next thing to be known. Some answers never come at all. The condition trains the mind to keep scanning, and the watching does not shorten the wait or improve the outcome; it only ensures exhaustion when the answer arrives. And deferred rest becomes a deferred life. If the scans stayed inconclusive for twenty years, Caleb would have spent twenty years at his post, guarding a life he was not living.
