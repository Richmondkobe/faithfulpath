## One-to-One Use

Much of the above applies when a pastor or counsellor uses the course with one person. Some things are different.

Agree at the outset what the course is for in your work together, and what would lead you to suggest additional help. Revisit that agreement if the person’s need turns out to be greater than a course can meet.

Let the person choose the pace and the chapters. The course is not a syllabus, and some chapters will not be relevant.

Be especially careful with Lessons 4, 7 and 18. Lesson 4’s exercise (“If This Happened, What Would It Mean to Me?”) can move quickly into material that belongs with a trauma-informed professional; if it does, stop, and do not press. Repair, Learn, Continue or Release must never be used to encourage contact with someone who has asked for none, or to bypass safeguarding or legal processes. The Faithful Decision Sheet must never be used to authorise an irreversible decision in a single conversation; the course requires appropriate review, and so should you.

If you are a counsellor working within a professional framework, that framework governs, and this course is a resource within it rather than a method.

And if you are a pastor, remember what the course says about yourself in Lesson 12: you are a person to whom people bring more than is yours to carry. Use the Support Map. Have your own rule. Know who you would call.
