## The Worksheets Are Not Diagnosis, and Sharing Is Not Compulsory

Two errors are so easy to make in a group that they deserve their own heading.

The first is treating a worksheet as though it revealed a condition. It does not. A person who completes the Overthinking Reflection and finds that their mind goes most often to the future does not “have” catastrophising, and a leader who says so has made a diagnosis they are not qualified to make. The worksheets describe patterns in ordinary language so that a person can recognise their own; they are not instruments, they are not scored, and the course removed numeric scoring on purpose. Do not put it back. Do not invent categories of member. Do not compare members’ pages.

The second is compulsory disclosure. Every reflection and exercise in the course is written to be done privately, and several of them touch memory, fear, past wrongs and current relationships. No member should ever be asked to share what they wrote. Offer a private alternative for every sharing moment: “You may share something from this page, or you may share what the chapter made you think about, or you may pass.” Say it every week, so that passing is normal. Watch for the member who shares because the room seems to expect it, and protect them. Some of the worksheets, the Lesson 4 exercise (“If This Happened, What Would It Mean to Me?”) and the Faithful Decision Sheet especially, should generally not be shared in a group at all; use them in the week, and discuss the teaching rather than the page.

If a member does disclose something painful, thank them, do not let the group rush to fix or advise, and check in with them privately afterwards. If the disclosure is one that triggers the safeguarding, suicide or domestic-abuse guidance above, follow it.
