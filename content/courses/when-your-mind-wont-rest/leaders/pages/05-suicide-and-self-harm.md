## Suicide and Self-Harm

Some people who read this course are in more danger than the course’s tone suggests. A person who cannot rest their mind may also, on some nights, not want to be here. You may be the first person they have told.

If someone discloses thoughts of suicide or self-harm, in the group or privately, stay with it. Do not change the subject, do not minimise, do not respond with a Bible verse in place of a conversation, and do not treat it as a spiritual failure. Ask directly and calmly whether they are thinking about ending their life. Asking does not plant the idea; it tells the person you can bear to hear the answer. Ask only enough direct questions to understand whether urgent danger may be present; do not invite unnecessary descriptions or explore methods in detail, and pass what you learn promptly to the emergency or crisis professional assisting you. Ask whether they believe they can remain safe, but do not rely on reassurance alone.

If they have current intent, a plan, access to means, a recent attempt, cannot remain safe, or you remain uncertain, treat it as an emergency: contact the emergency services or an urgent crisis service, stay with the person or arrange for someone to, and do not leave them alone while help is on its way. Do not promise confidentiality; you cannot keep such a promise and should not offer it.

If they are not in immediate danger, help them reach a doctor or a mental-health service promptly, ask whether there is someone they trust who can know, and follow up within a day. Write down what was said and what you did, and tell your safeguarding lead or the person you are accountable to.

Do not create a detailed clinical safety plan independently. Contact an emergency or crisis professional and follow their instructions about immediate safety and about reducing access to dangerous means. Do not personally handle weapons or place yourself in danger.

After the immediate response, address the group without inviting discussion of the person’s private circumstances. Wherever possible, consult the person about what may be said. Share only the minimum necessary, and follow safeguarding and organisational policy.

Telling a person in crisis that suicide is unforgivable can deepen shame, fear and hopelessness. It must not form part of any crisis response, and it must not be spoken by anyone leading on this course. Offer compassionate presence, and obtain immediate help. Grace is the message. Help is the action.
