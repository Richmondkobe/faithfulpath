## Confidentiality and Its Limits

People will disclose things in a group like this that they have told no one. That is part of why the group is valuable, and it creates obligations.

At the first session, before any sharing, state the confidentiality agreement plainly. Explain that every member is expected to protect the privacy of others: what is said in the group stays in the group, and members do not discuss one another’s disclosures outside it, even with spouses. Be honest that leaders can set and reinforce this agreement but cannot guarantee the conduct of every participant outside the group, and that members should share accordingly.

Then explain the circumstances in which information may need to be shared, because confidentiality has limits and it is dishonest to imply otherwise: safeguarding concerns, serious risk of harm to the person or to others, applicable legal duties, and necessary consultation with a safeguarding lead or professional supervisor. Say that in those circumstances you will share only what is necessary and only with those who need to know, and that you will, wherever possible, tell the person first what you intend to do. Say that you will not promise to keep secret anything that would leave someone in danger.

Do not make promises about what the police, social services or health services will do with information. You do not control that, and reassurances about it may be wrong.

Keep only the records that applicable law, safeguarding procedures and professional standards require. Record factual information, decisions and actions rather than speculation. Store records only in the organisation’s approved secure system, and do not keep disclosures in an informal personal notebook; a leader’s private notebook of group members’ disclosures is a safeguarding problem waiting to happen.
