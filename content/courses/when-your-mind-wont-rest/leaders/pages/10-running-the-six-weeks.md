## Running the Six Weeks

The course was written to be walked through in six weeks, and the Start Here page “How This Course Works” suggests the shape. Six weeks for twenty-one lessons is a guided overview, not a lesson-by-lesson study: leaders select the central chapters for each week, and participants should not attempt every worksheet. One carefully chosen practice each week is enough, and the course is not completed by covering every question. If your group would be better served by more time, run it over eight weeks by splitting Weeks One and Two below. Here is one way to run the six-week version. Adapt freely; the structure matters less than the boundaries.

**Before the course.** Members read "Welcome," "How This Course Works" and "When This Course Is Not Enough." Make "If You Are Not Sure You Belong to Christ" available to anyone who wants it, without treating it as required preparation. Leaders read everything, including this section, and have the referral list ready.

Week One: Understanding the Restless Mind (Lessons 1-5). Introduce the course and read the **Group Agreement** aloud: the confidentiality agreement, its limits, the private alternative for sharing, and the fact that nobody in the room will be told their anxiety is weak faith. Discuss thinking versus circling, the shame Christians attach to anxiety, and the body. Members choose one of the five exercises for the week.

**Week Two: Recognise the Pattern, part one** (Lessons 6–9). Predicting disaster, replaying the past, rehearsing conversations, reading minds. Teach the evidence question of Lesson 6 well; it recurs throughout. Note the safety material in Lessons 8 and 9.

**Week Three: Recognise the Pattern, part two** (Lessons 10–12). Certainty-seeking, perfectionism, carrying everyone. Lesson 10 will produce the most discussion, and the most theological disagreement about guidance; keep it charitable and keep the principles on signs and impressions in view. Lesson 12 will surface carers; care for them.

**Week Four: Renew Your Mind** (Lessons 13–15). Take every thought captive, renewal versus positive thinking, what is true. Guard against turning Lesson 13 into thought-policing; the course is explicit that it is not.

**Week Five: Prayer and Tomorrow** (Lessons 16–18). The Six-Movement prayer, giving tomorrow back, deciding when discernment is finished. Pray the six movements together, once, in silence, without sharing. Do not discuss members’ decisions from Lesson 18 in the group.

**Week Six: A Quieter Life** (Lessons 19–21). Peace before answers, the rule of life, the final prayer. Invite members to write a half-page rule in the week, not in the session, and to share one line if they wish. Close with the Final Prayer of Surrender, with the introductory line for uncertain readers read aloud.

**After the course.** Offer the 30-Day Mind-Renewal Journey for those who want it, and make clear it is optional. Follow up privately with anyone about whom you had concerns. Review the course with your co-leader and your safeguarding lead.

**A few practical boundaries.** Keep the group small enough to be cared for responsibly, normally no more than eight to ten, and establish passing as an ordinary option that requires no explanation. Start and end on time; a restless mind is not helped by a group that overruns. Do not add exercises of your own, and do not import material from deliverance ministry, inner-healing methods or therapeutic modalities you are not trained in. Do not use the sessions to identify who in the group “needs help”; if you are concerned about someone, speak with them privately. And do not run this course as a substitute for your church having a proper referral relationship with local mental-health services; the course should make such referrals more likely, not less.
