## Domestic Abuse and Coercive Control

Lessons 8, 9, 12 and 18 in particular may surface disclosures of a relationship in which a member is being harmed, controlled, frightened or isolated. The course has been careful to say, each time, that safety comes before disclosure and that safety planning belongs with a specialist service. You must hold the same line.

Do not attempt mediation or couple work where abuse is disclosed. Do not advise reconciliation, submission, more prayer, or “giving it time.” Do not contact the other partner. Do not counsel the person to confront, to leave immediately, or to stay; leaving is often the most dangerous moment, and the timing is a matter for the person and a specialist. Do not disclose what you have been told to family members, general church leaders or anyone who may increase the danger; the course says, correctly, that safety advice may need to come before disclosure to family or church leaders, and that applies to you. For a competent adult, seek consent before sharing wherever possible. Exceptions may apply where children or vulnerable adults are at risk, where serious imminent danger exists, or where law and safeguarding policy require action; in those cases consult the safeguarding lead or the specialist service, and disclose only what is necessary.

Do help the person reach a domestic-abuse service, offer to sit with them while they make the call, and be one place in their week where they are believed. Before you contact them afterwards, ask whether it is safe to call, text, leave a message or send material; a monitored phone or email account can create additional danger. If children are involved, safeguarding procedures apply.

And be careful with the course’s own material here. Worksheets on “other possible explanations,” on “what is mine to carry,” on “release,” and on “faithful, wise or perfect,” can be turned by a person in an abusive relationship into reasons to excuse, to endure, or to blame themselves. The course has tried to guard against this in each place. In a group, you are the second guard. If a member’s use of a worksheet consistently produces a more charitable reading of someone who is frightening them, notice it, and speak with them privately.
