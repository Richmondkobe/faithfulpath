## A Word About Theology in the Room

The course has been careful, in every chapter, not to say that anxiety is weak faith, that healing follows sufficient prayer, that any biblical figure had a modern disorder, that Scripture’s commands about anxiety condemn involuntary symptoms, or that any of its own tools are what the biblical author had in mind. It has marked its pastoral applications as such. It has said that Christians differ on the Sabbath, on signs and impressions, on the continuing application of some texts, and it has tried not to settle those differences on the reader’s behalf.

Please hold the same line in your group. Members will bring the teaching they have received elsewhere, some of it harmful, and some of them will have been told that their restlessness is unbelief, that medication is a lack of trust, or that they would be well by now if they had enough faith. You do not need to attack those teachings. You do need to make sure they are not spoken as the group’s teaching, and that the person who has been wounded by them hears something different from you. The course’s central messages are not complicated: fear is an experience and trust is a direction; a prediction is not yet established; you may bring the same need to God as often as faithful prayer requires; Christ gave peace before the answers; you do not have to solve your whole life tonight. Say them, in your own words, until the room believes you.
