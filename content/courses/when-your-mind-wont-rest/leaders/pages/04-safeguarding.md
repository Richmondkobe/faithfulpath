## Safeguarding

If anyone in the group discloses that a child or a young person is being harmed, or that a vulnerable adult is being abused or neglected, or if anything said in the group gives you reasonable cause to believe this, you must follow your church’s safeguarding procedure and, where the law requires it, report to the statutory authorities. This is not a matter for pastoral discretion, and it is not something to pray about first and decide later. If your church has a safeguarding lead, contact them the same day. If it does not, that is the first thing to put right.

The course contains material on repairing past wrongs, on carrying other people, and on family responsibility. In a group, that material may bring disclosures of historic abuse, of current abuse, or of a member’s own harm to others. You are not equipped to investigate any of these, and you should not try. Your role is to listen, to take the person seriously, to make sure they are safe now, to follow the procedure, and to help them reach the people whose job it is.
