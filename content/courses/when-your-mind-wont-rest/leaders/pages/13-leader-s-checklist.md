## Leader’s Checklist

Before the first session, I have:

☐ Read the whole course, including every help note and this section.

☐ Completed current safeguarding and suicide-awareness training, or ensured that a co-leader has completed it.

☐ Obtained and read my church’s safeguarding and confidentiality policies, and identified the safeguarding lead.

☐ Identified where any required records will be stored.

☐ Established a safe way to contact every participant.

☐ If the group meets online, planned how to respond if someone becomes distressed and obtained each participant’s physical location and emergency-contact procedure, as organisational policy permits.

☐ Written down current local referral routes and emergency numbers.

☐ Arranged a co-leader, or named someone outside the group whom I can consult.

☐ Identified someone with whom I can discuss my own responses to the material.

☐ Prepared an explanation of the group’s confidentiality agreement, its limits and the fact that confidentiality cannot be guaranteed.

☐ Prepared a private alternative for every sharing activity.

☐ Decided which exercises will not be shared in the group.

During the course, I will:

☐ Never diagnose, score or categorise members.

☐ Never require disclosure.

☐ Never advise on medication.

☐ Never attempt mediation where abuse is disclosed.

☐ Respond to disclosures of suicide or self-harm as this section directs.

☐ Follow safeguarding procedures the same day when they apply.

☐ Refer promptly, kindly and practically, and continue appropriate pastoral care afterwards.

☐ Stop a member’s use of a practice that has become compulsive, and help them find appropriate professional support.

☐ Preserve the course’s theological boundaries in the group.

If I cannot tick every item, I am not ready to lead, and the right thing is to wait until I am.

The people who come to a group like this have often been carrying their minds alone for years. You will be, for six weeks, one of the places where they do not have to. That is a good thing to be. Be it within your limits, and let the limits be part of the care.
