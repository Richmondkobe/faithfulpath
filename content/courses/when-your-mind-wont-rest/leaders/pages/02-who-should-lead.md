## Who Should Lead

You do not need clinical training to lead a group through this course, and most of the people who will use it with others will be pastors and mature lay leaders. But you do need several things.

You need to have read the whole course, including every help note, before the first session, and to be able to say without hesitation what the course is not for.

You need to know your own limits and be at peace with them. A leader who feels they must be able to handle whatever arises is a leader who will handle some things badly. The single most important skill in this section is knowing when to say, “This is beyond what I can help with, and I want to help you find someone who can.”

Before leading, at least one leader should have completed current basic safeguarding training and recognised suicide-awareness or suicide-first-aid training appropriate to the country. Reading this guidance is not a substitute for that training; it prepares you to recognise danger and activate professional help, which is what a lay leader needs to be able to do.

You need to know the referral routes in your area before you need them: how a person sees a doctor, how mental-health services are accessed, what specialist services exist for domestic abuse, for bereavement, for eating disorders, for substance use, and what the emergency numbers are. Write them down. Keep them with you in every session. Services and numbers change; check them at the start of each course.

You need a second person. A small group on this material should have two leaders, or one leader and a designated person who is not in the group whom the leader can consult. One-to-one work should sit within the accountability structures your church uses for pastoral care generally. Nobody should do this work alone.

And you need to be honest with yourself about your own restlessness. Many people are drawn to lead on this subject because they know it from the inside. That can be a gift. It can also mean that a group member’s story lands on your own unhealed material. Have someone you can talk to, and use them.
