Lesson 6 · When Your Mind Predicts Disaster · Slide 5 of 12

Esther's pattern is often called catastrophising. It is not simply noticing that something bad could happen; wisdom includes seeing danger and preparing for it. Catastrophising is a movement. A trigger: an email, a symptom, a delayed reply. A leap to the most threatening interpretation, within seconds. A chain, in which each imagined consequence becomes the basis for the next. And then real distress in the body, produced by an imagined future. It is hard to see from the inside because it is fast, and because it is plausible. Every link in Esther's chain was possible. That is exactly what made it convincing.
