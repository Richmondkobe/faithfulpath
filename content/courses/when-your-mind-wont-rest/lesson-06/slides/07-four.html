THE TOOL · FOUR QUESTIONS, IN ORDER

  1. What am I predicting?
  2. What do I actually know that supports or challenges it?
  3. What is actually happening right now?
  4. What does the present require of me?

Lesson 6 · When Your Mind Predicts Disaster · Slide 7 of 12

The tool is four questions, asked in order. One: what am I predicting? Say it as a prediction, not a fact. "I am predicting that I will be dismissed." Naming it lets it be examined instead of obeyed. Two: what do I actually know that supports or challenges it? Not what you imagine; what you know. A brief email tells you very little either way. And for health, legal, money or safety questions, ask the qualified person rather than relying on this lesson. Three: what is actually happening right now? This is the question catastrophising most wants you to skip. For Esther: I have received an email asking for a meeting. I do not know what it is about. That is all. Four: what does the present require of me? Sometimes something: reply, book the appointment, ask a question. Often nothing. Esther's present required her to eat dinner, mark some books, and go to the meeting.
