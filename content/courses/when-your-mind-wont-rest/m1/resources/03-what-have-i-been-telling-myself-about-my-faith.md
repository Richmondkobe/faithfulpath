# What Have I Been Telling Myself About My Faith?

*Worksheet 3 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 3*

> **Before you begin.** This worksheet is not an assessment of whether you have genuine faith, and completing it does not affect your standing with God. Complete it once, during the day; do not use it at night, and do not repeat it to obtain assurance. Persistent or disabling anxiety may need professional assessment.

This exercise works with the shaming sentences directly. Complete it once, carefully, rather than repeatedly.

Step 1. Write the sentences. List the things you have told yourself about your faith because of your anxiety. Write them exactly as they sound in your head, however harsh. Aim for three to six.

Step 2. Trace each one. Beside each sentence, note where it came from as best you can: a sermon, a book, a family member, a comparison with others, or simply your own conclusion.

Step 3. Sort each one. Ask which of the four the sentence is really about: a feeling, a physical reaction, an unwanted thought, or a chosen response. Most shaming sentences judge a feeling or a bodily reaction as though it were a chosen response.

Step 4. Test it against Scripture. For each sentence, write one thing God has actually said that addresses it. Use Psalm 56:3–4, 1 Peter 5:7, Romans 8:1, or another passage you know. Do not search for the perfect verse. One honest verse is enough.

Step 5. Rewrite it. Turn each shaming sentence into a truthful one. For example:

“If I trusted God, I would not feel this way.” → “I feel afraid, and I am choosing to trust God with it.”

“I should be able to pray this away.” → “Prayer is where I bring this, not a switch that removes it.”

Step 6. Choose one to keep. Select one rewritten sentence and carry it with you this week. Say it to yourself whenever the old sentence appears.

If you notice that you are returning to this exercise to make sure you have “got it right,” or to reassure yourself again about your standing with God, stop. Complete it once. Your standing with God does not depend on completing the worksheet correctly.

---

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
