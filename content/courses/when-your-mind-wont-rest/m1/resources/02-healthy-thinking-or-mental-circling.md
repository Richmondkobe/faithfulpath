# Healthy Thinking or Mental Circling?

*Worksheet 2 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 2*

> **Before you begin.** Choose one recurring subject and complete a maximum of one brief entry per day, over no more than three days. Do not record every recurrence, and do not begin a new copy for the same subject because uncertainty returns. Use it during the day; if the thought returns at night, use only the Night-Time Thought Download.

For the next three days, carry the five questions with you. Each time you notice your mind returning to a subject for the third time or more, stop and complete one entry.

The subject. In one line, what was I thinking about?

The kind. Which of the seven best describes it? (Reflection / Planning / Discernment / Problem-solving / Worry / Rumination / Obsessive thinking)

The five questions. Answer yes or no, and add one short note if it helps.

Is this producing a clear action?

Am I learning anything new?

Is this within my control?

Have I already considered this? Is continued thinking helping me obey God?

The verdict. Healthy thinking that has not finished, or mental circling?

If circling, what now? Choose one: Act. Wait. Seek help. Surrender. Write one sentence about what that would look like today.

If you find yourself repeating this exercise on the same thought in search of perfect reassurance, stop. Complete it no more than once for that thought during the day. Repeatedly checking your answer can become part of the cycle rather than a way out of it.

After three days, read your entries together. Notice which subjects appeared most often, which kind of unhealthy thinking dominates, and how often the honest answer to “Am I learning anything new?” was no. Keep this record. Lesson 4 will ask what fear sits beneath the subjects that kept returning.

---

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
