# What Has Been Occupying My Mind?

*Worksheet 1 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 1*

Most overthinkers have never written down what occupies their mind. They have only experienced it as a blur of pressure. Writing it down does three things. It slows the thoughts to the speed of your hand. It moves them from inside your head to outside, where they can be looked at. And it usually reveals that the crowd of worries is smaller and more repetitive than it felt.

Do not try to fix anything as you write. Do not argue with the thoughts or pray them away yet. Just notice.

This exercise works best over three to five days. Complete it during the day; if it is the middle of the night, use only the Night-Time Thought Download and return to this page tomorrow.

Step 1. Empty the room. Write down everything your mind has been returning to over the past week. Do not organise it. Do not judge it. Big things and small things go on the same list. A conversation, a bill, a decision, a health worry, a person, a regret, a fear about the future. Keep writing until nothing else comes.

Step 2. Mark the repeat visitors. Go back through the list and put a mark beside anything you have thought about more than three times this week. These are your repeat visitors. Many people discover that a relatively small number of concerns have been returning repeatedly.

Step 3. Describe each repeat visitor. For each one, answer briefly:

What exactly am I thinking about when this comes up?

Is there a question my mind keeps asking?

What would I need to know, or have settled, for this thought to stop?

Step 4. Notice the pattern. Looking across your repeat visitors, what do they have in common? Are they mostly about the past or the future? About other people’s opinions? About decisions? About money, health, or relationships? Write one sentence that describes what your mind seems to be trying to protect you from.

Step 5. Bring the list to God. Read your list aloud to him, slowly, item by item. You are not asking for anything yet. You are simply showing him what is in the room. Then read Psalm 94:19 once more.

Keep this page. You will return to it in Chapters 2 and 4.

---

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
