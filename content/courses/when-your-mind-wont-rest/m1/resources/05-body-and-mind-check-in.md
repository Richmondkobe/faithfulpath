# Body and Mind Check-In

*Worksheet 5 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 5*

> **Urgent physical symptoms.** Do not assume that chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, fainting, sudden confusion or another new or severe physical symptom is anxiety. Seek urgent medical assessment or contact your local emergency services. This check-in is not a symptom checker and must not delay medical care.
>
> Complete Parts One to Four during the day, once for the past week. Do not complete the full check-in at night; the six-question short version may be used then if it does not increase alertness.

> These questions do not diagnose the cause. They help you decide whether to rest, take a practical step, contact a healthcare professional or seek urgent support. If you cannot keep yourself safe, close this page and use Finding Help Where You Live now.

Complete this once for the past week, and then use the short version whenever your mind is racing.

Part One: The past week.

Sleep. Roughly how many hours have I slept each night this week? Has the sleep been restful?

Food and water. Have I eaten regular meals and had enough to drink? Have I skipped meals or eaten mainly whatever was quickest?

Caffeine and other stimulants. What caffeine-containing drinks, supplements or other stimulants have I used, how much and at what time?

Health. Am I unwell, in pain, or recovering from something? Has anything changed in medication I take?

Rest. When was my last full day off? When was my last unhurried hour?

Season. How long has this stretch of pressure lasted? Weeks? Months? Longer?

Part Two: The connection.

Looking at the past week, is there any pattern between the days my body was under most strain and the days my mind was most restless? Write a sentence describing what you notice. If there is no clear connection, write that. Not every restless mind has a physical explanation.

Part Three: One change.

Choose one physical factor you can realistically adjust this week. Not five. One. Write it in a single sentence. (This week I will stop drinking coffee after lunch. This week I will be in bed by eleven on four nights.)

Part Four: What belongs to a doctor.

Is there anything in Part One—persistent sleeplessness, ongoing symptoms, a change in medication, a season of exhaustion that is not lifting—that should be raised with a doctor or other professional? If so, write the name of the person you will contact and when.

The short version.

When your mind is racing, ask six questions:

How have I slept over the past few nights?

Have I eaten regularly and had enough water?

How much caffeine, alcohol, nicotine or other stimulant have I used, and when?

Am I unwell, in pain or experiencing new symptoms?

How long has this pattern lasted?

Is it affecting my safety or ability to function?

These questions do not diagnose the cause. They help you decide whether to rest, take a practical step, contact a healthcare professional or seek urgent support.

---

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
