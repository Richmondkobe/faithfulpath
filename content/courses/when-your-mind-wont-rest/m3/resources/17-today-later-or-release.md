# Today, Later or Release?

*Worksheet 17 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 17*

> **Before you begin.** Answer the urgency check first: if the tomorrow you have been living in contains credible danger or immediate need — abuse, violence, stalking, imminent homelessness, a symptom that should not wait, a person at risk — that item does not go into any group. Write it at the top of the page and seek safety or qualified help now. Complete the rest during the day, once for one matter; do not begin a full sorting in bed.

Use this for one tomorrow at a time: the one you have been living in most. Write it out the first time. Complete it once for that matter today; if you notice yourself returning to redo it in order to feel certain that the sorting is correct, stop, and use the later list’s review time instead.

Step 1. Name the tomorrow.

Write, in one or two plain sentences, the future you have been living in. Include the whole chain if there is one.

Step 2. Check for urgency.

Does this tomorrow contain credible danger or immediate need: abuse, violence, stalking, imminent homelessness, a symptom that should not wait, a person at risk? If so, write it at the top of the page under Urgent, and write the safety step or the qualified help you will seek now. Do not continue sorting until that is written.



**Safeguarding exception.** If the feared future concerns abuse, violence, stalking, homelessness or another credible danger, do not place it under “release” merely because the worst outcome has not yet happened. Safety planning is present action and should involve appropriate professional support: a domestic-abuse service, the police, a housing or social service, a doctor. The rest of this exercise is for worry that outruns the evidence, not for danger the evidence supports.

Step 3. Test it.

Take the four questions from Lesson 6 in turn and answer each in writing.

What am I predicting? Break the chain into its separate links.

What evidence supports or challenges each link? Mark each one: established, probable, possible, or resting on an earlier link. Remember that “not yet established” is not the same as “false.”

What is happening now?

What does the present require, if anything?

Step 4. List what you have been carrying.

Write down each thing you have found yourself doing, deciding, feeling or rehearsing in that tomorrow. One line each.

Step 5. Sort each item.

Beside each line, write today, later or release for now.

Today: a real responsibility that exists now. Write the action or the help to be sought.

Later: will need action, but not yet. Write the trigger: the date, result, decision or piece of information that makes it today’s business.

Release for now: not presently actionable. Write briefly why no action is appropriate now: insufficient evidence, dependence on an earlier event that has not happened, no responsibility of mine, or an outcome beyond my control. Writing the reason keeps release from becoming dismissal.

Check the sorting once, honestly: is anything under “later” really today’s, moved there because it is frightening? Is anything under “release” really “seek help”?

Step 6. Decide the review.

Write when you will look at the later list next. A date, or an event. Not “whenever I think of it.”

Step 7. Give tomorrow back.

Write a short prayer, in your own words, handing over what has been released and whatever else remains of that tomorrow. Then close the page and return to the day.

If your tomorrow is a real and present hardship, such as money that will not stretch, housing that is not secure, or a situation in which you are not safe, the “urgent” and “today” columns may be long and their actions may need other people. That is not a failure of the exercise or of your faith. It is the exercise doing what it is for: showing you what needs help now, so that you can seek it. Please do. And if the sorting is done and you still feel anxious, that does not mean it was done wrongly. Keep this page. The Today, Later or Release worksheet is in the Course Journal and Practical Toolkit, and the 30-day plan will return to it in the third week, alongside the Six-Movement Prayer.

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
