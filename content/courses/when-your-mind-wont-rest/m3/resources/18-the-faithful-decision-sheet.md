# The Faithful Decision Sheet

*Worksheet 18 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 18*

> **PRIVATE — NOT FOR GROUP SHARING.** This sheet is yours. It is not monitored, included in analytics or shown in ordinary administrator completion views, and you can edit or delete it. You are not required to disclose it to a group leader, pastor, spouse or other participant; you may discuss the relevant facts with an adviser without showing the page.
>
> **Safety first.** If the decision concerns a relationship in which you are harmed, controlled or threatened, stop the ordinary exercise and contact a specialist domestic-abuse service from a safe device or place. Moving to safety means following an individual safety plan; it does not necessarily mean leaving, confronting the person or announcing your decision, and do not announce a plan if that could increase danger. If danger is immediate, contact emergency services now.
>
> Decisions with serious medical, legal, financial or immigration consequences need qualified advice. Complete this sheet once, for one decision, during the day, at a time when you think clearly. Stop if distress rises. Do not redo it to obtain certainty.

Use this for one decision at a time, the one that has been waiting longest. Write it out. Complete it once; if you find yourself returning to redo it because the answer did not bring the feeling you wanted, stop, and read the “Practical Application” section again instead.

Question 1. What decision is actually before me?

One sentence, with a yes or a no in it, or with named options. Write separately any neighbouring decisions that can wait until this one is made.

Question 2. Is any option morally or legally impermissible?

For each option: does Scripture clearly prohibit or require it? Are there ethical obligations, promises, laws, professional duties or effects on vulnerable people that must be considered? If an option is ruled out, cross it off. If the interpretation is disputed, write the dispute down; do not declare it settled. If nothing is ruled out, write: These are morally permissible options. This is a matter of wisdom.

Question 3. What facts and relevant expertise do I have?

List what is established, what is probable, what has been said and by whom, and what competence you and others bring. Note any field in which you are not qualified.

Question 4. What important information remains obtainable?

Is anything genuinely missing that would change this decision, and can it be obtained within a reasonable time? If yes, write how, and pause the sheet until you have it. If no, write: I have what I need.

Question 5. Who is affected, and who has a legitimate voice?

For each option, write who will be materially affected. Then write who has a legitimate say because of a commitment you have made to them, and whether they have been consulted or only informed.

Question 6. What has relevant counsel said?

For each person consulted, write what they actually said. Note agreement, disagreement, and gaps. Note whether the subject needs a qualified adviser you have not yet consulted. If you are tempted to ask someone new, write why.

Question 7. What do I desire, and what do I fear?

Write what you want, honestly, and what the wanting tells you about yourself; not whether God has authorised it, but what it reveals. Note whether the relief you feel at one option might be wisdom, exhaustion, avoidance or passing emotion. Write what you fear, and briefly what it would mean to you if it happened. One or two lines; do not keep digging.

Question 8. What will each option require, risk and close?

For each option: what it will demand afterwards, what could go wrong, what ongoing commitments follow, and what it closes.

Question 9. How reversible is it?

For each option, write whether it can be revisited later, and how easily. Mark any option that is difficult or impossible to reverse.

Question 10. What is actually preventing a decision?

Write the honest answer or answers: missing information, courage, a particular person’s reaction, a feeling of certainty or peace, wanting someone else to decide, a sign, grief, fear of being wrong, outside pressure, something else.

My faithful response.

Circle one and complete it.

Act: I will ______ by ______ (date). If it is safe and appropriate, I will tell ______.

Wait for a defined reason: I will decide when ______ happens, expected around ______, and I will review on ______.

Seek qualified help: I will ask ______ about ______ this week.

Move to safety: I will contact ______ (emergency or specialist service) and will not announce a plan until I have their advice.

Surrender: This decision or its outcome belongs to ______, and I release it.

> **If the response is act and the decision is major or difficult to reverse,** seek the kind of review the subject requires before doing anything: the relevant person or professional, bounded, competent, and not repeated in search of certainty. Then decide. Keep this page. The Faithful Decision Sheet is in the Course Journal and Practical Toolkit, and the 30-day plan offers it as an optional exercise in the fourth week.

---

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
