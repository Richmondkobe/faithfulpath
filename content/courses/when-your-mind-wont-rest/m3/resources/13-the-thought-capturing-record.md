# The Thought-Capturing Record

*Worksheet 13 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 13*

> **Before you begin.** This record is for thoughts that make a claim you can examine. It is not for intrusive thoughts — sudden, unwanted, distressing thoughts or images — and fighting those usually makes them worse; a qualified professional can advise about appropriate support. Complete it once a day at most, for one thought, during the day. Do not use it to monitor your mind continuously, and do not use it in the middle of the night.

Use this record for one recurring thought at a time, once a day at most. It is for thoughts that make a claim you can examine. It is not for every thought, and it is not for the intrusive thoughts described in the box earlier in this chapter.

Step 1. Notice.

Write the thought as a sentence, as close as you can to the words your mind uses. (I am going to lose this client. She thinks I am useless. God has stopped listening.)

Step 2. Name.

What kind of thought is it? (A prediction. A replay. A rehearsal. Mind-reading. A demand for certainty. A perfectionistic standard. Carrying someone.) If it fits more than one, write them. If it makes no claim—if it is an image, a memory or mental noise—stop here; this record is not the tool for it.

Step 3. Test.

What does the thought claim? Write the claim in one sentence. Then answer briefly: Is it true? Is it the whole truth? Is it consistent with what God has said? What evidence supports it, and what does not?

Step 4. Refuse.

Write, in one sentence, what you will not do because of this thought. (I will not treat the loss of the client as certain. I will not withdraw from her because of what I imagine she thinks.) This does not remove the thought. It removes its authority.

Step 5. Reorient.

Write where your attention will go instead: a relevant truth, a present responsibility, or an ordinary activity. Keep it true rather than merely comforting, and do not search for the perfect sentence.

Step 6. Act.

Write the next faithful thing you will do, today, and when.

Step 7. When it returns.

Write a short sentence you can say to yourself the next time this thought arrives. (Tested this morning. Not certain. Back to the task.)

Once is enough. Complete this record once per thought per day. When the thought returns, reread Step 7 rather than redoing the record. Do not use the record to monitor your mind continuously; if you find yourself doing so, put it away for a day. And if the thoughts you are recording are intrusive in the sense described in the box above, this record is not the right tool; speak with a professional. Keep this page. The Thought-Capturing Record in the Course Journal and Practical Toolkit is a blank version for repeated use, and the 30-day plan will ask you to use it during the third week.

---

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
