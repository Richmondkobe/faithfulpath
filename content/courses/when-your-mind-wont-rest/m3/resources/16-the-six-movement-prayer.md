# The Six-Movement Prayer

*Worksheet 16 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 16*

> **Before you begin.** This exercise sets no limit on how often you pray; persistent prayer and honest lament are not unbelief. Do not repeat the sequence solely to neutralise fear, obtain certainty or make the prayer feel correct. If you feel compelled to repeat words or movements until they feel right, stop using it and speak with a professional familiar with OCD or scrupulosity.

Use this exercise for one matter at a time: the one your prayers have been circling. Write it out the first time; afterwards you may pray it in your head.

Movement 1. Become present.

Pause. Write one sentence about where you are and to whom you are speaking.

Movement 2. Name the reality.

In one or two sentences, what is actually known? Not what might happen. What is.

Movement 3. Pour out your heart.

What do I feel about it—fear, grief, anger, desire? Write it honestly and without tidying.

Movement 4. Make your request.

Write your requests, specifically, and add a line expressing openness to God’s will. If you find yourself writing the same request again within this prayer to make it feel complete, move to Movement 5.

Movement 5. Remember and discern.

Sit still for a minute or two. Write one or two things that are true about God and bear on this matter, or a verse that comes to mind; write what he has said, not what you hope he will say. Then write what is mine to do today, if anything: act, wait, seek help, or surrender.

Movement 6. Entrust the outcome.

Write a short prayer, in your own words, placing what you cannot control into God’s care. Then close the page and do what Movement 5 says.

Do not turn this structure into a rule. This exercise is not governed by a required number. You may return to the same request as often as faithful prayer and honest lament require; persistent prayer is not unbelief. Use the structure when it helps you pray, but do not repeat the sequence solely to neutralise fear, obtain certainty or make the prayer feel correct. If you feel compelled to repeat words or movements until they feel right, stop using the exercise and speak with a professional familiar with OCD or scrupulosity. Keep this page. The Six-Movement Prayer is included in the Course Journal and Practical Toolkit, and the 30-day plan will invite you to use it during the third week.

---

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
