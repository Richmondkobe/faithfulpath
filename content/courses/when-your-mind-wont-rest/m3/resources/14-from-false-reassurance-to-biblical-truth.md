# From False Reassurance to Biblical Truth

*Worksheet 14 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 14*

> **Before you begin.** Complete this worksheet during the day, once for one situation. Do not turn the true sentence into a declaration to be repeated until you feel certain. Persistent low mood, hopelessness or difficulty functioning deserves assessment by a doctor rather than another attempt at thinking well.

Use this exercise for one situation at a time. Choose the one you have been reassuring yourself about most.

Step 1. The situation.

Write, in two or three plain sentences, what is actually happening. Include the facts you have been avoiding.

Step 2. The reassurances.

Write the sentences you have been saying to yourself, or that others have said to you, to make it feel better. Write them exactly.

Step 3. Test each one.

Beside each reassurance, answer: Is it true? Is it the whole truth? Has God actually said this? Mark each true, unsupported, or partly true.

Step 4. The true sentence.

For each reassurance that failed, write a sentence that is true instead. Make it honest about what you do not know, honest about what may be lost, and anchored in what God has actually promised to all who are his. If you are not sure whether a promise applies to you, consult the passage in context and, if necessary, ask one appropriately informed pastor or Bible teacher. Do not continue asking different people solely in search of a more comforting interpretation.

Step 5. What the truth permits.

Write one or two things the true sentences now allow you to do that the reassurances did not: a conversation, a plan, a look at the accounts, a prayer that says what is the case.

Step 6. What I will say when the fear returns.

Choose one true sentence from Step 4 and write it on its own line. This is what you will say, not louder, but plainly, when the situation comes to mind.

Once is enough. Complete this exercise once per situation. When the fear returns, reread Step 6 rather than redoing the test. Do not turn the true sentence into a new declaration to be repeated until you feel certain; its value is that it is true, not that it produces a feeling. If you cannot find any true sentence that you can live in, that is worth bringing to a pastor or counsellor rather than working at alone. Keep this page. The Thought-Capturing Record from Lesson 13 and this exercise work together, and Lesson 15 will give you a fuller test for what counts as true.

---

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
