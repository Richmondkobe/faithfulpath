Lesson 15 · Think About What Is True · Slide 5 of 12

Andrew's question deserves an answer. He was not, mostly, reading lies. So why did it feel like poison? Because being accurate is necessary, but it is not the only question that governs sustained attention. Material can be accurate but incomplete: a mind fed only on possibilities of collapse is given a picture from which the ordinary, the stable and the good have been removed. Accurate but unresolved: a prediction is not yet an established fact. Accurate but outside any faithful response, producing the feeling of responsibility without the possibility of action, one of the most exhausting states a mind can be in. Accurate but structured around alarm, arranged for urgency rather than proportion. And accurate while carrying an unsupported claim about God. Andrew's evenings failed on nearly every count, and underneath them ran a claim he had never examined: that the future was in the hands of markets and ministers, and God was, at best, a spectator.
