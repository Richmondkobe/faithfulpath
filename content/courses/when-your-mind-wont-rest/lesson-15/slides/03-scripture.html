Our verse is Philippians 4, verse 8, in the English Standard Version. "Whatever is true, whatever is honorable, whatever is just, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely, whatever is commendable, if there is any excellence, if there is anything worthy of praise, think about these things." This verse is often quoted alone, and alone it can sound like a recommendation to think about pleasant things. Notice where it sits. Paul is in prison, uncertain whether he will be released or executed. He has just told the church not to be anxious but to pray, and promised that God's peace will guard their hearts and minds. Two things follow. The list is not a list of pleasant things; truth comes first, and "just" and "honourable" are not always comfortable. And the verb means considering, reckoning, giving sustained attention. Paul is not describing the involuntary arrival of thoughts. He is describing what the mind is deliberately given to dwell on.
