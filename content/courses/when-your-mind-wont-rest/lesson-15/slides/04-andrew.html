MEET ANDREW

"Everything I read is probably true. I am not reading lies. So why does it feel like poison?"

Lesson 15 · Think About What Is True · Slide 4 of 12

Let me tell you about a man I will call Andrew. He was not, by his own account, an anxious person until his mid-fifties. What changed was not his circumstances but his evenings. During a period of economic uncertainty he began following the news closely, which was reasonable; his pension was involved. But following the news became reading commentary, and commentary became forums where people predicted collapse, and by the time we spoke he was spending two or three hours each evening absorbing other people's alarm. He could not tell me what had actually happened to interest rates in the past month, because the sources he read were almost entirely about what might happen next. He had stopped sleeping. I encouraged him to see a doctor about the sleep. But we talked about the reading too. "Everything I read is probably true," he said. "I am not reading lies. So why does it feel like poison?"
