# Do I Need More Information or More Courage?

*Worksheet 10 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 10*

> **Before you begin.** This worksheet does not replace medical, legal, financial or safety advice. Complete it once for one decision, during the day, and not in the middle of the night. Fear returning is not new information. A major decision should not rest solely on an impression, a coincidence or a claimed prophetic word, and no leader may use "God told me" to control another person's decision.

Use this exercise for one decision at a time. Choose the one you have been circling longest.

Step 1. The decision.

Write it in one sentence, as a choice between named options. (Accept the post in the other city, or stay.)

Step 2. How long.

How long have you been discerning this? Is there a real deadline? Write both.

Step 3. The ordinary means.

Answer each briefly.

Scripture. Does God’s Word forbid or command either option? If neither, write “This is a choice between permissible things.”

Wisdom. What do the practical realities honestly suggest? What consequences and responsibilities does each option carry?

Counsel. Whom have you asked, and what did they say? If you have not asked anyone suitable, name a small, appropriate circle and ask them. If a question needs a professional—medical, legal, financial— name the professional. If you have already received careful answers, write them here and do not keep asking the same question of new people.

Circumstances. What do the facts actually permit or prevent?

Desire. What do you want to do? Write it plainly. Then write one sentence about what that desire might reflect—a gift, a healthy longing, a fear, or something mixed.

Step 4. The signs I have been collecting.

If you have been reading sermons, songs, verses, impressions or coincidences as signs, list them, and note whether they point in one direction or both. Then write one sentence about how much weight they should carry, given the principles in this chapter.

Step 5. What do I need: information, courage, or both?

Write your honest answer to: What information is genuinely missing, and what courage will still be required after I have it? Then mark which of these fits.

I need specific information. Name exactly what is missing, where you will get it, and by when. Return to this step once you have it.

I have sufficient information and need courage. Write what you are afraid of, in one sentence. Then write what you would advise a friend in your position who had all the same answers.

I need both. Do the first, and write the second now, so that you are ready for it.

Step 6. My present faithful response: act, wait for a defined reason, seek qualified help, or surrender.

Choose one, and write a sentence explaining why.

Act. Write what you will do and when.

Wait. Write the specific reason and the date by which the waiting ends.

Seek help. Write whom, about what, and when.

Surrender. Write what is not yours to decide or control, and hand it over.

Step 7. Decide with God.

Write a short prayer, in your own words, telling God what you intend and asking for humility and faithfulness in whatever follows.

Once is enough. Complete this exercise once per decision. Do not assume that the return of fear means the decision was wrong; fear after a decision is normal. Reconsideration is appropriate when significant new information emerges, circumstances materially change, or a trusted adviser raises a genuine concern—not because the feeling of certainty has not arrived. If you chose Wait and the date comes, return to Step 5 with whatever you now know, and do not extend the wait without a new reason. Keep this page. You will use Act, Wait, Seek Help or Surrender again in Lessons 12, 17 and 18, and the Control, Influence or Surrender worksheet in the Course Journal and Practical Toolkit is built on it.

---

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
