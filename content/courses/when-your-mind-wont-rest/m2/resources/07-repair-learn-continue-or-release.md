# Repair, Learn, Continue or Release?

*Worksheet 7 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 7*

> **Before you begin.** This worksheet is not for trauma memories. Stop if distress or a sense of reliving develops. Make no direct contact where it is unsafe, prohibited or contrary to a safeguarding or legal process. Do not use it in the middle of the night; use only the Night-Time Thought Download and return during the day. Complete it once for one event, and return only when genuinely new information emerges.

Use this exercise for one past event at a time. Choose the one your mind returns to most. Complete it once, carefully, and keep the page.

> **Stop the exercise if** the memory you are working with is of a traumatic event and writing about it produces intense distress, physical symptoms, or a sense of reliving it. This exercise is designed for regrets and mistakes, not for trauma. Close the page, do something grounding and ordinary, and speak with a qualified professional before returning to the memory.

Step 1. Name the event.

In two or three sentences, write what happened. State the facts as plainly as you can, without either excusing or exaggerating your part.

Step 2. Name my part honestly.

Was there sin in what I did? Carelessness? An honest mistake? A reasonable decision with a bad outcome? No fault at all? Write one sentence. If you are genuinely uncertain, consider discussing it once with a pastor or mature Christian who understands both Scripture and scrupulosity. If you have already received a careful answer but continue asking others in search of certainty, the repeated questioning may be part of the cycle.

Step 3. Repair.

Is there a safe and appropriate responsibility that remains—an apology, restitution, a conversation, a practical amend? If yes, write exactly what, to whom, and when you will do it. If the situation involves serious harm, abuse, legal concerns, safeguarding responsibilities or a request for no contact, write instead the name of the person or process you will consult before acting. If repair has already been done, write “Repair has been made,” and note when. If repair is not possible (the person has died, the loss cannot be recovered), write that plainly.

If repair has already been made and accepted, do not plan to repeat it.

Step 4. Learn.

Write, in one or two sentences, what truth or changed practice you intend to carry forward. Be specific. This is the sentence you will show your mind when it wants to run the scene again “so that you remember.”

Step 5. Continue. Is there an ongoing consequence or commitment you must faithfully maintain—a repayment, a boundary, an accountability relationship, a changed pattern of behaviour, a process you have agreed to? Write what it is and how you are keeping it. This belongs to the present, not the replay. If there is nothing ongoing, write “Nothing further is required.”

Step 6. Release.

Write what remains after repair, learning and continuing commitments: the part that cannot be changed. Then, if there was sin, confess it once, in writing or aloud, and write beneath it the words of 1 John 1:9. If there was no sin, write instead one sentence acknowledging that you did not know what you could not know, and that God’s providence is larger than your error. Then write one sentence handing what remains to him.

Step 7. What I will say when it returns.

Write a short sentence, in your own words, that you can say to yourself the next time the replay begins. (Repair is made, the lesson is written, I am keeping what I promised, and the rest is with God.)

Once is enough. Complete this exercise once per event. If the replay returns, use the sentence from Step 7 and turn your attention to the present. Do not redo Step 6 in search of a better feeling; forgiveness does not depend on the feeling. Return to the exercise only if something genuinely new has occurred or come to light. If the replay continues to intrude regularly despite this, speak with your pastor, a counsellor or your doctor. Keep this page. You will return to the Repair, Learn, Continue or Release pattern in Lesson 8, and the toolkit at the back of the book includes a version of it for repeated use.

---

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
