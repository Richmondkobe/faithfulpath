# What Is Mine to Carry?

*Worksheet 12 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 12*

> **Before you begin.** Do not stop established care or safeguarding action abruptly. Immediate danger requires appropriate emergency or crisis support. This worksheet does not determine legal or caregiving obligations, and feelings of relief or distress do not decide whose responsibility something is. Complete it during the day, once per person and situation, and do not repeat it solely because worry returns.

Use this exercise for the people currently occupying your mind. You may find it helps to do it for one person at a time, beginning with the heaviest.

Step 1. The list.

Write the names of the people whose difficulties you have been carrying. Beside each, write in a few words what you have been carrying for them.

Step 2. The five questions.

For each name, answer briefly:

Did they ask?

Do I have an established responsibility here?

Is there a serious need they cannot presently address alone?

Am I the appropriate person to respond?

Is the help I have been giving, or am considering, wanted, safe and sustainable?

Step 3. Sort the weight.

For each name, write one of the following, and one sentence explaining why.

Carry. This responsibility properly belongs to my role.

Share. This is too heavy for one person and I can appropriately help, in a specific and bounded way.

Offer. I may offer support, but the other person retains the choice.

Refer. This needs someone more qualified or authorised than me.

Release. I have no appropriate action, or my part is complete.

Be honest in both directions. Do not choose release to escape a duty, and do not choose carry because putting it down feels unbearable.

Step 4. The faithful response.

For each name, choose one and write what it means in practice.

Act. What is mine to carry, share or offer, I will do, today, and no more than today requires. Write what.

Wait. Help may be appropriate but is not yet wanted or timely. Write what I am waiting for.

Seek help. This person needs someone I am not, or the load that is mine is too heavy to carry alone. Write who I will point them to, or whom I will ask.

Surrender. This is not mine, or my part is complete. Write the sentence you will say when the weight returns. (My son’s marriage belongs to him, his wife and God. I have offered what was mine to offer.)

Step 5. The load that is mine.

Now write, in a few lines, what actually is yours to carry at the moment: your own conduct, your own decisions, the people who genuinely depend on you. Ask whether any of it has been neglected while you carried the rest. If the load that is yours is genuinely heavy, write what support it needs and whom you will ask.

Step 6. Hand them over.

Read your list aloud, one name at a time, and after each say: I entrust them to you. Then close the page.

Once is enough. Complete this exercise once per person, and reread Step 4 when the carrying returns rather than redoing the sort. Do not use the return of worry as evidence that you should pick the person back up. If sorting a particular person leaves you distressed rather than lighter, that may mean the load is genuinely heavy, and it is worth talking to someone rather than carrying on alone. Keep this page. The Control, Influence or Surrender worksheet in the Course Journal and Practical Toolkit is a shorter version of this exercise for repeated use, and you will use the four faithful responses again in Lessons 17 and 18.

---

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
