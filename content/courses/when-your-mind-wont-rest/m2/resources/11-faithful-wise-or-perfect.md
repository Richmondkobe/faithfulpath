# Faithful, Wise or Perfect?

*Worksheet 11 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 11*

> **Before you begin.** If checking feels compulsive, produces severe distress, or is connected to diagnosed or suspected OCD, do not use this worksheet to remove checks or resist compulsions on your own; leave it unused unless the professional supporting you considers it suitable. Do not use it for safety-critical or regulated work. Complete it once for one low-risk task, during the day. Once the work is closed or submitted, do not reopen it solely for reassurance; materially new information, a genuine error report or a required correction may justify review. Nothing here asks you to lower a standard that the task, your profession or someone's safety genuinely sets.

Use this exercise for one task at a time. Choose something you are currently redoing, over-checking or unable to finish. If checking is compulsive or connected to OCD, please read the box earlier in this chapter before using it.

Step 1. The task.

Write what it is, in one sentence, and who it is for.

Step 2. The four standards.

Answer each briefly.

Required. What must this task have to be done safely, ethically and competently? Include any check that your training, profession or process genuinely requires. If unsure, ask a competent colleague and write their answer.

Excellent. What one or two improvements would meaningfully serve the purpose of the work?

Wise. Roughly how much time and attention is proportionate, given the benefit and your other responsibilities?

Perfectionistic. What have you been doing beyond the above? Beside each item, note honestly whether it is required, excellent, wise, or seeking a certainty the task cannot provide.

Step 3. The cost.

Write how long the task should take and how long it has taken or is likely to take. Write what else that time was for.

Step 4. What the fear says.

Write, in a sentence or two, what you are afraid will happen if you stop at the standard in Step 2. Then write what would actually happen if that fear came true, and how you would handle it using Repair, Learn, Continue or Release.

Step 5. The stopping point.

Write, as specifically as you can, the point at which you will stop and release the work. (After the required check and one read for clarity, before 5 p.m., I will send it.) Make a reasonable judgement here and do not try to make it certain.

Step 6. Release.

When you have reached the stopping point, write one sentence handing the work, and its imperfections, to God. Then send, submit or close it.

Once is enough. Complete this exercise once per task. If the urge to check or redo returns after you have released the work, notice it, name it, and ask whether anything new justifies it. If nothing does, do not act on it. Do not reopen the work to reassure yourself. If you find that you cannot stop despite this, that is information, not failure; speak with a professional. Keep this page. The Faithful, Wise or Perfect worksheet in the Course Journal and Practical Toolkit is a short version for repeated use.
