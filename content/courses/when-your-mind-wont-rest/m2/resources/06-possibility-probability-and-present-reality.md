# Possibility, Probability and Present Reality

*Worksheet 6 in the printed toolkit · Lesson 6*

> **When caution is not catastrophising.** If there is evidence of abuse, violence, stalking, coercion, a medical emergency or another immediate danger, do not use this exercise to persuade yourself that the danger is probably imaginary. Move to safety and contact appropriate professional or emergency support. The purpose of testing predictions is to improve judgement, not to silence legitimate warning signs.

Use this exercise for one prediction at a time. Choose something your mind has been forecasting this week. If several come to mind, choose the one that has cost you the most sleep. Please read the box at the top of this page, When caution is not catastrophising, before you begin.

Step 1. Name the trigger.

In one sentence, what actually happened? Describe only the event, not what it means. (My manager asked to see me tomorrow. The test results have not come back yet. My daughter did not answer her phone.)

Step 2. Write out the chain.

Set down, link by link, the sequence of consequences your mind produced, in the order it produced them. Do not tidy it up or make it sound reasonable. Write it exactly as it ran.

Step 3. Take the first link only.

Copy the first link on its own line, beginning “I am predicting that…” This is the only link you will examine. If it does not hold, the rest need not be examined at all.

Step 4. Possible?

Is the first link possible? Answer honestly. Most things are. Write yes or no and move on; this step is here so that you do not mistake possibility for evidence.

Step 5. What evidence supports or challenges it?

Write, on separate lines, what you actually know that supports the prediction, and what you actually know that challenges it. Then write two or three other explanations that could equally account for the trigger.

Now write a single sentence stating your honest judgement: does the evidence make this outcome probable, unlikely, or genuinely unclear? You are not calculating a percentage. If you cannot judge— because the matter is medical, legal, financial or a question of safety —write the name of the qualified person who could help you judge it.

If your honest judgement is that the outcome is probable, write that plainly. It is a legitimate result, and Step 7 will tell you what to do with it.

Step 6. Happening now.

Write one sentence beginning “What is actually true right now is…” Include only what you know.

Step 7. Requiring action now.

Is there a specific, bounded action the present situation asks of you? If so, write it in one sentence and, if possible, do it today. If there is nothing, write “Nothing is required of me today except…” and complete the sentence with the ordinary things of your day.

If the feared outcome is probable, this is where you write your preparation. Write the concrete steps that are reasonably necessary now. Keep them specific and bounded, and distinguish today’s actions from those that can only be taken later. When today’s steps are written, today’s preparing is finished.

Step 8. Give tomorrow back.

Read Matthew 6:34 once, slowly. Then write one sentence handing whatever remains to God.

Once is enough. Complete this worksheet once for one prediction, and then stop. If the prediction returns, briefly reread your Step 6 sentence rather than completing the worksheet again. Return to it only if materially new information changes the situation, not merely because the fear has returned or the answer no longer feels reassuring; the aim is honesty, not reassurance. Keep this page. You will use a version of this tool again in Lesson 17, and the Possibility, Probability and Present Reality worksheet in the Course Journal and Practical Toolkit is designed for repeated use.

*Complete this once for one matter, then stop. If you find yourself redoing it to feel certain the answer is right, that is a signal to put it down, not to try again. This worksheet does not treat an anxiety disorder, depression, OCD or trauma; if a professional is supporting you, their guidance takes priority.*
