MEET HANNAH

Forty rehearsals. Thirty-eight wins.
Then her mother-in-law said sorry at the door.

Lesson 8 · Rehearsing Conversations That Have Not Happened · Slide 4 of 12

Let me tell you about a woman I will call Hannah. She spent most of a fortnight preparing for a family lunch. The previous visit had ended with a remark from her mother-in-law about how Hannah's children were being raised, and Hannah had been composing her reply ever since. In the shower she explained calmly why it had hurt. By the second week she had rehearsed versions where her mother-in-law wept, versions where she stormed out, and one, late at night, where Hannah said something so precise that the older woman had nothing left to say. She held the conversation forty times, and won it thirty-eight. On the day, her mother-in-law met her at the door, hugged her, and said, "I've been thinking about what I said last time. I'm sorry. It wasn't my place." Hannah noticed she had been cold to her for the first hour, responding to remarks that had never been made. "I had a whole argument with a person who wasn't there," she said. "And then the real person turned up, and I treated her as if she'd said all of it."
