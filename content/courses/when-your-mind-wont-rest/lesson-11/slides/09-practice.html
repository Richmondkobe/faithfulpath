  2. Write what finished looks like: required, meaningful improvement, proportionate time, the checks the task needs.

  4. Stop when the standard is met, not when it feels ready.
  5. Close it. Do not reopen it for reassurance.

Lesson 11 · Perfectionism and the Fear of Getting It Wrong · Slide 9 of 12

The practice is to define "finished" before you start, and stop when you reach it. Notice, and name it: I am perfecting. Write down what finished looks like: what is required, what would meaningfully improve it, how much time is proportionate, and what checks the task genuinely requires. Do the work to that standard, and stop, not when it feels ready, because it will not, but when the standard you wrote is met. Then close or submit it, and do not reopen it solely for reassurance. If a genuine error comes to light later, use Lesson 7: repair, learn, continue, release. A mistake is a mistake. It is not a verdict on your worth. And where perfectionism has reached into prayer, as it had for Sarah: God is not marking it. Pray very short, very plain prayers, deliberately imperfect, and let them stand. "Father, here I am. This is what I have. Thank you." That is a finished prayer.
