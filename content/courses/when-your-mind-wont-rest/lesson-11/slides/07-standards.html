Lesson 11 · Perfectionism and the Fear of Getting It Wrong · Slide 7 of 12

It helps to have names for the standards we use, because the perfectionist has only one. Required: what must be done for safety, ethics or competence, set by the task or profession, not negotiable, and not perfectionism. Excellent: what meaningful improvement would serve the purpose; the extra draft that makes the talk clearer. Wise: is the additional effort proportionate to its benefit and cost, weighed against time, other responsibilities, and the people waiting? Perfectionistic: am I pursuing certainty, or protection from criticism, that the task cannot provide? The first three are high and finite, and finite standards can be reached. One caution: a perfectionist can turn "Have I been wise enough?" into a new unreachable standard. If that happens, make a reasonable judgement and stop.
