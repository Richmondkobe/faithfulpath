# Before You Say Yes — International Resources

*This is the live version of the resources page printed at the back of* Before You Say Yes, *and the support page for the* Before You Say Yes *course.*

*Last reviewed: 18 September 2026, entry by entry, against each service's own published information. Two details remain outstanding and three are being confirmed with the services directly; all five are named at the foot of this page. The emergency numbers are a starting point rather than a verified list — see the note beside them. If a number or link is wrong, please tell us at info@faithfulpathcommunity.com so it can be corrected for everyone.*

This page lists places to begin. It cannot list everything, and helpline numbers, hours and websites change. If you are not in immediate danger and can search safely, begin with the global directories below; they are maintained by the services themselves. If you are in immediate danger, contact the appropriate emergency service or move to the safest available place without delaying to search this page.

**A note on safety.** If you think your phone or internet activity is being watched, use a safer device — one the other person has never used — to visit these websites or make these calls, and do not leave this page open. If you cannot identify a safer device, contact a specialist abuse service when you can do so safely. Do not immediately delete monitoring software, clear shared accounts or change settings; visible changes may alert the person monitoring you. Some services suggest creating a new email account that nobody else knows about, used only for getting help. See Chapter 19 and *When to Seek Help* in the book, or Lessons 6, 10 and 19 and *A Note on Safety* in the course.

## Crisis and emotional support

*If you are thinking about harming yourself or not wanting to live.*

Hours, languages, eligibility, confidentiality and call charges vary. A line listed as 24/7 may still experience delays or temporary interruption. If one service does not answer and you cannot remain safe, try another service or contact emergency help. Note the difference between a crisis line (trained to respond to immediate risk), a listening service (emotional support), an emergency mental-health service (clinical assessment) and a general counselling line; they do not all provide the same intervention.

**United Kingdom** — Samaritans, an independent charity (listening service, any age): 116 123, free from landlines and mobiles including pay-as-you-go without credit, 24/7, and the number does not appear on a phone bill. Email jo@samaritans.org. Reachable through Relay UK. Face-to-face at branches across the UK and Ireland · samaritans.org. Samaritans states it will only consider telling someone else if it is seriously concerned and believes you cannot make decisions, and that it can only act then if it has identifying information. If 116 123 cannot be dialled from your phone system, 0330 094 5717 works but is charged and will appear on a bill.

## Domestic abuse, coercion and sexual violence

*If you are afraid of the person you are dating, or have been controlled, threatened, followed, hit or sexually pressured.*

**Confidentiality and contact with authorities.** Confidentiality is not absolute at many services. A service may sometimes disclose information because of a legal requirement or a serious concern about the safety of a child or another person. Entries state published confidentiality limits and police relationships where these could be verified. The absence of a note does not mean that no limits apply. Before disclosing identifying details, you may ask what information the service records, when it may contact police or child-protection authorities, and whether you can speak anonymously.

Duties to report concerns about the safety of a child are common across services and jurisdictions, whether or not an entry below states one. If this affects your decision about what to say, ask the service about its reporting obligations before giving identifying details about yourself or anyone else.

**Age.** Several services below have a minimum age. Where a country has a service for children and young people, it is listed.

**United Kingdom** — National Domestic Abuse Helpline, run by Refuge, a charity (for women): 0808 2000 247, free and confidential, 24/7. ## Forced marriage and family-based coercion

*If you are being pressured into a marriage, or fear punishment, removal or travel abroad for refusing one.* Do not approach the family, arrange mediation or announce your plans unless a specialist familiar with forced marriage has advised that it is safe. Family involvement can increase risk or accelerate travel and marriage plans.

**Specialist forced-marriage services generally operate in office hours.** If you need help outside those hours, contact a 24-hour domestic abuse service or, where there is immediate risk — including being taken abroad — the police or emergency services.

**United Kingdom** — Karma Nirvana, an independent charity supporting people affected by honour-based abuse and forced marriage (all genders, any age or faith): 0800 5999 247, free, Mon–Fri 9am–5pm. Support in Urdu, Punjabi, Hindi and English. If you cannot call, email support@karmanirvana.org.uk · karmanirvana.org.uk.

Karma Nirvana states that calls are confidential and are not recorded; that you can remain anonymous and may withhold your number; and that it never shares details with other agencies without your permission. In exceptional circumstances it may have a duty to notify the appropriate authorities — if it believes someone is at risk of serious harm, or if it receives information that a child is being abused or neglected — and it states that **even then it never shares information with your family**. You are told this in a message when you call. If the line is busy you reach a voicemail service, and they call back when it is safe to do so.

Forced Marriage Unit, a joint Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office and Home Office unit: 020 7008 0151, Mon–Fri 9am–5pm. **Out of hours: 020 7008 1500, ask for the Global Response Centre.** From overseas: +44 20 7008 0151. Email fmu@fcdo.gov.uk · gov.uk/guidance/forced-marriage. Inside the UK the FMU supports any individual. Overseas it provides consular assistance to British nationals, including dual nationals, and can refer other nationals to local organisations. Its guidance to professionals is never to attempt mediation and never to share information with the family. The FMU also publishes a business-card-sized contact card that can be carried discreetly.

*Note: many directories still list fmu@fco.gov.uk, an address predating the FCDO. Use fmu@fcdo.gov.uk.*

**Australia** — My Blue Sky, run by Anti-Slavery Australia at UTS, a not-for-profit legal service, and Australia's national forced marriage service (all genders, including people aged 14–18): (02) 9514 8115, Mon–Fri 9am–5pm. Text 0481 070 844 or email help@mybluesky.org.au — texts are answered within 48 hours, and weekend texts on the next business day · mybluesky.org.au. Interpreters available. If you or someone else is at immediate risk of forced marriage or of being taken overseas to marry, contact the Australian Federal Police on 131 237.

**United States** — Forced Marriage Initiative at the Tahirih Justice Center, an independent non-profit (regardless of age, race, class, gender, immigration status, nationality or religion): 571-282-6161 or FMI@tahirih.org, Mon–Fri 9am–5:30pm Eastern · preventforcedmarriage.org. It is not a 24-hour hotline. Outside those hours, use the 24/7 services above — the National Domestic Violence Hotline or RAINN — or 911 in immediate danger.

**Elsewhere** — lila.help lists services by country. Where no specialist service is listed, contact a domestic-abuse service and ask whether it can refer you.

- **United Kingdom:** StepChange, which covers England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland and is an approved money adviser in Scotland (stepchange.org, 0800 138 1111, Mon–Fri 8am–8pm, Sat 9am–2pm; online advice tool; British Sign Language video interpreter service). National Debtline, England and Wales only (nationaldebtline.org, 0808 808 4000, Mon–Fri 9am–8pm, Sat 9:30am–1pm, with webchat). In Northern Ireland, Advice NI (0800 0838 018, Mon–Fri 9:30am–5:30pm).
- **Ireland:** MABS, the Money Advice and Budgeting Service, free, confidential and independent, funded by the Citizens Information Board (mabs.ie, 0818 07 2000, Mon–Fri 9am–8pm; live chat Mon–Fri 9am–5pm; email helpline@mabs.ie or request a call back; 58 offices nationwide). MABS states it will not discuss your situation with anyone without your signed agreement, and you may remain anonymous; calls are monitored for training. National Traveller MABS: 0818 07 2230.
- **Australia:** National Debt Helpline (ndh.org.au, 1800 007 007, weekdays 9:30am–4:30pm; live chat weekdays 9am–8pm, with a message service outside those hours). Interpreters via TIS on 131 450; Deaf callers via the National Relay Service.
- **New Zealand:** MoneyTalks, the national free financial helpline, run by FinCap under contract to the Ministry of Social Development (moneytalks.co.nz, 0800 345 123, Mon–Fri 8am–8pm, with weekend hours — check the site for current times). Free text 4029, email help@moneytalks.co.nz, or live chat on the website. Trained financial mentors, free and confidential, also for people enquiring on behalf of someone else, and for anyone being pressured into an immediate financial decision.
- **United States:** a non-profit credit-counselling agency accredited by the NFCC (nfcc.org); verify the individual agency's accreditation.
- **Canada:** an agency accredited by Credit Counselling Canada (creditcounsellingcanada.ca); verify the individual agency.
- **Elsewhere:** look for a government consumer-protection service, a regulated non-profit debt-advice organisation, a citizens' advice service, or an independent financial-counselling body. Avoid organisations that demand substantial upfront fees or promise to erase debt immediately.

**Checking that contact is genuine.** Never share bank details or identification numbers with someone who contacted you first. Treat any email claiming to be from a debt charity but sent from a personal address as fraud. Be wary of unsolicited approaches about debt solutions.

## Finding a counsellor

Regulation differs by country, and sometimes by state or province. Before booking, check the person's qualification, professional membership or licence, relevant experience, supervision, ethical code and complaints process — the questions are in *When to Seek Help*, or in the support sections of the course.

- **United Kingdom:** the BACP and UKCP registers, both accredited by the Professional Standards Authority. Note that *counsellor* and *psychotherapist* are not protected titles in the UK — anyone may use them — so checking a register matters. Use the BACP **Register** to verify someone, not the Therapist Directory, which is a paid advertising listing. If a therapist is not on a register, that may mean they never applied, are ineligible, or have been removed or suspended; UKCP publishes complaints decisions. For CBT specifically, the BABCP's CBT Register covers the UK and Ireland.
- **Ireland:** the IACP directory and any applicable statutory register. BACP and IACP recognise each other's accreditation schemes.
- **Australia:** PACFA or ACA for counsellors and psychotherapists. Note that counselling and psychotherapy are not regulated under the National Registration and Accreditation Scheme — these are membership registers, not state regulation — so checking one matters. Psychologists are regulated: verify them through the Psychology Board / AHPRA register.
- **New Zealand:** NZAC for counsellors, and ask to see a current Annual Practising Certificate. Note that *counsellor* is not a protected title in New Zealand and counselling is not state regulated, so anyone may use the word; *psychologist* and *psychotherapist* are protected under the Health Practitioners Competence Assurance Act, and registration can be verified with the relevant board.
- **United States:** verify the practitioner's licence through the relevant state licensing board. Directories such as the ACA's or Psychology Today's can help you find names, but they do not replace licence verification.
- **Canada:** regulation varies by province. Verify registration with the applicable provincial college where the profession is regulated; CCPA membership alone is not a provincial licence.
- **South Africa:** verify through the HPCSA or another applicable statutory body.
- **Singapore:** the Singapore Association for Counselling keeps a professional register; confirm the practitioner's training and current status.
- **Elsewhere:** ask a doctor, hospital or national mental-health helpline which professional body regulates counsellors or psychologists in your country.

A Christian counselling association may help you assess faith alignment, but membership does not replace recognised clinical training or professional accountability.
