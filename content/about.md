# About Richmond Kobe

I am a pastor. I have been one for more than twenty years, in ten countries, and most of what I know about people I learned by sitting with them one at a time.

## Who I am

My name is Richmond Kobe. I am Canadian, and I have lived in Thailand for twenty years with my wife, who serves alongside me. I hold a Master of Theology from Trinity Western University in Canada, and I am an ordained minister. I grew up Pentecostal and trained at a Baptist seminary, which means I am at home in more than one kind of church and have learned not to mistake a tradition for the faith itself.

## Where I have pastored

Canada · United States · Ghana · Philippines · Thailand · Kenya · Rwanda · Uganda · Nigeria · Singapore

Different countries, the same conversations: a marriage that has gone quiet, a mind that will not switch off, a leader who is tired in a place sleep does not reach, a decision that will not resolve no matter how much prayer is poured on it. The details change. The need for someone to listen carefully and speak plainly does not.

## What I do here

**I write.** The [articles](/articles) on this site are the things I find myself saying most often. They are free, and I try to give the useful part away rather than hold it back.

**I teach.** Five short, practical [books](/guides) — on overthinking and worry, Christian dating, spiritual burnout, church leadership and preparing for marriage — and a guided online retreat, [The Christian Spiritual Reset](/membership), which walks you through the burnout book with video, guided prayer and a workbook. I have written eighteen books in all; the others are on [my author page at Lulu](https://www.lulu.com/spotlight/toptierconsultant).

**I sit down with people.** A [Talk to a Pastor](/talk-to-a-pastor) session is one honest hour, online, with a written intake before and a written follow-up after. Premarital counselling has been a particular focus of my ministry for many years, and it still is.

## What I am not

I am not a therapist, a psychologist or a doctor, and nothing on this site replaces them. Pastoral counselling and clinical care are different kinds of help, and people often need both. Where I think professional care would serve you better, I will say so. The [Pastoral Conversation Terms](/pastoral-terms) explain how a session works and where its limits are.
